## How it carries the feeling
- **The hook is a folk form:** Suiti call-and-repeat. One voice calls, the next takes over the same words, the drone never stops, and the chain grows each hook until the whole square sings. The hooks step up in loudness: -9.6, -8.8, -8.5, -8.0 LUFS.
- **A sway, not a march:** hats and trīdeksnis on 8th-note triplets; a kick on beat 3 was moved because kick on 1 and 3 started to read as soldiers.
- **The cold never leaves:** wind under everything, raw driven voices, a dark register. That keeps it from turning into a campfire sing-along.
- **The threat stays at the edge:** engines grind "across the river" and come closer in the low point, but they never set the tempo.
- **Wrong feelings kept out:** no risers and no major lift (no triumph); the kokle is struck and stamped, never an idyll arpeggio (no postcard); nothing aggressive aimed at anyone (not rage). Morning comes, but nobody has won: the drone is the last sound.

## Arrangement
- **night (8):** wind, fire crackle, the drone voice growing in, one low kokle string per bar
- **call (4):** one voice throws the call, a long held D5 falling into the cell
- **answer (4):** a second voice repeats it, as in Suiti drone singing; stamping starts; no drums yet
- **hook1 (8):** the triplet-sway beat and 808; the line passed from voice to voice, held back
- **verse1 (16):** room for a rapper; in the second half the call comes every four bars and is answered; a low engine rumble starts across the river
- **hook2 (8):** the crowd joins the end of the line; a one-beat cut ends it
- **hour (8):** the low point: the beat gone, the drone thin, one voice, the staff stamping once a bar, the engines closer; a birch-bark horn climbs its harmonics to call people back
- **hook3 (8):** the whole crowd, with the dūdas doubling the line
- **verse2 (16):** dūdas cries answer the caller; the last bar drops the beat and the whole crowd sings the call over the drone alone
- **hook4 (8):** the longest chain, the kokle struck in open-fifth strums; the loudest section
- **morning (8):** the beat leaves, the call is sung once more and still answered, the stamping fades, the drone is the last sound

## Spectre's note
"I didn't want another fist in the air; the bravest thing that happened in Riga that January was people refusing to go home. So the whole track is one idea from a Suiti village: somebody calls, somebody repeats it, everyone holds the note underneath, and the chain gets longer until the whole square is singing the call. Twice I caught the beat telling the wrong story, a jig in the first loop and a soldier's two-step in the kick, and twice the kokle tried to turn into a postcard. The best moment is one bar of nothing but the crowd singing the call before the last hook; the ending is just the drone, still going, because in the morning they were still there."
