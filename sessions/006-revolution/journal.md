# Session 006 journal (Spectre). Brief: Latvian-folklore trap, subject: REVOLUTION

## 1. SIT WITH IT
Research (four short reads): the Singing Revolution used songs as the weapon; in Suiti drone singing a caller sings the line, a second singer (the "bender") repeats the same words, and the drone singers hold one long tone underneath, then the caller starts again; and in January 1991 hundreds of thousands built barricades of trucks, logs and concrete around Riga's key buildings and held them for about two weeks, through the winter nights, around bonfires, with foresters bringing firewood, until the night of the 20th when OMON attacked the Interior Ministry and people died. What I take: the revolution I want is not a charge, it's STAYING. The barricade night: you don't attack anybody, you just refuse to go home, and you keep each other awake and warm by singing. The structure of Suiti singing is the structure of that crowd: one voice calls, the next one takes the same words and passes them on, and the drone never stops. That is the chain, the Baltic Way in miniature.
The feeling: holding the line through the night. In the body: cold feet stamping in the snow, shoulders pressed into strangers, breath you can see, and under the ribs a small steady heat that grows when the person next to you sings. Fear is there but far away (an engine across the river), and it doesn't get to set the tempo. In time: slow and endless, no clock, only the sway of a crowd arm in arm; the night is long and the song is longer. In space: a wide dark square, the fire in the middle, voices close around you, the threat out at the edge of the picture, wind over everything.
Nearest wrong feelings and what separates them: (a) cosy campfire sing-along, the closest trap: separated by the cold (wind, dark low register, raw open-throated outdoor voices with grit, no warm keys, no major); (b) triumph / anthem: no riser, no major lift, no victory, dawn comes but nobody has won, the track ends on the drone still going; (c) rebellion (003) and rage (002): no heat, no fast hats, no aggression aimed at anyone, the grit is the cold's roughness, not a fist; (d) defiance (001): that's one chin raised, this is many bodies leaning together, patience instead of attitude; (e) war / the anti-war statement (005): the threat never gets its own groove or a siren, it is only a low rumble that once comes close; (f) lament: the people are not mourning, they sing OUT; (g) march / army: no even stomp on every quarter; the body of the track is a triplet sway, people rocking arm in arm, never soldiers stepping; (h) pastoral kokle idyll: the kokle is struck low and damped like stamping, its bright ring is earned only when the crowd is full.
Decisions: 136 bpm, trap felt half-time (68), snare on 3, the hats and the trīdeksnis on 8th-note triplets: a sway, not a march. G Dorian (G A Bb C D E F) in a narrow archaic cell G-A-Bb-C with a drone on G and D; the E natural is the small warmth in the cold. The sounds that hold it: (1) the burdon, a drone voice on G that NEVER stops, from the first bar to the last, through every cut; (2) the call and the repeat, a lead voice singing a two-bar line and a second voice taking it over, that is the hook: the line being passed on; (3) the kokle's low strings struck and damped as the stamping pulse, with the trīdeksnis staff as the crowd's own drum. Later: dūdas, loud outdoor reed, when the crowd is biggest. Arc I sense: wind and fire and one voice; the second voice answers; the beat as the sway; hooks where the chain gets longer; one section in the worst hour (the engines come close, the beat is gone, only the drone and stamping hold); then the biggest chain with the dūdas; and morning: the drone alone, still standing, unresolved. Working title: "Līdz rītam" (Until morning).

## 2. THE LOOP
v001: the shape is right on paper (burdon under everything, the call passed from left to right, kokle and staff stamping, the beat swaying in triplets) but the stems say the 808 is the loudest thing in the room at -3 dB and the wind, the fire and the staff are practically silent (-51, -44, -35). Without the cold and without the stamping feet it's a warm choir over a big bass: the campfire sing-along, the wrong feeling closest to mine. Move: put the night around the people: wind and fire up into hearing, the staff up to the level of a crowd's stamp, the 808 down 8 dB to a felt weight.

## 3. THE LOOP
v002: now the wind hisses and the fire crackles around the loop, the staff stamps on 1 and 3, and the triplet sway reads clearly in the zoom (hats long-short, kick and staff on the beat). But the line itself is a gentle stepwise G-A-Bb wander in the middle register: pleasant, indoors, a song you hum, not one you send across a frozen square. Move: the caller opens with a long held D5, a call thrown out into the dark, then falls into the narrow cell; both voices get more drive so they sound open-throated and outdoors.

## 4. THE LOOP
v003: the call now carries: a long D5 thrown out over the square, then the fall into the cell, and the bender takes it on the right. But under it the loop jigs: hats on every triplet, kokle on five strokes a bar, staff, kick, it bounces like a folk dance, and a dance is a celebration, not a vigil. Move: space. The hats only lean into each beat (the last triplet before it), the kokle keeps two strokes and two pickups per bar, so the sway is slow and heavy.

## 5. THE LOOP
v004: the sway is slow and heavy now, the zoom has air between the hits and every hat leans into the next beat. But the drone, the one thing that is supposed to never stop and hold everyone up, sits 5 dB under the singers and is invisible in the picture; without it the call and repeat are just two soloists. Move: the burdon becomes the ground they stand on: 5 dB up, wide, with a low G2 under the G3-D4.

## 6. THE LOOP
v005: the drone is a floor now, a dense band at 200-400 Hz that everything else stands on, and the call-and-repeat reads as a crowd's song instead of two soloists. But the air around it is warm: the kick blooms into a long low sweep under every hit, the snare is a soft thud 10 dB under the kick, the wind is a whisper. That's a room, not a January night; it leans cosy. Move: make the air cold: the wind up 8 dB with a whistling band at 1.4 kHz, a short dry kick that doesn't bloom, and a harder, cracking snare that sounds like it's hit outdoors.

## 7. THE LOOP
v006: the air is colder now: the wind whistles through the loop, the kick is a short knock instead of a bloom. But the harder snare throws a white plume up to 16 kHz on every 3, a crash more than a hit, and a hiss tail like that reads as anxiety, not endurance. Move: the backbeat becomes the crowd's own hands: the snare short and dark (top shelved off), a dry wide gang clap layered on it, people clapping to keep warm.

## 8. THE LOOP
v007: the plume on 3 is still there; I soloed the backbeat and measured it: the snare is dark now and the clap is a whisper at -29, the plume is the trīdeksnis, its jingles ringing all above 5 kHz for 0.4 s on 1 AND on 3, so the staff and the snare make one glittering crash together. A staff is struck on the ground, it should land like a foot. Move: the staff stamps on 1 only (a soft ghost leaning into 4), shorter jingle, more thud; the gang clap up 8 dB so the hands are heard on 3.

## 9. THE ARRANGEMENT
v008: the staff now lands like a foot on the 1 and the hands on the 3; what's left of the plume on 3 is the snare's own short tail with its reverb, normal for a backbeat. On repeat the loop holds the feeling: a slow sway, a floor of voices that never stops, a call thrown into the cold and taken over by the next throat, wind over everything. Time for the whole night, first pass, 92 bars: the night (wind, fire, the drone fading in, the low kokle); one voice calls alone; the answer (bender, stamping, no drums); hook 1; verse 1 with only a tag of the call at the end of each eight, and in its second half the engines across the river, faint; hook 2 where the crowd joins the cadence; THE HOUR (beat gone, the engines come close, only the drone, the stamping staff and one voice, and at its end the birch-bark horn calls the people back); hook 3 with the whole crowd and the dūdas doubling the line; verse 2 with dūdas calls and, in its second half, the kokle ringing; hook 4, the longest chain; morning: the beat gone, the voice once more over the drone, still standing.

## 10. THE ARRANGEMENT
v009: the night is there in outline (the voice alone, the answer, the hooks, the hour, morning) but two things are wrong. The hour, which should be the drone holding under pressure, is owned by the engines at -4.6 dB, louder than anything the people do all night; that is the threat taking the stage, the war-film feeling I ruled out. And from hook 1 to the end the loudness is a flat shelf. First the hour: the engines come closer across the section but stay 7 dB under the drone at their peak, and the lone voice comes up, so what you hear is people holding, with something huge out at the edge.

## 11. THE ARRANGEMENT
v010: the hour now does what I meant: the drone wide and steady, one voice, the staff stamping once a bar, the engines a low rumble growing under it, and in its last bars only the drone and the horn. Now the flat shelf: hook 1 already has every throat it will ever have and the verses are as loud as the hooks, so the chain never gets longer. Move: the chain grows hook by hook: hook 1 is two voices held back with less drone and 808; the verses pull the 808, kokle and drone back for a rapper; hook 3 brings the crowd and the dūdas; hook 4 adds the ringing kokle, the loudest crowd and the most hands.

## 12. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
v011: the chain grows now: hooks step up from -10.3 to -8.6 LUFS, the verses sit 1-2 dB under them. Zoomed into the end of the hour, though, the birch-bark horn that is supposed to call the people back is a faint smear at 400 Hz under the drone; nobody would come to that. Move: the taure calls for real: brighter and brassier, 8 dB louder, earlier, and as a shepherd's summons, climbing the natural harmonics D3-G3 and reaching once to A3, so hook 3 arrives as the answer to it.

## 13. THE ARRANGEMENT
v012: the horn now climbs its harmonics across the end of the hour and hook 3 lands as its answer. In verse 1 the beat leaves a rapper room (kick, hands, leaning hats, the drone pulled back), but its sixteen bars are one voice calling once every eight bars into nothing; the second half only adds the engines, so the threat grows and the people don't. Move: in the second half of each verse the call comes every four bars and the second voice answers it a bar later, quietly, the chain waking up under the rapper.

## 14. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
v013: the verse's second half wakes up: the call every four bars and the second voice answering it a bar later, quiet, under where a rapper would be. Reading the roll I caught the kick doubling the snare on 3 in every second bar, kick on 1 and kick on 3: a left-right two-step, the first hint of soldiers marching, exactly what a crowd holding arms must not sound like. Move: that kick moves off the 3 to the last triplet of beat 3, leaning into 4, so the bar sways instead of stepping.

## 15. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
v014: the kick now leans into 4 instead of stepping on 3, the bar sways. Then I went to the end: after hook 4 the morning is twelve seconds of the ringing kokle plucking G3 and D4 over the drone, one call and nothing else. That's the dreamy kokle idyll, the postcard I swore to keep out, and it says the night is over and everyone went home. Move: the morning keeps the people there: the call is sung once more and the second voice still answers it, the staff keeps stamping, quieter and quieter, the low kokle struck once a bar, and the ringing kokle is gone.

## 16. MIX
v015: the morning now keeps the people standing: one more call, one more answer, the staff stamping fainter, the drone last. The intro holds too (wind, a steady drone, one low string a bar). Trial master of v015: it had to cut 400 Hz by 4 dB and still leaves +6.7 dB at 315, while it lifts 31-160 Hz by its full 4 dB everywhere. The voices stack a box at 300-400 (burdon G3/D4, three singers, the kokle body) and the low body under them is thin. Move: shift the weight down: a 5 dB dip at 330 in the burdon, a 380 Hz dip in every singer and in the kokle, and the 808 gets body at 85, 130 and 170 Hz.

## 17. THE ARRANGEMENT
v016: the trial master's moves are down to 4 dB or less (the 315 box from +6.7 to +3.9 remaining, the low body lifted only 2-4 dB). In the overview, though, the answer section with no drums at all measures -9.7 LUFS, louder than hook 1 (-10.3) where the beat arrives: the beat comes in and the room gets smaller. The people at the start of the night are few. Move: the intro voices sit lower (the lone call and the first answer 5 dB down, the drone growing in from further away) so the night starts small and the beat's arrival is a step up.

## 18. MIX
v017: the night now starts small and climbs a staircase into hook 1 (-14, -12, -11, -10 LUFS). But reading hook 3's stems, the 808 has crept up to -4.4 dB with its new body EQ, six dB over every voice, and the dūdas, the loud outdoor reed that should cut over the crowd, sits at -26, invisible in the zoom. The hooks were growing by bass, not by people. Move: the 808 down 5 dB after its EQ, the dūdas up 8 dB so the bagpipe actually rides the crowd.

## 19. THE ARRANGEMENT
v018: the dūdas now rides the hooks with the voices, the 808 carries instead of leading, and the arc reads: a staircase into hook 1, verses 1-2 dB under the hooks, every hook louder than the last, hook 4 the top. But the hour, the coldest point of the night, measures -10.7, louder than both verses; the people are as big there as anywhere. It should be the smallest they ever get, a few voices and the wind, so that hook 3 is a whole square answering the horn. Move: in the hour the drone drops 6 dB, the lone voice sits lower, the wind rises, the engines stay a rumble at the edge.

## 20. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL

## 23. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL

## 24. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL

## 25. MIX
v024: the whole crowd singing the call over a bar of nothing before hook 4 is the moment the night was for; hook 4 lands as the answer. Mastered v024 (-10.5 LUFS, -1.02 dBTP, no warnings), then listened to the master from the top: in verse 1b and the hour the sub band jumps 3-5 dB over every other section, because the engines' D1 rumble sits at 37 Hz and the master's +4 dB lift at 31-40 Hz pumps it up; that's a pile of sub nobody's laptop will play and it eats the limiter. Move: the engines lose their sub (high-pass at 70) and get a 150 Hz growl instead, so the threat is heard on small speakers as a grinding, not felt as a sub swell.

## 26. MIX
v025: on the master the hour's sub still sits 2-3 dB over its low band and verse 1b's sub 3 dB over verse 1: the high-pass is too gentle for a D1 at 37 Hz, the rumble leaks through. ## 27. MASTER AND FINAL LISTEN
v026: the engines now growl at 150 Hz instead of leaking sub, the hour has no sub at all (the hollow of the night) and verse 1b's sub matches verse 1. Mastered v026 at -10.5 LUFS, -1.02 dBTP: limiter 1.1 dB, glue 2.5 dB max, laptop loss -1.8 dB, no warnings. EQ moves all at or under 4 dB (the +4 lifts sit at 31-40 Hz under a G1-C2 808, then +3.1 at 125, -1.7 at 400, -3.2 at 16 kHz). From the top on the master: fourteen seconds of wind and fire with a drone growing out of the dark and one low string a bar; one voice throws the call across the square; a second voice takes it on the other side and the feet start stamping; the beat comes in as a sway, never a step, and the call is passed hand to hand. The verses leave room for a rapper while the chain wakes underneath and the engines start up across the river. The hour is the hollow: the beat gone, the drone thin, one voice, the staff once a bar, the engines grinding closer, and the birch-bark horn climbing its harmonics to call everyone back; hook 3 is the square answering, crowd and dūdas. Verse 2 has the reed in the chain; then one bar of nothing but the whole crowd singing the call, and hook 4, the longest chain, the kokle struck like a drum of strings. Morning: the beat leaves, the call is sung once more and still answered, the stamping fades, the drone is the last sound. It stays out of the campfire (cold, raw, the wind never stops), out of triumph (no riser, no major, nobody wins, the morning just finds them still there), out of the march (triplet sway, the kick never steps on 3), and out of the idyll (the kokle is stamped and struck, never dreamed). One more move would be explaining it. v026 is the final.
