THE VIOLIN IS THE SONG: it will be judged by a professional violinist's ear, so the idiom has to be right, not just pretty:
- Ornaments are PLACED, not sprayed: graces (crushed into the note, usually a semitone below) on strong beats and on the target notes of a phrase; trills and the "false lăutar trill" (`ftr`) on long notes; mordents on short accented notes; slides into notes and short falls at phrase ends (the Moldavian touch); runs clean and without vibrato.
- Vibrato only on notes long enough to bloom (the engine starts it after the attack and widens it); none on 16ths.
- Bowing speaks: up-bow pickups into a down-bow downbeat, short sharp strokes in fast passages, runs slurred inside one bow (`/s`), a bitten accent (`/acc`) where it matters, double stops (`/d=...`) against the open strings for weight.
- Intonation: the augmented seconds (Bb-C#, Eb-F#) and the leading tones are characteristic, not wrong notes; the engine leans leading tones up a few cents (`intonation`).
- The phrasing of the style: three-16th pickups, phrases ending on a long note with a grace or a fall, 16-16-8-8-8 and 8-8-8-16-16 cells, the scale G A Bb C# D Eb F# (F natural in descending lines), harmony i, V7 (the MAJOR dominant, D7), the diminished seventh on C#, iv, III, V/V, one chord per 2/4 bar.
- WRITE YOUR OWN MELODY. The reference score was given only to learn the style; its analysis (described, not reproduced) is in docs/JOC_TIGANESC.md. Never copy its melody, and don't quote other people's tunes.

STUDIO NOTES FOR A LĂUTAR BAND (new instruments and features; details in docs/MANUAL.md, "Lăutar band", "Tempo changes", "Other meters"):
- **`violin`** (new; use it, not the old `fiddle`): a physically modelled bowed string with a modal violin body. Articulations go after the note length: `"8:G4:2/g-1"`, `"60:D5:4/tr+1"`, `"44:A4:4/d=D4"`, `"19:Eb5:1/s"` (slurred), `~` = portamento. Also `m` mordent, `ftr` false lăutar trill, `trem`, `turn`, `sl` slide-in, `fall`, `sp` spiccato, `pz` pizzicato, `h` flageolet, `nv`/`vib`, `up`/`dn`, `acc`. Main params: `cold`, `vibrato` (0.28), `vib_rate` (5.4), `pressure` (0.7), `bow_pos`, `attack`, `ring`, `intonation`. Keep `bow_speed` at about 1.1 or under: harder bowing can break the string into a raucous scratch or an octave whistle (zoom into any note that looks wrong). A slow tempo makes your grid coarse: at quarter = 50 a 16th lasts 0.3 s, so write 32nds as half steps (`"12.5:C#5:0.5/s"`) and 16th triplets in steps of 4/3.
- **`acguitar`** (new; dry acoustic guitar, nylon by default, `"strings": "steel"` for steel): afterbeat chops with stabs `"rhythm": "..x...x...x...x."`, `stab_len` 1, `"strum": "down"`, `chop` 0.7-0.9; `hpf` about 110 Hz. **`ebass`** on the beats (`pick` 0.2, `legato` 0.6, register G2-D2).
- **Percussion:** **`toba`** (new; the tobă mare: `stroke` `boom` on the beats, `cinel`/`both` for the cymbal on top, `rim`), `kitsnare` as the small drum on the afterbeats with ghosts and rolls, `darbuka` (doum/tek/ka) for the modern wedding colour, **`handclap`** and `snap` for the dancers (use `handclap`, never the old `clap`: its `spread` is in seconds and fires off-beat hits). Percussion is essential: give it its own arc (fewer pieces early, the whole room at the peak).
- **2/4 in the engine:** a pattern bar is always four beats, so one pattern bar holds two bars of 2/4 (steps 0-7 and 8-15). Count your strains in 2/4 bars, write them in pattern bars (an 8-bar strain of 2/4 = 4 pattern bars); the ears' bar numbers count pattern bars.
- **Tempo:** set the project `bpm` to 50 and give later sections their own `"bpm"` (a number, or `[from, to]` for a ramp; sections without it fall back to the project bpm, so a ramp's next section must say its own tempo). The shared delay and the `gate` fx stay on the project bpm: avoid them in tempo-changed sections. The render prints the tempo of each section.
- **Mix and phone:** dry and close ("rece"): small reverb sends, a short room; the violin on top and centre-ish, the guitar and claps panned, bass and toba in the centre. Check the top end on a phone before mastering: `listen --phone --genre joc`; the cinel, snare wires and claps are the noise sources to shape (hats rarely need much above 10-12 kHz).
- **Master:** `studio.py master vNNN --genre joc` (the measured `rock` curve reused, -11 LUFS, -1 dBTP, translate 0.3). Read the report: no warnings, `eq_at_limit` ideally empty, `top_end_max_abs_dev_db` about 1.5 or less, the phone check without notes. The rock curve asks for 100-400 Hz body: the guitar's body and the bass give it; if the EQ boosts hard there, fix the mix. Keep the arc's dynamics (quieter opening, loudest peak): folk masters keep a loudness range of at least 6 LU.
- Per-section track levels: read `stems_rms` in renders/vNNN.meta.json (see sessions/006-revolution/stems.py); solo renders keep their real level. Nothing in these instruments has been checked by a human ear yet: judge the timbres hard (zoom, solo), and if something reads synthetic, change its settings or play it differently.
