# Session 010 journal (Spectre). Brief: JOC ȚIGĂNESC, G minor, 2/4, quarter = 50 rising to dance tempo; subject: a wedding, the climax of two souls becoming one

## 1. SIT WITH IT
**The feeling.** Three in the morning in a village hall, the masa mare long cleared, the floor wet with spilt wine. In the body: knees springing on the afterbeat, shirt stuck to the back, hands up and snapping above the shoulders, the face hurting from laughing, and under the joy a little ache that is the minor mode: this is the night two families become one, and that is serious. In time: the night doesn't sit still; it rises. Not a party button, a slow climb, the band watching the room and the room pushing the band, every round a notch faster until the tempo is something you can only just dance. In space: dry and close, a low ceiling, the band two metres away, wood and gut and skin, no PA wash. You can hear the bow hair.

**Two souls becoming one, in sound.** Two melodic souls that live in the same violin and find each other on one note. HIM: strain A, low on the G and D strings, terse and rhythmic, a cell that CLIMBS through the augmented second, G4 A4 Bb4 C#5 D5, landing on D5 with a bitten accent. HER: strain B, high on the A and E strings, flowing, with slides and Moldavian falls, a cell that FALLS through the other augmented second, A5 G5 F#5 Eb5 D5. Both cells land on the same D5, the dominant, but apart, in separate strains (rar, round 1, round 2): the listener learns they share a home. Round 3: they turn to each other, bar by bar (he climbs, she falls, he climbs higher, she answers). Round 4, the climax: one bow plays both at once as double stops in contrary motion: octave G4/G5, sixth A4/F#5, fourth Bb4/Eb5, then the two leading tones C#5 (his, from below) and Eb5 (hers, from above) pressed together as a diminished third, collapsing by a semitone each into a UNISON D5 on two strings. That unison, held over D7 with the whole band and the whole room, is the peak. After it: the V7-i cadence, a stop-time bar where the band cuts and the primaș runs alone, and one last hit together: the violin's G octave on Gm with the toba and cinel. Nothing after; the night ends on its top.

**What lifts into it:** tempo (stepped, below), percussion entering piece by piece (toba first, then the small drum, then snaps, then the room's claps, then the cinel on every 1 and the whole room), the guitar's afterbeat from simple chops to a 16th push, the violin's daring (plain tune, then ornaments, then double stops, then the fused line). The bass and the afterbeat never change their job.

**Nearest wrong feelings, and what separates each:**
- Tourist csárdás showpiece: no presto for effect (the tempo grows through the rounds, never jumps from slow to frantic in a bar), vibrato only on long notes, ornaments on strong beats and target notes, no Monti or Dinicu quotes; every phrase lands for a dancer's foot.
- Kitsch wedding band: no synth, no reverb wash, no major bounce; acoustic nylon guitar dry, a cold-ish violin, short room, minor with augmented seconds and D7's Eb on top.
- Mournful doina: the rar opening at quarter = 50 keeps the afterbeat and the bass on the beats: it is a stately dance, metred, with the tune itself, not a free lament; and it starts lifting before it ends.
- Chaos: bass and guitar are metronomic and short; only the violin is free; percussion adds pieces round by round, never all at once until the peak.
- Balkan brass: no brass, no tuba line, the toba is a village drum played soft except at the peak.
- March / stampede: the toba never pounds every beat at full force; the downbeat is lighter than the afterbeat's chop, the claps and snaps sit on the afterbeats; it hops and turns.

**Tempo reading.** Quarter = 50 as written, read as the score's first practice tempo and as the door into the room: the rar. The dance itself climbs from 100 to about 140. Plan: prelude 50, rar strain A 50, rar strain B ramping 50 to 72 (the room standing up), a primaș "ridicare" ramp 72 to 100, round 1 at 100, round 2 at 112, round 3 at 124, round 4 ramping 126 to 138, the stop-time ending at 140. Steps between rounds (as the score's ladder and as a band lifts after each interlude), ramps only where the room gets up (after the rar) and in the last round (the accelerando).

**"Rece".** Dry, clean, close, natural acoustic: nylon `acguitar` with no reverb send to speak of, `violin` with `cold` about 0.45 (enough vibrato left to bloom on long notes, but an unsentimental, clear tone), a small short room, no delay. The warmth comes from the people (snaps, claps, the band's push), not from effects. I will let the violin warm a little in the rar (more vibrato) and turn colder and more biting as the dance heats up.

**Form (bars of 2/4; pattern bars in brackets):** prelude 4 [2] · rar A 8 [4] · rar B 8 [4] · ridicare 4 [2] · round 1 A A' B B' 32 [16] · interlude 4 [2] · round 2 A A' B B' 32 [16] · interlude 4 [2] · round 3 C (call and answer) 16 [8] + A'' B'' 16 [8] · round 4 union strain 16 [8] + peak 8 [4] + B''' 8 [4] · stop-time ending 4-6 [2-3]. About 3:20-3:40.

**Chords (one per 2/4 bar).** A: Gm Gm D7 Gm | Cm Gm D7 Gm. B: D7 Gm Cm Gm | Gm C#dim7 D7 Gm. The union strain: Gm D7 Gm D7 | Cm C#dim7 D7 (the held unison) Gm.

**The sounds that hold it:** (1) the dry nylon afterbeat chop, the țiitură, which never stops from the rar to the last bar (the one constant mechanism); (2) the violin's two cells, climbing and falling into the same D5; (3) the room, as hands: snaps, then claps, then the cinel, joining in steps.

**Breaking my habits.** Reading 003, 004, 005, 006, 008 and 009: I always open with a lone lead instrument, often behind a closing filter; I always put a valley in the middle (the wall, the hour, the cost, the march, the bad-day bridge) where the beat leaves; I use a bar or half bar of silence before a slam, more than once per track; the crowd joins in steps and the last hook is the biggest; and I end by winding down, the intro returning alone (004's lone fiddle, 006's morning, 008's coda, 009's figure breaking off). Departures for this track: **(a) no valley**: the arc never goes down; the subtraction lives inside the band (pieces held back until they're earned) and in one place only, the last stop-time bar. **(b) no outro**: the track ends at its highest point, on the union and one last hit, the way a band ends the hottest round at 3 a.m., not with a fade or a return of the intro. **(c) the intro is not a filtered loner**: the primaș prelude is short and the whole band is already in the room for the rar, playing slowly. The reason: a wedding's peak is a staircase that only goes up; a valley would be a sad song, and an outro would tell us the night ended before the union did.
