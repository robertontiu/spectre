## Arrangement
- **intro1 (4):** a lone fiddle behind a closed filter; the 3-3-2 hat "clock" fades in
- **intro2 (4):** the low fiddle, chopped cobza and tobă join; the filter opens; one beat of silence
- **clockin (4):** kick, 808, snare and cobza without the melody
- **hook1 (8):** the full grind: two fiddles an octave apart, cobza, a walking 808, a dry snare crack
- **verse1a (8):** no cobza; the low fiddle holds long notes, room for a voice
- **verse1b (8):** the cobza returns; a fill and an 808 drop into the hook
- **hook2 (8):** the hook plus a cimbalom tremolo; drums cut for 2 beats at the end
- **wall (8):** the trap leaves. A hoarse nai sings a falling doina over a soft cimbalom drone while the low fiddle saws the 3-3-2 and the clock keeps ticking
- **push (4):** cimbalom tremolo swells, the tobă doubles to 8ths; half a beat of silence
- **verse2a (8):** a drill pocket (off-grid snares, sliding 808) that keeps the 3-3-2 hats
- **verse2b (8):** the low fiddle starts sawing; cobza and cimbalom drone under it
- **hook3a (8):** the fiddle with ornaments (grace notes, a scrape into A#) over the saw
- **hook3b (8):** the drill pocket, the nai doubling the fiddle; half a beat of silence
- **outro1 (4):** the band winds down
- **outro2 (4):** the intro again: a lone fiddle and the clock closing behind the filter

## Spectre's note
"I built the hustle around one limp, the old 7/16 hidden in the hats and the cobza as 3-3-2, so the beat walks like a man carrying something and never stops. The two times it went wrong were a fiddle that danced like a wedding band and a drill verse that turned into a threat. Both times the fix was putting the work back in: dig the bow in, bring back the limp. The wall is where I'm proudest. The trap leaves and a tired voice sings the doina, but the low fiddle keeps sawing and the clock keeps ticking, because the job doesn't stop when you're tired. There's no victory lap. The last thing you hear is five in the morning again."
