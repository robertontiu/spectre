## How it carries the feeling
- **The long dark:** about 2:50 of minor groove before the first breakdown; the hook is held back for 80 bars and the first drop stays under a ceiling (filtered, no pad), so the light has somewhere to come from.
- **The landing is one note:** the A-flat that hangs on the sus chord in the dark becomes A natural over F major in the release.
- **The held breath:** the breakdown grows from almost nothing, and the bar before each release is true silence.
- **The second wave goes further:** a new top note, the octave double, a choir and the rolling bass.
- **A heart, not a machine:** kick and offbeat bass take turns like a heartbeat.
- **Wrong feelings kept out:** cheesy (one motif used with discipline, a sus that has to resolve), hollow (the release is the loudest and widest moment), aggressive (rounded kick, no hard stabs), chill (the kick returns at every peak), sad trance (the lift lands).

## Arrangement
- **introA (1-16):** DJ intro: kick, offbeat bass, soft shaker
- **introB (17-32):** open hats, claps, the hook as a filtered pluck (Fm-Db-Ab-Eb)
- **dark1 (33-48):** groove; a pluck arpeggio through the minor chords, opening under a filter
- **dark2 (49-64):** gated supersaw chords and a pad
- **build1 (65-72):** snare roll, high-pass sweep, riser, one beat of silence
- **drop1 (73-96):** the held-back first drop: the minor hook on the lead under a low-pass, no pad, hanging on Ebsus4
- **bd1a (97-112):** breakdown without kick: a far pad, then the voice sings the hook at half speed
- **bd1b (113-128):** the chords turn major (Db-Ab-Eb-F), the pad opens
- **build2 (129-144):** 16-bar build: snare roll, pitch riser, sweeps, the hook as a major pluck, one beat of silence
- **rel1 (145-176):** first light: the full hook lands on A natural over F major
- **bd2 (177-192):** the voice sings a higher variant; the dark arpeggio returns in major
- **build3 (193-200):** short build, one beat of silence
- **rel2 (201-232):** second wave: rolling bass, the hook climbs to new top notes (F6, G6), octave double, choir
- **outro (233-256):** DJ outro, drums and bass, the bass filter closing

## Spectre's note
"This one started from a heartbeat, not a hook. The hardest move was keeping the hook away for eighty bars, so that the one changed note (A-flat to A natural) could feel like a door opening. Most of the late work was in the low end: the heart has to sit in your chest, not under the floor, and the clap I'd turned up in the second loop turned out to be the spike the master was fighting. I stopped when the silence before the light was black and the second wave reached a note the first never touched."
