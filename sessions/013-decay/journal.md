# 013: decay (trance, radio edit) — Spectre's journal

## 0. SIT WITH IT (before the studio)

**On alert, in the body.** Not fear. It's the back of the neck: shoulders half an inch up, weight on the balls of the feet, breath taken in and not quite let out. In time it is a pulse that doesn't give me the rest I expect (three 16ths of bass between every kick, no hole to lean into) and an event I know is coming but can't date. In space it is close and dry in front (the kick, the bass, a voice near my ear) with a long cold room behind it, so every hit has something still ringing after it. You can dance to it; you can't put your drink down.

**What decay IS here: a half-life, and a parent becoming a daughter.** Something whole halves, and halves again, and never reaches zero; and what leaves it does not vanish, it becomes something else. That gives the track a body and a mechanism:
- *The parent* is the lead's hook: an 8-note cell on a hard-edged supersaw. Every time it is restated at "one half-life" it has lost half of its notes (8, 4, 2, 1). It never reaches zero: one note is still sounding at the very end.
- *The daughter* is the voice. Each note the lead loses is picked up as a vocal chop ("hold"), so the daughter grows 1, 2, 4, 8 as the parent halves. Energy is conserved: the drop stays as full and as hard, but it is turning into something else under your feet. That is why the nerve can't stand down: nothing ever arrives, it only transmutes.
- *The unstable nucleus* is one pitch, G natural (the flat second over F#). The hook hangs on it, the voice holds it, and it never falls to F# when you expect it. The harmony refuses a major lift: F# Phrygian, i and bII, with D and Em for size, no dominant, no relative major landing.
- *The unpredictable moment*: in the second build the snare roll tightens and then *stops early*; the voice holds "hold" alone on the G over nothing for a bar and a half, and lets go when nobody chose. A tuned tick (a pluck on C#/F#, in key, on the 16th grid, part of the groove) has a hit rate that halves every two bars in the breakdown: 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. It is a rhythm instrument, not a sound effect.

**Nearest wrong feelings and what separates them.**
- *Horror soundtrack*: I keep the kick, the rolling bass and the clap; no drones, screeches or stops meant to frighten. The dissonance is one note (G over F#) inside a groove.
- *Documentary bed*: no polite arpeggio-and-pad. The lead has edge (resonance, saturation), the bass rolls on every 16th, the voice is a hard call.
- *Festival anthem / First Light*: no major lift anywhere, the breakdown's chords stay i-bII-VI-bII, the last thing heard is the unresolved note, cut off.
- *Flat wall*: intro and theme under a filter, first drop smaller than the main one (offbeat-plus-roll held under a ceiling is 007's habit, so here drop 1 is full motif but narrower: no pad, no daughter), a breakdown that thins to one tick, a real gap.
- *Geiger gimmick*: no noise clicks, no crackle. The tick is pitched, in key, on the grid, and it is the hook's rhythm thinning; decay lives in the hook's note count, the voice's fate and the form.
- *My own drift to watch*: the halving reading as a fade-out (energy leaving) rather than a transmutation. The daughter has to be harder and brighter than what it replaces.

**The arc across about 3:30 (140 bpm, a bar is 1.714 s; 124 bars = 3:33).**
intro 8 (the voice says the hook line far away over the filtered rolling bass and the tick: you know the track in 7 seconds) → theme 16 (kick, bass, the parent as a filtered pluck; the voice's chop once per 8) → build 8 → drop 1, 16 (the parent whole, 8 notes, twice; one "hold" answers) → breakdown 16 (no kick; the line "half of me is light" sung close then far; the tick halving 16→1; not a rest, a count) → build 8 (roll tightens, stops early, the voice holds the G alone) → main drop 32 (four half-lives of 8 bars: lead 8, 4, 2, 1 notes; chops 1, 2, 4, 8; bass filter opening, pad of dark voices arriving in the second half) → tail 12 (kick gone, the one remaining note and the chops, hard cut on an upbeat: no cadence).

**Tempo, key, mode.** 140 bpm (tech-trance tempo; 007 was 138). F# Phrygian-coloured minor (007 was F minor): F# G A B C# D E. Bass on F#1/F#2; the voice's notes (F#4, G4, A4, B4, C#5) sit in the voices' sweet spot.

**The sounds that hold it.** (1) The rolling `trancebass` on F#, never resting. (2) The parent: an edgy `supersaw` lead, the 8-note cell. (3) The voice as daughter: hard chops, plus the far line. Under them `clubkick`, `handclap` kept well under the kick, darkened hats.

**The voice (mandatory, as an instrument).**
- `hanami-power`: the hook line with words, in the intro (filtered, far), the breakdown (exposed, wet) and as short calls in the drops.
- `leona-power`: the chop "hold" (one word, a held vowel cut by a gate and short notes) = the daughter in the drops.
- `hanami-dark` + `leona-soft` (gender -0.5): a wordless "ah" dark choir a third/sixth apart for the breakdown and the main drop's second half; `choir` for a low layer if needed.
- Words (my own, few): **"half of me is light"** / answer **"half of what is left holds on"**; the chop word **"hold"**. Physics and feeling only.

**Break my own habits (007 and the rest).** What I keep doing: a long drum intro; the hook withheld for most of the track; the first drop under a low-pass ceiling; a breakdown that grows from almost nothing with a wordless voice singing the hook at half speed; the chords turning major; true silence before every release; the landing as one changed note; a second wave that adds (new top notes, octave double, choir, rolling bass saved for last); an outro where a filter closes; and, across the trap sessions, "the last hook is the biggest, then a wind-down". The radio form removes the DJ ends. My deliberate departures here:
1. **The hook arrives first and is then taken apart**: the voice states the line in bar 1, the lead plays the cell whole in drop 1, and the *main drop withholds what the first gave*, halving the lead every 8 bars while a different sound (the voice) takes its place. The second wave doesn't add to the hook, it transmutes it. This IS the half-life; a bigger second wave would be First Light again.
2. **The breakdown starts dense and thins** (ticks 16→1) instead of growing from nothing, and stays minor.
3. **No silence before the main drop**: the gap is filled by the held voice on the unstable note; the release is a letting-go, not a door opening.
4. **The ending cuts off** on the unresolved note, mid-phrase: a named, deliberate break (never zero, never home).
5. The rolling bass is there from bar 1, not saved for last.
