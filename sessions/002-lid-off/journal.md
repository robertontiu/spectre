# Run 2 journal: RAGE (Odell Marsh)

## 0. SIT WITH IT
Rage in the body: jaw locked, blood in the ears, breath through the teeth, hands that need something to hit. In time it doesn't wait. It leans forward, it arrives early, it repeats the same thing harder instead of saying something new, and then it snaps. In space it's close, in your face, dry, no reverb halo, no distance; the room is too small for it. It's HOT: grit, clipping, overloaded mids, hard transients, things colliding (semitone rubs, tritones), density that crowds the air.
Nearest wrong feelings: HYPE/excitement (bright supersaw euphoria, major-ish leads, festival build-drop; separated by dissonance, grind and no joy in the harmony), DEFIANT SWAGGER (half-time bounce, sliding 808 flex, space and cool; separated by no laid-back pocket, everything pushed early, no space to strut), MENACE (cold, composed, minimal; separated by heat, density and loss of control), ANXIETY (thin, high, skittery, no weight; separated by weight and blunt force: rage hits, anxiety twitches), and last session's LONGING (melodies that reach upward; so here melody hammers ONE note and falls, never climbs to resolve).
Decisions: 160 bpm, straight (swing 0.5), kick and snare pushed early. Groove family: rage-trap crossed with industrial stomp; the core loop is FULL-TIME (backbeat on 2 and 4 at 160, a stampede, not a strut), with a half-time verse to let a rapper in and then the full-time stampede comes back worse. Key: E Phrygian with the flat-5: E-F rub and the Bb tritone. The sounds that hold it: (1) a clipped, saturated 808 on E that growls in the mids, (2) a blown-out supersaw cluster stab (E+F+B, a fist of notes) hammering on 8ths, (3) a crushed, overdriven lead hammering one note and falling a semitone. Drums: a hard driven kick, a cracked snare+clap, relentless 16th hats with no swing.

## 1. THE LOOP
Heard: a wall. The spectrogram is solid from top to bottom, the onset curve only spikes on the snare; the kick stampede is swallowed by the sustained saw cluster and the long 808. It feels like a noise drone, closer to "dark and oppressive" than rage, and rage has to HIT. Also the ear guessed E major: the saturated 808 is throwing a G# harmonic and nothing in the loop says G natural. Move: make it punch. Chop the saw stabs to tight 16ths and duck them under the kick, shorten the 808 so every hit is a blow, not a hum.

## 2. THE LOOP
Shortening the stabs barely moved the picture; soloing told me why: the lead is one unbroken E5 buzz, mono-legato 16ths glued together, and the kick on its own is hard and fine. A continuous tone is a siren, it reads as alarm or anxiety, not a fist. Move: make the lead stab: half-length notes with air between them, so the hammering is felt as blows, and accents on the F rub.

## 3. THE LOOP
The lead now stabs, good, but there is still a hiss-wash over 2-10k from top to bottom and the kicks don't show as onsets in the full mix. Solos: the hats sit about as loud as the snare and the saw cluster is the loudest thing in the loop. That's the wrong pecking order; a wall of hiss reads as anxiety/static, rage is a fist. Move: put the drums on top: hats down hard, saw down, snare and kick up, so the blows lead and the noise follows.

## 4. THE LOOP
Hats down and the wash is still there, so it isn't the hats. Solos say the 808 is the loudest thing in the loop by 2 dB over the kick and 5 over the lead; that sub, hitting the bus saturation, is probably smearing into a fizz that fills every gap and eats the transients. Same mistake as last time from the other side. Move: low-end discipline now, not later: 808 down 4 dB, quick-master saturation down, so the distortion lives in the instruments where I put it, not in a bus that blurs every blow.

## 5. THE LOOP
Looked closer: the wash isn't constant, it ignites on every snare and fades just before the next one. It's the snare's own tail, an 8 dB drive holding the noise decay up for half a second, so the backbeat smears into hiss instead of cracking. A hiss that hangs is tension; a crack that stops is violence. Move: snare decay cut to a short slap and the drive pulled back so it's a hit with a hard end.

## 6. THE LOOP
Now it cracks: kicks and snares spike out of the picture and the stampede is audible as blows. But what it does to me is closer to HYPE than rage: a clean, locked, full-time workout groove, the 808 a polite sub that only the kick-flesh sits on. Rage lives in the bass you feel in your chest and hear tearing on a phone speaker. Move: tear the 808: real overdrive into the mids (growl at 200-1k) with a lowpass to keep it from fizzing, sub level held where it is.

## 7. THE LOOP
The torn 808 changes the body of it: the low band now growls and there's grit under every kick; that's closer to rage. But the ear still calls it E major, and I believe it: the saturated E1 throws a G# up into the mids and nothing in the loop contradicts it, so a stranger gets a bright power-chord sheen: hype, triumph. Move: put the minor third and the tritone into the fist: the stab becomes E-G-Bb with the F on top (a diminished cluster with a flat-9 bite), so the harmony itself is sour and hot.

## 8. THE LOOP
The sour cluster is in, but it's buried: the stab barely shows above the 808's growl, so the colour of the loop is still decided by the bass overtones and the hammering lead. The supersaw is supposed to be one of the two sounds that hold this thing, and right now it's a background chug. Move: blow it out and bring it forward: harder drive into clipping, a mid push at 1-2k where rage screams, and +4 dB.

## 9. THE LOOP
Barely moved, and I see why in the roll: every stab lands with a kick, and I had the saw ducking under the kick, so I was gating my own fist exactly when it swings. Rage doesn't duck. Move: drop the saw's sidechain so stab and kick land as one blow, the cluster riding on top of the kick transient.

## 10. THE LOOP
The fist is whole now: stab, kick and 808 land together and the cluster is as loud as the lead. But four bars of identical, locked blows is composed: it's a machine stamping, which leans menace or a workout, not a person losing it. Rage builds pressure and spills. Move: make the last bar of the loop overflow: the cluster fires on every 16th with a crescendo, the lead hammers the tritone, the kick doubles into the turnaround, so each loop ends with the lid blowing off before it slams back to the 1.

## 11. THE LOOP
The overflow works on paper: bar 4 is a pile-up of kicks, cluster 16ths and the lead hammering Bb-A up top; it feels like someone losing it. But the slam back to the 1 is the weakest hit in the picture: the pile-up runs straight into the downbeat so the 1 has nothing to stand out from. Rage spills, then strikes. Move: choke everything for the last eighth of the loop (a breath held through the teeth), then hit the 1 with an impact and a crash.

## 12. THE ARRANGEMENT
The choke-and-slam made the 1 the biggest hit on the page, three times taller than anything else; now the loop breathes like rage: pressure, overflow, a held breath, a blow. On repeat it carries; I'm leaving it. Move: build the whole body. Arc: a simmer that's already too hot (lead and cluster alone, filtered, opening up), the stampede, a half-time verse that clenches instead of relaxing (snare on 3, the 808 still stomping, room for a voice), the stampede again, an abrupt break, a second verse that won't sit still, and a last hook a semitone higher, worse, then a hard cut instead of a fade. 112 bars, about 2:48.

## 13. THE ARRANGEMENT
The skeleton stands, but the loudness line is a flat wall from 0:12 to the end: verses sit barely a dB under the hooks and are actually heavier in the low band (the long verse 808s). A stranger would go numb by the second minute; nonstop maximum isn't rage, it's fatigue. Move: make the verses clench instead of blast: 808 notes shorter and 3 dB down, the cluster muffled and pulled back, so there's pressure building under a lid and the hooks erupt out of it.

## 14. THE ARRANGEMENT
The verse-to-hook seam is right: the verse clenches with space in the 1-4k pocket for a voice, the eighth of silence, the slam. But inside the hook the kick and snare peaks come out SMALLER than in the verse: the hook is so dense that the bus limiter flattens it, so the eruption hits softer than the simmer. That's backwards. Move: drums over the wall in the hooks: kick and snare up 2 dB, the cluster and lead down 2, so the stampede punches through instead of being part of the noise.

## 15. THE ARRANGEMENT
Kick and snare now poke up through the hook a little more; it's a stampede with a wall behind it, which is where it should be. But each hook's second eight is a carbon copy of the first, and rage doesn't plateau, it escalates. Move: the back half of every hook gets worse: the 808 stops following the kick and pumps straight eighths on E like a pulse in the temples, and the snare grows a flam/pickup into every backbeat, so the heart rate climbs halfway through.

## 16. THE ARRANGEMENT
The back half of the hook now pumps: eighth-note 808 under the stampede and a flam dragging into every snare; it climbs instead of plateauing. But the last hook is still the same hook a third time, and the brief said come back worse. Move: the final hook goes up a semitone into F, the same rub and tritone a half-step higher and tighter, and it runs 24 bars instead of 16 so the end overstays its welcome the way rage does; that takes the beat to 112 bars, about 2:48.

## 17. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
The lift into F works on paper and the ear stopped calling it major. The weak spot on the page is the break: eight bars of lead and 808 just idling at -14 LUFS, it reads like someone wandering off, not someone about to explode. Rage goes quiet only to gather. Move: rebuild the break as a pressure cooker: the 808 holds one long growling E, the lead hammers the F rub through a closed filter that slowly opens and gets louder, the kick comes back on quarters, then eighths, then sixteenths, so the stampede reassembles itself before verse 2 slams in. No snare-roll build; that's festival hype.

## 18. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
The break now gathers: a growling held E with a pulse in it, then the kick reassembling quarter, eighth, sixteenth into the cut. It's dread for four bars, but it's hot dread, and it pays off. The ending though is just another hook that stops; rage doesn't fade and doesn't politely finish. Move: the outro's back half becomes pure overflow (cluster and kick on every 16th, the lead hammering the tritone), then two beats of dead silence, then one last slam that rings out alone: the final word, the door that gets slammed.

## 19. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
The silence-then-slam ending lands: a black gap and one blow ringing out. But four bars of sixteenths-on-everything blur into a flat buzz: the onset line goes dead, it stops being blows and becomes a machine-gun hum, which is numb, not hot. Move: cut the overflow to two bars and make it accelerate: eighths on the cluster and kick in the first bar, sixteenths with a crescendo in the second, so it's a fist speeding up, not a motor.

## 20. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
The ending now speeds up like a fist swinging faster, the eighth-cut, the two-beat black hole, the slam. That's the shape. Back up the timeline, hook3c is still a copy of hook3b, the warning says so and I'd feel it: the last 8 bars before the outro have to be the worst. Move: in hook3c the riff screams: the hammering lead doubles an octave up, rawer and split to the right, so the top of the mix tears open right when it should be breaking.

## 21. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
The octave scream is in and it tears the top open in hook3c. But this zoom showed me something I lost when I stretched the loop into 8-bar sections: the choke-and-slam only happens at section ends now; at the mid-section turnaround the overflow just runs into the 1 again, the exact flaw I fixed in the loop. Move: write the held breath into the patterns themselves: every 4-bar hook phrase carries its own overflow bar and its own eighth of silence before the 1, so the whole hook is blow, blow, blow, spill, gasp, SLAM, all the way through.

## 22. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
The notes stop on time at the mid-hook turnaround, but the picture shows no hole there: the 808 tails, the snare ring and the saw release fill that eighth, so it's a hiccup, not a held breath. The section-end cut is pitch black and that's the one that hits. Move: split every hook into 4-bar halves so each phrase gets the hard all-track cut; the slam on every fourth bar becomes the heartbeat of the hook.

## 23. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
The hook now gasps and slams every four bars; that's the heartbeat I wanted. The verse, zoomed: half-time stomp, a cluster stab once a bar, and big empty stretches between snares. It has room for a voice but it has gone cool: this is where a stranger would hear MENACE, Metro-style composure, not a jaw clenched. Move: put a palm-muted chug under the verse: the cluster (minus its top F) on driving eighths, muffled under the lid and accented with the kick, so the engine keeps grinding under the rapper and the pressure never drops.

## 24. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
Verse now grinds: a muffled chug on the eighths under the half-time stomp, pressure without stealing the vocal pocket. Zooming the intro into hook 1, the first slam is limp: the onset at 0:12 is smaller than the intro's quarter-note kicks, because the riser I put on the intro exit fills the eighth of silence with white-noise swoosh. A riser is a festival move anyway: it says "here comes the drop", that's hype, the nearest wrong feeling. Move: kill the risers (intro and break) so every entrance is preceded by dead air and lands as a blow.

## 25. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
The hole before the first hook is black now, the slam is clean. But the first four bars are the riff and the cluster alone through a filter, thin and buzzing, with nothing underneath: a stranger's first impression would be a nervous video-game arpeggio, ANXIETY, not rage. Rage should be in the room from the first second. Move: under the filtered riff, the torn long 808 holds a growling E from the very first beat (the same pulsing growl as the break), and the track opens with an impact, so the intro is a held snarl, not a twitch.

## 26. MIX
The intro now snarls from the first beat. Mix check with a trial master: the premaster has a +16 dB spike at 50 Hz, exactly last session's sin, and the master had to cut 7.4 dB there; meanwhile 2.5-16k sits a plateau too high (the EQ cut up to 7.7 dB, near its limit). The rage should live in 200 Hz-2 kHz grit, not in a sub balloon or in fizz. Move this round: low-end discipline: notch the 808s at 45 Hz, dip the kick at 50 and give it 100 Hz knock instead, so the weight moves up to where small speakers can hear the blows.

## 27. MIX
Sub spike down 3 dB and the master's 50 Hz cut eased from 7.4 to 5.2; the low end is now a fist, not a balloon. The top is the problem now: 2.5k-16k sits flat and 5-10 dB above where a hip-hop record lives, so the master is shaving it at its limit. Fizz on top of rage reads as harsh/anxious; heat lives lower. Move: high shelves down 5 dB from 3.5k on everything bright (cluster, leads, hats, snare, clap, crash), and pull the lead's bitcrush up a bit so it grinds instead of hisses.

## 28. MIX
Highs shelved: the master's top cuts went from ~7.5 to ~4 dB, and the mono/low-passed squint of the ending still bangs: the blows, the black hole, the slam all survive on a phone. What's left is the low end: the master is still cutting 5.7 dB at 50 Hz and there's a 160 Hz lump from the 808's growl harmonics. Move: finish the low-end discipline: deeper 45 Hz notch on both 808s and a dip at 160, keeping the 250-1k growl that carries the anger.

## 29. MIX
The 808 notch didn't move the 50 Hz number, so I soloed: the 50 Hz lump is the KICK (tuned to 52 Hz), not the 808, which sits at 41. They're fighting for the same floor. Move: retune the kick up to 62 Hz and shorten its body so it's a knock in the chest above the 808's sub, the 808 owns the floor, the kick owns the blow; take out the 50 Hz dip I'd put on it since its fundamental moves.

## 30. MIX
The floor separated, but the lump just followed the kick up to 63 Hz (master cutting 7.7 there): the kick's fundamental is simply too fat for what's sitting under it. Laptop loss also got worse. Move: thin the kick's fundamental by 5 dB at its tune and keep the 100 Hz knock and the click, so it hits like a knuckle, not a pillow.

## 31. MIX
The trial master is now quiet about everything: biggest cut 4.1 dB at 63 Hz, top end only shaved 2-4 dB, laptop loss about 2 dB, and the ear finally calls the key E minor. As a whole it reads hot, fast, sour and relentless, gasping every four bars. But hook 2 is a carbon copy of hook 1, so the escalation only starts at hook 3. Move (an arrangement detail I can't leave): from hook 2 on, an industrial stomp, an overdriven, crushed metal-tom doubling the kick, so each return of the hook is heavier than the last: hook 1 plain, hook 2 plus iron, hook 3 plus iron, a semitone up and the scream.

## 32. MASTER AND FINAL LISTEN
Zoomed the break into verse 2 (the kick reassembling quarter, eighth, sixteenth over the growl, the black hole, the clench) and verse 2 into the semitone-up hook (stomp, scream, gasp every four bars): it moves through time the way rage does. From the top: a snarl, a stampede, a clenched verse that keeps grinding, heavier returns, a forced silence, then the last hook a half-step worse, an overflow that gets cut off mid-swing, two beats of nothing and one blow ringing out. The nearest wrong feelings, hype, menace, anxiety, swagger, each got shut out by a specific choice (no risers, the chug, the growl under the intro, no half-time strut in the hooks). One more move would be explaining it. Mastered v032 at -10 LUFS, -1 dBTP: no warnings, largest EQ cut 4.3 dB at 63 Hz, limiter max 1 dB. Done.
