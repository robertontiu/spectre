STUDIO NOTES FOR LATIN POP (details in docs/MANUAL.md and docs/LATIN_POP.md):
- Rhythm patterns in engine notation are in docs/LATIN_POP.md "Rhythm patterns per instrument": bachata derecho, majao and mambo (bongó high and low as two tracks, güira, segunda bass notes and upbeat strums, the bass's "1, (2)&, 3, 4", the requinto as an 8th-note `arp`), bolero (bass half-quarter-quarter, chords on the ands of 2 and 4, martillo, cinquillo), son clave, a soft dembow.
- Low end: keep bass lines on A1 and above (E1/F1 put 40 Hz 8 dB over the curve in the test); give the bass body at 100-150 Hz and the guitars at 180-250 Hz: the `latinpop` master uses the `rock` curve, which wants body there, and a nylon-guitar band leaves a hole at 100-160 Hz (accept some of it; a bare bolero is lighter there than a pop-rock record).
- Humanize the guitars and percussion like players (`humanize_ms` 4-10); bachata and bolero are played, not programmed, even when the dembow is.
- **Phone check:** `listen --phone --genre latinpop` before mastering; the güira, shaker and guitar scrape are the likely hiss sources: shape them in the mix.
- **Master:** `studio.py master vNNN --genre latinpop` (-11 LUFS, -1 dBTP, the `rock` curve, eq 0.6, translate 0.3). An argued deviation (a bare bolero at `--lufs -12.5`, a dembow-driven track at `--lufs -10`) is allowed; write the reason in the journal. If the master warns, or the EQ sits at its limits where the mix could give it what it asks, fix the mix, re-render and master again.
