STUDIO NOTES FOR THE CHAMBER SCORE (details in docs/MANUAL.md and docs/CHAMBER_SCORE.md):
- Two depths, on purpose: the piano near and fairly dry (the narrator in the room), the strings and the remembered colours further back with more reverb. One room per moment; a change of space should mean something.
- The struck colours (glockenspiel stand-in, music box) carry almost all of the energy above 5-8 kHz in a sparse mix: treat their top as a casting decision and check it on the phone view; keep `bell` velocities under 0.6.
- Humanize like players: `humanize_ms` 6-12 on piano, guitar and plucked parts, rubato through tempo ramps, not a quantized grid.
- Low end: there is no bass instrument in most of this idiom; the piano's left hand (and a pizzicato or bowed low line, if you build one) gives body at 100-250 Hz. The master's borrowed curve will ask for sub at 31-63 Hz and hit its +4 dB limit there: that is the curve, not a mix fault; don't add an 808-like sub to satisfy it.
- **Phone check:** `listen --phone --genre chamberscore` before mastering.
- **Master:** `studio.py master vNNN --genre chamberscore` (-15 LUFS, -1 dBTP, the `rock` curve at eq 0.4, translate 0.2, mono below 80 Hz). An argued deviation (a piano-led track at `--lufs -16`, a swell-led track at `--lufs -14`) is allowed; write the reason in the journal. If the master warns, or the EQ sits at its limits anywhere above 80 Hz where the mix could give it what it asks, fix the mix, re-render and master again.
