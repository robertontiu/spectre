## How it carries the feeling
- **Childhood is the word "again!"** Two pickup notes (A-B leaning into D) ask for the tune every time it ends.
- **The ache comes from the clock, not the mode.** The watcher's version of the tune is the child's at exactly half speed, so the two are superimposed instead of alternated: in the middle a slow felt piano plays it while a far celesta keeps running the child's tune at full speed inside it; in the one swell the strings carry the slow version over the full-speed dance. Joy and looking back become one moment.
- **The finger arrives late.** On the three high notes the child's finger is about 45 ms late; the grown hand keeps the same hesitation, longer.
- **The ending answers, then doesn't.** The close is resolved on D; the last "again" is asked twice and answered, then asked a third time and left open.
- **Wrong feelings kept out:** a lullaby (no hum, the dance is awake from bar one), a toy commercial, a sad piano elegy (the key never turns minor), generic "inspirational" music, a Disney-sugar finale, music-box horror.

## Song form (engine bars, start)
- **again (1, 0:00):** the two glockenspiel pickup notes
- **A1, A2 (2-25, 0:01):** the tune on glockenspiel, then celesta and plucked violin, over piano waltz and plucked cello; the guitar's feet join
- **B (26-37, 0:35):** the run down the hill, a Lydian E major
- **A3, turn (38-49, 0:51):** the bowed violin sings the tune; the last phrase slows
- **M1-M4 (50-91, 1:09):** the watcher: felt piano at half speed, a solo cello line, the far celesta still running the child's tune, violas and celli blooming
- **T (92-115, 2:12):** the one swell: the dance at full speed, the strings carrying the tune at half speed, violins taking the octave
- **G (116-121, 2:46):** the piano inherits the run; the dancers leave
- **C1-C3 (122-147, 2:56):** the piano plays the child's version, one finger, high; a resolved D; the last far "again"

## Departures from Spectre's habits
Two clocks superimposed instead of sections alternated; no lone intro (two notes and the game is going); no silence before the swell; a resolved ending, with one deliberate unanswered gesture.

## Spectre's note
"I kept wanting to write the sad part, and the track kept telling me the sad part is just the happy part at half speed. Once the far celesta was running at the old tempo inside the slow bars I stopped arranging and started listening. The detail I'd keep if I lost everything else is the finger arriving late on the high note, and the grown hand doing the same thing thirty years on. The top end is duller than I'd like; that's the piano I had."
