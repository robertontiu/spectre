# 015: childhood (cinematic chamber score) — Spectre's journal

## 0. SIT WITH IT (before the studio)

**In the body, in time, in space.** It isn't one feeling, it's two speeds in the same chest. One is fast and light: weight on the toes, a skip that lands late on purpose, breath high, laughing before the joke is done. The other is slow and in the hands: someone sitting still at a piano, watching, and the stillness is full, not empty. In time: the fast one has no past (every bar is "again!"), the slow one is all past. In space: the game is across the room, bright, small, a bit far; the one who watches is close enough that I hear the felt.

**What childhood IS in this track: "again!".** The word a small child says the moment anything good ends. In sound it is a two-note pickup, A up to B (sol-la), leaning into the D that starts the tune. Every time the theme has finished, those two notes ask for it again, and it comes again, in a new colour. The adult can't have "again"; the adult can only play it slower. So:
- *The child's tune* is a one-finger tune: five notes only (D E F# A B, the black-key shape moved to D), long-short skipping in a waltz felt in one, on struck metal (glockenspiel, celesta) with an uneven hand.
- *The watcher's version* is the same tune at exactly half the speed on a close felted piano, and it knows more notes: it fills the child's gaps with the two notes the child never used (G and C#), and once, under the same E, it puts the borrowed G minor. The cello carries that one changed note (B falling to B-flat) as a line.
- *The mechanism for both halves at once:* because the slow version is exactly 2:1, the child's tune can keep running at its own speed INSIDE the watcher's slow bars (a far celesta in the next room), and the watcher's slow tune can ride as a long string line OVER the child's dance. The joy and the looking back are not alternated, they are superimposed: one theme, two clocks, same key, same notes. Pentatonic is what lets them overlap without rubbing.

**Lean: a waltz montage with the piano as the watcher** (the score idiom first; the neo-classical piano is one character in it, not the bed). The joy has to be first and real, and a waltz at dance tempo is the only thing in this toolbox that is awake and playing from bar one.

**Nearest wrong feelings, and the separation.**
- *Lullaby:* the slow passages are in 3 and soft. Separation: they keep the old dance pulse running (the left hand's eighths are the waltz's quarters; the far celesta is at full speed), nothing rocks, and the track spends more than half its time at 172.
- *Toy commercial:* glockenspiel on a major waltz is the formula. Separation: no claps, no whistle, no ukulele strum pattern; the glockenspiel is the THEME's voice, plays alone like one finger, and goes silent on the borrowed chord and in the close.
- *Sad piano elegy:* the watcher's piano alone. Separation: it arrives only after a minute of real play, stays major except one chord, and is never left without the child's tempo for more than eight slow bars.
- *Stock inspirational:* piano ostinato plus string build. Separation: no ostinato-and-pad; the string line IS the theme at half speed; one swell; the form is a story of two clocks.
- *Disney-sugar finale:* my two-clock tutti could be it. Separation: no key lift, it is not the end, the brightest colour drops out at its own peak chord (G minor), and it dissolves into the piano inheriting every part.
- *Music-box horror:* the far celesta alone before the swell. Separation: in tune, warm, major, over a held dominant, nine seconds only.

**Arc (about 3:45).** Pickup "again" → the game, already in flight: A (glockenspiel, one finger) → A' (celesta and plucked violin; the glockenspiel copies a bar late, a smaller sibling) → B, the run down the hill (eighth-note runs, a Lydian E major for the wide-open second) → A'' (a bowed violin sings it: someone is older already), slowing through its last phrase → the watcher: the piano alone at half speed, the tune with its gaps filled → the same with a cello and, far off, the child's tune still at full speed → the hill again, slowly, strings blooming → the far celesta alone over a held dominant → "again" → the one swell: the dance back at full speed with the strings carrying the slow version over it → the dancers leave and the piano picks up what each one drops → the close: the piano plays it the child's way, one finger, high, slow; resolved; and far off the two notes ask once more.

**Meter, tempo, key.** 3/4 on the 12-step grid, quarter = 172 felt in one; the watcher's passages written in doubled values at about 156-164 (quarter = 78-82); ritardando into the turn (174 → 112), a small accelerando in B (172 → 178), the close at 72-75 slowing to about 55. D major, the tune pentatonic; I-iii (D to F#m) as the opening step, one borrowed iv (Gm6), one Lydian II (E major) in B. Major, because the ache has to come from the clock, not the mode.

**The sounds that hold it.** (1) The glockenspiel, soft mallet: the child's finger. (2) The felted piano, close and nearly dry: the watcher. (3) The bowed strings (solo cello, then the section): time. Around them: celesta (the child in the next room), cello pizzicato as the floor, a nylon guitar and plucked violin as feet.

**Voice: none.** A soft woman's hum over a slow piano in 3 is a lullaby however I mix it, and the engine's bright voices would be an adult pretending to be the child. The child is metal bars, the adult is felt and bow.

**My habits (read from 008, 009, 010, 012, 013 and the rest).** The intro is the main figure alone; things join in steps; a valley where the band leaves and comes back one by one; a beat of silence or a held chord before the arrival; the last section the biggest with every motif passing; a key change as door or lift; a coda with the figure alone, unresolved or cut off; meaning as two lines meeting on one note.
**Departures for this one.**
1. *Two clocks superimposed instead of sections alternated* (the main one): the peak is not a louder repeat, it is the theme in two tempos at once, and the valley before it is never empty of the fast clock. This is the only honest way I know to make "joyful AND looking back" one moment instead of two.
2. *No lone intro:* two pickup notes and the game is already going. Joy first, not as a reward.
3. *No silence before the arrival:* the swell is entered from a far celesta that never stops; the waltz is re-found by regrouping the same pulse (2:1 metric relation), not by a gap.
4. *A resolved ending:* the piano finishes the tune on the tonic, complete. The only unfinished thing in the whole track is deliberate and named here: the last "again" pickup (A-B on a far celesta) that nothing answers. A pickup belongs to the next phrase; there is no next phrase. That is the looking back.
