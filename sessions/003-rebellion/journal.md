# Session 003 journal (Spectre). Brief: oriental trap, rebellion

## 1. SIT WITH IT
Rebellion in the body is feet, not fists: a lot of feet landing together, chest open, voice raised with other voices. In time it is a pulse that does not hurry and does not stop; it gathers people as it goes, so it has to get denser and louder in steps, never explode early. In space it is a street: wide, crowded, echoing off walls, a chant thrown from the front and thrown back by the many. Wrong feelings to keep out: rage (chaos, one hot man, I just did that), defiance (one figure planted still), triumph (resolution, major lift, celebration), menace (cold, sparse, predatory), and tourism (an oud sitting on top like a postcard).
Decisions: 140 bpm, trap felt half-time but with a marching quarter-note stomp under it so the crowd is always walking forward. Straight time (swing 0.5) because a march is straight; the darbuka plays maqsum underneath so the street is in the groove itself. D Hijaz (D Eb F# G A Bb C): the Eb half-step above the root is the grit, the F# gives the chant its open-throated cry, and I will keep it out of any major resolution so it never turns into a victory. The sounds that hold it: a gritty oud riff with tremolo picking that drives like a chant, a unison crowd "oh" answering it, and the stomp (kick + tom + gang clap). The 808 sits on D and slides to Eb like a shove.

## 2. THE LOOP
v001: the ears call it D major. The riff camps its tremolo on F# and G, so it rings bright and open; with the doum and a boomy tom it's a wedding dance more than a street, festive, the triumph/tourism side. Move: rewrite the riff so the hammering (tremolo) sits on D and the Eb grit, F# only passing through, and double it with a second oud an octave down in unison so it sounds like many hands on one line, a chant not a solo.

## 3. THE LOOP
v002: the riff is darker in the notes now, but the ears still say D major, and the zoom shows why: the floor under 500 Hz is a solid wall, and two pitched drums are singing wrong notes into it (the stomp tom at 95 Hz is an F#, the doum at 105 is a G#). The feet of the crowd are blurring into a boomy wedding drum. Move: tune the street into the key: stomp up to D3 and shorter, so it's a hard knock of feet, doum to A2, so the floor holds D and A, the open fifth of a chant.

## 4. THE LOOP
v003: the floor is in key now (the ear's "major" is the Hijaz F# talking; I'm not chasing it). But the loop is one flat slab: everything plays everywhere, the crowd only grunts under the snare, and nobody answers anybody. That's a parade loop, a postcard. Rebellion is a call and a crowd throwing it back. Move: the oud calls in bars 1 and 3, and in bars 2 and 4 the crowd sings the answer in unison octaves on the riff's own rhythm, so the chant belongs to the many.

## 5. THE LOOP
v004: the zoom shows the crowd singing on top of the oud the whole time, so it's a duet, a choir over a lute, more concert hall than street. A chant only works if the leader stops and the many answer. Move: make it a real exchange: oud calls in bars 1 and 3, then falls silent while the crowd answers in bars 2 and 4; under the answers the low oud hammers a D tremolo like the ground shaking.

## 6. THE LOOP
v005: now it talks: the oud throws a line, stops, and the crowd throws it back over a shaking low D. But every backbeat is a half-second plume of white noise up to the ceiling; that hissing tail is the sound that read as anxiety last session, and it smears the march. Move: make the backbeat a dry, tight slam of many hands: shorter snare, less reverb, a wider gang clap with more spread, and the snare's top shelved down.

## 7. THE LOOP
v006: the backbeat plume is shorter and the hands land drier. Now the answer: it's three perfectly aligned synth voices, a choir, too clean to be a street; a crowd is many throats a little out of step. Move: double the crowd with a second section on another vowel, chorused and panned the other way, and loosen both hands (humanize) so the answer comes back ragged and wide, like a few hundred people.

## 8. THE LOOP
v007: the answer is wider and ragged now, but the lanes show it as a thin stripe next to the oud, and the band meter tells me why the loop feels heavy-dark: the 60-250 Hz band sits 8 dB over the mids. Four drums are all thumping in that band (kick boost, stomp, doum, 808), and a dark, bottom-heavy loop is how menace sounds. Rebellion is voices, mids, heat. Move: drain the thump (doum and stomp down and high-passed higher, no 100 Hz boost on the kick) and push the crowd up so the chant leads.

## 9. THE LOOP
Raising the crowd barely moved it, so I read the real stem levels from the render: kick at -5 dB and 808 at -7 own the room, the crowd sits 12 dB under them, the hats at -40 are basically absent and the darbuka tek is a whisper. That's why the loop is a dark thump: the street is drowned by the floor. Move: rebalance for the story: kick and 808 down, gang clap, tek and hats up, both crowd sections up, so the hands and the voices are on top and the weight is felt under them.

## 10. THE LOOP
v009: hands and voices have come up and the air band breathes now; the chant is finally on top of the hats. But the kick barely moved (its drive eats the gain change) and its lane is a solid bar: a long 60 Hz pillow that never stops, so the floor still hums under everything. Feet in a march are short. Move: shorten the kick into a knock and pull it down after its drive, and take the 808 down after its drive too, so the weight comes in strikes, not a hum.

## 11. THE LOOP
v010: the floor now has gaps between strikes and the mids carry the voices. Listening to the groove as a stranger: half-time snare on 3 with an even stomp on every quarter is a stately, swaggering walk, the classic villain Hijaz-trap strut: menace is the danger. A crowd doesn't stomp evenly, it stomps-stomps-CLAPS, the oldest stadium/street rhythm there is. Move: stomp on 1 and 2, the gang clap on 3 (with the snare), rest on 4, so the backbeat becomes the crowd's own body rhythm; stomp up so the feet are felt.

## 12. THE ARRANGEMENT
The loop carries it: a call, an answer, a crowd's body rhythm under a half-time trap pocket. Now the arc as an uprising: an intro where the oud calls alone through a closed filter with only the darbuka rims (somebody starts it), a 4-bar gathering where the crowd and the stomps join, hook, a pared-down verse (the crowd only answers once every four bars), hook, an 8-bar "march" with no kick and no 808 (just feet, darbuka and a held chant swelling), verse 2, a 16-bar last hook, and an outro where the march walks away into the distance. First pass of the whole shape.

## 13. THE ARRANGEMENT
v012: the shape is there on paper, but the loudness curve is a flat line from hook1 to the outro, and hook1, hook2 and hook3 are the same crowd at the same size. An uprising that is as big at minute one as at minute two isn't gathering anyone; a stranger hears a parade loop. Move: the crowd grows hook by hook: hook1 one section singing, hook2 both sections, hook3 the whole street with a third, higher "ee" section an octave up, so the answer gets more throats every time it comes back.

## 14. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
v013: the march itself walks right, feet on every quarter, hands on 3, the chant swelling, but its last bar was a crescendo snare roll into a full-band white-noise wall: an EDM riser, festival hype, the triumph side. A crowd doesn't build with a riser, it builds with hands. Move: the fill becomes the crowd speeding up: stomps doubling, claps on 3, 4 and the "and", a darbuka rim roll swelling under it and three snare cracks, then the half-beat silence and the slam into verse 2.

## 15. THE ARRANGEMENT
v014: the fill now gathers hands instead of hiss, but the whole middle and end are the same oud call over and over; the crowd grows, the leader never does. In a street the voice at the front cracks open as the thing builds. Move: in the last hook a ney, driven gritty, doubles the oud's call an octave up with grace notes and bends, like a voice shouting the line from the front, and falls silent while the crowd throws it back.

## 16. MIX
v015: the ney landed as a thick pure line at 600 Hz sitting 6 dB over the crowd, a sweet flute floating on top: that is exactly the postcard. A voice shouting from the front is hoarse and part of the crowd. Move: ney 7 dB down, more breath, its 600 Hz body notched, harder drive and a 1.8 kHz bite, shorter legato so the notes are thrown, not sung.

## 17. THE ARRANGEMENT
v016: the ney now sits inside the crowd, hoarse, a voice and not a flute. Zooming into verse 2 though, it was the same strut as verse 1, as if the march never happened. People who marched don't stand still afterwards. Move: in verse 2 the feet keep walking: the march's stomp on every quarter stays under the verse groove, so the second verse moves forward where the first one nodded.

## 18. THE ARRANGEMENT
v017: verse 2 walks now. But going from verse 2 straight into the last hook there is no moment where the crowd faces the line; the arc had gathering and marching but no clash. Move: a 4-bar standoff before hook 3: the beat drops away, only feet and hands, the ney shouts the call and the full crowd throws it back bare, the feet and hands get louder, half a beat of silence, then hook 3 slams in with an impact.

## 19. MIX
v018 (trial master): the master had to cut 40 Hz by 5 dB, lift 50-100 Hz by 4 dB and cut 160-200 Hz by 3 dB. Read in the premaster curve: the 808's D1 sub spikes at 40 while its body at 80 is a hole, and the stomp's tuned D3 knock plus the snare's 220 boost pile up a woolly hump at 160-200 that makes the feet sound like drums in a hall. Move: notch the 808 sub after its drive and push more 2nd harmonic and 80 Hz so it sings in the body, cut the stomp's 170 Hz and give its 700 Hz knock more, and take the boost off the snare body.

## 20. MIX
v019: the sub spike and the 200 Hz wool are gone, but the curve is still a comb: the kick at 63 Hz rings and the 808 leaves holes at 50 and 80-125, so the bass is a rumble under the floor rather than a voice that sings the Eb shove. Move: lift the 808's 52 Hz and a broad 85 Hz shelf of its own harmonics, and ease the sidechain so the 808 isn't gasping after every kick.

## 21. MIX
v020: the low end sings now and the master's moves are all under 4 dB. Through the lowpass squint the arc reads (march and standoff breathe, hook 3 is the top), but the hooks measure only 0.16-0.2 wide: the crowd is a block in the centre, a choir on a stage, when rebellion is people on every side of you. Move: spread the crowd sections wide left and right with the high section across the whole field, push the darbuka rims further out, and take 3 dB of the 640 Hz vowel honk off the crowd so the width doesn't become mud.

## 22. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
v021: the crowd sections now open out wide where they stand alone (march and standoff measure 0.45 wide), and mono only loses 0.4 dB, so it holds on one speaker. Hook 3 is the loudest section, but it's the same darbuka maqsum as hook 1: the street's own drum hasn't caught the heat. Move: in hook 3 the darbuka goes hot, a second hand on the rims filling the 16ths around the maqsum, so the march speeds up under the same tempo without the trap drums getting busier.

## 23. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
v022: the roll shows the second hand playing every 16th, but the ka strokes sit at ghost level, around -34 dB; in the spectrogram the hook 3 rims are invisible, so a stranger hears no change at all. Heat you can't hear isn't heat. Move: in hook 3 push the ka up 5-7 dB and the tek 2-3 dB across the section, so the street drum is audibly running by the end.

## 24. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
v023: the hook 3 rims now run audibly between the strikes. Then the outro: just the oud calling over feet and darbuka, the crowd gone. One man with a lute walking off is a postcard and a loner, the defiance ending, not a movement. Move: the crowd keeps answering the call through the outro, filtering and fading into the distance with the feet, so the last thing you hear is many voices still going, not resolved and not finished.

## 25. TRANSITIONS AND DETAIL
v024: the outro now ends on many voices fading down the street, and the overview reads as a journey: gathering, hooks, the march dip, the standoff dip, hook 3 on top, the crowd walking off. But hook 1 slides into verse 1 and hook 2 into the march with no breath, like a playlist crossfade. Move: before verse 1 the kick, 808, hats and low oud drop for one beat so the crowd's last answer hangs alone, and before the march the trap drums fall out for two beats so only the street (feet, hands, darbuka, voices) carries you into the march.

## 26. MASTER
v025: the drop-outs work: before the march the low end vanishes for two beats and only the street carries you in. Mastered v025 at -10.5 LUFS, -1.02 dBTP: no warnings, limiter only 1.3 dB, every EQ move at or under 4.1 dB (80 Hz +4 lifting the 808 body, 40 Hz -3.9, 16 kHz -4.1 taming air). The mix holds without the master fighting it, so nothing to fix upstream.

## 27. FINAL LISTEN
From the top on the master: a filtered oud calling alone over rims, the feet and voices gathering, hooks where the crowd throws the line back louder each time, verses with room for a rapper, the march where the trap falls away and only the street walks, the standoff where the crowd shouts bare into half a beat of silence and hook 3 slams in hottest, then the voices fading down the road still unresolved. It stays out of triumph (no major lift, no riser), out of menace (the hands, voices and heat lead, not the sub), and the oud and ney are the chant, not a postcard. One more move would be explaining it. v025 is the final.
