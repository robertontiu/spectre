# Mastering: research brief and implementation

Mastering turns the raw mix bus (the premaster) into a release: balanced, controlled, loud enough, safe on every playback system. In Spectre it is a separate final stage (`engine/master.py`, run as `studio.py master vNNN`).

## Why it was added
Session 001 ("Bleachers") sounded clunky on MacBook speakers. Analysis showed the 808 fundamental around 50 Hz sitting about 17 dB above the midrange in the long-term spectrum, so most of the track's energy sat where laptop speakers physically can't play, plus inter-sample true peaks around -1.4 dBTP.

## Principles (from the research)
- **Chain order:** corrective EQ, then dynamics (gentle glue; multiband only where needed), then saturation or enhancement, then stereo, then a limiter last. Working mastering engineers use multiband compression much less than tutorials suggest.
- **Loudness:** streaming platforms normalize to about -14 LUFS integrated (Apple about -16). A louder master just gets turned down. Spotify asks for a true peak under -1 dBTP, or under -2 dBTP for masters louder than -14 LUFS.
- **True peak, not sample peak:** reconstruction between samples can exceed 0 dBFS. Measure and limit at 4x oversampling.
- **Mono low end:** keep everything below about 120-200 Hz mono (high-pass the side channel).
- **Small-speaker translation:** laptops and phones can't reproduce sub bass. Harmonics of the bass, generated by saturation, let the brain infer the missing fundamental. Mid-range energy, not sub, is what makes a track read on small speakers.
- **Fix in the mix when you can:** a master that has to make huge corrections is a sign the mix needs work.

## Implementation (`engine/master.py`)
| Stage | What it does |
|---|---|
| 0. Analyse | Integrated LUFS, true peak (4x), 1/3-octave long-term spectrum (gated to the loud parts) |
| 1. Clean-up | 28 Hz 8th-order high-pass; side channel high-passed below 120 Hz (linear phase) so the low end is mono |
| 2. Tonal EQ | Linear-phase EQ that moves the spectrum toward a genre target curve (`--target`). Version 2 (default since 2026-11-24): the curve is laid over the mix at its level around 1 kHz (mean deviation over 500 Hz-2 kHz); two open-loop passes with strength `--eq`, except that from 2 kHz up cuts are made at full strength and boosts from 5 kHz up are capped at +2 dB; then a closed loop: the finished master (after stages 3-7) is measured and every band from 1 to 16 kHz more than 1 dB off the curve is corrected and stages 3-7 re-run (at most 4 runs). At most -8/+4 dB per band in total (-12 dB at 12.5-16 kHz). Version 1 (`--legacy-master`): two passes aligned on the median deviation over 200 Hz-5 kHz, strength `--eq` everywhere, -8/+4 dB, no closed loop (see "Top end" below) |
| 3. Multiband | 4 linear-phase bands (120 / 600 / 4000 Hz crossovers) that sum back perfectly; each compresses only its own loudest peaks (threshold from its 95th percentile) |
| 4. Translation | 55-150 Hz bass body to tanh saturation to a 160-700 Hz band-pass, mixed in (`--translate`) |
| 5. Glue | 2:1, 30 ms attack, 200 ms release, about 1-2 dB of reduction |
| 6. Clip + limit | Soft clipper (4x) into a look-ahead true-peak limiter running 4x oversampled, ceiling `--tp` |
| 7. Loudness | The input drive is solved iteratively so integrated loudness lands on `--lufs` (±0.15 LU). A true-peak guard then turns the master down if the 4x down-sampling recreated inter-sample peaks above `--tp` (reported as `tp_guard_db`; it never acts on a master that already meets the ceiling) |
| 8. QC | LUFS, true peak (WAV; `studio.py master` also measures the MP3, `mp3_true_peak`), PLR, loudness range, low-end L/R correlation, stereo width (side minus mid, LU), laptop loss (loudness change after a 180 Hz high-pass), remaining tonal deviations, `top_end` (1-16 kHz deviations with the curve at the master's level around 1 kHz) and `top_end_max_abs_dev_db`, the phone check (`phone`, premaster and master), EQ bands at their limit, warnings, `master_version`; before/after picture |

Settings come from a **genre configuration** (`engine/genres.json`), chosen with `--genre`; explicit flags override single values. There is **no default genre**: `studio.py master` stops with an error listing the genres when neither `--genre` nor `--target` is given. An old `--target`-only command still works and takes the matching genre's settings (`--target beat` is an alias of `trap`). Every report records `genre`, `settings` (the values used), `eq_at_limit` and `warnings`, plus the stereo width (`width_side_minus_mid_lu`).

### Strands and the dynamic path (2026-11-26)
- **`--strand`**: `studio.py master vNNN --genre <genre> --strand <name>` takes the genre's `master_by_strand` entry from engine/genres.json (target, lufs, tp, eq, translate, mono_below, max_low_boost_db, dynamics) over the genre's `master`; explicit flags still override one value. A strand can point to another genre (hip hop's `boombap`, `golden`). An unknown strand stops the master and lists the valid ones; `engine/master.py --help` lists them per genre. Since this date every `master_by_strand` entry is a machine-readable object with a short key and a `label` (the description it had); the genre docs' strand names map onto those keys (hiphop: default, trap, boombap, golden, folkfusion, ua_ethno, lv_folkpop, ro_fusion; trance: progressive, mainfloor, hard, melodic_techno, era_2015; rock: current, punk, altpop, 1990s; joc: taraf, fanfare, transylvania_strings, acoustic_unmeasured, urban_period, wedding_band, manele, fusion; caroling: choir, acappella, brass, street, handbells, crooner, midcentury_era; latinpop: bolero, acoustic, ballad, bachata, urbano, mexicano, tropical; chamberscore: default, score, piano; dubstep: modern, newest, riddim, melodic, deep, quiet_section; techno: default, newest, hard, industrial, hypnotic, dub, minimal, melodic, minimal_era, roots).
- **`dynamics`** (a genres.json master or strand setting; `--dynamics normal|keep` overrides): `normal` is the chain every master had (multiband peak riding, 2:1 glue taking 1-2 dB, soft clipper, true-peak limiter; unchanged, sample for sample). `keep` is the dynamic path for the acoustic genres: no multiband, a light 1.5:1 glue from the loudest 5% (about 0.5 dB on average: "about .5 to 1dB of steady compression", Sage Audio), no clipper, the true-peak limiter alone ("mild peak-limiting" on a score album: SOS on *Encanto*). Set for `chamberscore` (its master and every strand; docs/CHAMBER_SCORE.md "Granularity" item 9) and for the caroling `choir`, `brass` and `handbells` strands (dynamic complexity 7.7-8.3, 5.6, 7.2; "little limiting or none": docs/CAROLING.md "Granularity"). Not for the a cappella, crooner, street and mid-century caroling strands (DC 4.0-4.8, 3.3: pop-dynamic), nor for any joc scene (taraf 3.7, fanfare 3.0, fusion 3.3: docs/ROMANIAN.md; only the band-limited period recordings, 4.9, move more, and they are not a target). `--published` keeps each genre's old chain.
- **Measured on 015** (`examples/production/dynamics/`: the same premaster, `chamberscore_ref`, -15 LUFS, -1 dBTP): normal DC 3.92 (the released MP3 3.96), LRA 8.2, PLR 13.9, true peak -1.08; keep DC **4.77**, LRA **10.6**, PLR 13.9, true peak -1.09; the limiter now takes up to 6.2 dB on the loudest transients (0.6 dB in normal, where the clipper took them). The chamber-score records measure DC 5.6-6.8: the master gets 015 part of the way, the rest is the arrangement's (keep the quiet passages quiet).

### Loudness: what the research says and what Spectre uses
- **Streaming normalization:** Spotify plays at -14 LUFS by default (-11 "loud", -19 "quiet"), turns louder masters down, and asks for true peaks under -1 dBTP, or under -2 dBTP for masters louder than -14 LUFS ([Spotify](https://support.spotify.com/artists/article/loudness-normalization/)). Apple Music about -16, YouTube, Amazon and Tidal -14, Deezer -15, SoundCloud none ([iZotope 2025](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/mastering-for-streaming-platforms)).
- **Commercial masters:** 54 Billboard Global 200 top-10 songs (H1 2024) averaged -8.3 LUFS integrated (sd 1 LU; -11.1 to -6), max short-term -6 LUFS, LRA mostly 3.7-6.8 LU, 80% peaking at or above 0 dBTP ([iZotope 2024](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/mastering-trends)). Hot Dance/Electronic top 10 (2025): -8.3 average (-11.3 to -6.2) ([iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/dance-electronic-analysis)). Hot Rock & Alternative (July 2025): -8.1 average (-11.9 to -5.1) ([iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/rock-alternative-analysis)). Billboard year-end top 30: -12.5 (1990), -8.0 (2010), -8.8 (2020) ([Țînț & Drăgulin 2025](https://webbut.unitbv.ro/index.php/Series_VIII/article/view/11335)).
- **Dynamics (PLR):** the same Billboard year-end sets had a mean PLR of 12.6 dB (1990), 10.6 (2000) and 9.3 (2010 and 2020) (Țînț & Drăgulin 2025). One dance hit measured 7.5 dB (iZotope, "Beautiful People" stereo master).
- **Stereo width:** side-minus-mid integrated loudness of the 2024 top-10 set averaged -8.5 LU, half of the songs between -10.9 and -6 LU; pop widest, hip-hop/rap narrowest; no per-genre numbers (iZotope 2024). The report's `width_side_minus_mid_lu` uses the same definition.
- **Quiet masters on streaming (2026-11-25, chamber-score and caroling research):** with normalization the playback level of a dynamic master is set by its PLR, not by its integrated loudness. Spotify normalizes each track to -14 but raises a quiet master only until its true peak reaches -1 dBTP (its own example: -20 LUFS with -5 dBTP peaks is raised to -16), so a master of PLR *p* plays at most at -1 - *p* LUFS ([Spotify](https://support.spotify.com/artists/article/loudness-normalization/)). For the acoustic genres (PLR 12-16) that is -13 to -17 in a playlist whatever the integrated target; a louder target only adds limiting, and a classical-level master (a -22 LUFS choir) stays quieter than its playlist neighbours.
- **Dynamics by genre (measured 2026-11-25):** Essentia dynamic complexity (DC) on AcousticBrainz: pop rock 2015-21 3.6 (238 tracks); trailer albums 5.1, `trailer music`/`epic music` tag 4.8; video-game tag 4.6; chamber idioms 5.6-6.8 (score albums 6.6, European school 5.9, felt piano 6.8, game chamber 5.6); neo-classical before 2010 7.5; choir carols 7.7-8.3, a cappella carols 4.0. Session 015's master (v030) measured DC 3.96 (Essentia 2.1-b6; AcousticBrainz ran 2.1-beta2), PLR 13.9, LRA 8.2; session 012's 2.9 (LRA 5.2). DC is a loose proxy for LRA (r 0.44 on Spectre's masters). Proposed QC check (not built): DC or LRA next to PLR, with a warning when a chamber or caroling master falls below about 5 (hand-tuned threshold from the medians). Nobody publishes true peak or LRA per subdivision of a genre, and AcousticBrainz has none.
- **Spectre's choice (updated 2026-11-24):** trap/hip-hop stay at -10.5 LUFS (sessions 001-006); trance masters at -9 LUFS and rock at -8.5 LUFS, close to the chart averages (dance/electronic -8.3, rock/alternative -8.1), with -1 dBTP. Engine tests showed the true-peak guard only trims about 0.2 dB, so -8 is reachable; for trance the soft clipper is the real limit (it shaves about 8 dB off clubkick peaks at -9), so trance mixes must tame kick transients. Streaming normalizes to about -14 LUFS, so louder masters only matter where nothing normalizes (DJ decks, SoundCloud, downloads). Sources and tests: docs/proposals/mastering-trance-poprock.md. The proposal's "modern" tonal curves are unmeasured estimates and are not used; the measured Pestana curves stay until reference tracks are measured.

### Measured targets: derivation
**Source.** P. D. Pestana, Z. Ma, J. D. Reiss, A. Barbosa and D. A. A. Black, "Spectral Characteristics of Popular Commercial Recordings 1950-2010", AES 135th Convention, New York, 2013 ([AES e-library 17010](https://www.aes.org/e-lib/browse.cfm?elib=17010); [author PDF](https://joshreiss.github.io/documents/2013/Pestana%20Ma%20Reiss%20-%20AES135.pdf)). 772 US and UK number-one singles (about half of all #1s 1950-2010), uncompressed audio, mono (L+R)/2, 4096-point FFT at 44.1 kHz (about 10.8 Hz per bin), each song's spectrum normalised before averaging. Fig. 5 shows average spectra for pop, rock, electronic, hip-hop, jazz, folk and R&B (genre tags from Wikipedia, EchoNest and Last.fm). The paper's summary: a roughly linear decay of about 5 dB per octave from 100 Hz to 4 kHz, steeper above, with a severe low cut around 60 Hz; electronic and hip-hop have an "unmistakeable" extended low end, and the low end is the only region where genre confidence intervals don't overlap.

**Reading the figure.** Fig. 5 is vector graphics in the PDF, so the curves were read exactly, not digitised by eye: the page was converted to SVG, the seven curves identified by their legend styles, and the axes calibrated from the figure's own grid (decade lines at 100 Hz, 1 kHz, 10 kHz; dB lines -100 to -30; fit residual 0.06 dB). Check: the extracted per-bin slope from 100 Hz to 4 kHz is -5.0 dB/octave for rock and electronic, -5.15 pop and -4.8 hip-hop, matching the paper's "about 5 dB per octave". Script: `tools/pestana_ltas.py` (reproduces the engine's numbers from the PDF).

**Conversion.** The figure plots level per FFT bin (constant bandwidth). The engine measures energy per 1/3-octave band (constant percentage bandwidth), and a band holds a number of bins proportional to its width. So each band's energy is the sum of 10^(dB/10) over its frequencies (interpolated on a 1 Hz grid), which adds about +3 dB per octave to the per-bin slope. Bands are normalised to 1 kHz and rounded to 0.5 dB. No further smoothing: the 1/3-octave integration already removes the per-note "comb" the paper shows in the mids.

**Genre mapping.** `hiphop` = Fig. 5 hip-hop. `trance` = Fig. 5 electronic. `rock` = the mean of Fig. 5 pop and rock (pop rock; the two differ by at most 1.5 dB per band).

**Measured on 2026-11-25** (docs/PRODUCTION.md "Era-appropriate targets"; tables in docs/TRANCE.md, section C, and in each genre doc): against open catalogue data (MusicBrainz tags with AcousticBrainz spectra, 1995-2021, power-averaged like this engine's analyser) all three measured curves share one shape error: 3-6 dB too little in the kick-and-bass band (about 32-97 Hz), 3-5 dB too much at 97-183 Hz, and 2-6 dB too little above 7.7 kHz; for choirs and scores, which borrow `rock`, the curve is 7-12 dB too bright above 4 kHz. The same day the catalogue-fitted curves were adopted as era targets ("Era targets" below); these curves stay available by name.

**What's uncertain.**
- *Era.* The genre curves pool 1950-2010. The paper shows spectra getting louder, flatter and more extended over time, with more low end after 2000. Rock and pop #1s lean on 1960s-1980s records, so `rock` is probably too dark above 8 kHz for a modern master, and `hiphop` (largely 1990-2010) probably has less sub at 31-50 Hz than 2010s-2020s 808 trap. The per-decade panels of Fig. 2 have no common mid reference, so an era correction couldn't be derived without guessing; none was applied.
- *Genre labels.* "Electronic" means #1 singles tagged electronic (mostly dance-pop and house-pop), not trance specifically; no trance-only measurement was found. Genre sample sizes aren't reported.
- *Low end.* At 31.5-50 Hz a 1/3-octave band holds only one or two FFT bins, so those values are interpolations (about ±2 dB). The paper averages normalised magnitude spectra, not power, which slightly understates bands dominated by short loud events (kicks).
- *Not used:* Elowsson & Friberg (AES 142, 2017, [e-library 18638](https://www.aes.org/e-lib/browse.cfm?elib=18638)) found that the LTAS differences between genres are mainly explained by how prominent the percussion is (more percussion, more relative bass and treble); their numbers weren't accessible. iZotope's genre targets (Tonal Balance Control) aren't published as numbers.

## Era targets (2026-11-25)
**What they are.** One curve per genre measured on what that genre sounds like in its current period (trance, hip hop and pop rock: 2015-2021, the last years with open data) or on the reference records its genre doc chose (joc, caroling, Latin pop, chamber score), with provenance next to it. They are the interim targets docs/PRODUCTION.md recommends (source 2, the open catalogue); the definitive ones are reference masters measured locally (source 1), with the same tool.

**How they were made: `tools/refcurve.py`.**
- `catalogue`: MusicBrainz recordings by tag and first-release year (`--tag trance --years 2015-2021 --per-year 300`), by artist (`--artist "Taraf de Haïdouks" --limit 300`) or by release (`--release <mbid>`, repeatable), joined to AcousticBrainz low-level data (Essentia's analysis of users' own files, CC0; no audio is downloaded). Per track the mean energy in Essentia's 27 Bark bands, in dB relative to the track's level over 510 Hz-2 kHz (the mean of those eight bands' dB values); medians and interquartile ranges per band; tracks under 90 s are skipped for tag sets, under 30 s for album sets (film cues are short). Then a smooth 1/3-octave curve is fitted (least squares over all Bark bands, a second-difference smoothness penalty of 0.4; the band under 32 Hz, which reads unreliably, at half weight; 15.5-20.5 kHz at half weight on the lossless files only, when at least 20 are lossless, because lossy files are cut there) so that the curve, read through the same Bark analysis, reproduces the medians. The Bark reading of a curve is computed analytically: the expected Hann-windowed (2048-point) spectrum of noise shaped like the curve after this master's 28 Hz clean-up filter.
- `measure <files>`: the same output from local reference masters with this master's own analyser (gated 1/3-octave mid and side spectra, loudness, true peak, PLR, LRA, width, low-end correlation, crest per region, and the Bark reading for comparison with the catalogue). Analysis only: the files stay in `~/.cache/spectre/refs/<genre>/`, only medians, spreads and the list of titles enter the repo.
- `check <curve> --against <file>`: any curve read through the Bark analysis against a catalogue file's medians (the comparison tables in the genre docs). `add <file>`: puts a curve into `engine/targets.json` (never over a built-in curve).
Downloads are cached in `~/.cache/spectre/refs/catalogue/` (only the few numbers used); the run for all nine curves took about 20 minutes, mostly MusicBrainz's one-request-per-second limit. **The exact commands** for all nine: `python3 tools/catalogue_stats.py targets` prints them (`--run` runs them and adds the results); the tags, years, artist and release lists are in `data/catalogue_references.json`. Re-run on 2026-11-25 from the cache, `caroling_ref`, `chamberscore_ref`, `latinpop_dark`, `joc_taraf` and `hiphop_2015` came out identical to `engine/targets.json` (curve, n, loudness).

**Strands against eras (granularity research, 2026-11-25).** In some genres the strand moves the curve more than the period: pop rock (`rock_punk` and `rock_altpop` beside `rock_2015`; a 2018-2021 refit is within 1.5 dB of `rock_2015`, pop punk 1997-2012 within 1 dB of 2013-2021, so no period curve), Latin pop (12-20 dB apart at 31.5-50 Hz between urbano and banda / norteño, up to 13 dB at 12.5-16 kHz between bachata and bolero) and caroling (choir and a cappella 20-25 dB apart at 50-100 Hz). In hip hop the era does: golden-age boom-bap is about 4 dB quieter and 3-6 dB darker above 6.4 kHz than 2015-2021 trap. Mastering beyond the curve per strand (loudness, dynamics, low end, top, playback; what open data can and cannot measure) is in each genre doc's granularity section, e.g. docs/LATIN_POP.md 'Granularity' D: loudness differs little between Latin strands before 2022 (-9.6 to -11.6 LUFS, bolero -12.7) while the low end differs by 12-20 dB at 31.5-50 Hz.

**What they ask of a mix.** In trance, hip hop and pop rock: the kick-and-sub band about 5-9 dB over 1 kHz at 50-80 Hz (where the older curves had their hump at 100-125 Hz), less 100-300 Hz, and a top 2-7 dB brighter at 8-16 kHz. Session 007's kick fundamental was carved by 5-11 dB to satisfy the old `trance` curve (docs/TRANCE.md C); with `trance_2015` that carving is no longer asked for. In the acoustic genres the top is far darker than the borrowed `rock` curve (caroling and chamber score -15 to -25 dB at 8-12.5 kHz) and the low mids leaner. **Unchanged rules:** the master boosts the top by at most +2 dB from 5 kHz up, so a brighter target must be met by the sounds (tonal content: saw harmonics, air layers, metallic hats), not by lifting synthesized noise (see "Top end"); the -8/+4 dB limits per band stay.

**Caveats.** Catalogue data are user-submitted files (12-51% lossless per set), mono, coarse (Bark bands) and end in 2021-2022: nothing here measures 2023-2026. Tags are broad (`trance` spans uplifting, progressive, psy and vocal; `hip hop` mixes boom-bap revival and trap). The reference-album sets are small (66-103 tracks for joc, caroling and the Latin strands) and wide (the middle half of the caroling albums spans 15-18 dB at the low end and on top; chamber score 9-21 dB above 4 kHz), which is why caroling, joc and chamber score keep a light `eq` (0.3-0.4) and caroling gets no low-end boosts at all. 31.5-40 Hz rest on one Bark band and the smoothness term (±3 dB); 16 kHz on the lossless files or, where fewer than 20 are lossless (joc, caroling, the two Latin strands), on the smoothness term (±2-4 dB). The caroling strands differ more from each other than any other genre's (20-25 dB at 50-100 Hz between choir and a cappella), which is why `caroling_ref` had its 15-18 dB spread; dynamics per strand rest on Essentia's dynamic_complexity, a loose proxy for LRA (r 0.44 on Spectre's masters). The granularity sets (2026-11-25) share every limit above and add their own: several are thin (`lv_folk_nometal` 26 tracks, `ua_dakhabrakha` 48, `ro_transylvania_revival` 69, `latinpop_mexicano` 75) or one act; AcousticBrainz stops in 2022, so no 2022-2026 strand is measured; and no subdivision of any genre has true-peak, PLR, LRA or width data anywhere (open or published). The cinematic sets are album lists of trailer houses whose library albums often have no AcousticBrainz data (Brand X Music, most Hidden Citizens, the *Humanity* chapters, all 2020-2021 Two Steps From Hell albums): the 2019-2021 curve rests on four albums; the sound-design (34 tracks) and pop-anthem (20) sets are lossy-only and are not targets.

## Top end (master version 2, 2026-11-24)
**The complaint.** Listening on a phone and on Mac speakers, the owner heard the rain, the hi-hats and the hissy elements of the released tracks stand out and sound like static. The orchestrator measured the released masters against the measured hip-hop curve, relative to the 1 kHz band: 006 2-7 dB hot from 2 kHz up, 001-005 3-5 dB hot at 12.5-16 kHz; a test re-master of 006 with `--genre hiphop` barely moved.

**Diagnosis** (006 v026 premaster, `--genre hiphop`, version 1; spectrum measured after every stage; deviation from the curve at 4k / 8k / 12.5k / 16k with the curve laid over the mix at the EQ's own reference, the median over 200 Hz-5 kHz):

| After stage | 4k | 8k | 12.5k | 16k | reference offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| premaster | -0.9 | +1.4 | +3.0 | +8.3 | +1.5 |
| tonal EQ | -0.6 | +0.4 | -0.3 | +2.3 | +2.9 |
| multiband | -0.8 | -0.3 | -0.5 | +2.2 | +2.6 |
| translation | -0.8 | -0.4 | -0.5 | +2.2 | +2.6 |
| glue | -0.8 | -0.5 | -0.2 | +2.5 | +2.7 |
| clip + limit (final) | -0.8 | -0.4 | -0.2 | +2.6 | +2.5 |

- **The stages after the EQ are not the cause.** Translation, glue, the clipper and the limiter change no top band by more than 0.3 dB; the multiband's high band takes 0.5-0.8 dB *off* 5-10 kHz. The hypothesis that the clipper or translation adds high-frequency energy is ruled out.
- **The EQ's reference drifted.** Version 1 laid the curve over the mix at the median deviation over 200 Hz-5 kHz, recomputed on every pass. That range includes the 2-5 kHz presence region it is supposed to judge, and in 006 its own first pass (+1.3 at 2 kHz, +0.8 at 4 kHz, -0.9 to -1.7 at 400 Hz-1 kHz) raised that median from +1.5 to +2.9 dB. Measured against 1 kHz, the master stayed 1.5-2.5 dB hot from 1.6 kHz up and the EQ never saw it.
- **The 1 kHz band is a poor reference for one mix.** The curves are averages over hundreds of songs, normalised at 1 kHz, so their 1 kHz band is smooth. 006 has a dip at 1 kHz between two note peaks (800 Hz +5.3, 1250 Hz +5.6 dB relative to 1 kHz). Relative to that single band everything above looks hot: the orchestrator's "2-7 dB hot" for 006 is about 2 dB of real excess plus the dip. Relative to the mix's level around 1 kHz (mean of 500 Hz-2 kHz), the released 006 master read -0.5 at 2k, -0.8 at 4k, -0.6 at 8k, +1.1 at 12.5k and +5.2 at 16k.
- **The top octave was under-corrected in every track.** Moving each band by only `eq` = 0.7 of its deviation, the [1/4, 1/2, 1/4] smoothing over neighbouring bands (which pulls 16 kHz toward 12.5 kHz), and the -8 dB limit left 12.5-16 kHz 3.6-5.3 dB over the hip-hop curve in all six released masters. Part of that was the target itself: the hand-tuned `beat` curve used for the releases asks for -16 dB at 16 kHz where the measured hip-hop curve has -20.

**The fix (version 2, the default; `--legacy-master` or `--master-version 1` keeps version 1, which still reproduces 001-006's first masters sample for sample, checked on 005 v025 and on fresh re-renders of 001 and 002):**
1. The reference is the mean deviation over 500 Hz-2 kHz: the mix's level around 1 kHz, where every curve is normalised. One dipped or peaked band no longer moves the whole curve, and the presence region no longer sets its own reference.
2. From 2 kHz up, cuts are made at full strength. Excess top end is what reads as harsh static on small speakers, and a cut there takes away nothing the mix needs. Boosts keep `eq` and are capped at +2 dB from 5 kHz up: boosting a dull top end mostly boosts hiss, so a dull mix is reported instead.
3. The 12.5 and 16 kHz bands may be cut by up to 12 dB (8 dB elsewhere). Synthesized hats and noise run flat up there, while every measured curve falls 3.5-7 dB per third-octave above 10 kHz.
4. A closed loop: the finished master is measured, and any band from 1 to 16 kHz more than 1 dB off the curve is corrected (only the part beyond 1 dB) and stages 3-7 are re-run, at most 4 runs. Whatever the dynamics stages do, the output lands on the curve within about 1-1.5 dB wherever the limits allow.
Masters take 1-3 runs of the dynamics stages, about 1.5-4 minutes. The limiter and clipper now oversample one channel at a time (same numbers, about 25% less peak memory).

No warnings in any remaster. 005 misses by up to 1.9 dB at 5-6.3 kHz on the dull side (the +2 dB boost cap, on purpose). Measured against the single 1 kHz band, the 006 remaster still reads +0.4 / +0.1 / +1.0 / +1.8 at 4k / 8k / 12.5k / 16k because of the 1 kHz dip in the mix.

**Side effects.** The hip-hop curve has much less sub than the hand-tuned `beat` curve (50 Hz: -0.5 against +6.5 dB relative to 1 kHz), so the remasters carry less 31-63 Hz than the first releases. At 40 / 50 Hz relative to the curve: 001 +10.5/+10.5 → +8.8/+10.2, 002 +10.5/+3.7 → +6.5/-0.3, 003 +12.4/+5.1 → +6.6/+0.1, 004 +11.5/+4.2 → +7.6/0.0, 005 +11.2/+1.7 → +7.2/-0.4, 006 +6.5/+8.7 → +0.7/+2.3. Every track except 006 still sits well above the curve at 40 Hz, and laptop loss, PLR and loudness are unchanged (±0.3). If the 808 weight is missed on big speakers, `--genre trap` with version 2 keeps the old low end and still corrects the top (its curve is 1-4 dB brighter than hip-hop at 12.5-16 kHz).
**MP3 true peak.** The 320 kbps encoder raises true peak by 0.2-0.7 dB, so every shipped MP3 (first releases and remasters alike) peaks between -0.9 and -0.3 dBTP, over the -1 dBTP aim but under 0 dBFS. `studio.py master` now records `mp3_true_peak` in the report. Not fixed yet: master a little lower or trim before encoding if the MP3 itself has to meet -1 dBTP.

**What mastering cannot fix: the noise sources.** On the phone check, the long-term top end of every track, before and after, sits at or below the curve (6-12 kHz -3.3 to 0 dB). Seen whole-track, the static is not a level problem; it is specific noises exposed in specific sections, and a static EQ can't separate them from the music. The 4-16 kHz content of every track is entirely noise (`noise_likeness_4_16k` 0.88-1.01): synthesized hats, snares, rain and breath, with no tonal shimmer. Measured with solo renders (share of the section's 4-12 kHz energy):
- **006 Līdz rītam: the wind** (the `rain` texture at +28 dB, then a 4.5 kHz low-pass) is 100% of 4-12 kHz in "night", "call" and "hour" and about 40% everywhere else; it also sits at 1-4.5 kHz, right in a phone's presence peak. "night" reads +3.0 dB over the curve on the phone after the remaster. Test in a /tmp copy: a second 3 kHz low-pass on the wind (and the first moved to 3 kHz) takes "night" from +3.9 to +1.7 and "hour" from +1.2 to -0.9 (premaster phone check). **Applied in v027 (2026-11-24):** on the full render "night" fell from +3.8 to +1.2 dB of 6-12 kHz against the curve, "call" from -0.3 to -3.1, "hour" from +1.9 to 0.0, the other sections by 0.3-0.6 dB; verse 1 still reads +3.1 (its hats).
- **004 Tura: the nai's breath** (`breath` 0.65, band-passed 2-9 kHz noise, driven 9 dB) is 100% of 4-12 kHz in "the wall" and 4-12 kHz there sits 1.5 dB brighter relative to the mids than in the hooks (-6.0 against -7.3 to -7.6 dB); the wall's 3-second window is the track's worst. Test: `breath` 0.3 lowers the wall's 4-12 kHz by 3.4 dB and 2-5 kHz by 1.9 dB, making it about 1.5 dB darker than the hooks, with no change elsewhere except hook 3b (-0.3 dB). **Applied in v027 (2026-11-24):** on the phone model "the wall" fell from +0.2 to -2.8 dB of 6-12 kHz against the curve, the other sections moved by 0.4 dB at most, and the master's worst 3-second window from +2.2 to +0.6 dB.
- **002 Lid Off: the snare, not the hats.** Folded over the 16th grid, about 95% of 4-12 kHz energy in the hooks falls on the backbeat snare hits; the 16th hats are -33 dB of it (under 1%) and -24 dB of 9-18 kHz. The remaster's 12.5/16 kHz cuts (-5.0/-10.4 dB against -2.8/-3.9 in the first release) take the hats' own band down about 2-6 dB more, but lowering the hats in the mix would change almost nothing measurable. If the backbeat still sounds like sizzle, darken the snare (its 3.5 kHz high shelf from -5 to about -9 dB, or a 7 kHz low-pass). **Applied in v033 (2026-11-24): the shelf at -9 dB.** On the snare stem it takes 3.4 dB off 4-12 kHz and 1.0 dB off 1-4 kHz (the 7 kHz low-pass mostly cuts 12-20 kHz, -10.5 dB, and only 2.6 dB of 4-12 kHz, so it misses the sizzle band); in the premaster, 6-12 kHz against the mids on the phone model fell from +3.5 to +0.2 dB over the curve, the worst window from +4.9 to +1.7 dB (the new clap, darker than the old click, is part of that).
- **Hats elsewhere** (001-006, `tone` 8-9 kHz, half white noise) are most of 9-18 kHz in 004 and the main 12.5-16 kHz content everywhere, which is why the top-octave cut matters on Mac speakers.
