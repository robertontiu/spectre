# Studio manual

You make the beat by editing `project.json` in your working folder, rendering it, and listening with the ears.

```
python3 engine/studio.py render              # render project.json -> renders/vNNN.wav (~15-25 s)
python3 engine/studio.py render --solo 808   # render one track alone -> renders/solo_808.wav (real level, not loudness-normalized; doesn't change latest.json)
python3 engine/studio.py listen              # overview -> ear.png + stats (whole beat)
python3 engine/studio.py listen --bars 9-12  # zoom: hi-res spectrogram + onset grid + timing roll -> ear_zoom.png
python3 engine/studio.py listen --section hook1
python3 engine/studio.py listen --mono / --lowpass    # squint checks (combine with --bars)
python3 engine/studio.py listen --wav renders/solo_808.wav --bars 1-4   # listen to a solo render
python3 engine/studio.py listen --phone --genre hiphop   # phone-speaker check: top end vs the genre curve, hiss per section
python3 engine/studio.py master [vNNN] --genre trap|hiphop|trance|trance_radio|rock|joc|caroling|latinpop|chamberscore [--lufs .. --tp .. --eq .. --translate .. --target ..]
        # FINAL STEP: masters the premaster of vNNN -> renders/vNNN.master.wav/.mp3/.png/.report.json
        # (--legacy-master reproduces the masters released before 2026-11-24; never use it for new work)
```
Every render also writes `renders/vNNN.premaster.wav` (the raw mix bus). The quick master inside `render` is only for listening while you work. The release master comes from `master`, which applies sub-sonic cleanup, mono low end, two-pass linear-phase tonal EQ toward the genre's target curve, gentle 4-band compression, bass harmonics for small speakers, glue compression, and a soft clip into a 4x-oversampled true-peak limiter. It reports LUFS, true peak (of the WAV and of the MP3, which the encoder pushes up by about 0.5 dB), PLR, loudness range, stereo width (side minus mid), laptop-speaker loss, remaining tonal deviations, `top_end` (each band from 1 to 16 kHz against the curve, with the curve laid over the master at its level around 1 kHz) and a `phone` check of the premaster and the master. Since master version 2 (2026-11-24) the master measures its own output and corrects again until every band from 1 to 16 kHz is within about 1.5 dB of the curve; it cuts excess top end at full strength but boosts a dull top end by at most +2 dB, so a dull mix stays dull and has to be fixed in the mix. If the report notes the EQ at its limit (-8/+4 dB, `eq_at_limit`) or the limiter working hard (over 6 dB), fix the mix rather than the master.

**The phone check (`listen --phone`, and `phone` in the master report).** Phones play almost nothing below 300 Hz and lift 2-5 kHz, so on a phone the top end and any raw noise is what you hear first; a hat, rain, vinyl or breath noise that sits fine on big speakers can read as loud "static". The check plays the track through a small-speaker model (high-pass 280 Hz, +4 dB at 3 kHz, low-pass 12 kHz) and prints: `6-12 kHz vs 1-2.8 kHz` against the same ratio of the genre curve heard through the same speaker (`air_excess_db`; 0 = on the curve), `5-16 kHz` likewise, the spectral flatness of 4-16 kHz (1 = white noise, 0 = pure tones; a mix's top made of hats and hiss reads about 0.15-0.25, a top that is nothing but noise 0.35 and up), the worst 3-second window, and a per-section table (`<- bright`: more than 3 dB over the curve; `<- hissy`: bright and noise-like). Run it on your render before you master (`listen --phone --genre <genre>`; add `--section`/`--bars` to zoom). What to do: a section flagged bright or hissy, or a worst window more than about 6 dB over the curve, is a mix fix, not a master fix. Find the source with solos (`render --solo hat`) and shape it: lower it, low-pass or high-shelf its top (hats rarely need much above 10-12 kHz; rain, wind, vinyl and breath noise usually belong under 4-6 kHz), or give it less time exposed on its own. The master can move the whole track's top end onto the curve, but it can't turn down one noise in one section.
**`--genre` is required** (there is no default): it sets the target curve, loudness, true peak, EQ strength and translation from `engine/genres.json`; any flag you add overrides that one value. Genres: `trap` (808 beats; target `beat`, a hand-tuned curve, -10.5 LUFS: the setup of sessions 001-006 as first released), `hiphop` (the same with the measured hip-hop curve; use it for beat sessions: sessions 001-006 were remastered with it on 2026-11-24), `trance` (-10 LUFS, translate 0.4), `rock` (pop rock; -10 LUFS, translate 0.3), `joc` (Romanian lăutar wedding dance music, joc țigănesc: the measured `rock` curve reused, -11 LUFS, translate 0.3; docs/JOC_TIGANESC.md), `caroling` (American Christmas caroling: the `rock` curve reused with a lighter EQ, eq 0.5, -14 LUFS, translate 0.2; a choir-and-bells arrangement without low instruments will show low-end boosts at the EQ limit, which is the borrowed curve, not a mix fault; docs/CAROLING.md), `latinpop` (Latin pop, intimate strands: bachata, bolero, ballad, acoustic pop, soft reggaetón-pop: the `rock` curve reused, eq 0.6, -11 LUFS, translate 0.3; a nylon-guitar-and-bass band shows a hole at 100-160 Hz against it: give the bass and guitars body in the mix and keep bass lines on A1 and above; docs/LATIN_POP.md), `chamberscore` (cinematic chamber score, whimsical and nostalgic: piano, chamber strings, glockenspiel, pizzicato, guitar, a whistle, neo-classical piano: the `rock` curve reused at eq 0.4, -15 LUFS, translate 0.2, mono below 80 Hz; without a bass instrument the master shows +4 dB boosts at 31-63 Hz at the EQ limit, which is the borrowed curve, not a mix fault; a waltz is 12 steps per 3/4 bar, 4 waltz bars = 3 engine bars; docs/CHAMBER_SCORE.md). The trance, rock and hiphop curves are measured from commercial records (docs/MASTERING.md): `rock` asks for a lot of 100-400 Hz body and `trance` for a 80-160 Hz punch with little 31-50 Hz sub, so a sub-heavy kick or thin guitars show up as EQ at its limit. Louder than about -9.5 LUFS gains little: a true-peak guard turns the master down if inter-sample peaks would pass `--tp`, and the report then shows `tp_guard_db`.
Always work from inside your session folder with `BEAT_DIR` set to it: `cd sessions/NNN-name && export BEAT_DIR=$PWD`, then call the engine as `python3 ../../engine/studio.py ...` (the commands above are written relative to the repo root for readability). After `listen`, open the printed image with the Read tool. That image plus the printed numbers are your ears.

How to read the ears:
- **Arrangement lanes**: which track sounds where and how loud. This is your arrangement view.
- **Mel spectrogram**: low notes at the bottom. Vertical stripes are drum hits (bright, full-height = snare/clap/hat; bottom = kick/808). Horizontal lines are sustained tones: chords and melody. Dense = busy; dark gaps = space.
- **Band energy** (sub, low, lowmid, high, air): the weight of the mix over time. Contrast between sections shows as steps.
- **Loudness curve**: the dynamics of the arrangement. A flat line from start to finish means no journey.
- **Zoom timing roll**: every hit the hand played against the 16th grid. Dot size = velocity. It shows swing, push/drag, ghost notes and rolls exactly.
- Stats: integrated LUFS, per-section loudness, centroid (brightness), onsets per second (busyness), stereo width, band levels, plus warnings.

## project.json

```json
{
  "title": "...",
  "bpm": 84,
  "swing": 0.58,                 // 0.5 straight .. 0.66 triplet shuffle (applies to odd 16ths)
  "seed": 1,
  "tracks": { "<name>": TRACK, ... },
  "patterns": { "<name>": PATTERN, ... },
  "arrangement": [ SECTION, ... ],
  "mix": {"reverb": {"size": 0.75, "damping": 0.55, "return_db": -4, "lpf": 7000},
          "delay": {"beats": 0.75, "feedback": 0.35, "return_db": -6, "lpf": 3500}},
  "master": {"lufs": -10.5, "sat": 0.15, "tape": 0.0, "glue": true}
}
```

### TRACK
```json
{"inst": {"type": "...", ...sound params},
 "gain_db": 0, "pan": 0.0, "width": 0.0,          // gain is relative to a sensible default per type
 "push_ms": 0,                                     // + = late (laid back), - = early (urgent)
 "humanize_ms": 4, "vel_jitter": 0.06,             // the hand's looseness
 "swing": null,                                    // override global swing for this track
 "dyn": [1.0, 0.7, 0.88, 0.66],                    // velocity shape per 16th position (drums)
 "roll_pitch": 0,                                  // semitones a roll climbs (+) or falls (-)
 "legato": 0.95, "choke": false,
 "send_reverb": 0.0, "send_delay": 0.0,
 "reverb_predelay_ms": 0,                           // the reverb hears this track this much later (vocals: 40-80 keeps words clear)
 "sidechain": {"source": "kick", "depth": 0.6, "release_ms": 120},   // works on any track, from any drum track
 "choke_by": "hat",                                // another track's hits cut this track's ring (open hat choked by the closed hat)
 "fx": [ {"type": "...", ...}, ... ],
 "mute": false}
```

Instrument types and their main params (all optional):
- **Drums**: `kick` (tune Hz 40-70, decay, punch 0-2, click 0-1, drive), `snare` (tone, decay, snap 0-1, body, drive), `clap` (the old clap, kept so earlier sessions reproduce; use `handclap` for new work: tone, decay, spread **in seconds**, default 0.009 = 9 ms between its 3 bursts; over 0.05 the bursts become separate clicks off the beat and the render prints a warning), `hat` (decay 0.02-0.08, tone Hz, tune, noise), `openhat` (decay 0.15-0.6), `rim`, `shaker`, `snap`, `tom` (tune, decay), `crash` (decay, tone), `cowbell`, `tridksnis` (Latvian trīdeksnis: a staff of metal plates and rings: a scatter of clinks over a thud; decay, thud 0-1), `darbuka` (stroke: doum = deep centre, tek = bright rim, ka = muted rim, slap; tune, decay). Use one darbuka track per stroke type
- **Monophonic with glide**: `808` (decay 0.3-3, drive 0-1, punch, tone 0-0.5 = 2nd harmonic, glide_ms, release), `bass` (cutoff, sub, drive, attack, release), `lead` (cutoff, square, vibrato, glide_ms), `ney` (breathy reed flute: breath 0-1, vibrato, attack, glide_ms; use `~` glides and short grace notes for maqam-style ornaments), `nai` (Romanian pan flute: purer tone with a breathy chiff on each new note; breath, vibrato, glide_ms), `dudas` (Latvian bagpipe: constant-pressure reedy chanter, no dynamics; pulse 0.2-0.45 = reed colour, drone = Hz of a built-in drone under the chanter, drone_level; ornaments via short grace notes since a bagpipe can't articulate with silence), `trembita` (Hutsul long wooden horn: natural-harmonic, swells brassier; attack, bright 0-1, blare 0-1, breath; sounds best on roots and fifths, low register, glides between harmonics), `fiddle` (folk violin, bowed: vibrato, grit 0-1 for a rough lăutar edge, bow_noise, attack, glide_ms; glides and grace notes make the ornaments; the older, synthetic-sounding violin: for new work use `violin`, a physically modelled bowed violin with articulations, see "Lăutar band" below)
- **Polyphonic**: `kokle` (Latvian box zither: plucked, bright, long ring with a soft shimmer; ring 0-1, bright, damped true for short notes; traditionally diatonic with low strings used as drones), `lira` (hurdy-gurdy: steady nasal wheel-bowed tone; long notes = drones; buzz 0-1 and buzz_rate for the rhythmic trompette buzz), `cimbalom` (țambal, hammered dulcimer: hard 0-1, damp 0-1; repeated 32nds = the classic tremolo roll; chords as arpeggiated or rolled accompaniment), `oud` (fretless lute (with `bright` near 1 and `damp` about 0.99 it doubles as a Romanian cobza): damp 0.98-0.997, bright; fast repeated 16ths/32nds = tremolo picking), `ep` (electric piano: bright, sustain, release), `piano` (felt 0-1, release), `pad` (attack, release, cutoff, voices, detune cents), `strings` (attack, release, cutoff, vibrato, detune), `choir` (vowel ah/oo/ee/oh, attack, release), `bell` (decay, ratio), `musicbox`, `pluck` (damp 0.99-0.999, bright), `organ` (drawbars [..]), `sine` (attack, release)
- **Textures** (continuous while a pattern lists them with the value `"on"`): `vinyl` (density), `tape`, `rain`
- Any tonal instrument accepts `"transpose": semitones`.

**Hand clap** (`handclap`, drum; use it instead of `clap`): a clap as several hands slightly out of time, fitted to recorded claps (model 2, the default since 2026-11-24). Each hand is a short noise burst through its own hand-cavity resonance (0.5-1.6 kHz, from Peltola et al. 2007, scaled to where recorded claps peak, 1.25-1.6 kHz) plus the slap of skin; the hands bunch in the first ~20 ms (later hands quieter), then a dense tail that starts with the first hand and decays over about 100 ms (-40 dB), a soft 100-400 Hz flesh thump and a top that rolls off above ~10 kHz, as measured on real claps (docs/TRANCE.md "Claps in trance"; A/B and spectrograms in `examples/claps/`). Every hit draws new hands and timing (round robin, `rr` variants, default 4), so repeated claps differ. Params: `clappers` (4; 1 = one person, 4-6 = a tight group clap), `spread_ms` (9; average gap between hands, clamped to 2-30 ms; 5-15 is the classic clap), `hands` (`mixed` default, `flat` = palms flat, brighter, `cupped` = domed palms, lower), `tone` (1.0; scales the resonances), `bright` 0-1 (0.35; the skin slap above 1.5 kHz), `body` 0-1 (0.5; the low thump), `room` 0-1 (0.35; the tail's level), `decay` s (0.034; the tail's time constant, clamped 0.01-0.4; highs fade twice as fast), `limit_db` (6; tames the bursts' needle peaks), `seed` (another set of hands), `drive` (0), `model` (2; `1` = the first handclap, a drier, brighter, ticking clap: only to reproduce session 007 v029). Level: it is spiky (peak about 18 dB over its loudness), so set it by its peaks: about 6-10 dB under the kick's peaks (check per-hit peaks, not `stems_rms`). Add `hpf` 200-300 Hz and a reverb send for a longer tail. Example: `{"inst": {"type": "handclap", "clappers": 5, "spread_ms": 9, "room": 0.3}, "gain_db": -7, "send_reverb": 0.25, "width": 0.4, "fx": [{"type": "hpf", "hz": 250}]}`.

**Trance** (see `docs/TRANCE.md`; example project `examples/trance-test.json`):
- `clubkick` (drum): four-on-the-floor club kick. tune Hz 45-60 (default 50), punch 0-2 (pitch drop), click 0-1, top 0-1 (909-style noise top), drive, hold s (0.045), tail s (0.085; short so the bass rolls between kicks), length s (0.32).
- `supersaw` (polyphonic, **stereo**): the JP-8000-style supersaw for leads, chords, plucks and pads. voices (7), detune cents (25; under 10 thin, over 30 out of tune), mix 0-1 (side voices vs centre, 0.7), spread 0-1 (stereo width of the side voices, 0.8), cutoff Hz (9000), reso 0-1, env Hz (filter envelope, 0 = off), fdecay s, attack, decay, sustain_level, release. `pan` acts as balance. Pluck: `"sustain_level": 0, "decay": 0.15, "cutoff": 900, "env": 6000, "fdecay": 0.07`. Pad: `"attack": 1.2, "release": 2.0, "cutoff": 2500`. High-pass chords (`hpf` 200-300).
- `trancebass` (polyphonic, short notes): offbeat/rolling bass, two detuned saws through a plucky filter over a sine sub. cutoff Hz (260), env Hz (2200), fdecay s (0.07), reso, sub 0-1 (0.8), detune, drive, decay, sustain_level, release. Play it with chord stabs and `"voicing": "root"` and sidechain it to the kick: offbeat `"..x...x...x...x."` stab_len 2, rolling `".xxx.xxx.xxx.xxx"` stab_len 1.

**Pop rock** (see `docs/POP_ROCK.md`; example project `examples/poprock-test.json`):
- Acoustic kit (drums; each repeated hit cycles through `rr` slightly different variants, default 4, so it doesn't machine-gun): `kitkick` (tune 50-65, decay 0.15-0.4, beater 0-1 felt to plastic click, shell, drive), `kitsnare` (tune 170-260, wires 0-1, rim 0-1 rimshot crack, decay = wire ring, body, drive; soft hits get darker), `kittom` (tune ~150-200 rack, 80-100 floor; decay 0.3-0.8), `kithat` (open 0 closed, ~0.4 half-open sizzle, 0.7-1 open; pedal true = foot chick; decay, tone, tune), `kitride` (decay 2-5, bell 0-1), `kitcrash` (decay 1.5-3.5, tone <1 darker >1 brighter; the old `crash` is a thin hiss above 4 kHz). Use one track per articulation (closed hat, open hat with `"choke_by"`, pedal hat).
- `eguitar` (polyphonic): electric guitar. Each note is a plucked string; the **amp runs on the whole track afterwards**, so chords distort together like a real amp. drive 0-1 (0.7; 0.3-0.5 crunch, 0.7-0.9 rock rhythm), mute 0-1 (palm mute; 0.7-0.9 = chug), dark 0-1 (cabinet darkness, 0.4), scoop 0-1 (mid cut), pickup 0.08-0.25 (bridge bite to neck warmth, 0.1), bright 0-1 (pick), pick_pos, sustain s. Use voicings `power` or `guitar` and stab `strum`. Double-track: two `eguitar` tracks at pan -0.85/0.85 with different drive, dark, pickup or voicings.
- `cleanguitar` (polyphonic, **stereo** after its amp): clean electric guitar (neck pickup) through a clean amp and stereo chorus. chorus 0-1 (0.5), chorus_rate Hz, drive 0-0.3, bright, pickup, mute, sustain. Arpeggios: `"style": "arp", "voicing": "guitar"`.
- `ebass` (polyphonic, play one note at a time): electric bass guitar. pick 0-1 (fingers to pick, 0.7), mute 0-1 (palm mute), drive 0-1 (0.2), tone Hz (amp low-pass, 3500), sustain s. Roots with stabs and `"voicing": "root"`, `register` D2-E2, `legato` 0.9.
- `vox` (monophonic with glide): wordless vocal-like lead ("ooh"/"ah") for the sung melody. An additive voice through a 5-formant vocal tract that stays put while the pitch moves, so glides and vibrato sound sung. voice `soprano` / `alto` (default) / `tenor`, vowel `ah` `eh` `ee` `oh` `oo`, vowel2 + morph s (each note moves from vowel to vowel2), vibrato semitones (0.3), vib_rate (5.3), vib_delay s (0.22), scoop semitones (0.5: singers slide up into a fresh note), glide_ms (90, for `~` notes), breath 0-1 (0.25), bright 0-1 (0.6), ring 0-1 (singer's formant around 2.9 kHz, 0.5), attack, release, sustain_level. Write phrases with breaths (rests) between them; `~` glides; reverb and delay sends. It reads as a flute more than a voice (little 1.5-4 kHz presence); use `vox2` or `singer` for new work.

**Lăutar band** (violin, acoustic guitar, tobă; see docs/JOC_TIGANESC.md for the idiom; A/B and spectrograms in `examples/joc/`, rebuilt by `examples/joc/build.py`):
- `violin` (monophonic, a bowed string): a physically modelled violin for solo lines, the one to use for lăutar and classical playing (the older `fiddle` is a filtered sawtooth, kept so earlier sessions reproduce). The string is a digital waveguide bowed through a friction curve (the bow sticks and slips, so Helmholtz motion, the scratch of the first periods, bow changes, slurs and slides happen in the string itself), and the whole track then goes through a modal violin body (the A0 air mode near 275 Hz, the B1 corpus modes near 470-540 Hz, dense modes under a 2-3 kHz bridge hill), so vibrato makes the partials shimmer through the body's peaks. Tuned within about 2 cents from G3 to A6. Params: `vibrato` (0.28 semitones peak; lăutar vibrato is wide and slow), `vib_rate` (5.4 Hz, wanders a little), `vib_delay` (0.12 s; vibrato develops after the onset and grows over long notes by `vib_grow` 0.3; notes under 0.16 s get none), `pressure` 0-1 (0.7; bow force: lower = airier, higher = more bite; high notes get more automatically), `bow_speed` (1.0; loudness of the string), `bow_pos` (0.12; bow-bridge distance as a fraction of the string: 0.06 sul ponticello, glassy and edgy, 0.2 sul tasto, soft), `bow_noise` (0.35; rosin and hair noise), `attack` (0.03 s from silence; bow changes inside a phrase are quicker, accents bite), `release` (0.06 s), `ring` 0-1 (0.5; after a note followed by a rest, the chance the bow lifts and the string rings on, against stopping on the string), `glide_ms` (110; `~` portamento, a finger slide: slow start, fast middle), `grace_ms` (45), `grace_at` (`before` default: the grace takes its time from before the beat; `on`: on the beat), `trill_hz` (13 notes a second), `trem_hz` (14 bow strokes a second), `intonation` 0-1 (0.6; expressive intonation: a note a semitone under the next one leans up by up to 12 cents, a note a semitone over it leans down by 6), `cold` 0-1 (0; a drier, clearer, less sweet tone: less and later vibrato, a leaner low body, more 2.5-5 kHz: one reading of the "vioară rece" in docs/JOC_TIGANESC.md), `warm` 0-1 (0.5; the A0/B1 body warmth), `dark` 0-1 (0.3; string losses and top roll-off), `damp` (0.3), `tune_cents` (0), `body` (0; another violin body), `seed`. Level: about -8 dB by default; set it against the guitar and bass by `stems_rms`.
  - **Articulations** (violin only): write them after the note's length, after a `/`, comma-separated: `"8:G4:2/g-1"`, `"60:D5:4/tr+1"`, `"44:A4:4/d=D4,acc"`. `s` slurred to the previous note (same bow, the finger changes: write runs as one stroke plus slurred notes); `~` on the length keeps its meaning (portamento, also in the same bow); no mark = a new bow stroke (the bow direction alternates, `up`/`dn` set it). `g` grace note (acciaccatura) a semitone below (`g-2`, `g+1`, or `g=C#5`); `m` mordent (main, upper neighbour, main; `m+1`, `m-1`, default +2); `tr` trill with the upper neighbour (`tr+1`, `tr+2`, default +2; ends on the main note); `ftr` the "false lăutar trill" (a bowed tremolo with brief touches of the upper note: `ftr+1`); `trem` bowed tremolo; `turn` (upper, main, lower, main); `sl` slide into the note from below (`sl-3`, `sl+2`, default -2); `fall` terminal glissando off the end of the note (`fall-4`, default -3; the Moldavian phrase ending); `sp` spiccato (the bow bounces off, the string rings); `pz` pizzicato; `h` natural harmonic (flageolet; play it at the sounding pitch); `nv` no vibrato, `vib` more vibrato; `d-5`, `d+3`, `d=D4` double stop (a second string at that interval or note, same bow; open-string drones D4/A4/E5/G3 sound best); `acc` a bitten accent (also `!` on the length). Mind the grid: a pattern's `step` and `len` are in 16ths, so at slow tempos 32nds are half steps (`"12.5:C#5:0.5/s"`) and 16th triplets are steps of 4/3.
- `acguitar` (polyphonic): acoustic guitar, nylon strings by default (`"strings": "steel"` for a steel-string guitar), Karplus-Strong strings with a finger or pick excitation, then a modal guitar body over the whole track (air mode ~100 Hz, top-plate modes ~190 and ~380 Hz, dense modes above), dry: no amp, no chorus. Params: `chop` 0-1 (0; damps each chord right after its length, with the thud of the palm: 0.7-0.9 for the afterbeat "țiitură" chops), `scrape` 0-1 (0.3; pick or nail noise on the strings), `bright` 0-1 (0.4 nylon, 0.55 steel), `sustain` (2.0 s nylon, 3.2 steel at 220 Hz), `pluck_pos` (0.16), `mic` 0-1 (0.5; soundhole warmth to 12th-fret clarity), `body`, `seed`. Afterbeat chords: `{"chords": "Gm:16 D7:16", "style": "stabs", "rhythm": "..x...x...x...x.", "stab_len": 1, "voicing": "guitar", "register": "G3", "strum": "down", "strum_ms": 9}` with `chop` 0.8; an `hpf` at 100-120 Hz keeps it off the bass.
- `toba` (drum): the tobă mare, the double-headed bass drum of village bands and fanfares with a cymbal (cinel, talger) mounted on top, struck by one player with a felt mallet and a rod. `stroke`: `boom` (the mallet on the head), `cinel` (the cymbal alone), `both` (the two together: the accented downbeat), `rim` (a dry knock on the hoop). Params: `tune` (60 Hz), `decay` (0.2 s), `cinel_decay` (0.35 s; 0.1-0.2 = held by the hand), `bright` 0-1 (0.5), `seed`. Use one track per stroke, like the darbuka. Round robin: repeated hits differ slightly.
- The rest of a lăutar band is already here: `ebass` (bass guitar: roots and fifths on the beats, `pick` 0.2 for fingers), `kitsnare` (the tobă mică / snare on the afterbeats; ghost notes), `darbuka` (the modern wedding and manele colour), `handclap` and `snap` (the dancers' claps and finger snaps), `cimbalom`, `fiddle` (the old violin), `oud` as cobza.

**Chamber score instruments** (celesta, glockenspiel, vibraphone, cello, viola, a string section; see docs/CHAMBER_SCORE.md for the idiom; code in `engine/chamber.py`; A/B against the old stand-ins and spectrograms against recordings in `examples/chamberscore/`, rebuilt by `examples/chamberscore/build.py`). Each is modelled on measured recordings (partial ratios, levels, decays and spectra measured, then the model rendered at the same pitches and measured the same way). The recordings are only analysed, never used in a track; they live in `~/.cache/spectre/refs/chamber/` (out of git): University of Iowa MIS (theremin.music.uiowa.edu, 2012 sets, "may be downloaded and used for any projects, without restrictions"), Versilian VSCO-2-CE and VCSL (CC0, github.com/sgossner), and a celesta pack by pjcohen on Freesound (pack 23108, CC BY 4.0, HQ previews). Write every note at its **sounding** pitch.
- `celesta` (polyphonic): struck steel plates over wooden resonators. An almost pure fundamental that rings, a soft felt-hammer onset, the plate's own overtones far down and gone within a second, a damper. Params: `hammer` 0-1 (0.5; harder lifts the overtones and quickens the onset), `pedal` (false: the damper stops the note at its length plus `release` 0.15 s; true: every note rings out), `decay` (1.0; scales the ring), `ring` (8 s; the longest a note rings), `release`. Range: a celesta sounds C4-C8 (some C3-C8); it reads best C4-C7. **Measured** (12 notes, A#2-F#6): after 30 ms the fundamental stands 35-55 dB over everything else; its T60 is about 5.4 s x (349 Hz / f)^0.2 (6.2-7.6 s low, 3.6-4.5 s at the top); the plate's first overtone at 2.71-2.76 x f1 and the second at 6.2 (low notes) to 5.36 (F5 and up), each about 40 dB down at 30 ms with T60 0.6-0.9 s; low notes add modes near 7.1-7.7 and 14.5-14.85; 2nd and 3rd harmonics about 50 dB down (the 3rd about 37 dB at F#6); 2-4 kHz about -30 dB and 4-8 kHz about -50 dB under the fundamental in the first 100 ms; the onset rises over about 10 ms (-20 dB at 2 ms, -9 dB at 10 ms), with a dull action knock about 30 dB down in the first 0.2 s; above 10 kHz -65 to -88 dB of the total. **Match:** 1/3-octave spectrum of the first second within 1.9 dB on average down to 40 dB under the peak (8.3 dB down to 60 dB: the recordings carry a -40 to -60 dB floor of room, sympathetic plates and MP3 coding that the model leaves out); envelope 3.9 dB; fundamental T60 within 15% on 9 of 12 notes (median ratio 0.99). The old stand-in `musicbox` misses the envelope by 18.4 dB (its fixed 2.5 s ring).
- `glockenspiel` (polyphonic): steel bars struck by a mallet, no resonators, no damper. The modes ring at the ratios measured per register (the nearest of four anchors: G5, C6, G6, C7), each with its own level and decay; the fundamental rings long. Params: `hardness` 0-1 (0.5 = the Iowa plastic mallet; 1 = brass, much brighter above 5 kHz; 0-0.3 = rubber or yarn, softer), `decay` (1.0), `ring` (7 s; the longest a note rings, faded out, no click), `damped` (false; true stops each note 80 ms after its length, a hand on the bar). Velocity also softens the stroke (a softer stroke is a duller one). Range: a glockenspiel sounds G5-C8 (written two octaves lower); write sounding pitches. **Measured** on the Iowa orchestral bells (ff, plastic and brass mallets, G5-D7), checked against Versilian's glockenspiel: (ratio : level at 30 ms with the plastic mallet, dB re f1 : T60 s) G5 3.20 : -52 : 0.38, 4.20 : -41 : 4.0, 6.65 : -17 : 0.50, 9.39 : -13 : 1.17, 15.0 : -26 : 0.86; C6 3.12 : -18 : 0.78, 6.22 : -18 : 0.18, 8.05 : -18 : 1.06, 9.71 : -25 : 0.38, 14.03 : -19 : 0.91; G6 3.01 : -17 : 0.70, 5.81 : -12 : 0.40, 6.42 : -34 : 1.15; C7 2.53 : -35, 2.92 : -26, 5.42 : -27 : 0.70; fundamental T60 7.9-11.6 s; the brass mallet lifts modes above 5 kHz by 12-20 dB; above 10 kHz -35 to -43 dB of the total in the first second with plastic, -17 to -28 with brass (discrete partials, no noise spray). Versilian's instrument has different ratios (2.78-3.04, 5.3-5.7, 8.9) and a shorter top (T60 2-5 s above C7): glockenspiels differ. **Match** (1/3-octave spectrum of the first second, mean |difference| down to 40 dB / 60 dB under the peak): 1.3 / 4.8 dB against the Iowa plastic mallet at `hardness` 0.5 (envelope 2.7 dB, fundamental T60 within 15% on 5 of 5, above 10 kHz 6 dB darker than the reference), 3.6 / 6.1 dB against brass at 1.0 (its fundamental rings longer, 9.6-18 s: the model's is 24% shorter). The old stand-in (`bell`, decay 1.3, ratio 2.76) sits 19.5 / 28.9 dB from the same recordings, with 24.7 dB too much above 10 kHz: the metallic spray.
- `vibraphone` (polyphonic, notes may overlap): aluminium bars tuned so the first overtone sits two octaves up, over tube resonators, a motor turning discs in the tubes, a damper pedal. Params: `pedal` (false: the damper stops each note at its length plus `release` 0.12 s; true: every note rings out, as with the pedal held), `motor` Hz (0 = off; 1-12 Hz is the instrument's range, Wikipedia "Vibraphone"; about 3-6 for the classic shimmer), `depth` 0-1 (0.5; how far the discs close the resonators: the fundamental breathes, the overtones don't; **hand-tuned**: no motor-on recording was reachable to measure it), `hardness` 0-1 (0.35 = the reference's mallets; lower = softer yarn, higher = brighter overtones), `decay` (1.0), `ring` (10 s). Range F3-F6 (C3-F6 on larger instruments). The motor turns once for the whole track, so chords tremble together. **Measured** on the University of Iowa vibraphone (2012 "sustain" ff: pedal down, motor off; C4, G4, C5, G5): fundamental T60 23 s (C4), 18.8 (G4), 14.7 (C5), 9.3 (G5), about 23 s x (263 Hz / f)^0.8; first overtone at 4.0 x f1, 5-21 dB down at 30 ms, T60 0.7-4 s; the second near 10 x f1 at C4 (Wikipedia: an octave and a major third above the first overtone, 10.08) falling to 7.9 at G5, from -9 dB at C4 to -49 dB at G5, T60 0.2-0.4 s; 2nd and 3rd harmonics 38-47 dB down; the fundamental builds over 5-20 ms in its tube. **Match:** envelope within 1.15 dB on average, fundamental T60 within 3-11%, 1/3-octave spectrum within 0.9-3.7 dB on C4-C5 (G5 9.5 dB: its 7.9 x mode). Level about -14 dB by default.
- `bell` with `"clean": true` (opt-in): the same FM bell, but each note rings on to -60 dB (at most `ring`, 9 s) and fades over its last 50 ms, instead of being cut 22 dB down at 2.5 x `decay` (the documented click). Without `clean`, `bell` plays exactly as before. For a glockenspiel use `glockenspiel`.
- `cello` and `viola` (monophonic lines, like `violin`): the violin's bowed waveguide string with a cello or viola body measured from recordings, and classical defaults: `vibrato` 0.12 semitones at `vib_rate` 5.4 Hz (measured: Versilian solo violin 4.3-7.1 Hz, median 5.2, ±5-17 cents; sections 5.4 Hz, ±10 cents), `vib_delay` 0.18, `intonation` 0.4, `bow_noise` 0.3, `ring` 0.6. Every `violin` param and articulation works (`/s` slurs, `~` slides, `/acc`, `/trem`, `/sp`, `/h`, `/d=...`, ...), plus the new **`/cresc`, `/dim`, `/swell`** (bow speed and force grow across the note, fade, or swell and fade: messa di voce; also on `violin`). **Pizzicato** (`/pz`, or `"pizz": true` for the whole track) is a plucked string with the measured decay (cello T60 2.2 s x (150 Hz / f)^0.5: C2 about 3.3 s, A3 1.8 s; viola 1.1 s x (300 Hz / f)^0.7) and its measured first-tenths drop (about 10 dB in 100 ms, 20 dB in 300 ms). Ranges: cello C2-A5 (reads best C2-E5), viola C3-E6 (C3-A5). **Body, measured:** the University of Iowa recordings (2012, arco ff, played without vibrato: 16 cello notes C2-D5, 14 viola notes C3-C6); each recorded note's harmonic levels divided by those of the bare waveguide at the same pitch, pooled and smoothed over 1/6 octave, then folded back in two closed-loop passes. Cello: about +4 dB at 100-200 Hz, +10-11 dB at 200-280 Hz, a +4 dB bridge hill near 2.5 kHz, -7 to -10 dB at 3.5-6 kHz; viola: a hole below 160 Hz, +10 dB at 350-450 Hz and 1.3-1.6 kHz, a +10 dB hill near 4.5 kHz. Dense modes under that curve make vibrato shimmer through the body as on `violin`. **Match:** the rendered notes sit 0.87 dB (cello) and 0.74 dB (viola) from the recordings on average, 1/6-octave, 100 Hz-8 kHz; pizzicato at the recordings' level against the arco (median -0.1 dB), fundamental T60 within 25% on 7 of 10 notes (median ratio 1.02), its first 100 ms about 4 dB brighter at 2-8 kHz than the recordings (a finger pluck rolled off from 3 x f). Level: about -12 (cello) and -10 dB (viola) by default, set to sit like `violin`.
- `strings_section` (polyphonic, **stereo**): a real section, so strings stop reading as a synth pad. Every player is its own bowed string with its own body, intonation, vibrato rate and depth, bow position and timing, spread across the stage; chords are played divisi (player j takes the (j mod k)-th note of a k-note chord). Params: `section` `violins` (default, 8 players, violin body), `violas` (6, viola body), `celli` (5, cello body); `players`; `spread_ms` (18; each player's onset scatter, sd); `detune_cents` (6; each player's intonation, sd, plus 3 cents per note); `width` 0-1 (0.7; how wide the players sit); `bow_env` `swell` / `cresc` / `dim` (applied to every bowed note without its own: the long swelling bow of the cinematic lift); `pizz` (true = the whole track pizzicato; or `/pz` per note); `attack` (default from velocity: about 0.05 s at forte, 0.3-0.65 s at piano, as measured; set it to force one); `air` 0-1 (0.5; bow hair and rosin above 7 kHz: the recorded sections carry 15-20 dB more above 10 kHz than bare strings); `seed`; plus every violin param (`vibrato`, `vib_rate`, `pressure`, `bow_pos`, `dark`, ...). Notes and chords both work (`{"chords": "F:12 Am:12", "style": "sustain", "voicing": "close"}`); articulations go on notes. **Measured** on Versilian's sections (VSCO-2-CE `susvib`, 16 notes of violins, violas and celli): harmonic width (the -10 dB width of the partials, vibrato and players' scatter together) median 34 cents, the model 36; level flutter over the sustain 2.35 dB, the model 1.6; attack to -3 dB 32-76 ms at forte, 300-700 ms at piano (the model: about 80-100 ms at velocity 0.8-1, about 250 ms at 0.3); spectrum above 10 kHz within 1-3 dB after `air`; 1/3-octave spectrum 7.8 dB from the recordings on average, mostly the hall and noise between the harmonics. Cost: one waveguide string per player (8 players render about as fast as one `violin` line per second of audio times 8; a 3-minute section track takes a few seconds more). Level: about -17 dB by default (sections trimmed so violins, violas and celli sit alike at the same velocity). Tip: three tracks (violins on top with `voicing` `close`, violas in the middle, celli on the roots) make a full string group; give them a long `send_reverb` (0.3-0.4: strings at a distance, docs/CHAMBER_SCORE.md "Mixing").

**Voices** (`singer`, `vox2`; code in `engine/singer.py` and `engine/svs.py`; voice auditions and studies in `examples/vocals/svs/`, wordless `vox`/`vox2` demos in `examples/vocals/`). A voice is your choice per track, never required; the genre docs say when and how each genre uses one (docs/TRANCE.md "Vocals"). **Mixing and mastering a voice:** [docs/voice/MIXING.md](voice/MIXING.md) holds the researched principles (words and masking, level against the band, reverb and pre-delay, compression and sibilance, doubles, chops vs lines vs choirs, what a synthesized voice needs, choirs and ensembles, what the master does to a voice, the measured reverb decay per `size`, and how to check a voice with Whisper and the vocal-versus-band loudness); each genre doc has a "Mixing the voice" section with a starting chain in engine terms (docs/CAROLING.md, TRANCE.md, LATIN_POP.md, POP_ROCK.md, HIPHOP.md, JOC_TIGANESC.md). Read the genre's section before setting sends and EQ on a voice. For words, use `singer` (below; approved by the owner by ear, 2026-11-24: "a clear improvement"). It is the only place Spectre uses neural models, approved by the owner, and only for sung words: every other sound is synthesized, with no recordings.
- **`singer`: words sung by a singing voice synthesizer.** `"source": "svs"` is its only source and the default: a `singer` without `source` sings with svs, and any other `source` stops the render with an error. A DiffSinger voicebank (a neural model trained on a real singer's recordings; OpenVPI ONNX, the format OpenUtau uses; code `engine/svs.py`) sings the line in one piece: its duration model sets every phoneme's length, its pitch model draws the melody's scoops, transitions and vibrato around your notes, its acoustic model and a vocoder make the sound. Nothing is stitched, so joins and cadence sound sung. Lyrics: one syllable per note, hyphens split words, `_` a melisma ("Writing lyrics" below); the vowel lands on the note and the onset consonants come just before it (DiffSinger's convention). A rest of more than about 12% of a note's slot starts a new phrase; shorter gaps are sung legato.
  - **`voice`: pick by id from the voice catalogue, [docs/voice/VOICES.md](voice/VOICES.md)** (tags for timbre, emotions, genres, what each is bad at, range, auditions in `examples/vocals/svs/voices/`; machine-readable in `engine/voices.json`): `hanami` (default; warm, clear, natural female), `hanami-power` (bright, firm), `hanami-soft` (soft, airy, dark), `hanami-dark` (low formants: solemn, rounded; less clear), `leona` / `leona-power` / `leona-soft` (brighter, younger character voices; beta); **Spanish:** `hanami-es` (Root) and `hanami-es-soft` (Nectar) sing Latin American Spanish (96% of words right by Whisper; VOICES.md "Spanish"). All of those are female; range: write A3-D5 (the render warns outside the voice's range). **Male voices: `male-light` (light, open; tenor or high baritone) and `male-deep` (dark, round; baritone), write B2-E4, English only. They are new and wait for the owner's verdict (VOICES.md "Male voices"): don't use them in a session until that section says they are approved; the render prints a note.** They are another model (SoulX-Singer continuing a six-second recording of a singer; `engine/svs_soulx.py`), so they behave differently: a new phrase takes about 100 s (then it is cached; a `double` is a second phrase), they take `seed`, `double`, `svs_steps` (16) and `svs_tune` (1: every note pulled onto the written pitch in a second pass; 0: the model's own pitch, 10-60 cents off) and none of `gender`, `velocity`, `inhale`, `svs_vibrato` or `lang`; they have nothing above 12 kHz (don't add an air shelf) and their syllable timing is looser than Hanami's, so give them held lines, calls, refrain fragments and choir parts (tenor, bass) and check a rhythmic hook by ear. Their credit line is its own (VOICES.md), and Lotte V's ban on political subjects does not apply to them.
  - **`lang`** (`"en"` default, `"es"`): the language the lyrics are read in; the Spanish voice ids set `"es"`, and `"lang": "es"` works with any Hanami voice (`hanami-soft` with `"lang": "es"` equals `hanami-es-soft`; LEONA has no Spanish). Spanish lyrics: plain Spanish spelling with accents (`co-ra-zón`, `a-quí`, `ma-ña-na`), hyphenated by Spanish syllables (one consonant between vowels starts the next syllable, stop or f plus l/r start one together: `pa-la-bra`, `es-cri-bo`; a diphthong is one syllable: `quie-ro`, `cuan-do`); one syllable per note; to merge vowels across words (sinalefa, "te escribo" on three notes) spell the merged syllable as one word (`tes-cri-bo`). The engine reads words with `engine/lang_es.py` (seseo, yeísmo, b/d/g softened after vowels also across words in a phrase) and the bank's Spanish dictionary; a syllable-count mismatch warns. Put stressed syllables on strong beats or longer notes: the voice has no stress marks, the notes make the stress. Rules and sources: [docs/voice/languages/es.md](voice/languages/es.md). **Romanian:** no voice sings it (docs/voice/languages/ro.md: a Hanami phoneme-mapping experiment exists in `examples/vocals/svs/romanian.py`, not wired, awaiting a native ear); use a wordless voice.
  - Params: `gender` -1..1 (the voice's own; negative = formants down, darker; positive = brighter; past about ±0.5 diction suffers), `velocity` (1; consonant speed), `seed` (each phrase's pitch curve and timbre noise; another seed = another take), `double`, `double_ms`, `double_cents`, `double_pan` (a second take: its own pitch curve, phrases moved a few ms, a few cents off, panned), `inhale` (0; > 0 = the voice takes a breath (its own sung breath) before a phrase that follows a rest of `breath_after_s`, 0.4 s), `svs_fade_in_ms` (5) / `svs_fade_out_ms` (5) (phrase edges), `svs_steps` (20), `svs_vibrato` (1; scales the model's vibrato and other fast pitch movement inside held notes, keeping the transitions between notes: 0.3-0.5 for straight-tone choir textures, pads and tight doubles, the way ensembles narrow vibrato for blend; 0 keeps only the slow movement; above 1 more vibrato; measured in docs/voice/GENERAL.md section 5, A/B `examples/vocals/svs/choir-vibrato-1.mp3` vs `-0.3.mp3`). There are no tuning or timing params beyond these: the model sings pitch and timing itself, with the vowel on the note and the onset consonants just before it (the right placement for a voice heard against a band: docs/voice/GENERAL.md "Timing").
  - **Licence (must follow):** credit every voice in the session README's **Credits** line and the site notes, with the `credit` text from `engine/voices.json` (e.g. "Vocals: Hoshino Hanami (DiffSinger voicebank by Lotte V, lottev.moe), Potluck Vocoder by tigermeat"). **Never use these voices in a track with a political subject** (Lotte V's terms forbid political use). Model files are not in the repo: banks go in `~/.cache/spectre/svs/<bank>/bank/` and the Potluck vocoder in `~/.cache/spectre/svs/potluck/dsvocoder/` (download links in VOICES.md; `SPECTRE_SVS_DIR` moves them); rendered phrases are cached in `~/.cache/spectre/svs/phrases/` (safe to delete: they regenerate bit for bit). Needs `pip install onnx cmudict pyyaml` besides onnxruntime. Cost: about real time on 2 CPU threads per new phrase (a 6 s line 5-6 s; first use of a bank also loads and patches its models once), cached phrases are free.
  - **Mix: put it in the band, not on top of it** (measured on the c1 chorus, `examples/vocals/svs/README.md` "Blend"): through a chain with hpf 150 and +3 dB at 500 Hz the svs voice sounded like "someone singing along to a speaker": 4 dB short of air at 8 kHz against the band, more mono than the band (L/R coherence 0.77 vs 0.52) and with its reverb 11 dB under the dry voice (8.4 dB in the version that worked best). (It is also 7-8 dB heavier at 500 Hz-1 kHz than the guitar-and-drum band, but that is the voice itself: EQ moved it only about 1 dB.) What worked best (Whisper word accuracy on the full mastered mix 62%, vs 42% before): hpf 100 Hz, a small 800 Hz dip, high shelf +3 dB at 8 kHz, more reverb send behind a 60 ms pre-delay (`reverb_predelay_ms`, a track key: the words stay clear and the tail joins the band's reverb), a quieter, wider double, soft phrase edges and a breath, and 2.5 dB less 2.5 kHz in the guitars. Early reflections (`room`) on the voice made it blend but smeared the words (Whisper fell to almost nothing). Example (session 009's chorus, pop rock guitars; vocal about 1 LU under the instrumental): `{"inst": {"type": "singer", "source": "svs", "voice": "hanami", "double": 0.2, "double_pan": 0.7, "inhale": 0.3, "svs_fade_in_ms": 30, "svs_fade_out_ms": 120}, "send_reverb": 0.32, "reverb_predelay_ms": 60, "send_delay": 0.12, "fx": [{"type": "hpf", "hz": 100}, {"type": "eq", "hz": 800, "gain_db": -2.5, "q": 0.9}, {"type": "eq", "hz": 3000, "gain_db": -3, "q": 0.9}, {"type": "highshelf", "hz": 8000, "gain_db": 3}, {"type": "comp", "threshold_db": -22, "ratio": 3}, {"type": "gain", "db": 13}], "gain_db": 12}` plus `{"type": "eq", "hz": 2500, "gain_db": -2.5, "q": 0.7}` on the guitars. **Measure the vocal in the section:** the vocal as heard in the mix is the premaster minus a render with the vocal track muted (`"mute": true`; a `--solo` render leaves out its reverb and delay returns, so it reads several dB low). A `comp` in its chain eats `gain_db`, so set the level with a `gain` fx at the end of the chain. **Level:** lead vocals in records sit about +3 dB (pop, rap) to 0 dB (rock) over the accompaniment (Gerdes & Siedenburg 2023); a voice used as an instrument sits at or under that, a pad or texture lower still (docs/voice/GENERAL.md section 4, with the sources for the pre-delay and the 2.5-3 kHz carve).
- **Writing lyrics for `singer`:** put the lyrics next to the notes in the pattern, one syllable per note:
  `"hook": {"bars": 8, "sung": {"notes": "0:C5:6 6:E5:10 16:D5:4 20:C5:4 24:A4:8", "lyrics": "first light break-ing through"}}`.
  Words are separated by spaces, a hyphen splits a word into syllables (`a-round`, `ev-ery-thing`), `_` holds the previous syllable over the next note (a melisma), punctuation is ignored. The render warns if the syllable count doesn't match the note count. Lyrics can also go in the instrument as `"lyrics"` (applied to all the track's notes in order) when one pattern holds the whole line. Keep it singable: open vowels on long notes ("are", "go", "ride", "high"), short words with simple consonants on 8ths, each word's stressed syllable on a strong beat or a longer note (the voice has no stress marks: the notes make the stress; docs/voice/languages/). A word the dictionary doesn't know is sung as "ah" with a warning: respell it.
  - **Register: write sung words at A3-D5** (F#4-B4 is the sweet spot). Higher up the harmonics are too far apart to show the first formant of most vowels, so vowels converge and words blur (a limit of real sopranos too: Joliveau, Smith & Wolfe 2004; docs/voice/GENERAL.md "High notes"); the render warns outside the voice's range. A hook written for a lead synth or `vox` at F#5-B5 should be sung an octave lower.
  - **Syllable rate:** aim for about 1-3 syllables a second: quarter notes or longer on the words that matter (at 136 bpm a quarter is 0.44 s), 8ths (0.22 s) only for light syllables with simple consonants ("and", "the", "you"), never 16ths (the model is left too little vowel).
- `vox2` (monophonic with glide): the improved **wordless** voice for lines and chops, pure synthesis (no model). Over `vox` it has an LF-model glottal source (Fant's `rd` voice quality: 0.3 pressed, 0.7 default, 1 modal, 2.7 breathy), small jitter and shimmer, breath pulsed with each glottal cycle, a vowel per note (`vowels`: `"oo ah ee"` cycles through the notes; formants move over `transition_ms` (80) on `~` glides), and soft consonant onsets (`onset`: a sequence like `"h . n . l d"`, one per note in turn; `h` breath, `m`/`n` hum, `l`/`w`/`y` glides into the vowel, `d` a tiny tap; `.` none) for "da", "na", "la" chops. Other params as `vox`: voice `soprano`/`alto`/`tenor`, vibrato, vib_rate, vib_delay, scoop, glide_ms, breath (0.3), ring (0.5), attack, release, sustain_level, jitter (0.006), shimmer (0.06), seed.
- Usage, trance: for a sung vocal, `singer` in the breakdown and hook (docs/TRANCE.md "Vocals in a radio edit" for the voices that suit it), A4-D5 (not higher: see Register), long reverb and dotted-8th delay sends, a light kick sidechain (depth 0.2-0.3) in the drop. For chops, `vox2` with `onset` consonants on short notes, or `singer` on one repeated word. Keep DJ intros and outros free of voice.
- Usage, pop rock: `singer` (`hanami`) for the lead (the melody a `vox` line carried) with the mix chain above (put it in the band), the verse in its lower range (A3-D5) where words are clearest, the guitars cut around 2.5-3 kHz rather than the voice pushed, and a `double` on the final chorus. A harmony is a second `singer` track with the same lyrics (another `seed`, or another voice a third away).

FX types (in order): `hpf` {hz}, `lpf` {hz}, `eq` {hz, gain_db, q}, `lowshelf`/`highshelf` {hz, gain_db}, `comp` {threshold_db, ratio, attack_ms, release_ms}, `drive` {db}, `sat` {amount}, `bitcrush` {bits}, `srr` {hz}, `lofi` {amount 0-1: wow/flutter, sample-rate, dust}, `chorus` {rate, depth, mix}, `phaser`, `tremolo` {rate, depth}, `delay` {beats, feedback, mix}, `reverb` {size, mix}, `pitch` {semitones}, `width` {amount}, `gain` {db}, `gate` {pattern, bars, depth 0-1, duty 0-1, smooth_ms} (trance gate: a step pattern from bar 1, string length / bars = grid like drum steps; `x`/`X` open, `o` half, `1`-`9` level, `.` closed; each open step stays open for `duty` of the step, e.g. `{"type": "gate", "pattern": "x.xx.xx.x.xx.x.x", "depth": 0.85, "duty": 0.75}`), `room` {size 0-1, mix, damping} (small-room early reflections plus a short tail: put it on each acoustic kit piece so the kit sounds like one room)

### PATTERN
A pattern is a set of parts for several tracks, `bars` long. Sections loop patterns.
```json
"beatA": {"bars": 1,
  "kick":  "X.....x...x.....",
  "snare": "....X..o....X...",
  "hat":   "x.x.x.x.x.x.x.xr"}
```
- **Drum steps**: the string length / bars = grid (16 = 16ths, 12 = 8th-note triplets, 24 = 16th triplets, 32 = 32nds). `|` and spaces are ignored.
  `X` accent 1.0, `x` normal 0.82, `o` ghost 0.45, `g` faint 0.3, `1`-`9` exact velocity, `.` rest.
  Rolls inside one step: `r` 2 hits, `t` 3, `q` 4, `s` 6, `z` 8. Uppercase (`R`, `T`, `Q`, `S`, `Z`) = crescendo roll.
- **Notes** (808, bass, lead, any instrument as a melody): `"step:note:len"` tokens, step and len in 16ths from the pattern start.
  `"0:F1:6 6:F1:2 10:Ab1:6~ 16:Db2:8"`. Suffix `~` on len = glide into this note from the previous one, `!` = accent, `.` = soft; an optional 4th field sets velocity (`"8:C5:2:0.5"`). Articulations go after the length behind a `/` (`"8:G4:2/g-1,acc"`); only `violin` reads them (see "Lăutar band"), other instruments ignore them.
- **Chords** (polyphonic tracks): `{"chords": "Fm9:16 Dbmaj7:16 Bbm9:8 C7b9:8", "style": "sustain", "register": "Ab4", "voicing": "keys", "vel": 0.7}`
  Lengths in 16ths (16 = one bar). `-:8` = rest. Slash chords work (`Ab/C`). The hand voice-leads between chords.
  style: `sustain`, `strum` (strum_ms), `stabs` (with `"rhythm": "x..x..x...x....."` per bar, `stab_len`; the string length sets the grid like drum steps, so 12 characters = 8th-note triplets), `arp` (`rate` in 16ths, `pattern` up/down/updown/random, `octaves`).
  voicing: `keys` (left-hand root plus right-hand close voicing), `close`, `spread`. Instrument shapes (no voice leading; the lowest note lands within a tritone of `register`): `root` (just the root: bass lines), `octave` (root + octave), `power` (root, fifth, octave: guitar power chord), `guitar` (an open-chord shape: root, fifth, octave, third and any 7th/9th above, up to 6 notes).
  Strumming (guitars): stabs accept `"strum": "down" | "up" | "alt"`, `strum_ms` (time across the strings, default 12) and `strum_grid` (8 or 16: with `alt`, hits on the grid's on-beats strum down, the others up). Up-strokes start on the top string, are lighter, and skip the two lowest strings of big chords. `"style": "strum"` takes `"strum_dir"` the same way.
  Qualities: "", m, maj7, m7, 7, m9, maj9, 9, add9, madd9, 6, m6, sus2, sus4, 7sus4, dim, dim7, m7b5, aug, 7b9, 7#9, m7b9, 11, m11, 13, maj13, 6/9, maj7#11, 7b13, mmaj7, m(maj9), 5
- **Texture**: `"vinyl": "on"`

### SECTION
```json
{"name": "hook1", "bars": 8, "play": ["keysA", "beatB", "bassA"],
 "fill": {"pattern": "fillA"},                       // replaces those tracks in the last bar(s) of the section
 "bpm": [100, 130],                                  // optional: this section's tempo (a number, or [from, to] = a ramp)
 "auto": {"keys": {"lpf": [600, 8000], "hpf": [20, 800], "gain_db": [-6, 0], "mute": false}},   // ramps across the section
 "enter": {"impact": 0.8, "crash": 0.6, "downlifter": 2},                       // on the first downbeat (downlifter in beats)
 "exit":  {"riser": 4, "reverse": 2, "rise": 16,                                // ending exactly at the section end (beats); rise = pitched build riser
           "cut": {"tracks": ["kick", "808"], "beats": 1},                       // drop-out before the next section ("all" for every track)
           "lpf": {"tracks": ["keys"], "beats": 4, "to_hz": 400},
           "tapestop": 2, "silence": 1}}
```
Note: an exit `cut` only silences its own window; 808 and reverb tails can ring on into the next section. For true silence in a section, also add `"auto": {"<track>": {"mute": true}}` there.
Section names must be unique. Bars per section should be multiples of 4 (2 for short breaks).

**Tempo changes** (`"bpm"` in a section): a number sets that section's tempo; `[from, to]` ramps the tempo across the section (linear in beats: an accelerando or a ritardando). Sections without it play at the project `bpm`, so a ramp that should continue needs the next section to start where it ended. Everything the hand plays (steps, swing, humanizing, rolls, note lengths) follows the local tempo, and so do the section's `exit` and `enter` lengths in beats. Two things stay on the project `bpm`: the shared delay (`mix.delay.beats`, and `delay` fx) and the `gate` fx grid, so don't rely on them in tempo-changed sections. The render prints each section's tempo, `listen --bars` and the zoom grid follow the real bar positions, and section names work as before. Only projects that set a section `bpm` are affected.

**Other meters:** a pattern bar is always four beats of the project `bpm`. For 2/4 music, one pattern bar holds two bars of 2/4 (steps 0-7 and 8-15); write sections in those double bars (the ears' bar numbers count them too). For 3/8 or 7/16 feels, see the step-grid notes above (12 or 24 characters per bar for triplets).

## Tips from the bench
- Trance: `"swing": 0.5` and `humanize_ms` 0-1 on drums and bass (machine-tight is the style). Sidechain bass, chords and pads to the kick (bass depth 0.8-0.9 / release 80-110 ms, chords 0.5-0.7 / 140-180 ms). A build: snare roll pattern, `"exit": {"rise": 16, "riser": 8, "cut": {"tracks": "all", "beats": 1}}` and `auto` `hpf` sweeps on the music. The breakdown has no kick: leave the kick's pattern out (a muted pattern still sends sidechain triggers).
- Pop rock: `humanize_ms` 4-8 on kit pieces, `push_ms` +3..+6 on the snare for a laid-back backbeat, `room` on kit pieces plus a shared reverb send. Guitars and bass are thin at 100-300 Hz: add body with `eq`/`lowshelf` there before mastering (the measured rock target expects +5 to +7 dB at 100-250 Hz relative to 1 kHz). Kick and bass guitar want different homes (kick body 60-80 Hz with `eq`, bass growl 150-250 Hz).
- Melodic and pad instruments sit far below drums by default; raise `gain_db` in steps of 2-3 dB.
- Hats live and die by `dyn`, `humanize_ms`, swing and rolls. The 808 should almost always sidechain to the kick or follow it.
- High-pass melodic tracks (`hpf` 120-200) so the 808 owns the low end.
- `lofi` plus `send_reverb` on keys gives the "sampled record" feel; `width` 0.3-0.7 on pads and keys opens the stereo field (the default is quite mono).
- For per-section track levels, read `stems_rms` in `renders/vNNN.meta.json` (one RMS value per 0.05 s per track); see `sessions/004-hustle/levels.py` or `sessions/005-fuck-war/stems.py`.
- The ears' "no contrast" warning only compares loudness, band levels and onset density; a purely rhythmic or textural change between sections can trip it. Check with a zoom before acting on it.
- The key detector reads modal material (Dorian, Romanian minor, Hijaz) as the nearest major/minor key; trust your own mode. In the zoom timing roll, row labels can look shifted by one track when several drum tracks share a hit; identify rows by their timing.
- `gain_db` acts before the track's fx: on tracks with heavy drive, saturation flattens the change; use a `gain` fx at the end of the chain to change the post-drive level.
- A render is about 20 s; an overview listen about 25 s. Zoom listens are faster. Projects with many acoustic cymbals or long distorted guitar parts render slower (the first cymbal hits are costly; later ones are cached).
