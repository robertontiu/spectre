# Studio manual

You make the beat by editing `project.json` in your working folder, rendering it, and listening with the ears.

```
python3 engine/studio.py render              # render project.json -> renders/vNNN.wav (~15-25 s)
python3 engine/studio.py render --solo 808   # render one track alone -> renders/solo_808.wav (real level, not loudness-normalized; doesn't change latest.json)
python3 engine/studio.py listen              # overview -> ear.png + stats (whole beat)
python3 engine/studio.py listen --bars 9-12  # zoom: hi-res spectrogram + onset grid + timing roll -> ear_zoom.png
python3 engine/studio.py listen --section hook1
python3 engine/studio.py listen --mono / --lowpass    # squint checks (combine with --bars)
python3 engine/studio.py listen --wav renders/solo_808.wav --bars 1-4   # listen to a solo render
python3 engine/studio.py listen --phone --genre hiphop   # phone-speaker check: top end vs the genre curve, hiss per section
python3 engine/studio.py listen --clarity --genre trance [--section drop2 | --section all | --bars 33-40]
        # stems: layer census, masking per stem, occupancy, low-mid build-up, crest, EQ map flags -> clarity.json
python3 engine/studio.py listen --seams       # form: every section boundary, how abrupt and how prepared -> seams.png/.json
python3 engine/studio.py listen --coherence   # form: the through-line, section x section similarity, two-songs split -> coherence.png/.json
python3 engine/studio.py listen --tension --events --loops --ring   # the ride: tension into each drop, events, loop runs, ringing -> ride.png/.json
        # both: analysis of a render (~10 s); --wav any render or MP3, --map its vNNN.meta.json (or a project.json)
python3 engine/studio.py master [vNNN] --genre trap|hiphop|trance|trance_radio|rock|joc|caroling|latinpop|chamberscore|dubstep [--strand ..] [--dynamics normal|keep] [--lufs .. --tp .. --eq .. --translate .. --target ..]
        # FINAL STEP: masters the premaster of vNNN -> renders/vNNN.master.wav/.mp3/.png/.report.json
        # (--legacy-master reproduces the masters released before 2026-11-24; --published the genre's settings
        #  before its era target of 2026-11-25; never use either for new work)
```
Every render also writes `renders/vNNN.premaster.wav` (the raw mix bus). The quick master inside `render` is only for listening while you work. The release master comes from `master`, which applies sub-sonic cleanup, mono low end, two-pass linear-phase tonal EQ toward the genre's target curve, gentle 4-band compression, bass harmonics for small speakers, glue compression, and a soft clip into a 4x-oversampled true-peak limiter. It reports LUFS, true peak (of the WAV and of the MP3, which the encoder pushes up by about 0.5 dB), PLR, loudness range, stereo width (side minus mid), laptop-speaker loss, remaining tonal deviations, `top_end` (each band from 1 to 16 kHz against the curve, with the curve laid over the master at its level around 1 kHz) and a `phone` check of the premaster and the master. Since master version 2 (2026-11-24) the master measures its own output and corrects again until every band from 1 to 16 kHz is within about 1.5 dB of the curve; it cuts excess top end at full strength but boosts a dull top end by at most +2 dB, so a dull mix stays dull and has to be fixed in the mix. If the report notes the EQ at its limit (-8/+4 dB, `eq_at_limit`) or the limiter working hard (over 6 dB), fix the mix rather than the master.

**The phone check (`listen --phone`, and `phone` in the master report).** Phones play almost nothing below 300 Hz and lift 2-5 kHz, so on a phone the top end and any raw noise is what you hear first; a hat, rain, vinyl or breath noise that sits fine on big speakers can read as loud "static". The check plays the track through a small-speaker model (high-pass 280 Hz, +4 dB at 3 kHz, low-pass 12 kHz) and prints: `6-12 kHz vs 1-2.8 kHz` against the same ratio of the genre curve heard through the same speaker (`air_excess_db`; 0 = on the curve), `5-16 kHz` likewise, the spectral flatness of 4-16 kHz (1 = white noise, 0 = pure tones; a mix's top made of hats and hiss reads about 0.15-0.25, a top that is nothing but noise 0.35 and up), the worst 3-second window, and a per-section table (`<- bright`: more than 3 dB over the curve; `<- hissy`: bright and noise-like). Run it on your render before you master (`listen --phone --genre <genre>`; add `--section`/`--bars` to zoom). What to do: a section flagged bright or hissy, or a worst window more than about 6 dB over the curve, is a mix fix, not a master fix. Find the source with solos (`render --solo hat`) and shape it: lower it, low-pass or high-shelf its top (hats rarely need much above 10-12 kHz; rain, wind, vinyl and breath noise usually belong under 4-6 kHz), or give it less time exposed on its own. The master can move the whole track's top end onto the curve, but it can't turn down one noise in one section.
**`--genre` is required** (there is no default): it sets the target curve, loudness, true peak, EQ strength and translation from `engine/genres.json`; any flag you add overrides that one value. **Since 2026-11-25 every genre masters toward an era-appropriate target** (measured on what the genre sounds like now or on its reference records, with provenance: docs/MASTERING.md "Era targets"). Genres: `trap` (808 beats; target `beat`, a hand-tuned curve, -10.5 LUFS: the setup of sessions 001-006 as first released, kept as it was), `hiphop` (target `hiphop_2015`: hip hop of 2015-2021, 347 tracks; use it for beat sessions), `trance` and `trance_radio` (`trance_2015`: trance of 2015-2021, 558 tracks; -9 LUFS, translate 0.4), `rock` (pop rock; `rock_2015`: pop rock of 2015-2021, 408 tracks; -8.5 LUFS, translate 0.3), `joc` (Romanian lăutar wedding dance music, joc țigănesc: `joc_taraf`, 103 recordings of Taraf de Haïdouks, the acoustic string sound, at eq 0.4; -11 LUFS, translate 0.3; docs/JOC_TIGANESC.md), `caroling` (American Christmas caroling: `caroling_ref`, the five reference albums, at eq 0.3 and with no low-end boosts at all (`max_low_boost_db` 0: a choir without low instruments is not pushed toward a bass it doesn't have); -14 LUFS, translate 0.2; docs/CAROLING.md), `latinpop` (Latin pop, intimate strands: `latinpop_ref`, the 24 reference albums, eq 0.6, -11 LUFS, translate 0.3; for a bolero or acoustic track add `--target latinpop_dark`, for bachata `--target latinpop_bright`: the strands differ by up to 10 dB on top; keep bass lines on A1 and above; docs/LATIN_POP.md), `chamberscore` (cinematic chamber score, whimsical and nostalgic: `chamberscore_ref`, the 30 reference albums, eq 0.4, -15 LUFS, translate 0.2, mono below 80 Hz; a waltz is 12 steps per 3/4 bar, 4 waltz bars = 3 engine bars; docs/CHAMBER_SCORE.md). What the era targets ask of a mix: in trance, hip hop and pop rock **more kick-and-sub (+5 to +9 dB at 50-80 Hz against 1 kHz), less 100-300 Hz and a brighter top** than the older curves; the top has to come from tonal content (saw harmonics, an air layer, metallic hats), not from the master lifting noise (it boosts the top by at most +2 dB); in the acoustic genres (joc, caroling, chamber score) **a much darker top** (about -15 to -25 dB at 8-12 kHz) and leaner low mids. If the master reports its EQ at the limit against one of these curves, say so in the journal rather than carving the sound to fit. The older curves stay available as `--target hiphop`, `trance`, `rock` (Pestana et al. 2013: number-one singles 1950-2010), `beat`, and `--published` masters with a genre's settings as they were before its era target (what its released tracks used). Louder than about -9.5 LUFS gains little: a true-peak guard turns the master down if inter-sample peaks would pass `--tp`, and the report then shows `tp_guard_db`. The report records the target's provenance (`target_provenance`). **Strands (`--strand`, since 2026-11-26):** a brief that names a strand masters with `--genre <genre> --strand <name>`: the genre's `master_by_strand` settings (target, loudness, EQ, dynamics) from engine/genres.json; `engine/master.py --help` lists the strands of every genre (e.g. `--genre latinpop --strand bolero`, `--genre caroling --strand choir`, `--genre chamberscore --strand piano`, `--genre dubstep --strand riddim`); an unknown name stops with the list. **Dynamics (`--dynamics normal|keep`):** `keep` is the dynamic path for acoustic records (no multiband, a light glue, no clipper, only the true-peak limiter); it is the default of `chamberscore` and of the caroling `choir`, `brass` and `handbells` strands, and `normal` (the old chain) everywhere else. Measured on 015: dynamic complexity 3.92 to 4.77 and LRA 8.2 to 10.6 LU at the same -15 LUFS and true peak (docs/MASTERING.md "Strands and the dynamic path"); the rest of an acoustic track's movement must come from the arrangement.
Always work from inside your session folder with `BEAT_DIR` set to it: `cd sessions/NNN-name && export BEAT_DIR=$PWD`, then call the engine as `python3 ../../engine/studio.py ...` (the commands above are written relative to the repo root for readability). After `listen`, open the printed image with the Read tool. That image plus the printed numbers are your ears.

How to read the ears:
- **Arrangement lanes**: which track sounds where and how loud. This is your arrangement view.
- **Mel spectrogram**: low notes at the bottom. Vertical stripes are drum hits (bright, full-height = snare/clap/hat; bottom = kick/808). Horizontal lines are sustained tones: chords and melody. Dense = busy; dark gaps = space.
- **Band energy** (sub, low, lowmid, high, air): the weight of the mix over time. Contrast between sections shows as steps.
- **Loudness curve**: the dynamics of the arrangement. A flat line from start to finish means no journey.
- **Zoom timing roll**: every hit the hand played against the 16th grid. Dot size = velocity. It shows swing, push/drag, ghost notes and rolls exactly.
- Stats: integrated LUFS, per-section loudness, centroid (brightness), onsets per second (busyness), stereo width, band levels, plus warnings.

**Clarity: ears for the stems (`listen --clarity --genre <genre>`).** The mix view can't tell whether the pad is audible under the lead, how many things live at 300 Hz or who supplies the air. `--clarity` re-renders one section in memory from the render's project file (two bars of pre-roll; an 8-bar section takes about 8-20 s, more with many voices), catches every track plus the reverb, delay and fx returns as stems, and prints for that section (default: the loudest; `--section NAME[,NAME]`, `--section all`, `--bars a-b`), writing everything to `clarity.json`:
- **census**: per region (sub 20-60, bass 60-150, low-mid 150-400, mid 400-2k, presence 2-6k, air 6-18k Hz) its share of the energy, the stems within 10 dB of the loudest stem there, and the **occupancy** (stems within 6 dB of the loudest in a 46 ms time-frequency cell, energy-weighted: about 1 = one sound at a time, 2-3 a blend, more a wash). An empty or single-source region is a missing role; five sources within 10 dB at 150-400 Hz is a crowd.
- **per stem**: its role (from `"role"`, or inferred from the name and instrument, marked `*`), its A-weighted level against the section, **masked** (the share of its energy in cells where the other stems together are at least 6 dB louder; over about 90% it is, in practice, not in the mix), **own** (the share in cells where it is the loudest), the stem that masks it most, and the **EQ map** check: its level per third octave in the regions the genre's map says to cut against the region the role owns, relative to the same balance in the genre's target curve (`cut +7.0 dB` = 7 dB heavier in the cut region, against its own region, than the genre's average record; 0 = balanced like it), the share of its energy under the map's high-pass point, how masked it is in its own region.
- **against the target**: the section's 50-80 Hz, 100-250 Hz (the low-mid build-up), 315-400 Hz, 2-5 kHz and 8-12.5 kHz against the genre's target curve laid over the mix at its level around 1 kHz (as the master does), and the **crest** per region (peak over RMS of the 10 ms envelope: a low-mid crest that collapses when you add layers is the measurable side of mud).
- **FLAG lines**: a core role empty in a peak section (the genre map's `core` list), a region with a single source in a peak section (thin), a stem over 90% masked, more than four sources within 10 dB at 150-400 Hz, 100-250 Hz more than 3 dB over the target, and EQ-map deviations beyond the genre's tolerance. All thresholds are hand-tuned; treat flags as candidates to check by solo and by zoom, not as verdicts. Spectre's earlier drops read **thin, not muddy**: one stem per role and 1.0-1.5 stems per cell.
The EQ maps are data in `engine/eqmaps.json`, one per genre, taken from the genre docs' "Layering and EQ carving" tables (trance: the professional producer's verified map); `trance_radio` uses trance's and `trap` hip hop's. Without `--genre` you get the census and masking but no map, target or role checks. The analysis is a simplified masking model (no spreading function): it finds candidates; your knowledge of the sounds and the owner's ears decide.

**Seams and coherence: ears for form (`listen --seams`, `listen --coherence`, since 2026-11-26).** The overview shows each section; these show what happens *between* them and what holds the whole together (the research: docs/PRODUCTION.md "Seams and the through-line"). Both read an existing render and never re-render (about 10 s each).

`--seams` prints one row per section boundary and draws `seams.png` (top: the step per dimension, stacked; bottom: prepared and carried):
- **steps**: ten dimensions compared between the last two bars before the line and the first two after it, each in units of one clearly audible step (hand-tuned): level 3 dB, low end (20-150 Hz against the mix) 5 dB, balance (the bands above 150 Hz) 2.5 dB, brightness (centroid) 0.4 octave, pulse (the low band's onsets: the kick) 4 dB, density (onsets per second) x1.74, groove (onset strength on the 16 sixteenths) 0.25, timbre (MFCC, against the track's own spread), harmony (chroma) 0.15, width (side/mid) 4 dB. They are grouped into five **families** (loudness, spectrum, rhythm, harmony, space), each family's step its largest dimension's, plus **streams**: a sound you carry across the line whose automated filter or gain jumps at it (one octave or 6 dB = one step; `jumps:` names it, e.g. `acid.core cutoff 3200->500 Hz`). `score` = the sum of the family steps (each capped at 3), `#` = the families stepping a whole step or more.
- **prep** (0-1), how much of the change was prepared: 0.4 x the audio's own preparation (for each stepping dimension, the share of its move from eight bars before to the new section that already happened before the line: a filter opening toward the drop, the hats thinning into the break), 0.2 x **carry** (the share of what played before the line still playing after it), 0.25 x **ret** (the new section lands somewhere the track has already been, like a drop after a breakdown), 0.15 x the declared devices (the old section's `exit` riser/rise/reverse/lpf/tapestop, a `fill`, the new section's `enter` impact/crash/downlifter, `auto` ramps), minus 0.1 for each dimension that moved *away* from where the next section lands, when it lands on new ground (`against:`; a squeal that opens up into a section that closes down).
- **gesture**: `drop-in` / `drop-out` (level, low end, pulse and density moving the same way: the arrangement's grammar, read at once), `layering` (60% or more of the bed kept: entries or exits over it), `swap` (the content changes at about the same energy), `small`. `in:` and `out:` list the stems that enter or leave.
- **FLAG** when the seam is broad (3 or more families, or a score of 5 or more; song genres 6) **and** prep is under 0.3, **and** it is not a drop-in, drop-out or layering, **and** it does not lead into a section of 2 bars or less (a cliff, a stop, a squeal: judged at the seam after it). Genre families: DJ genres (techno, trance, psytrance, dubstep) and song genres (hip hop, rock, pop, folk, chamber, cinematic); a genre's `"seams"` entry in engine/genres.json overrides the numbers. Calibrated on sessions 016-020 and 007 (2026-11-26): it flags Redline's bar 81 (the owner's "forced and way off") and nothing in the tracks the owner liked. A flag is a candidate: listen to the zoom on those bars before acting.
- **A deliberate hard cut**: put `"seam": "cut"` on the incoming section and say in the journal why the cut serves the feeling; the row then reads `cut` instead of FLAG.

`--coherence` prints the section-by-section similarity and draws `coherence.png` (five matrices; bright = shared, the red line the split):
- **motif**: the melodic cells (four notes as three intervals, transposition-free and rhythm-free, from every pitched track's top line, repeated notes merged) two sections share; a riff moved to another instrument, transposed or played at half speed still counts. **pitch**: the pitch-class profiles of the notes. **palette**: which pitched stems play, from the render's stem levels. **timbre**: the audio's MFCC and chroma. **combined** = 0.4 motif + 0.2 each of the others. The first three come exactly from the render's events; a published MP3 with only a project.json gets timbre alone.
- **the motivic split**: where the motif matrix breaks into two blocks (Foote's checkerboard), the share of the second block's cells already heard in the first, each later section's best match in the first block, the pitched stems sounding in both blocks and those heard only in the second (not foreshadowed).
- **FLAG two songs** when under 58% of the second block's cells were heard in the first and its best motif match there is under 0.45 (hand-tuned, raised 2026-11-26 after the owner heard All Hands v035, at 53% and 0.37, as still two acts; v030 was 19% and 0.20; the liked tracks 61-97%; Redline 49% with a best match of 0.53, heard as one piece). The motifs are not everything: v035 shares every cell across its act break and still splits by ear, so read `--tension` and `--loops` on a second act too (a 47-bar respiro and 16-bar loops separate acts as well). The cure is a through-line, not a fade: a motif of the first act transformed in the second (re-voiced, half speed, inverted, in the new key), the second act's new material foreshadowed in the first, a sound that stays. **Deliberate acts**: `"form": {"acts": "intended"}` at the top of project.json, with the reason in the journal.

**Tension, events, loops and ringing: ears for the ride (`listen --tension --events --loops --ring`, since 2026-11-26).** What happens *inside* the sections: does a breakdown build tension, does the groove have moments, how long does a loop run unchanged, does one tone ring over the track (the research: docs/PRODUCTION.md "4c. Tension, events and repetition"). Any combination of the four flags, also together with `--seams --coherence`; they read an existing render (about 20 s; `--ring` adds a 4-bar window render in memory to name the stem, `--no-stems` skips it) and draw `ride.png` (one panel per check) and `ride.json`. A published MP3 works with `--wav` and `--map` (the render's meta.json, or a project.json and `--genre`; without the render's events the kick and the notes come from the audio).
- **`--tension`**: a curve per bar after Farbood (2012): 0.30 loudness + 0.20 pitch (the top of the notes and the audio's brightness) + 0.25 onsets per second + 0.10 harmony (the bar's chroma against the track's home) + 0.15 noise sweep (the noisy 3-11 kHz level: risers), each in track z-units clipped at ±3. One row per **respiro** (four bars or more with the kick out, inside the track; consecutive kickless sections merge) and per section named build/rise/lift: `rise` (the last four bars over the lowest point), `climb` (bars from the last one in the bottom quarter of the range to the end), `slope/8` and `end/8` (per eight bars, over the whole span and its last eight bars), `step` (into what follows: negative is the release, normal), and which components rose. **FLAG dull** (DJ genres: techno, trance, psytrance, dubstep; reported elsewhere): rise under 0.6, or the last eight bars falling with a rise under 1.2, or a respiro of 12 bars or more that climbs over less than 40% of its bars (it waits at the bottom, then a short build: the owner's "dull respiro"). Fix by building it as tension: rising pitch or filter, a riser or noise swell, a roll that accelerates, pads or chords swelling, a held suspension, a voice entering. A deliberate plateau: `"tension": "flat"` on the section, with the reason in the journal.
- **`--events`**: every beat against the median of the same beat in the four bars before it (24 mel bands in dB; the departure is the mean of its six largest band departures, so a hit in the top of the spectrum counts). 6 dB or more is an event: `*` **highlight** (six beats or less) or **change** (longer: an entry, an exit, a new section). Kinds: `freeze` (the mix 9 dB or more under the bars before), `kick stop` (the kick's events missing, or its stem 20 dB down), `fill` (onsets x1.4), `hit:<stem>` (a stem heard only there), `rise`/`dip`. Every freeze and kick stop gets `approach:` (riser, filter dip, fill/roll, swell) or `bare`. Then the count per 16 bars (highlights/changes), the highlights per 16 bars in the body (DJ genres: from the first to the last bar with the kick), the longest stretch without a highlight and without any event. **FLAGs**: more than 32 bars without a highlight; more than 16 without any event; a DJ track with no freeze or kick stop in its body; any bare freeze (approach it, or say in the journal why it is bare).
- **`--loops`**: runs of bars in which no beat departs 4 dB from the learned beat and the bar stays within 4 dB of the bar eight before (a slow ramp ends a run after eight bars); `notes repeat every N` or `notes vary` (from the render's events: a run can be one pattern repeating, or changing notes under a sound that does not change). **FLAG** runs over 12 bars in DJ genres, over 8 in song genres; in DJ genres a run inside the first or last 32 bars is reported as `DJ end`, not flagged. The total share of the bars in flagged runs is printed. A deliberate long loop: `"loop": "long"` on the section, with the reason.
- **`--ring`**: a 0.74 s spectrum on a log grid (48 per octave), each bin against the median of ±1/6 octave. Per candidate: `share` (sounding frames where it stands out 8 dB or more), `prom` (median prominence), `excess` (the tone against the rest of its third-octave band), `dBA vs mix`, the 8-bar blocks where it stands out, `notes:` (tracks whose notes or `tune` sit on it, harmonics up to the 8th) and `stems:` (a 4-bar window render: each stem's energy at that frequency against the mix's; a stem near 0 dB or above owns it). **FLAG** a tone at or above 150 Hz with share 0.5 or more, excess -3 dB or more, standing out in a third of the 8-bar blocks or more. The cure is musical first (retune or alternate the drum, shorten its ring, vary the pitch), then a narrow cut. Validated on Redline v030: 659.5 Hz, the open bongo tuned to E5, in 21 of 28 blocks.
- Calibrated 2026-11-26 on the owner's verdicts (Redline v030, All Hands v035) and on the published tracks he liked; 018 and 008 pass everything, the earlier trance tracks (007, 016) and 013, 017 flag dull builds, long loops or missing freezes, in line with his later verdict on them (docs/PRODUCTION.md 4c, "Calibration"). Thresholds are hand-tuned; a flag is a candidate to check by zoom.

## project.json

```json
{
  "title": "...",
  "bpm": 84,
  "swing": 0.58,                 // 0.5 straight .. 0.66 triplet shuffle (applies to odd 16ths)
  "seed": 1,
  "seeds": "per-track",          // new projects: each track draws its own random streams (see below)
  "voice_defaults": 2,           // new projects: the owner's voice treatment as defaults (see below)
  "tracks": { "<name>": TRACK, ... },
  "patterns": { "<name>": PATTERN, ... },
  "arrangement": [ SECTION, ... ],
  "mix": {"reverb": {"size": 0.75, "damping": 0.55, "return_db": -4, "lpf": 7000},
          "delay": {"beats": 0.75, "feedback": 0.35, "return_db": -6, "lpf": 3500},
          "returns": { "<name>": RETURN, ... }},      // optional: more reverbs and delays ("Production" below)
  "genre": "trance",                 // optional: role EQ presets and listen --clarity read it ("Production" below)
  "buses": { "<name>": BUS, ... },   // optional: group buses ("Production" below)
  "master": {"lufs": -10.5, "sat": 0.15, "tape": 0.0, "glue": true},
  "form": {"acts": "intended"}       // optional: the track is two acts on purpose (listen --coherence won't flag it; say why in the journal)
}
```
**`"seeds"`** (since 2026-11-25): `"per-track"` gives every track its own random streams (the hand's humanizing and the instrument's random phases, noise and detune), seeded from `seed` and the track's name; the transition fx get their own. Then adding, removing or reordering a track never changes how the others sound, so a layer added in the mix stage, or a remix, leaves every other track sample-identical, and `render --solo` plays a track exactly as it sounds in the mix. Without it (`"shared"`, the default, used by every project made before 2026-11-25) all tracks draw from one stream in order and adding a track shifts the others' random details; keep it that way on old projects so they reproduce. **Start every new project with `"seeds": "per-track"`.** Renaming a track gives it new random details.

**`"voice_defaults"`** (since 2026-11-26): `2` gives every `singer` track the owner's chosen treatment as defaults, for whatever the track does not set itself: a voice (any role but `choir`) gets the blend-d chain (examples/vocals/svs/blend-d.mp3, chosen by the owner's ear 2026-11-26): `fx` hpf 100 Hz, 800 Hz -2.5 dB (q 0.9), 3 kHz -3 dB (q 0.9), high shelf +3 dB at 8 kHz, `comp` -22 dB 3:1; `send_reverb` 0.32 behind `reverb_predelay_ms` 60 (not added when the track uses named `sends`), `send_delay` 0.12; in the instrument `double` 0.2, `double_pan` 0.7, `inhale` 0.3, `svs_fade_in_ms` 30, `svs_fade_out_ms` 120. A choir voice (`"role": "choir"`) gets `svs_vibrato` 0.3 (examples/vocals/svs/choir-vibrato-0.3.mp3, chosen over 1). A key you set wins, and `fx` is one key: give a voice its own `fx` and you replace the whole chain (start from the one above). Set the voice's level with `gain_db`; the guitars' 2.5 kHz dip of blend-d is a mix move on the band, not part of the default. Without the key (`1`, every project before 2026-11-26) nothing is added, so old projects reproduce. **Start every new project with `"voice_defaults": 2`.**

### TRACK
```json
{"inst": {"type": "...", ...sound params},
 "gain_db": 0, "pan": 0.0, "width": 0.0,          // gain is relative to a sensible default per type
 "push_ms": 0,                                     // + = late (laid back), - = early (urgent)
 "humanize_ms": 4, "vel_jitter": 0.06,             // the hand's looseness
 "swing": null,                                    // override global swing for this track
 "dyn": [1.0, 0.7, 0.88, 0.66],                    // velocity shape per 16th position (drums)
 "roll_pitch": 0,                                  // semitones a roll climbs (+) or falls (-)
 "legato": 0.95, "choke": false,
 "send_reverb": 0.0, "send_delay": 0.0,
 "reverb_predelay_ms": 0,                           // the reverb hears this track this much later (vocals: 40-80 keeps words clear)
 "sidechain": {"source": "kick", "depth": 0.6, "release_ms": 120},   // works on any track, from any drum track
 "choke_by": "hat",                                // another track's hits cut this track's ring (open hat choked by the closed hat)
 "role": "pad",                                    // what this track supplies (listen --clarity checks it against the genre's EQ map)
 "fx": [ {"type": "...", ...}, ... ],
 "mute": false,
 // production keys (opt-in, see "Production: stacks, buses, returns" below):
 "layers": [ LAYER, ... ],                         // a stack: several instruments play this track's notes
```

**Hand clap** (`handclap`, drum; use it instead of `clap`): a clap as several hands slightly out of time, fitted to recorded claps (model 2, the default since 2026-11-24). Each hand is a short noise burst through its own hand-cavity resonance (0.5-1.6 kHz, from Peltola et al. 2007, scaled to where recorded claps peak, 1.25-1.6 kHz) plus the slap of skin; the hands bunch in the first ~20 ms (later hands quieter), then a dense tail that starts with the first hand and decays over about 100 ms (-40 dB), a soft 100-400 Hz flesh thump and a top that rolls off above ~10 kHz, as measured on real claps (docs/TRANCE.md "Claps in trance"; A/B and spectrograms in `examples/claps/`). Every hit draws new hands and timing (round robin, `rr` variants, default 4), so repeated claps differ. Params: `clappers` (4; 1 = one person, 4-6 = a tight group clap), `spread_ms` (9; average gap between hands, clamped to 2-30 ms; 5-15 is the classic clap), `hands` (`mixed` default, `flat` = palms flat, brighter, `cupped` = domed palms, lower), `tone` (1.0; scales the resonances), `bright` 0-1 (0.35; the skin slap above 1.5 kHz), `body` 0-1 (0.5; the low thump), `room` 0-1 (0.35; the tail's level), `decay` s (0.034; the tail's time constant, clamped 0.01-0.4; highs fade twice as fast), `limit_db` (6; tames the bursts' needle peaks), `seed` (another set of hands), `drive` (0), `model` (2; `1` = the first handclap, a drier, brighter, ticking clap: only to reproduce session 007 v029). Level: it is spiky (peak about 18 dB over its loudness), so set it by its peaks: about 6-10 dB under the kick's peaks (check per-hit peaks, not `stems_rms`). Add `hpf` 200-300 Hz and a reverb send for a longer tail. Example: `{"inst": {"type": "handclap", "clappers": 5, "spread_ms": 9, "room": 0.3}, "gain_db": -7, "send_reverb": 0.25, "width": 0.4, "fx": [{"type": "hpf", "hz": 250}]}`.

**Pop rock** (see `docs/POP_ROCK.md`; example project `examples/poprock-test.json`):

- `eguitar` (polyphonic): electric guitar. - `ebass` (polyphonic, play one note at a time): electric bass guitar. pick 0-1 (fingers to pick, 0.7), mute 0-1 (palm mute), drive 0-1 (0.2), tone Hz (amp low-pass, 3500), sustain s. Roots with stabs and `"voicing": "root"`, `register` D2-E2, `legato` 0.9.
- `vox` (monophonic with glide): wordless vocal-like lead ("ooh"/"ah") for the sung melody. **Lăutar band** (violin, acoustic guitar, tobă; see docs/JOC_TIGANESC.md for the idiom; A/B and spectrograms in `examples/joc/`, rebuilt by `examples/joc/build.py`):
- `violin` (monophonic, a bowed string): a physically modelled violin for solo lines, the one to use for lăutar and classical playing (the older `fiddle` is a filtered sawtooth, kept so earlier sessions reproduce). The string is a digital waveguide bowed through a friction curve (the bow sticks and slips, so Helmholtz motion, the scratch of the first periods, bow changes, slurs and slides happen in the string itself), and the whole track then goes through a modal violin body (the A0 air mode near 275 Hz, the B1 corpus modes near 470-540 Hz, dense modes under a 2-3 kHz bridge hill), so vibrato makes the partials shimmer through the body's peaks. Tuned within about 2 cents from G3 to A6. Params: `vibrato` (0.28 semitones peak; lăutar vibrato is wide and slow), `vib_rate` (5.4 Hz, wanders a little), `vib_delay` (0.12 s; vibrato develops after the onset and grows over long notes by `vib_grow` 0.3; notes under 0.16 s get none), `pressure` 0-1 (0.7; bow force: lower = airier, higher = more bite; high notes get more automatically), `bow_speed` (1.0; loudness of the string), `bow_pos` (0.12; bow-bridge distance as a fraction of the string: 0.06 sul ponticello, glassy and edgy, 0.2 sul tasto, soft), `bow_noise` (0.35; rosin and hair noise), `attack` (0.03 s from silence; bow changes inside a phrase are quicker, accents bite), `release` (0.06 s), `ring` 0-1 (0.5; after a note followed by a rest, the chance the bow lifts and the string rings on, against stopping on the string), `glide_ms` (110; `~` portamento, a finger slide: slow start, fast middle), `grace_ms` (45), `grace_at` (`before` default: the grace takes its time from before the beat; `on`: on the beat), `trill_hz` (13 notes a second), `trem_hz` (14 bow strokes a second), `intonation` 0-1 (0.6; expressive intonation: a note a semitone under the next one leans up by up to 12 cents, a note a semitone over it leans down by 6), `cold` 0-1 (0; a drier, clearer, less sweet tone: less and later vibrato, a leaner low body, more 2.5-5 kHz: one reading of the "vioară rece" in docs/JOC_TIGANESC.md), `warm` 0-1 (0.5; the A0/B1 body warmth), `dark` 0-1 (0.3; string losses and top roll-off), `damp` (0.3), `tune_cents` (0), `body` (0; another violin body), `seed`. Level: about -8 dB by default; set it against the guitar and bass by `stems_rms`.
  - **Articulations** (violin only): write them after the note's length, after a `/`, comma-separated: `"8:G4:2/g-1"`, `"60:D5:4/tr+1"`, `"44:A4:4/d=D4,acc"`. `s` slurred to the previous note (same bow, the finger changes: write runs as one stroke plus slurred notes); `~` on the length keeps its meaning (portamento, also in the same bow); no mark = a new bow stroke (the bow direction alternates, `up`/`dn` set it). `g` grace note (acciaccatura) a semitone below (`g-2`, `g+1`, or `g=C#5`); `m` mordent (main, upper neighbour, main; `m+1`, `m-1`, default +2); `tr` trill with the upper neighbour (`tr+1`, `tr+2`, default +2; ends on the main note); `ftr` the "false lăutar trill" (a bowed tremolo with brief touches of the upper note: `ftr+1`); `trem` bowed tremolo; `turn` (upper, main, lower, main); `sl` slide into the note from below (`sl-3`, `sl+2`, default -2); `fall` terminal glissando off the end of the note (`fall-4`, default -3; the Moldavian phrase ending); `sp` spiccato (the bow bounces off, the string rings); `pz` pizzicato; `h` natural harmonic (flageolet; play it at the sounding pitch); `nv` no vibrato, `vib` more vibrato; `d-5`, `d+3`, `d=D4` double stop (a second string at that interval or note, same bow; open-string drones D4/A4/E5/G3 sound best); `acc` a bitten accent (also `!` on the length). Mind the grid: a pattern's `step` and `len` are in 16ths, so at slow tempos 32nds are half steps (`"12.5:C#5:0.5/s"`) and 16th triplets are steps of 4/3.
- `acguitar` (polyphonic): acoustic guitar, nylon strings by default (`"strings": "steel"` for a steel-string guitar), Karplus-Strong strings with a finger or pick excitation, then a modal guitar body over the whole track (air mode ~100 Hz, top-plate modes ~190 and ~380 Hz, dense modes above), dry: no amp, no chorus. Params: `chop` 0-1 (0; damps each chord right after its length, with the thud of the palm: 0.7-0.9 for the afterbeat "țiitură" chops), `scrape` 0-1 (0.3; pick or nail noise on the strings), `bright` 0-1 (0.4 nylon, 0.55 steel), `sustain` (2.0 s nylon, 3.2 steel at 220 Hz), `pluck_pos` (0.16), `mic` 0-1 (0.5; soundhole warmth to 12th-fret clarity), `body`, `seed`. Afterbeat chords: `{"chords": "Gm:16 D7:16", "style": "stabs", "rhythm": "..x...x...x...x.", "stab_len": 1, "voicing": "guitar", "register": "G3", "strum": "down", "strum_ms": 9}` with `chop` 0.8; an `hpf` at 100-120 Hz keeps it off the bass.
- `toba` (drum): the tobă mare, the double-headed bass drum of village bands and fanfares with a cymbal (cinel, talger) mounted on top, struck by one player with a felt mallet and a rod. `stroke`: `boom` (the mallet on the head), `cinel` (the cymbal alone), `both` (the two together: the accented downbeat), `rim` (a dry knock on the hoop). Params: `tune` (60 Hz), `decay` (0.2 s), `cinel_decay` (0.35 s; 0.1-0.2 = held by the hand), `bright` 0-1 (0.5), `seed`. Use one track per stroke, like the darbuka. Round robin: repeated hits differ slightly.
- The rest of a lăutar band is already here: `ebass` (bass guitar: roots and fifths on the beats, `pick` 0.2 for fingers), `kitsnare` (the tobă mică / snare on the afterbeats; ghost notes), `darbuka` (the modern wedding and manele colour), `handclap` and `snap` (the dancers' claps and finger snaps), `cimbalom`, `fiddle` (the old violin), `oud` as cobza.

**Chamber score instruments** (celesta, glockenspiel, vibraphone, cello, viola, a string section; see docs/CHAMBER_SCORE.md for the idiom; code in `engine/chamber.py`; A/B against the old stand-ins and spectrograms against recordings in `examples/chamberscore/`, rebuilt by `examples/chamberscore/build.py`). Each is modelled on measured recordings (partial ratios, levels, decays and spectra measured, then the model rendered at the same pitches and measured the same way). The recordings are only analysed, never used in a track; they live in `~/.cache/spectre/refs/chamber/` (out of git): University of Iowa MIS (theremin.music.uiowa.edu, 2012 sets, "may be downloaded and used for any projects, without restrictions"), Versilian VSCO-2-CE and VCSL (CC0, github.com/sgossner), and a celesta pack by pjcohen on Freesound (pack 23108, CC BY 4.0, HQ previews). Write every note at its **sounding** pitch.
- `celesta` (polyphonic): struck steel plates over wooden resonators. An almost pure fundamental that rings, a soft felt-hammer onset, the plate's own overtones far down and gone within a second, a damper. Params: `hammer` 0-1 (0.5; harder lifts the overtones and quickens the onset), `pedal` (false: the damper stops the note at its length plus `release` 0.15 s; true: every note rings out), `decay` (1.0; scales the ring), `ring` (8 s; the longest a note rings), `release`. Range: a celesta sounds C4-C8 (some C3-C8); it reads best C4-C7. **Measured** (12 notes, A#2-F#6): after 30 ms the fundamental stands 35-55 dB over everything else; its T60 is about 5.4 s x (349 Hz / f)^0.2 (6.2-7.6 s low, 3.6-4.5 s at the top); the plate's first overtone at 2.71-2.76 x f1 and the second at 6.2 (low notes) to 5.36 (F5 and up), each about 40 dB down at 30 ms with T60 0.6-0.9 s; low notes add modes near 7.1-7.7 and 14.5-14.85; 2nd and 3rd harmonics about 50 dB down (the 3rd about 37 dB at F#6); 2-4 kHz about -30 dB and 4-8 kHz about -50 dB under the fundamental in the first 100 ms; the onset rises over about 10 ms (-20 dB at 2 ms, -9 dB at 10 ms), with a dull action knock about 30 dB down in the first 0.2 s; above 10 kHz -65 to -88 dB of the total. **Match:** 1/3-octave spectrum of the first second within 1.9 dB on average down to 40 dB under the peak (8.3 dB down to 60 dB: the recordings carry a -40 to -60 dB floor of room, sympathetic plates and MP3 coding that the model leaves out); envelope 3.9 dB; fundamental T60 within 15% on 9 of 12 notes (median ratio 0.99). The old stand-in `musicbox` misses the envelope by 18.4 dB (its fixed 2.5 s ring).
- `glockenspiel` (polyphonic): steel bars struck by a mallet, no resonators, no damper. The modes ring at the ratios measured per register (the nearest of four anchors: G5, C6, G6, C7), each with its own level and decay; the fundamental rings long. Params: `hardness` 0-1 (0.5 = the Iowa plastic mallet; 1 = brass, much brighter above 5 kHz; 0-0.3 = rubber or yarn, softer), `decay` (1.0), `ring` (7 s; the longest a note rings, faded out, no click), `damped` (false; true stops each note 80 ms after its length, a hand on the bar). Velocity also softens the stroke (a softer stroke is a duller one). Range: a glockenspiel sounds G5-C8 (written two octaves lower); write sounding pitches. **Measured** on the Iowa orchestral bells (ff, plastic and brass mallets, G5-D7), checked against Versilian's glockenspiel: (ratio : level at 30 ms with the plastic mallet, dB re f1 : T60 s) G5 3.20 : -52 : 0.38, 4.20 : -41 : 4.0, 6.65 : -17 : 0.50, 9.39 : -13 : 1.17, 15.0 : -26 : 0.86; C6 3.12 : -18 : 0.78, 6.22 : -18 : 0.18, 8.05 : -18 : 1.06, 9.71 : -25 : 0.38, 14.03 : -19 : 0.91; G6 3.01 : -17 : 0.70, 5.81 : -12 : 0.40, 6.42 : -34 : 1.15; C7 2.53 : -35, 2.92 : -26, 5.42 : -27 : 0.70; fundamental T60 7.9-11.6 s; the brass mallet lifts modes above 5 kHz by 12-20 dB; above 10 kHz -35 to -43 dB of the total in the first second with plastic, -17 to -28 with brass (discrete partials, no noise spray). Versilian's instrument has different ratios (2.78-3.04, 5.3-5.7, 8.9) and a shorter top (T60 2-5 s above C7): glockenspiels differ. **Match** (1/3-octave spectrum of the first second, mean |difference| down to 40 dB / 60 dB under the peak): 1.3 / 4.8 dB against the Iowa plastic mallet at `hardness` 0.5 (envelope 2.7 dB, fundamental T60 within 15% on 5 of 5, above 10 kHz 6 dB darker than the reference), 3.6 / 6.1 dB against brass at 1.0 (its fundamental rings longer, 9.6-18 s: the model's is 24% shorter). The old stand-in (`bell`, decay 1.3, ratio 2.76) sits 19.5 / 28.9 dB from the same recordings, with 24.7 dB too much above 10 kHz: the metallic spray.
- `vibraphone` (polyphonic, notes may overlap): aluminium bars tuned so the first overtone sits two octaves up, over tube resonators, a motor turning discs in the tubes, a damper pedal. Params: `pedal` (false: the damper stops each note at its length plus `release` 0.12 s; true: every note rings out, as with the pedal held), `motor` Hz (0 = off; 1-12 Hz is the instrument's range, Wikipedia "Vibraphone"; about 3-6 for the classic shimmer), `depth` 0-1 (0.5; how far the discs close the resonators: the fundamental breathes, the overtones don't; **hand-tuned**: no motor-on recording was reachable to measure it), `hardness` 0-1 (0.35 = the reference's mallets; lower = softer yarn, higher = brighter overtones), `decay` (1.0), `ring` (10 s). Range F3-F6 (C3-F6 on larger instruments). The motor turns once for the whole track, so chords tremble together. **Measured** on the University of Iowa vibraphone (2012 "sustain" ff: pedal down, motor off; C4, G4, C5, G5): fundamental T60 23 s (C4), 18.8 (G4), 14.7 (C5), 9.3 (G5), about 23 s x (263 Hz / f)^0.8; first overtone at 4.0 x f1, 5-21 dB down at 30 ms, T60 0.7-4 s; the second near 10 x f1 at C4 (Wikipedia: an octave and a major third above the first overtone, 10.08) falling to 7.9 at G5, from -9 dB at C4 to -49 dB at G5, T60 0.2-0.4 s; 2nd and 3rd harmonics 38-47 dB down; the fundamental builds over 5-20 ms in its tube. **Match:** envelope within 1.15 dB on average, fundamental T60 within 3-11%, 1/3-octave spectrum within 0.9-3.7 dB on C4-C5 (G5 9.5 dB: its 7.9 x mode). Level about -14 dB by default.
- `bell` with `"clean": true` (opt-in): the same FM bell, but each note rings on to -60 dB (at most `ring`, 9 s) and fades over its last 50 ms, instead of being cut 22 dB down at 2.5 x `decay` (the documented click). Without `clean`, `bell` plays exactly as before. For a glockenspiel use `glockenspiel`.
- `cello` and `viola` (monophonic lines, like `violin`): the violin's bowed waveguide string with a cello or viola body measured from recordings, and classical defaults: `vibrato` 0.12 semitones at `vib_rate` 5.4 Hz (measured: Versilian solo violin 4.3-7.1 Hz, median 5.2, ±5-17 cents; sections 5.4 Hz, ±10 cents), `vib_delay` 0.18, `intonation` 0.4, `bow_noise` 0.3, `ring` 0.6. Every `violin` param and articulation works (`/s` slurs, `~` slides, `/acc`, `/trem`, `/sp`, `/h`, `/d=...`, ...), plus the new **`/cresc`, `/dim`, `/swell`** (bow speed and force grow across the note, fade, or swell and fade: messa di voce; also on `violin`). **Pizzicato** (`/pz`, or `"pizz": true` for the whole track) is a plucked string with the measured decay (cello T60 2.2 s x (150 Hz / f)^0.5: C2 about 3.3 s, A3 1.8 s; viola 1.1 s x (300 Hz / f)^0.7) and its measured first-tenths drop (about 10 dB in 100 ms, 20 dB in 300 ms). Ranges: cello C2-A5 (reads best C2-E5), viola C3-E6 (C3-A5). **Body, measured:** the University of Iowa recordings (2012, arco ff, played without vibrato: 16 cello notes C2-D5, 14 viola notes C3-C6); each recorded note's harmonic levels divided by those of the bare waveguide at the same pitch, pooled and smoothed over 1/6 octave, then folded back in two closed-loop passes. Cello: about +4 dB at 100-200 Hz, +10-11 dB at 200-280 Hz, a +4 dB bridge hill near 2.5 kHz, -7 to -10 dB at 3.5-6 kHz; viola: a hole below 160 Hz, +10 dB at 350-450 Hz and 1.3-1.6 kHz, a +10 dB hill near 4.5 kHz. Dense modes under that curve make vibrato shimmer through the body as on `violin`. **Match:** the rendered notes sit 0.87 dB (cello) and 0.74 dB (viola) from the recordings on average, 1/6-octave, 100 Hz-8 kHz; pizzicato at the recordings' level against the arco (median -0.1 dB), fundamental T60 within 25% on 7 of 10 notes (median ratio 1.02), its first 100 ms about 4 dB brighter at 2-8 kHz than the recordings (a finger pluck rolled off from 3 x f). Level: about -12 (cello) and -10 dB (viola) by default, set to sit like `violin`.
- `strings_section` (polyphonic, **stereo**): a real section, so strings stop reading as a synth pad. Every player is its own bowed string with its own body, intonation, vibrato rate and depth, bow position and timing, spread across the stage; chords are played divisi (player j takes the (j mod k)-th note of a k-note chord). Params: `section` `violins` (default, 8 players, violin body), `violas` (6, viola body), `celli` (5, cello body), `basses` (4, double-bass body; since 2026-11-26: write the sounding pitch, E1 = 41 Hz; see "Orchestra and trailer"); `players`; `spread_ms` (18; each player's onset scatter, sd); `detune_cents` (6; each player's intonation, sd, plus 3 cents per note); `width` 0-1 (0.7; how wide the players sit); `bow_env` `swell` / `cresc` / `dim` (applied to every bowed note without its own: the long swelling bow of the cinematic lift); `pizz` (true = the whole track pizzicato; or `/pz` per note); `attack` (default from velocity: about 0.05 s at forte, 0.3-0.65 s at piano, as measured; set it to force one); `air` 0-1 (0.5; bow hair and rosin above 7 kHz: the recorded sections carry 15-20 dB more above 10 kHz than bare strings); `seed`; plus every violin param (`vibrato`, `vib_rate`, `pressure`, `bow_pos`, `dark`, ...). Notes and chords both work (`{"chords": "F:12 Am:12", "style": "sustain", "voicing": "close"}`); articulations go on notes. **Measured** on Versilian's sections (VSCO-2-CE `susvib`, 16 notes of violins, violas and celli): harmonic width (the -10 dB width of the partials, vibrato and players' scatter together) median 34 cents, the model 36; level flutter over the sustain 2.35 dB, the model 1.6; attack to -3 dB 32-76 ms at forte, 300-700 ms at piano (the model: about 80-100 ms at velocity 0.8-1, about 250 ms at 0.3); spectrum above 10 kHz within 1-3 dB after `air`; 1/3-octave spectrum 7.8 dB from the recordings on average, mostly the hall and noise between the harmonics. Cost: one waveguide string per player (8 players render about as fast as one `violin` line per second of audio times 8; a 3-minute section track takes a few seconds more). Level: about -17 dB by default (sections trimmed so violins, violas and celli sit alike at the same velocity). Tip: three tracks (violins on top with `voicing` `close`, violas in the middle, celli on the roots) make a full string group; give them a long `send_reverb` (0.3-0.4: strings at a distance, docs/CHAMBER_SCORE.md "Mixing").

**Voices** (`singer`, `vox2`; code in `engine/singer.py` and `engine/svs.py`; voice auditions and studies in `examples/vocals/svs/`, wordless `vox`/`vox2` demos in `examples/vocals/`). A voice is your choice per track, never required; the genre docs say when and how each genre uses one (docs/TRANCE.md "Vocals"). **Mixing and mastering a voice:** [docs/voice/MIXING.md](voice/MIXING.md) holds the researched principles (words and masking, level against the band, reverb and pre-delay, compression and sibilance, doubles, chops vs lines vs choirs, what a synthesized voice needs, choirs and ensembles, what the master does to a voice, the measured reverb decay per `size`, and how to check a voice with Whisper and the vocal-versus-band loudness); each genre doc has a "Mixing the voice" section with a starting chain in engine terms (docs/CAROLING.md, TRANCE.md, LATIN_POP.md, POP_ROCK.md, HIPHOP.md, JOC_TIGANESC.md). Read the genre's section before setting sends and EQ on a voice. For words, use `singer` (below; approved by the owner by ear, 2026-11-24: "a clear improvement"). It is the only place Spectre uses neural models, approved by the owner, and only for sung words: every other sound is synthesized, with no recordings.
- **`singer`: words sung by a singing voice synthesizer.** `"source": "svs"` is its only source and the default: a `singer` without `source` sings with svs, and any other `source` stops the render with an error. A DiffSinger voicebank (a neural model trained on a real singer's recordings; OpenVPI ONNX, the format OpenUtau uses; code `engine/svs.py`) sings the line in one piece: its duration model sets every phoneme's length, its pitch model draws the melody's scoops, transitions and vibrato around your notes, its acoustic model and a vocoder make the sound. Nothing is stitched, so joins and cadence sound sung. Lyrics: one syllable per note, hyphens split words, `_` a melisma ("Writing lyrics" below); the vowel lands on the note and the onset consonants come just before it (DiffSinger's convention). A rest of more than about 12% of a note's slot starts a new phrase; shorter gaps are sung legato.
  - **`voice`: pick by id from the voice catalogue, [docs/voice/VOICES.md](voice/VOICES.md)** (tags for timbre, emotions, genres, what each is bad at, range, auditions in `examples/vocals/svs/voices/`; machine-readable in `engine/voices.json`): `hanami` (default; warm, clear, natural female), `hanami-power` (bright, firm), `hanami-soft` (soft, airy, dark), `hanami-dark` (low formants: solemn, rounded; less clear), `leona` / `leona-power` / `leona-soft` (brighter, younger character voices; beta); **Spanish:** `hanami-es` (Root) and `hanami-es-soft` (Nectar) sing Latin American Spanish (96% of words right by Whisper; VOICES.md "Spanish"). All of those are female; range: write A3-D5 (the render warns outside the voice's range). **Male voices: `hanami-male` and `hanami-male-es`** (English and Spanish; write B2-E4; approved by the owner by ear, 2026-11-25; VOICES.md "Male voices"). Hanami sings an octave above your notes and the synthesizer moves the line down, with `gender` -0.2: no other model, real time, 44.1 kHz. Her timing, diction and controls are kept (`seed`, `double`, `velocity`, `inhale`, `svs_vibrato`, phrase fades; Spanish lyrics as for `hanami-es`). A dark, soft, covered low voice, not a trained singer's ring: give it the 8 kHz shelf of the standing chain; `gender` -0.3 is brighter and larger, below -0.4 the words fail. About one take in four blurs a word for Whisper: check an exposed line and change `seed` if needed. Credit and terms are Hanami's (her line in voices.json): **no political subject.**
  - **`lang`** (`"en"` default, `"es"`): the language the lyrics are read in; the Spanish voice ids set `"es"`, and `"lang": "es"` works with any Hanami voice (`hanami-soft` with `"lang": "es"` equals `hanami-es-soft`; LEONA has no Spanish). Spanish lyrics: plain Spanish spelling with accents (`co-ra-zón`, `a-quí`, `ma-ña-na`), hyphenated by Spanish syllables (one consonant between vowels starts the next syllable, stop or f plus l/r start one together: `pa-la-bra`, `es-cri-bo`; a diphthong is one syllable: `quie-ro`, `cuan-do`); one syllable per note; to merge vowels across words (sinalefa, "te escribo" on three notes) spell the merged syllable as one word (`tes-cri-bo`). The engine reads words with `engine/lang_es.py` (seseo, yeísmo, b/d/g softened after vowels also across words in a phrase) and the bank's Spanish dictionary; a syllable-count mismatch warns. Put stressed syllables on strong beats or longer notes: the voice has no stress marks, the notes make the stress. Rules and sources: [docs/voice/languages/es.md](voice/languages/es.md). **Romanian:** `hanami-ro` (or `"lang": "ro"` on a Hanami voice) sings Romanian through a phoneme mapping (`engine/lang_ro.py`: Hanami has no Romanian data, so ă and î are approximations and the accent is foreign), approved by the owner's ear as "good enough" (2026-11-26; audition `examples/vocals/svs/romanian-hanami-experiment.mp3`). Write the lyrics in Romanian spelling with diacritics, hyphenated by syllable, one syllable per note (`a-cum e pri-mă-va-ră`, `noap-tea vi-ne în-cet`); a diphthong is one syllable (`noap-`, `mai`); a syllable-count mismatch warns. Details: docs/voice/languages/ro.md. **Never in a track with a political subject** (Lotte V's terms: no anthem, no protest; use `vox2` or no voice there).
  - Params: `gender` -1..1 (the voice's own; negative = formants down, darker; positive = brighter; past about ±0.5 diction suffers), `velocity` (1; consonant speed), `seed` (each phrase's pitch curve and timbre noise; another seed = another take), `double`, `double_ms`, `double_cents`, `double_pan` (a second take: its own pitch curve, phrases moved a few ms, a few cents off, panned), `inhale` (0; > 0 = the voice takes a breath (its own sung breath) before a phrase that follows a rest of `breath_after_s`, 0.4 s), `svs_fade_in_ms` (5) / `svs_fade_out_ms` (5) (phrase edges), `svs_steps` (20), `svs_vibrato` (1; scales the model's vibrato and other fast pitch movement inside held notes, keeping the transitions between notes: 0.3-0.5 for straight-tone choir textures, pads and tight doubles, the way ensembles narrow vibrato for blend; 0 keeps only the slow movement; above 1 more vibrato; measured in docs/voice/GENERAL.md section 5, A/B `examples/vocals/svs/choir-vibrato-1.mp3` vs `-0.3.mp3`). There are no tuning or timing params beyond these: the model sings pitch and timing itself, with the vowel on the note and the onset consonants just before it (the right placement for a voice heard against a band: docs/voice/GENERAL.md "Timing").
  - **Licence (must follow):** credit every voice in the session README's **Credits** line and the site notes, with the `credit` text from `engine/voices.json` (e.g. "Vocals: Hoshino Hanami (DiffSinger voicebank by Lotte V, lottev.moe), Potluck Vocoder by tigermeat"). **Never use these voices in a track with a political subject** (Lotte V's terms forbid political use). Model files are not in the repo: banks go in `~/.cache/spectre/svs/<bank>/bank/` and the Potluck vocoder in `~/.cache/spectre/svs/potluck/dsvocoder/` (download links in VOICES.md; `SPECTRE_SVS_DIR` moves them); rendered phrases are cached in `~/.cache/spectre/svs/phrases/` (safe to delete: they regenerate bit for bit). Needs `pip install onnx cmudict pyyaml` besides onnxruntime. Cost: about real time on 2 CPU threads per new phrase (a 6 s line 5-6 s; first use of a bank also loads and patches its models once), cached phrases are free.
  - **Mix: put it in the band, not on top of it** (measured on the c1 chorus, `examples/vocals/svs/README.md` "Blend"): through a chain with hpf 150 and +3 dB at 500 Hz the svs voice sounded like "someone singing along to a speaker": 4 dB short of air at 8 kHz against the band, more mono than the band (L/R coherence 0.77 vs 0.52) and with its reverb 11 dB under the dry voice (8.4 dB in the version that worked best). (It is also 7-8 dB heavier at 500 Hz-1 kHz than the guitar-and-drum band, but that is the voice itself: EQ moved it only about 1 dB.) What worked best (Whisper word accuracy on the full mastered mix 62%, vs 42% before): hpf 100 Hz, a small 800 Hz dip, high shelf +3 dB at 8 kHz, more reverb send behind a 60 ms pre-delay (`reverb_predelay_ms`, a track key: the words stay clear and the tail joins the band's reverb), a quieter, wider double, soft phrase edges and a breath, and 2.5 dB less 2.5 kHz in the guitars. Early reflections (`room`) on the voice made it blend but smeared the words (Whisper fell to almost nothing). Example (session 009's chorus, pop rock guitars; vocal about 1 LU under the instrumental): `{"inst": {"type": "singer", "source": "svs", "voice": "hanami", "double": 0.2, "double_pan": 0.7, "inhale": 0.3, "svs_fade_in_ms": 30, "svs_fade_out_ms": 120}, "send_reverb": 0.32, "reverb_predelay_ms": 60, "send_delay": 0.12, "fx": [{"type": "hpf", "hz": 100}, {"type": "eq", "hz": 800, "gain_db": -2.5, "q": 0.9}, {"type": "eq", "hz": 3000, "gain_db": -3, "q": 0.9}, {"type": "highshelf", "hz": 8000, "gain_db": 3}, {"type": "comp", "threshold_db": -22, "ratio": 3}, {"type": "gain", "db": 13}], "gain_db": 12}` plus `{"type": "eq", "hz": 2500, "gain_db": -2.5, "q": 0.7}` on the guitars. **Measure the vocal in the section:** the vocal as heard in the mix is the premaster minus a render with the vocal track muted (`"mute": true`; a `--solo` render leaves out its reverb and delay returns, so it reads several dB low). A `comp` in its chain eats `gain_db`, so set the level with a `gain` fx at the end of the chain. **Level:** lead vocals in records sit about +3 dB (pop, rap) to 0 dB (rock) over the accompaniment (Gerdes & Siedenburg 2023); a voice used as an instrument sits at or under that, a pad or texture lower still (docs/voice/GENERAL.md section 4, with the sources for the pre-delay and the 2.5-3 kHz carve).
- **Writing lyrics for `singer`:** put the lyrics next to the notes in the pattern, one syllable per note:
  `"hook": {"bars": 8, "sung": {"notes": "0:C5:6 6:E5:10 16:D5:4 20:C5:4 24:A4:8", "lyrics": "first light break-ing through"}}`.
  Words are separated by spaces, a hyphen splits a word into syllables (`a-round`, `ev-ery-thing`), `_` holds the previous syllable over the next note (a melisma), punctuation is ignored. The render warns if the syllable count doesn't match the note count. Lyrics can also go in the instrument as `"lyrics"` (applied to all the track's notes in order) when one pattern holds the whole line. Keep it singable: open vowels on long notes ("are", "go", "ride", "high"), short words with simple consonants on 8ths, each word's stressed syllable on a strong beat or a longer note (the voice has no stress marks: the notes make the stress; docs/voice/languages/). A word the dictionary doesn't know is sung as "ah" with a warning: respell it. A part without lyrics (no `lyrics` anywhere) sings "oo" on every phrase: each note after a rest starts a new one, so the voice stops at the rest and at the end of a section that ends in one (since 2026-11-26; it used to hold one "oo" through every rest and ring on past the section end). For a wordless line prefer `vox2`.
  - **Register: write sung words at A3-D5** (F#4-B4 is the sweet spot). Higher up the harmonics are too far apart to show the first formant of most vowels, so vowels converge and words blur (a limit of real sopranos too: Joliveau, Smith & Wolfe 2004; docs/voice/GENERAL.md "High notes"); the render warns outside the voice's range. A hook written for a lead synth or `vox` at F#5-B5 should be sung an octave lower.
  - **Syllable rate:** aim for about 1-3 syllables a second: quarter notes or longer on the words that matter (at 136 bpm a quarter is 0.44 s), 8ths (0.22 s) only for light syllables with simple consonants ("and", "the", "you"), never 16ths (the model is left too little vowel).
- `vox2` (monophonic with glide): the improved **wordless** voice for lines and chops, pure synthesis (no model). Over `vox` it has an LF-model glottal source (Fant's `rd` voice quality: 0.3 pressed, 0.7 default, 1 modal, 2.7 breathy), small jitter and shimmer, breath pulsed with each glottal cycle, a vowel per note (`vowels`: `"oo ah ee"` cycles through the notes; formants move over `transition_ms` (80) on `~` glides), and soft consonant onsets (`onset`: a sequence like `"h . n . l d"`, one per note in turn; `h` breath, `m`/`n` hum, `l`/`w`/`y` glides into the vowel, `d` a tiny tap; `.` none) for "da", "na", "la" chops. Other params as `vox`: voice `soprano`/`alto`/`tenor`, vibrato, vib_rate, vib_delay, scoop, glide_ms, breath (0.3), ring (0.5), attack, release, sustain_level, jitter (0.006), shimmer (0.06), seed.
- Usage, trance: for a sung vocal, `singer` in the breakdown and hook (docs/TRANCE.md "Vocals in a radio edit" for the voices that suit it), A4-D5 (not higher: see Register), long reverb and dotted-8th delay sends, a light kick sidechain (depth 0.2-0.3) in the drop. For chops, `vox2` with `onset` consonants on short notes, or `singer` on one repeated word. Keep DJ intros and outros free of voice.
- Usage, pop rock: `singer` (`hanami`) for the lead (the melody a `vox` line carried) with the mix chain above (put it in the band), the verse in its lower range (A3-D5) where words are clearest, the guitars cut around 2.5-3 kHz rather than the voice pushed, and a `double` on the final chorus. A harmony is a second `singer` track with the same lyrics (another `seed`, or another voice a third away).

FX types (in order): `hpf` {hz}, `lpf` {hz}, `eq` {hz, gain_db, q}, `lowshelf`/`highshelf` {hz, gain_db}, `comp` {threshold_db, ratio, attack_ms, release_ms}, `drive` {db}, `sat` {amount}, `bitcrush` {bits}, `srr` {hz}, `lofi` {amount 0-1: wow/flutter, sample-rate, dust}, `chorus` {rate, depth, mix}, `phaser`, `tremolo` {rate, depth}, `delay` {beats, feedback, mix}, `reverb` {size, mix}, `pitch` {semitones}, `width` {amount}, `gain` {db}, `gate` {pattern, bars, depth 0-1, duty 0-1, smooth_ms} (trance gate: a step pattern from bar 1, string length / bars = grid like drum steps; `x`/`X` open, `o` half, `1`-`9` level, `.` closed; each open step stays open for `duty` of the step, e.g. `{"type": "gate", "pattern": "x.xx.xx.x.xx.x.x", "depth": 0.85, "duty": 0.75}`), `room` {size 0-1, mix, damping} (small-room early reflections plus a short tail: put it on each acoustic kit piece so the kit sounds like one room). Production fx (since 2026-11-25, below): `ott`, `dyneq`, `clip`, `mseq`, and `"slope"` on `hpf`/`lpf`.

### Production: stacks, buses, returns, the modern synth voice (since 2026-11-25)
The engine side of docs/PRODUCTION.md ("Layering", "EQ carving", "Gaps and proposals" 3, 4, 5, 7): what a listener hears as one sound is several sources playing the same notes, each supplying what the others lack (sub, body, mid bite, air, transient, width), each carved by the genre's EQ map, glued on a bus. Everything here is opt-in: a project without these keys renders exactly as before. Code: `engine/mixbus.py` (stacks, buses, returns, fx, humanizing), `engine/synth.py` (instruments). Read your genre doc's "Layering and EQ carving" first: it has the roles, the map and a "How to build it in Spectre" recipe.

**`"genre"`** (project key): the genre whose EQ map (`engine/eqmaps.json`) gives the role presets below; `listen --clarity` uses it when you give no `--genre`. Set it in every new project.

**Stacks (`"layers"`).** A track with `"layers"` plays its pattern through several instruments at once; the hand plays the notes once (humanizing, swing, legato, velocities are the track's), every layer gets the same events:
```json
"lead": {"role": "lead", "sidechain": {"source": "kick", "depth": 0.3, "release_ms": 150}, "send_reverb": 0.3,
  "fx": [{"type": "ott", "depth": 0.3}, {"type": "comp", "threshold_db": -18, "ratio": 2}],
  "layers": [
    {"name": "main", "inst": {"type": "supersaw", "model": 2, "detune": 22, "mix": 0.65, "drift": 0.35}, "role": "lead"},
    {"name": "support", "inst": {"type": "supersaw", "model": 2, "detune": 30, "attack": 0.03, "release": 0.55},
     "octave": 1, "role": "support", "gain_db": -7, "width": 0.5},
    {"name": "air", "inst": {"type": "air", "octave": 1}, "role": "air", "gain_db": 2}]}
```
- LAYER keys: `name`, `inst` (any instrument), `role` (as a track's `role`; it picks the layer's EQ preset), `octave` and `semi` (added to the instrument's `transpose`), `gain_db` (relative to the instrument's default level, as on a track), `pan`, `width`, `fx` (after the preset), `eq` (`"auto"`, the default when the layer has a role; `"none"`; or overrides: `{"hp": 90, "lp": null, "cut_db": -2, "own_db": 0, "slope": 24, "genre": "...", "role": "..."}`), `notes` (`"all"`; `"lowest"` or `"highest"`: one note per chord, e.g. a sub under chords), `offset_ms` (this layer later or earlier: a support layer that blooms in), `vel` (a factor on the velocities), `note` and `len` (a tonal layer under drum steps, e.g. a tuned sine under a kick: the note held `len` 16ths), `mute`.
- The pattern is read as the first layer's instrument (drum steps if it is a drum, notes or chords otherwise), unless the track has its own `inst`.
- The track's other keys act on the **stack's bus** (the sum of its layers): `fx`, `gain_db` (relative to 0 dB: the layers carry the per-instrument defaults; it acts before the bus fx, so for a level change after a compressor or `clip` put a `gain` fx last), `pan` (balance), `width`, `eq`, `sidechain`, `choke_by`, sends, section `auto`/`exit` by the track's name. Glue on the bus, as the sources do it: a 2:1 compressor taking 2-4 dB, a little `sat`, OTT at partial depth, one shared send (docs/TRANCE.md "Glue").
- With `"seeds": "per-track"` each layer has its own random stream (`<track>.<layer>`): adding a layer changes nothing else. `render --solo lead` plays the whole stack.
- `listen --clarity` treats a stack as one stem (it is one perceived sound) and prints a **stack view** under the table: each layer's share of the stack per region (sub/bass/lowmid/mid/presence/air), its level against the stack's loudest layer, and the EQ map check of its own role (when no other layer of the stack shares that role). A layer that supplies nowhere more than a few percent adds nothing: take it out or give it a job. Layer roles count for the "role empty in a peak section" check (an `air` layer fills the air role).

**Role EQ presets (`"eq": "auto"`)**, on a layer or on any track with a `role`: the genre map's row as a starting point [H, amounts]: a high-pass at the geometric centre of the map's high-pass range (12 dB/oct), a low-pass where the map has one, a cut of -3 dB at the centre of each region the map cuts (Q from its width) and +1.5 dB at the centre of each region the role owns (the reciprocal EQ of Houghton, Sound On Sound 2022: a small cut on the secondary, a small boost on the primary). In the acoustic genres (joc, caroling, Latin pop, chamber score: map tolerance 9 dB) the cut is -1.5 dB and nothing is boosted: orchestration and balance come first. See a preset with `python3 engine/mixbus.py trance lead` (trance lead: hpf 155 Hz, -3 dB at 316 Hz, +1.5 dB at 2 kHz). It is where carving starts; confirm on the sound with `--clarity` and solos.

**Group buses (`"buses"`, a track's `"bus"`).** `"buses": {"drums": {"fx": [{"type": "clip", "ceiling_db": -2}], "gain_db": 0, "send_reverb": 0.1}, "music": {"fx": [{"type": "comp", "threshold_db": -20, "ratio": 2}], "sidechain": {"source": "kick", "depth": 0.4, "release_ms": 160}}}` and `"bus": "drums"` on the kick, clap and hats: those tracks are summed on the bus (after their own fx, sidechain, automation and sends), then the bus runs `gain_db`, `pan`, `width`, `fx`, `sidechain`, section `auto`/`exit` (by the bus's name), `send_reverb`/`send_delay`/`sends`, and goes to the mix or, with its own `"bus"`, into another bus. Typical: a drum bus with `clip` (trance: clip the kick or drum bus in the mix so the master does not have to, docs/TRANCE.md "Mastering"), a music bus ducked by the kick, a guitar bus with one compressor and a shared room (pop rock), the strings on one bus with a broad 1-2 dB cut at 200-500 Hz (chamber score, docs/CHAMBER_SCORE.md).

**Returns: more than one reverb, filtered and ducked.** `"mix": {"returns": {...}}` adds named returns; tracks and buses feed them with `"sends": {"name": amount}` (a track's `reverb_predelay_ms` applies to its named sends too):
```json
"returns": {"room": {"type": "reverb", "size": 0.3, "damping": 0.6, "predelay_ms": 8, "return_db": -10},
            "hall": {"type": "reverb", "size": 0.9, "predelay_ms": 30, "hpf": 350, "lpf": 8000, "return_db": -6,
                     "fx": [{"type": "eq", "hz": 450, "gain_db": -3, "q": 1}],
                     "duck": {"key": "lead", "depth_db": 6, "release_ms": 300}},
            "echo": {"type": "delay", "beats": 0.75, "pingpong": true, "offset_ms": 9, "feedback": 0.35, "return_db": -10}}
```
RETURN keys: `type` (`reverb`: size, damping, width; `delay`: beats, feedback, `pingpong` true = repeats alternate left and right, `offset_ms` = off the grid on purpose, "when a track is too tight, it can sound a little sterile", Corsten; `shimmer` and `granular`: see "Modern strands", their own `hpf`/`lpf` defaults 250 Hz / 12 kHz and none), `predelay_ms`, `hpf` and `lpf` (12 dB/oct; defaults 250 Hz / 7 kHz for reverbs, 300 Hz / 3.5 kHz for delays; the trance map: high-pass 200-500 Hz, cut 300-600 Hz, low-pass 6-12 kHz), `fx` (any chain), `duck` (`key`: a track or bus; the return dips by up to `depth_db` while the key is loud, attack_ms 10, release_ms 250: the return sits higher without masking its source, Sound On Sound 2022), `return_db`. The same keys (`fx`, `duck`, `predelay_ms`, `hpf`, `width`, `pingpong`, `offset_ms`) on `mix.reverb` / `mix.delay` switch the shared returns to this processing; without them they stay exactly as before. Two spaces are the usual minimum: a short room for drums and plucks, a long hall for leads and pads (docs/PRODUCTION.md "Return processing"). `listen --clarity` lists each named return as a stem `(name)`.

**Production fx:**
- `ott` {depth 0-1 (0.5; dry/wet, "about 50%" is MusicRadar's starting point, 30% on drums), time (1; scales attack and release), upward 0-1, downward 0-1, rb (4; the upward ratio), max_up_db (24), low/mid/high (dB per band), makeup ("auto" or dB)}: three-band upward and downward compression after Ableton's "OTT" preset (crossovers 88.3 Hz and 2.5 kHz, ratios 66:1 above and 4:1 upward below the thresholds, input +5.2 dB, outputs +10.3/+5.7/+10.3 dB; EDMProd and MusicRadar; attack/release per band [H]). It brings up quiet detail and tails and flattens peaks: density, "upfront, bright". The input is brought to a fixed level first and the output matched to the input's level, so it changes density and tone, not loudness, and does not change with `gain_db`. Measured on a plucked `supersaw` line: at depth 1.0 the 4-10 kHz band rises 9.2 dB against 1 kHz and the spread of its 50 ms levels (10th to 90th percentile) shrinks from 11.3 to 9.4 dB; at 0.5, +4.0 dB and 10.8 dB; at 0.3, +1.5 dB and no change in spread (its onsets get sharper: the downward stage is slower than the attack). Use it on a stack's bus or a pad, at partial depth.
- `dyneq` {hz, q (1.4), depth_db (-6), key (a track), attack_ms (2), hold_ms (30), release_ms (120)}: a cut at `hz` that opens only while the key plays (by its hits, scaled by their velocity): the 808 or bass under the kick, notched at the kick's fundamental only when the kick hits ("less collateral damage" than a static cut, Houghton; iZotope's 808 notch). Example on an 808: `{"type": "dyneq", "hz": 55, "q": 1.5, "depth_db": -8, "key": "kick", "release_ms": 90}`.
- `clip` {drive_db (0), ceiling_db (-1 dBFS), knee 0-1 (0.4), out_db (0)}: a soft clipper, 4x oversampled. On a kick or drum bus, 1-3 dB of clipping in the mix so the master's limiter has less to do ("spread clipping over stages of at most 1-2 dB each", docs/TRANCE.md "Mastering").
- `mseq` {mid: [fx], side: [fx], side_hpf (Hz), mid_db, side_db}: mid/side processing. `{"type": "mseq", "side_hpf": 200}` keeps a wide layer's lows mono; `{"type": "mseq", "mid": [{"type": "eq", "hz": 2500, "gain_db": -2, "q": 1}]}` clears the centre of a supporting layer for the lead (Houghton; NI).
- `hpf` / `lpf` with `"slope"` 12, 24, ... dB/oct (Butterworth); without `slope` they stay the gentle first-order filters of every earlier project.
- `shimmer`, `granular`, `suppress`: the shimmer reverb, the grain cloud / freeze and the resonance suppressor, as fx (and the first two as return types); see "Modern strands" below.
- `multiband`, `fold`, `asym`, `hardclip`, `comb`, `flanger`, `resonator`, `vocoder`, `autofilter`, `autopan`, `vowel`: the bass-music fx; and a return's `gate` (gated reverb); see "Bass music" below.

**The modern synth voice:**
- `supersaw` with **`"model": 2`** (model 1, the default, is unchanged): Szabo's (2010) measured JP-8000 behaviour on a general synth core. The **pitch-tracking high-pass** at each note's fundamental (instead of a fixed `hpf` at 200-900 Hz, which thins low notes: on A2 the fundamental keeps -2.3 dB of the total against -4.6 dB through the old practice of a 300 Hz first-order high-pass; `hpf_track` false turns it off); the **mix curve** (`mix` lowers the centre saw as it raises the six side saws: centre 1.0/0.72/0.44 and sides 0.04/0.50/0.59 at mix 0/0.5/1); `knob` 0-1 instead of `detune`: Szabo's 11th-order detune curve (outer voice 0.6, 9, 19, 46, 181 cents at 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1: a slow rise and a steep end; Shore's listeners heard a linear detune as rougher); `detune` in cents works as in model 1. Its level matches model 1 within 0.3 dB. Plus everything `polysynth` has: `osc2` (a second oscillator: `{"shape": "supersaw", "octave": -1, "level": 0.5}` or `{"shape": "square", "level": 0.3}`; supersaw by default, inheriting voices, detune, mix), `sub` 0-1 (a sine an octave down, `sub_octave`, `sub_shape`), `noise` 0-1 (band-passed noise, `noise_hp` 6000, `noise_lp` 16000: an air layer inside the patch), `drift`, `lfo`, `env2`, `vibrato`, `keytrack`, `filter_spread`. Free-running: every note starts with new phases.
- `polysynth` (polyphonic, **stereo**): `osc` a list of oscillators, each `{"shape": "saw" | "square" | "pulse" | "triangle" | "sine" | "noise" | "supersaw", "level", "octave", "semi", "cents", "voices" (unison), "detune" (cents of the outer voice), "spread" 0-1 (stereo width of the unison), "mix" (side voices), "pw" (pulse width), "knob"}`; `sub`, `noise` as above; filter: `filter` (`lp` default, `hp`, `bp`, `none`), `cutoff`, `reso`, `env` (Hz), `fdecay`, `keytrack` 0-1 (the cutoff follows the note from middle C), `filter_spread` (octaves between the left and right filters); amp: `attack`, `decay`, `sustain_level`, `release`; `hpf_track` (Szabo's high-pass, off by default); `out` (0.55). **Modulation:** `lfo` a list of `{"dest": "cutoff" | "pitch" | "level" | "pan" | "pw" | "detune", "rate" Hz, "depth" (cutoff: octaves, pitch: cents, level: dB, pan: -1..1, pw: fraction, detune: fraction of the detune), "shape": "sine" | "triangle" | "saw" | "square" | "random" | "smooth", "delay" s, "fade" s, "phase": "free" (random per note, default) or "retrig"}`; `env2` `{"dest", "depth", "attack", "decay", "sustain", "release", "vel": true}` (a second envelope, e.g. on pitch for a pluck's blip or on cutoff); `drift` 0-1 (analogue drift: each unison voice wanders by a few cents, slowly, and the cutoff by about a tenth of an octave: "no part of the track can sound the same", van Buuren); `vibrato` (cents), `vib_rate` (5.5 Hz), `vib_delay` (0.25 s: delayed vibrato, fades in over 0.3 s). Default level -12 dB. Pluck: `{"type": "polysynth", "osc": [{"shape": "saw", "voices": 5, "detune": 15}], "cutoff": 600, "env": 6000, "fdecay": 0.08, "sustain_level": 0, "decay": 0.2}`; pad body: `{"osc": [{"shape": "saw", "voices": 5, "detune": 14}, {"shape": "triangle", "octave": -1, "level": 0.4}], "attack": 1.0, "release": 2.0, "cutoff": 2400, "lfo": [{"dest": "cutoff", "rate": 0.15, "depth": 0.4}], "drift": 0.5}`; mono centre for a lead: `{"osc": [{"shape": "square"}], "cutoff": 2200}`.
- `sub` (monophonic, mono): a clean sine (`shape` `triangle` for a little more on small speakers) line, phase-continuous, `~` glides over `glide_ms` (60), `attack` (0.005), `release` (0.06), `decay` (0 = held; > 0 an 808-like fall), `harmonics` 0-1 (adds the 2nd and 3rd harmonic so the line reads on phones, iZotope), `drive`. Default level -4 dB. The only thing below about 120 Hz besides the kick (Cold Rush; Attack Magazine: a sine sub peaking near 60 Hz, rolled off above 190 Hz).

**Low end and top end:**
- `kicksub`, `kickpunch`, `kickclick` (drums): a modern kick in three layers, stacked in one track (recipe below). `kicksub`: a tuned sine, `note` (e.g. `"Ab1"`, tune it to the key) or `tune` Hz (50), a short pitch drop of `punch` semitones (12) over `drop` s (0.012), `hold` (0.04), `tail` (0.12), `length` (0.45), `drive`; it owns 45-80 Hz. `kickpunch`: the 100-200 Hz knock, `tune` (130 Hz), swept from `sweep` x tune (3), `decay` (0.045), `thump` (0.3), `drive`. `kickclick`: the 2-5 kHz transient, a fast downward chirp at `tone` (3200 Hz) plus a noise band (`noise` 0.5), `decay` (0.012). **Measured** against eight TR-808 and TR-909 kick samples (oramics/sampled; measured only, not stored), 1/3-octave power over the first 300 ms relative to 45-80 Hz: the stack (A1, defaults) has 100-200 Hz -17.3 dB (909s -9.5 to -21.8), 200-500 Hz -38.3 dB (909s -22.5 to -35.8: leaner, as the trance map's cut asks), 2-5 kHz -53 dB (909s -26 to -55); `clubkick` as session 007 used it (with its carving for the old curve): 200-500 Hz only -13.8 dB under its body, the boxiness the map cuts.
- `metalhat` (drum): hats and cymbals made of tones: the TR-808's six square oscillators (205.3, 304.4, 369.6, 522.7, 540, 800 Hz x `tune`) band-passed at `tone` (8500 Hz), plus `partials` (0.5) high modal partials (6-15 kHz, each with its own decay: shimmer; fixed per `seed`) and `noise` (0.12) as seasoning; `decay` (0.05 closed; `open` true = 0.4), `bright`, `lp` (15000); each hit draws new phases (round robin). **Measured** (spectral flatness 4-16 kHz, 1 = white noise; centroid): `metalhat` closed 0.31 / 8.3 kHz, open 0.32 / 8.4 kHz; TR-808 open hats 0.29-0.31 / 8.0 kHz; TR-808 closed 0.47-0.51 / 10.8 kHz; TR-909 closed 0.33-0.53 / 4.9-6.6 kHz; the noise `hat` 0.57 / 11.9 kHz and session 007's open hat 0.62 / 10.3 kHz (noisier and brighter than every reference). It meets a brighter target with tones instead of hiss: what the phone check reads as static is noise-like top (docs/MASTERING.md "Top end"). Default level -13 dB; give it the hats role preset (`"eq": "auto"`: high-pass about 1 kHz in trance).
- `air` (polyphonic, **stereo**): an air layer for a lead or pad: `voices` (5) saws `octave` up (1), detuned by `detune` (18 cents) and spread (`spread` 1.0), plus pitched noise (noise gated at the note's period, `noise` 0.5), all band-passed `hp` 7000 to `lp` 16000 Hz (12 dB/oct); `attack`, `release`. Default level -20 dB; as a layer, about 8-12 dB under the main layer is typical, check its share of the air region in the stack view.

**Structured humanizing (`"humanize": {...}`)**: instead of `humanize_ms` (independent jitter: the kind listeners liked least, Hennig et al. 2011, docs/PRODUCTION.md section 4), a drift over song time: `ms` (standard deviation of the timing drift, 4), `beta` (1.0: 1/f noise, long-range correlated like a human player's deviations; 0 = independent), `phrase_bars` (4) and `phrase_ms` (0: each phrase pushed or pulled by a random amount of that size, joined smoothly), `vel` (0: a correlated velocity drift, a fraction, replacing `vel_jitter`), `seed`. Notes of one chord share the offset. Measured on 64 bars of 8ths at ms 6: lag-1 autocorrelation of the deviations +0.66 (independent jitter +0.01), lag-16 +0.24, spectral slope -1.0 (jitter 0.0). In machine genres keep it off the grid-tight parts (trance kick and bass) and use it on what a player would play (hats, percussion, keys); in played genres it replaces `humanize_ms` at the same size or smaller (Senn et al.: never more than a good player's looseness).

**Recipes: trance, following the trance EQ map** (docs/TRANCE.md "Modern trance production", B; ranges are the map's; levels are starting points to set by ear and by the stack view):
- **Kick stack** (one track, `"role": "kick"`): `{"layers": [{"name": "sub", "inst": {"type": "kicksub", "note": "<the key's root or fifth near 48-55 Hz>", "tail": 0.09}, "role": "kick", "eq": {"own_db": 0, "cut_db": 0}}, {"name": "punch", "inst": {"type": "kickpunch", "tune": 125, "decay": 0.035}, "role": "kick", "eq": {"hp": 90, "own_db": 0}, "gain_db": -2}, {"name": "click", "inst": {"type": "kickclick", "tone": 3200}, "role": "kick", "eq": {"hp": 1500, "cut_db": 0, "own_db": 0}}], "fx": [{"type": "clip", "drive_db": 2, "ceiling_db": -1.5}]}`. Do not carve its fundamental to please a curve (docs/TRANCE.md C).
- **Bass stack** (rolling or offbeat; sidechained on the track): sub (`sub`, role `sub`: the map's low-pass near 100 Hz comes with the preset), rolling bass (`trancebass` with `"sub": 0`, `cutoff` 600-900, role `bass`: high-pass about 115 Hz, -3 dB at 265 Hz, +1.5 dB at 195 Hz and 1.2 kHz), upper bass an octave up (`polysynth` saw pluck or an acid, role `upper-bass`, about 6 dB down: high-pass 210 Hz, speaking at 0.8-3 kHz); bus: `comp` 2:1 and a little `sat`.
- **Lead stack**: main `supersaw` model 2 (role `lead`: high-pass 155 Hz, -3 dB at 316 Hz, +1.5 dB at 2 kHz; centre), support `supersaw` model 2 an octave up, slower attack and longer release, wide (role `support`, 6-8 dB down), `air` (role `air`), optionally a mono centre (`polysynth` square at pitch, `"eq": "none"`, low-passed, 10 dB down) and a transient (a pluck above 1 kHz, van Buuren); bus: `ott` 0.3, `comp` 2:1, one reverb and delay send from the bus, sidechain on the bus.
- **Pad stack**: a warm body (`polysynth` saws with a slow cutoff LFO and `drift`, role `pad`: high-pass about 210 Hz, -3 dB at 316 Hz, +1.5 dB at 1.7 and 9.8 kHz), a bright layer an octave up (`supersaw` model 2 with `noise` 0.2, role `support` or `air`), optionally `strings` or `choir` (roles `strings`, `choir`); bus: `ott` 0.2, sidechain 0.5-0.75 / 180 ms, the hall send.
- **Hats**: `metalhat` closed on the 16ths and open on the offbeats, role `hats` with `"eq": "auto"`; a `drums` bus with `clip` for kick, clap and hats.
- **Spaces**: `mix.returns` with a short `room` for the drums and plucks and a long `hall` for the lead and pads, the hall ducked by the lead and cut at 300-600 Hz; the delay `pingpong` with a few ms of `offset_ms`.

### Modern strands: acid, 909 drums, shimmer, granular, resonance suppressor, moving sounds (since 2026-11-25)
The tools docs/TRANCE.md "Modern trance production" G and I found missing (the bleeding-edge checklist and the engine gaps). All opt-in: a project that does not name them renders exactly as before. Code: `engine/acid.py`, `engine/tr909.py`, `engine/spacefx.py`, section automation in `engine/studio.py`. A/B and demos for the ear: `examples/production/modern/` (README there).

**Shimmer reverb** (a return `"type": "shimmer"` in `mix.returns`, or an fx `{"type": "shimmer"}` on a track, layer or bus): a reverb whose tail is pitch-shifted and fed back into itself, so every pass climbs an interval and fades: the "Valhalla Shimmer on everything" wash of the newest strands (an 8-line feedback delay network with input diffusion, per-line damping and modulated lines; a two-tap pitch shifter in its feedback path). Keys: `decay` (s, RT60 of the tank, 6), `size` 0-1 (0.6), `damping` 0-1 (0.45; highs die first), **`amount`** 0-1 (0.5; how much of the tank comes back pitched: 0 is a plain reverb, 0.9 a cascade of octaves), `shift` (semitones, 12), `shift2` (19 or 7) with `mix2` 0-1 (0: off; a second interval in the climb), `grain_ms` (90; the shifter's window: longer is smoother and more smeared), `mod` 0-1 (0.4; chorus of the tank), `mod_rate` (Hz, 0.35), `low_cut` (Hz, 350; high-pass on the shifted feedback), `width` 0-1, plus the return keys (`predelay_ms`, `hpf` 250 and `lpf` 12000 by default, `fx`, `duck`, `return_db`). As an fx it adds `mix` (dry/wet, 0.35) and the same `predelay_ms`, `hpf`, `lpf`. **Measured** on a plucked A4, decay 6 s, 1-2.5 s after the pluck: the octave above (880 Hz) against the note: -40 dB at amount 0, +4.8 dB at 0.5, +9.3 dB at 0.9; two octaves up (1760 Hz) -53 / +6.3 / +14.6 dB. Its level is compensated, so `amount` changes colour, not loudness (the tank is within 1.6 dB from amount 0 to 1). Typical trance: a return fed by the pads, plucks and the voice at 0.2-0.4, `decay` 5-9, `amount` 0.4-0.7, `predelay_ms` 20-40, `hpf` 300-400, `lpf` 10000-12000, ducked by the lead, `return_db` -8 to -4; `shift2` 19 with `mix2` 0.3 for a brighter climb; automate `amount` up through a breakdown (`"auto": {"return:<name>": {"amount": [0.3, 0.9]}}`).

**Granular / freeze** (a return `"type": "granular"`, or an fx `{"type": "granular"}`): grains read from what the return or track is fed, behind the playhead, laid over each other into a cloud. It is an effect, not an instrument fed by another track: in this engine a track renders on its own and its audio reaches others only through sends and buses, so the way to "freeze a pluck into a pad" is to send the pluck (or a bus) into a granular return; on a single track or bus it works as an insert. The **read head** starts `position_ms` (150) behind the playhead at every section start and moves at **`scan`** x real time (1: follows the music; 0.5 or 0.25: the section stretched; **0: a freeze**, the cloud holds what came just before the section began; `"anchor": "none"` never resets it). When a section starts frozen and the 300 ms before its read head are silent (the section before ended in a rest), the head moves back to the last sounding moment, so the freeze holds the music, not the rest (**`seek`**, true by default since 2026-11-26; `false` freezes the silence as before; a head that already reads sound is left alone). Each grain reads from a random point up to **`spray_ms`** (800) further back, so the cloud is many moments of the source at once. Keys: `grain_ms` (300; 5-2000), `density` (grains per second, 80), `spray_ms` (800), `pitch` (semitones, 0), `pitch_spread` (semitones, 0; each grain detuned at random within +-), `intervals` (a list, e.g. `[0, 12]` or `[0, 0, -12]`: each grain picks one), `window` (`hann`, `triangle`, `tukey`, `gauss`; `window_shape`: tukey's fade fraction 0.5, gauss's sigma 0.18), `pan_spread` 0-1 (0.8), `reverse` 0-1 (0; the share of grains played backwards), `jitter` 0-1 (0.6; how irregular the onsets are), **`smooth`** 0-3 (2: the attacks of what it is fed are flattened before the grains read it, over `smooth_ms` 40, so a grain that starts on an attack does not replay it as a stray note), **`blur_ms`** (1000: each frequency's level in the cloud is averaged over this time, phases kept: a spectral smear that keeps single grains and the source's melody from showing through), **`tone_hz`** (7000; a 12 dB/oct low-pass on the cloud, 0 = off), **`dissolve`** (s, 0 = hold: wherever the head stands still, `scan` 0, the cloud falls 60 dB and its low-pass closes from 16 kHz by four octaves over this time, and stays gone until the next section: a freeze that fades like a tail instead of holding and cutting), `seed`; as an fx `mix` (0.5). A grain never reads audio that has not happened yet. The cloud is level-matched to its input; `density` x `grain_ms` sets how many grains overlap (24 at the defaults), and the level stays steady when you move them. Deterministic: its grains come from the project seed and the owner's name.

The defaults make a **pad**: wide reads (800 ms of source per moment, about seven 16th notes at 138 bpm), long dense grains, flattened attacks and a 1 s smear, so a stretched line becomes a chord that moves slowly, not a slowed copy of the melody. For an audible **slow-motion** or stutter effect (the line replayed at half speed, single grains heard): `spray_ms` 40, `grain_ms` 90, `density` 30, `smooth` 0, `blur_ms` 0, `tone_hz` 0 (`jitter` 0 for a regular stutter). **Measured** on the pluck arp of `examples/production/modern/` (granular-B: stretched at `scan` 0.5 for 4 bars, then frozen with `dissolve` 6), the cloud alone, against the demo the owner heard second (grain 160 ms, density 40, spray 120 ms, blur 200 ms, no tone or dissolve). Harmonic events (12-bin chroma averaged over 100 ms, novelty = 1 - cosine with 150 ms earlier, peaks over 0.15):
- **stretch**: 2.4 per second, novelty p95 0.63, before (the owner's "second bip, off beat, competes": the line's notes coming back slower, off the grid); now 0.16 per second, p95 0.07. For scale: the dry pluck 4.4/s and 0.95; a long hall on it 4.3/s and 0.50; the slow-motion settings 2.9/s and 0.82.
- **freeze**, bars 5-6: before, centroid 1702 Hz and one peak at 1315 Hz holding 49% of the energy (E5 an octave up through `intervals` +12: "one bip squeaks"); now centroid 1116 Hz and the strongest peak (A4, 440 Hz) 19%, 11% of the energy above 2 kHz (the dry pluck: 1082 Hz, 20%, 13%). The wide reads did most of this; `tone_hz` 7000 lowers the centroid by a further 30 Hz.
- **dissolve 6 s**: -9, -21, -31, -41, -52 dB after 1-5 s (before: held within 2 dB until the section's end cut it). No 10 ms level bump over 3 dB in the freeze (151 with the first demo's settings).

Typical trance (the melodic-techno wash): the pluck sent at 0.3-0.6 into a return at the defaults with `pitch_spread` 0.05-0.15 and `intervals` [0, 12] or [0, 0, -12], low-passed; `scan` 0.25-0.5 while it plays; for the breakdown, `"auto": {"return:<name>": {"scan": 0}}` in the section after the pluck stops (the last bar hangs in the air) with `dissolve` 4-8 for a fade.

**Acid voice** (`acid`, monophonic, mono): a TB-303-style line. One `wave` (`saw` default, or `square`) into a resonant ladder low-pass with a tanh at its input (`slope` **18** dB/oct, the 303's diode ladder as heard, or **24**; measured 17-19 and 22-25 dB/oct two to three octaves above the cutoff), `cutoff` (Hz, 320), `reso` 0-1.05 (0.75; self-oscillates near 1), `env_mod` 0-1.5 (0.55; up to 4 octaves of filter envelope x the value), `decay` (s, 0.45; the filter envelope falls to 10% in this time), `accent` 0-1.5 (0.6), `drive` 0-1 (0; tanh distortion after the VCA), `slide_ms` (60; the RC-like pitch lag), `accent_decay` (0.2 s: accented notes take this shortest decay, as on the 303), `accent_release` (0.35 s; how slowly the accent "capacitor" lets go), `attack` (0.003 s), `release` (0.008 s), `accent_vel` (off; a velocity at or over it also counts as an accent, for parts written with numeric velocities). In the notes: **`!` = accent** (louder, a shorter and deeper filter envelope, and a charge on the accent "capacitor" that raises the cutoff and lets go slowly, so accents in a row climb: the "wow"), **`~` = slide** into this note (the gate stays up, the envelope is not retriggered, the pitch glides). A note shorter than its step leaves a gap (the 303's gate); `legato` sets it. **Measured** (A1 16ths at 138 bpm, defaults): an accented step against a plain one: +3.3 dB, spectral centroid above 200 Hz over its first 30 ms 707 vs 612 Hz; six accents in a row climb 709, 795, 835, 852, 857, 860 Hz (six plain steps stay at 602-616); a slide from 55 to 110 Hz is at 66 / 82 / 97 / 107 Hz after 0 / 15 / 30 / 60 ms (no slide: 110 Hz at once); a slid note keeps the falling envelope (centroid 494 Hz against 612 for a retriggered one). Default level -6 dB. Typical (raw and hard strands, an upper bass or the lead): 16ths with accents on a few offbeats and slides into the octave jumps, `cutoff` 200-500, `env_mod` 0.4-0.8, `reso` 0.7-0.9, `decay` 0.3-0.8, `accent` 0.5-0.9, `drive` 0.2-0.5 then `suppress`; the classic move is a slow "knob ride": automate `cutoff` and `decay` over 8-16 bars (`"auto": {"acid": {"inst": {"cutoff": [200, 800, "log"]}}}`). Role `upper-bass` (an octave above the sub) or `lead`; sidechain it lightly (0.3-0.5).

**Slide and accent on the polysynth** (`polysynth` and `supersaw` `"model": 2`, opt-in): with `glide_ms` set, a note marked `~` slides from the nearest note of the previous onset (for a chord, voice by voice to the nearest; an exponential approach, 90% in `glide_ms`); with `accent` (0-1) set, a note marked `!` gets a deeper (x 1 + accent) and shorter filter envelope and is louder (x 1 + accent/2). Without the two keys `~` and `!` do what they always did (velocity only).

**909 drums** (drums, round robin; docs/TRANCE.md G 7: the raw and hard strands' hardware colour): `snare909` (two oscillators, a body near 181 Hz falling from about twice that over 7 ms and a second at x 1.97, plus high-passed noise; `tune`, `bend`, `bend_ms`, `ratio2`, `level2`, `tone_decay` 0.0196 s, `body`, `snappy` 0.34 = the noise level, `noise_decay` 0.043 s = the 909's tone knob, `noise_hp` 1990, `noise_lp` 8760, `drive`), `clap909` (`bursts` 4 noise pulses `spacing_ms` 10.7 apart, each a sawtooth decay of `burst_decay` 2.6 ms, band `lo` 775 to `hi` 9800 Hz, then the tail: `tail` 0.55, `tail_decay` 0.0685 s, band `tail_lo` 440 to `tail_hi` 2500 Hz; `drive`), `ride909` (a cymbal sample made of partials and noise, quantized to `bits` 6 as the 909's ROM, through the VCA `decay` 0.28 s and the output filters `hp` 50 / `lp` 16000; `low` 0.044, `mid` 0.07, `bell` 0.116, `tilt` 0.85, `noise` 0.25, `seed`). Default levels -1, -7, -19 dB (by loudness within 1 dB of `kitsnare`, `handclap` and `kitride`). **Fitted and measured** against a recorded TR-909 (oramics/sampled "TR-909 Detroit", measured only; `examples/production/modern/measure909.py`, picture `909-vs-reference.png`): 1/3-octave spectrum over the first 300 ms (600 for the ride) as RMS dB difference, and the time each band takes to fall 20 dB as log2 error (0.1 = about 7%):

The clap's bursts land at 0, 10.7, 21.4 and 32 ms (the recording: 0, 11, 23, 32 ms); the snare's pitch fall matches the recording's (254 Hz averaged over the first 23 ms, 184 Hz after 20 ms). The fit used the same recordings (one snare, two claps, one ride of one machine): the numbers show how close the models get, not an independent test. Typical: `clap909` on 2 and 4 (alone in the raw strand, or under `handclap` for a layered clap), `ride909` on the offbeat 8ths or the 16ths at `decay` 0.15-0.3, `snare909` for rolls in a build (with `roll_pitch`); a `drums` bus with `clip`.

**Resonance suppressor** (fx `{"type": "suppress"}`, on a track, layer or bus; G 5: the step after a saturation chain): finds the bands where the spectrum's peaks stand out of its broad trend (a resonant whistle, a harsh band after `drive` or `sat`) and turns them down while they last, linked across the channels. Keys: `depth` (dB, 6: the most it takes), `threshold` (dB, 2: how far over the trend before it acts), `sharpness` 0-1 (0.5: the trend's width, 1 octave at 0 to 1/3 octave at 1; sharper = only narrower resonances), `ratio` (3: of the excess over the threshold, 1 - 1/ratio goes), `lo` / `hi` (Hz, 300 / 16000), `attack_ms` (5), `release_ms` (80), `mix` 0-1 (1), `delta` (true: hear only what it removes). The trend is read from the line through the harmonics' peaks (so a plain saw's own harmonics are not "resonances"). **Measured**: a 220 Hz saw with a resonance at 2.6 kHz (Q 12, +10 dB over its trend) after tanh saturation: the bulge falls from 10.0 to 5.2 dB at the defaults (to 3.2 with `depth` 12, `ratio` 6, `threshold` 1), the whole signal by 0.18 dB; white noise passes within 0.03 dB and the same saw without the resonance within 0.1 dB. Typical: after `drive`/`sat`/`clip` on an acid, a distorted lead or a lead stack's bus, `depth` 4-8, `threshold` 2-3; check with `delta` that it takes the whistle, not the note.

**Automating sounds** (section `auto`, see SECTION): a section can move an instrument's own parameters and those of these three fx, not only a track's filters and level: the sound itself grows or settles across the arrangement. Deterministic, and written per section like the other `auto` moves.

### Bass music: FM growl, wavetable, synced modulation, vowel, resampling, multiband and distortion (since 2026-11-25)
The tools docs/DUBSTEP.md "Engine mapping" ranks essential and important for modern dubstep (and useful in any bass-led genre). All opt-in: a project that does not name them renders exactly as before. Code: `engine/bass.py` (voices, modulation, formant filter, sampler), `engine/bassfx.py` (fx). A/B for the ear: `examples/production/dubstep/` (README there); the test project `examples/dubstep-test.json`. Set `"genre": "dubstep"` for the role EQ presets (the `dubstep` map: mid-bass high-passed about 116 Hz, sub low-passed about 120 Hz).

**`fmgrowl`** (polyphonic, **stereo**): frequency modulation, the most common growl core. `ops`: 2-4 operators, the first the carrier: `[{"ratio": 1}, {"ratio": 2, "index": 1.5}, {"ratio": 1, "index": 1.0}]` (`ratio` x the note; `index` = how hard that operator modulates the one below it, 0-10; a modulator at +2 octaves is `ratio` 4); `algo` `"stack"` (default: the last operator modulates the one before ... down to the carrier) or `"parallel"` (every modulator into the carrier); `feedback` 0-3 (0: the top operator modulates itself: grit and a vocal edge), then the bass chain and unison below. Static FM with integer ratios is harmonic (52 dB harmonic-to-rest on an F1 note); moving the index is what makes it growl.

**`wavetable`** (polyphonic, **stereo**): a single-cycle table scanned at `pos` 0-1 (morphing smoothly between 32 frames, band-limited per note so high notes do not alias). `table`: `basic` (sine, triangle, saw, square), `vocal` (a saw shaped by a formant envelope moving oo-oh-ah-eh-ee: "formant-ready"), `harsh` (a hard-synced saw, slave ratio 1 to 8: digital tearing), `fm` (2-op FM, index 0 to 8), `pwm` (pulse width 0.5 to 0.03). Typical: `vocal` or `harsh` with `pos` swept by an LFO at "1/2" or "2/1" (the neuro morph).

**The bass chain** (both voices, in this order): `fold` 0-1 (wavefolder), `drive` 0-1 (tanh), `bits` (0 = off; bit reduction, 8-12 for grit), `vowel` (a formant filter: `"oo"`, `"oh"`, `"ah"`, `"eh"`, `"ee"` or a position 0-4; `vowel_mix` 0-1 (1), `vowel_q` (1; narrower above), `formant_shift` (1: x the formant frequencies)), `filter` (`lp` default, `bp`, `hp`, `none`), `cutoff` (16000), `reso` 0-1, `keytrack`; amp `attack` (0.004), `decay`, `sustain_level` (1), `release` (0.08); unison `voices` (1), `detune` (cents of the outer voice, 12), `spread` 0-1 (0.7); `out` (0.5). `~` slides (always on: `glide_ms` 60). Default level -10 dB.

**Synced, per-note modulation (`mod`)**, on `fmgrowl` and `wavetable`: a list of modulators, each to a `dest`: `index` (all modulators' index; `index.1`, `index.2` one operator), `feedback`, `pos` (wavetable position), `vowel` (0-4), `fold`, `drive`, `cutoff` (octaves), `reso`, `pitch` (semitones), `level` (dB), `pan`. An LFO: `{"dest": "index", "rate": "1/8t", "shape": "tri", "depth": 4}`: `rate` a note value (`"1/4"` a beat, `"1/8"`, `"1/16"`, `"1/8t"` triplet, `"1/8d"` dotted, `"1/1"` a bar, `"2/1"` two bars) or Hz (a number), `shape` `sine`, `tri`, `saw` (falling: the classic "wub"), `ramp`, `square`, `steps` (with `"steps": [0, 1, 0.5, ...]`, one per cycle step), `random`, `smooth`; values run 0 to 1 x `depth` (`"bipolar": true` for -1 to 1); `retrig` (true: restarts on every note; false: locked to the song's grid, phase 0 at the song's start), `phase` (0-1), `delay` and `fade` (s), `vel` (scale by velocity). An envelope: `{"type": "env", "dest": "pitch", "attack": 0, "decay": 0.08, "depth": 7}` (`sustain`, `release`; `"loop": "1/8"` repeats it at a note value: the looping-envelope "honk"). **A note can change the first LFO's rate**: `"0:F1:6/r=1/8t 8:F1:4/r=1/16 16:F1:6/r=1/8 24:Eb1:8~/r=1/4"` (`r2=` the second LFO): the wobble's rhythm is the riff (docs/DUBSTEP.md "Sound design"). **`polysynth`** (and `supersaw` model 2) takes the same note values in its `lfo` `rate` (`"phase"`: `"retrig"` (default for note values), `"grid"`, `"free"`) and the same `/r=` tokens. **Measured** (the `mod` A/B: one polysynth patch, rates per note asked 1/8t, 1/16, 1/8, 1/4 at 140 bpm = 7.0, 9.33, 4.67, 2.33 Hz): the level of 0.3-6 kHz moves at 7.19, 9.16, 4.86, 2.43 Hz (median of 4 phrases; the method resolves about 3%); the old patch, one rate in Hz, 7.2 Hz on every note.

**Formant (vowel) filter**: three band-passes (gains 0, -6, -12 dB; Q 5, 9, 12) at the formants of Peterson & Barney (1952), adult male means: oo 300/870/2240, oh 570/840/2410, ah 730/1090/2440, eh 530/1840/2480, ee 270/2290/3010 Hz, in that order so F2 rises from 0 to 4 and a sweep reads "oo-ah-ee" (the "yoi"). Between positions the formants glide (a morph, not a crossfade). **Measured** on white noise: every formant within 2.5% of its target (largest: oh F2 861 Hz for 840). In the voices (`vowel`, mod dest `vowel`) and as an fx: `{"type": "vowel", "vowel": "oo", "rate": "1/4", "shape": "tri", "depth": 4, "q": 1.2, "shift": 1, "mix": 1}` (without `rate`, a fixed vowel). Keep it on the mid-bass (not the sub): "don't leave too much low end in the sound".

**Resampling: `sampler`** (polyphonic, **stereo**): plays audio the engine renders from another track: `{"type": "sampler", "source": "growl", "from_bar": 9, "bars": 1}`: the source track as its instrument, stack and fx make it (before its sidechain, section moves and sends), bars `from_bar` (1-based) to `from_bar + bars`; the source may be muted (print it and keep only the sampler), and may itself be a sampler (serial resampling). `mode` `"pitch"` (default: the note transposes the region from `root`, "C3", by resampling: lower is slower, like tape) or `"slice"` (`slices` 8: the region cut into equal slices, the note picks one chromatically from `root`, C3 = slice 0; `pitch` semitones for all; a note's `/s=3` picks slice 3); `reverse`, `start` (0-1), `attack_ms` (2), `release_ms` (30), `gain_db`; the note's length gates it; `~` slides the rate (`glide_ms`). Default level 0 dB (the source's own level). Not for tempo-mapped projects (bars are counted at the project `bpm`). The source's random streams are its own (per-track seeds) or restored afterwards, so adding a sampler changes no other track.

**Bass fx** (any track, layer or bus; LFO rates in note values, locked to the song's grid):
- `multiband` {`split`: 1-3 crossovers (Hz, [150, 2500]), `bands`: an fx chain per band (low to high), `gains_db`, `widths` (per band; 0 = mono)}: Linkwitz-Riley 4th-order bands that sum flat. **Measured** (the `multiband` A/B, an F1 growl): one drive over the whole sound leaves the fundamental band (40-50 Hz) 3.3 dB under the distortion products at 50-150 Hz; low band compressed and clean, mid folded, high clipped: 13.3 dB under them (the low stays a clean fundamental, the top carries the grit).
- `fold` {`amount` 0-1 (0.5), `bias` (0; even harmonics), `mix`}: wavefolder, 4x oversampled. `asym` {`amount` (0.5), `bias` (0.3), `rectify` 0-1 (0; full-wave adds the octave), `mix`}: asymmetric saturation. `hardclip` {`drive_db` (6), `ceiling_db` (-1), `mix`}: 4x oversampled.
- `comb` {`note` or `hz` (110), `feedback` (0.85; negative = hollow, odd harmonics), `damp` (0.2), `depth` (0; sweep of the delay, a fraction), `rate`, `shape`, `mix`}: a tuned feedback comb: metallic screeches. `flanger` {`rate` ("1/2"), `base_ms` (2.5), `depth_ms` (2), `feedback` (0.6), `mix` (0.5)}.
- `resonator` {`notes` (["A2"]), `feedback` (0.97), `damp` (0.3), `mix` (0.5)}: tuned combs that ring at the notes on what is near them (noise, distorted tops, drums; +34 dB at its note on white noise); level-matched.
- `vocoder` {`notes` (a chord), `bands` (20), `lo`/`hi` (120-8000 Hz), `q` (6), `smooth_ms` (12), `noise` (0.05), `mix` (0.7)}: the track's band envelopes shape a chord of saws, so a growl's movement rings as that chord (colour bass). **Measured** (the `vocoder` A/B, a growl on F2 into Fm7): energy within a quarter tone of Ab3, Eb4, Ab4 (not harmonics of F2): -25.4 dB of 150-1500 Hz without, -6.5 dB with.
- `autofilter` {`rate` ("1/8"), `shape`, `lo` (200), `hi` (4000), `reso` (0.3), `mode` (`lp`, `bp`, `hp`), `follow` (true: the signal's own envelope opens it, `curve` 0.6)}; `autopan` {`rate` ("1/4"), `shape`, `depth` (0.8)}.

**Gated reverb**: a return's `"gate": {"hold_ms": 140, "release_ms": 25, "threshold_db": -30}`: the wet passes while the return's input plays and `hold_ms` after, then closes. **Measured** (the `gatedverb` A/B, 909 snare into a size 0.8 reverb): per 16th after the hit 0, +3.0, -0.3, -9.5, -50.7 dB (the tail cut after about 150 ms) against 0, +3.0, -0.3, -3.2, -6.2 ... -15.4 dB ungated.

Section `auto` reaches these voices like any polyphonic instrument, including their modulators (`"inst": {"mod.0.depth": [1, 5], "mod.0.rate": "1/16"}` per section; a note's `/r=` still wins for that note).

Not built (docs/DUBSTEP.md gaps 9, part of 4 and 5): formant shift on `singer` and `vox2` (shift formants without pitch); wavetable warp modes (sync, bend, mirror, FM-from) and user tables; time-stretch in the sampler; a modulated downsampler (`srr` takes a fixed rate).

**Recipe: a modern dubstep drop** (docs/DUBSTEP.md; `examples/production/dubstep/` "drop"): a kick stack on 1 (and a ghost), `snare909` `tune` 200 and `clap909` on 3 into a gated reverb, `metalhat`, all on a `drums` bus with `clip`; `sub` (role `sub`) under everything, sidechained to the kick; the lead bass an `fmgrowl` (or `wavetable`) with `mod` on `index` and `vowel` at note-value rates and `/r=` per note, then `multiband` (low band clean, mid `fold` + `ott`, high `hardclip` and wider), `suppress`, `mseq` `side_hpf` 150-180, role `upper-bass`; a `sampler` of the growl for chops in the second half; a `bass` bus with `comp` and `clip`, sidechained; master `--genre dubstep` (below).

**Mastering a loud drop (measured 2026-11-26, `examples/production/dubstep/mastertest.py`)**: the drop above (clipped `drums` and `bass` buses) as a 16-bar drop between an 8-bar intro and a 16-bar outro fading 14 dB, through `master --genre dubstep`: at the genre's `--lufs -8` the drop reads **-6.5 LUFS short-term** (median; max -6.4), true peak -1.03 dBTP, PLR 7.1, at most 2.6 dB of limiting: inside the -6.5 to -6 of current releases without changing the master (it already soft-clips before its true-peak limiter; the clipping in the mix's buses does the rest: "clip first, then a gentle limiter"). Without the outro the drop sits 0.2 dB lower (-6.7). `--lufs -8.5` gives -6.9 (-7.3 without the outro): a long quiet section lifts the drop by 0.2-0.4 dB only, because the loudness gate ignores most of it. `--lufs -7` reaches -5.3 (PLR 6.2) and `-6.5` reaches -4.8 (PLR 5.7): louder than the releases measured; don't. Read the drop's short-term loudness in the master report, not the integrated figure.

### Bass music, fitted: presets, `curve`, `transient`, `widen`, stereo transitions (since 2026-11-26)
The bass-music voices above are patches; this pass measured 118 openly licensed recorded references (growls, riddim, wobble, reese, sub, snare, clap, kick, hats, screech, vocal chops, risers, impacts, downlifters; engine/third_party.json `refs-dubstep-cc0`, `refs-dubstep-ccby`) and fitted sounds to them (examples/production/dubstep-v2/: README, `tables.md`, A/B demos). All opt-in: a project that names none of this renders exactly as before. Code: `engine/dubstep2.py`, presets in `engine/bass_presets.json`.

**Presets**: a track's `"preset": "<name>"` fills in an instrument (or a stack of layers), role, EQ and fx chain fitted to the references; whatever the track sets itself wins (its `inst` keys over the preset's `inst`, e.g. `{"preset": "growl", "inst": {"mod": [...]}}`; any other key, such as `fx`, `layers`, `gain_db`, `bus`, `sends`, replaces the preset's); on a stacked preset (`kick`, `snare`, `hardkick`) `inst` keys without a `type` go to its first layer, the body: `{"preset": "hardkick", "inst": {"note": "A1"}}`, since 2026-11-26). Write notes as for the underlying voice (`/r=` rates on `growl`). What each is and what it was fitted to (reference median against the old engine sound against the preset; full tables in the examples folder):
- `growl` (`fmgrowl` 3 operators, the FM index and a low-pass both swept by a falling saw at 1/8, so the brightness moves with the wub; multiband: low compressed and mono, mid OTT, high OTT and hard clip, then a soft clip). Energy above 150 Hz in 2-5 / 5-16 kHz: references -6.8 / -7.5 dB, 017's captor -13.7 / -17.8, preset -5.1 / -6.9; centroid movement 0.69 / 0.2 / 1.1 octaves; loudness at a -1 dBFS peak -7.8 / -9.0 / -7.6 LUFS.
- `riddim` (`wavetable` harsh, square position LFO at 1/8 and a saw low-pass at 1/4, flanger, multiband OTT and clip, wide above 150 Hz). 2-5 kHz: -5.4 / -21.5 (017's husk) / -5.4 dB; side against mid above 1 kHz: about 0 / -15 / +2.5 dB; loudness at -1 dBFS peak -5.9 / -9.4 / -7.4 LUFS.
- `wobble` (`polysynth` saws, resonant low-pass LFO at 1/8, asym, OTT, mono below 150 Hz, clip): loudness at -1 dBFS peak -8.5 / -14.6 (the old wobble) / -9.1 LUFS; crest 10.8 / 15.2 / 10.8 dB.
- `reese` (`polysynth` two pairs of detuned saws, asym, OTT, mono below 250 Hz, clip): 5-16 kHz -17.7 / -34.2 / -19.4 dB; side below 150 Hz -24 / -3 / -14 dB. Still under the references' density (-13.4 against -5.9 LUFS at -1 dBFS peak, the old one -18.2: the beating of the detuned saws).
- `screech` (`fmgrowl` 1:3 with feedback, index at 1/16, a slow pitch wander, flanger, OTT, clip): play it an octave or two above the bass.
- `snare` (909 body at 200 Hz with a longer bend and noise, a `kitsnare` shell, the 909 clap 6 dB down, fitted tone, `transient` sustain -3, clip, `widen` above 2 kHz): 500 Hz-2 kHz -13.3 / -3.6 (017's stack) / -14.2 dB of the total; crest 12.2 / 18.8 / 10.6 dB; loudness at -1 dBFS peak -9.5 / -15.0 / -8.9 LUFS; -20 dB decay of the highs 242 / 117 / 274 ms.

The presets sit at the references' loudness density, so a preset track is louder at the same peak than the patch it replaces: set its level by ear (`gain_db`).

**New fx** (any track, layer or bus):
- `curve` {`points`: {Hz: dB, ...} (third-octave gains), `gain_db`}: a minimum-phase EQ through the points (log-frequency interpolation; flat outside). The presets' fitted tone; usable as a hand-drawn EQ.
- `transient` {`attack_db` (6), `sustain_db` (0), `fast_ms` (1), `slow_ms` (25), `release_ms` (60), `range_db` (12)}: a transient designer from two followers of the same signal; where the fast one leads the attack gain applies, where it trails the sustain gain. Level-independent.
- `widen` {`from_hz` (300), `amount` (0.5), `delay_ms` (11), `second_ms` (17)}: width for mono sources: a delayed, high-passed copy of the mid (two taps of opposite sign) added to the side; L+R unchanged, so it vanishes in mono.

**Stereo, full-bodied transitions**: `"fx_model": 2` in the project (top level, next to `"seeds"`): the section `enter` `impact` and `downlifter` and `exit` `riser`, `rise` and `reverse` become stereo (the old ones are mono) and gain a body: the impact is a pitched boom (170 to 50 Hz) with a noise body and a room (60-150 Hz -6.3 dB of the total, references -4.8, old -28; side against mid at 1-5 kHz / above 5 kHz -1.8 / -0.6 dB, references -1.4 / -1.9, old mono; crest 10.8 against 12.2, old 2.7 dB); the riser adds the rising saws and a low noise to the noise sweep; the downlifter a falling tone (260 to 65 Hz). `crash` is unchanged. Without the key (every project before 2026-11-26) the old ones play.

### Techno, fitted: 909 kick, hats, toms, rim; congas and bongos; the rumble; hard kick and hoover presets; free-running loops (since 2026-11-26)
For docs/TECHNO.md (hard techno, hard groove, acid; the brief's "Engine mapping" gaps 1-7). Fitted to recordings: a recorded TR-909 (the "TR-909 Detroit" kit, as the 909 snare, clap and ride) and 56 openly licensed Freesound sounds (engine/third_party.json `refs-tr909-detroit`, `refs-techno-cc0`, `refs-techno-ccby`; measured only). Method, every number and A/B demos: examples/production/techno/README.md. All opt-in: a project that names none of this renders exactly as before. Code: `engine/techno.py`; presets in `engine/techno_presets.json` (used like the bass presets: `"preset"`, the track's own keys win).

**Drums** (drum tracks, step strings as any drum):
- `kick909`: the TR-909 kick (a VCO swept down fast then slowly, a rounded triangle, the DECAY knob, an ATTACK click). `tune` (51.5 Hz) or `note`, `bend` / `bend_ms` (2.62 x over 3.7 ms), `bend2` / `bend2_ms` (0.69 x over 31 ms), `decay` (0.143 s after `hold` 0.011), `shape` (0.86; 0 sine, 2 triangle), `attack` (0.59), `click_hz`, `click_ms`, `noise`, `noise_ms`, `drive`. A longer 909 kick: `decay` 0.25-0.35. Under heavy processing (hard groove's "909 that needs heavy processing") put `asym` / `clip` on the track.
- `hat909`, `openhat909`: the 909's hats, a 6-bit cymbal sample (inharmonic partials and noise quantized to `bits` 6) through the VCA and the output filter. Closed: `decay` 0.0215 s, a high-pass at 11.8 kHz on the top plus `body` 0.77 of the whole sample (the grain and the stick: the recorded 909 hat has as much energy at 300 Hz-3 kHz in its first 30 ms as 10 dB under its top). Open: `hold` 0.042 + `decay` 0.073 s (the OH DECAY knob; 0.15-0.4 for a long open hat; vary it per section with `auto`). Levels `low`, `mid`, `high`, `top`, `noise`, `tilt`, `hp`, `lp`, `body`, `body_hp`, `seed` (another cymbal). Set `"choke_by": "<closed hat track>"` on the open hat. Rolls shift the sample's rate.
- `tom909`: `pitch` "low" | "mid" | "high" (75.8, 93, 114.3 Hz) or `tune` / `note`; `bend` (0.63) over `bend_ms` (31), `decay` 0.083 s after `hold` 0.0185, a noise skin (`noise` 0.3, `noise_ms` 15, `noise_lp`), `shape`, `drive`.
- `rim909`: three resonators (318, 789, 2017 Hz) struck by a pulse, a click, overdriven (`drive` 1.57), high-passed 217 Hz; `f1`-`f3`, `l1`-`l3`, `d1`-`d3`, `click`, `drive`, `hp`.
- `conga`, `bongo`: hand drums as damped membrane modes with the head's pitch relaxing after the stroke and the hand's slap noise. `stroke` "open" (fitted), "slap" (short, a crack of noise), "mute" (short, dull), "bass" (heel: an octave lower thump) [H]: one track per stroke (`{"type": "conga", "stroke": "slap"}`). `tune` (conga 231 Hz; a quinto about 300, a tumba 180; bongo 480 Hz the macho, about 330 the hembra) or `note`, `decay`, `modes`, `bright`, `bend`, `bend_ms`, `slap`, `slap_hz`, `drive`. Swing them (track `swing` 0.52-0.54) and humanize them; keep the kick straight.
Default levels: `kick909` -1, `hat909` -12, `openhat909` -14, `tom909` -6, `rim909` -9, `conga` -7, `bongo` -8 dB.

**The rumble** (hard and peak-time techno's sub): the kick into a long reverb (100% wet), then distortion, then a steep low-pass, mono, silent on each kick and swelling back between them. In one step: on the kick track, `"rumble": true` (or a dict of settings) creates a return named `rumble` keyed by that track's hits and sends the kick to it. Or by hand: `"mix": {"returns": {"rumble": {"type": "rumble", "key": "kick", ...}}}` and a send from the kick. Settings (defaults fitted to 8 recorded kick-and-rumble loops): `decay` 0.67 s (the reverb's T60), `predelay_ms`, `damp_hz` 1800; `drive_db` 24 and `bias` 0.15 (the distortion after the reverb); `lpf` 390 Hz or `tone`: "sub" 96.6, "deep" 150, "warm" 250, "hard" 390, "grit" 800 (the sources' range, docs/TECHNO.md); `slope` 48 dB/oct; `hpf` 28 Hz; `notch_hz` (0 off; 135 for MusicRadar's boxy note); the duck: `duck_db` 60 (silent at the hit), `attack_ms` 2, `release` 1.43 beats, `curve` 0.92 (above 1: stays down longer, then swells); `level_db` -9.5 (its RMS against the dry kick's while both play: the send amount does not change it); `return_db` (0). Section automation as any return: `"auto": {"return:rumble": {"lpf": [90, 160], "gain_db": [-6, 0]}}` (opening the rumble in the intro). Measured (fitrumble.py): its 30-150 Hz level halfway through the beat sits 6.8 dB under the kick's (references -6.1, median of 8 loops; the parts built by hand -10.0; the kick alone -20); the beat's level shape 4.0 dB RMS from the references' (by hand 4.2), its 25-800 Hz spectrum 4.9 dB (by hand 5.2). The kick should have no sub of its own fighting it: in a rumble track high-pass the kick near 50 Hz or let the rumble carry the sub (docs/TECHNO.md "Layering").

**Presets**:
- `hardkick`: a distorted hard-techno kick: `kicksub` (tuned with the track's `"inst": {"note": "A1"}`, which goes to the body layer of this stacked preset; a 24-semitone fall over 16.5 ms), `kickpunch`, `kickclick` through `asym`, `hardclip`, `transient`, a fitted `curve` and a clip. Against 15 recorded hard-techno kicks: third-octave RMS 7.0 dB (the stack before the curve 13.3); crest 4.1-3.8 dB (references 7.0: denser than they are), loudness at a -1 dBFS peak -9.3 to -10.1 LUFS (-8.6), the low band 20 dB down in 173-191 ms (192). Tune it to the key; play it straight.
- `hoover`: the rave hoover (detuned PWM pulses over a saw an octave down, a pitch swoop up into each note, chorus, OTT, clip, a fitted curve), for the revival's stabs and "sweeping hoovers"; play it in octaves 2-3. Third-octave RMS 6.2 dB against 6 recorded hoovers (13.9 before the curve); it is peakier than they are (crest 17 against 12.4 dB).

**Mastering hard techno**: `studio.py master vNNN --genre techno --strand hard` (target `techno_hard`, -8 LUFS integrated, mono below 150 Hz). Tested on a 4:45 hard-techno track with 32-bar DJ ends (examples/production/techno/README.md "Master test"): the main and peak sections land at -7.4 to -7.5 LUFS short-term (max -7.3; Hard Techno releases -7.3), true peak -1.03 dBTP, the limiter at most 1.7 dB. Read the loudest 2 minutes after mastering (`listen` on the master); if they read under -8, the mix lacks density (drive the kick and rumble, clip the drum bus), not the master. The default `--genre techno` (-9.5) puts peak-time loud sections near -8.8. A kick-and-rumble intro is only about 1.5 dB under the main sections; a breakdown without the kick about 3 dB.

**Free-running loops** (polymeter): a pattern with `"free": true` keeps the song's clock: its loop position is the song's bar count modulo its length, as if it had played from bar 1, so it does not restart at each section. With a fractional `bars` it drifts against the 4/4: `{"bars": 0.1875, "free": true, "tom": "x.."}` is a 3-step tom loop (3 sixteenths), `{"bars": 0.3125, "free": true, "rim": "x...."}` a 5-step one; a 12-step string in `"bars": 0.75` is a 3-beat loop. Fractional `bars` works on any pattern (drums, notes, chords); without `free` the loop restarts at every section start, as before. Steps keep their 16th-note grid when the string length / bars is 16.

### Orchestra and trailer: brass, woodwinds, orchestral and epic percussion, basses, trailer effects, hall, choir (since 2026-11-26)
The essential and important gaps of docs/CINEMATIC_HYBRID.md "Engine mapping", code in `engine/orchestra.py` (and the basses in `engine/chamber.py`, the hall in `engine/spacefx.py`). All opt-in: used only when a track names them. A/B against the stand-ins the brief names, measurements and the choices made: `examples/production/cinematic/README.md`. Articulations go after the note length (`"0:D4:12/swell"`, `"48:D2:16/roll,cresc"`).

**`brass`** (polyphonic, **stereo**): `section` `horns` (default; 4 players), `trumpets` (3), `trombones` (3), `bass_trombone` (1), `tuba` (1). An additive model whose harmonic levels are measured on recordings (University of Iowa MIS ff and VSCO-2-CE's velocity layers: `engine/orchestra_winds.json`, fitted by `examples/production/cinematic/fit_winds.py`), interpolated by pitch and dynamic; the tone **brightens with the note's level as it moves** (louder = more upper harmonics: soft-to-loud, the recordings gain 13 dB at 2 kHz on the trumpet, 30-36 on trombone, horn and tuba), the attack time is measured per instrument (horn about 79 ms at 220 Hz, trumpet 47, trombone 66, tuba 85; shorter up high). Velocity is the dynamic (`dyn` overrides it). Articulations: `/stab` (short, `stab_len` 0.22 s), `/sfz` (forte-piano then a crescendo), `/swell` or `/cresc`, `/dim`, `/rip` (a fast smear up into the note), `/fall` (drops off the end). Params: `players`, `width` (0.5 horns), `spread_ms`, `detune_cents`, `release`, `vibrato` (semitones; 0 for horns and trombones, a little on trumpet), `attack_scale`. Ranges as written: horns about F2-F5, trumpets E3-C6, trombones E2-C5, tuba D1-G3. Default level -12 dB. **Measured** (third-octave distance to the recordings over the bands within 30 dB of the loudest, median): horn 2.5 dB (the `polysynth` stand-in 6.0), trumpet 1.7 (5.7), trombone 3.7 (6.5), bass trombone 2.9 (9.9), tuba 6.3 (7.6); the model is the average of two collections that disagree on a few notes, where the stand-in can come out closer (6 of 45 notes).

**`woodwind`** (polyphonic, **stereo** field, one player): `instrument` `flute` (default), `oboe`, `clarinet`, `bassoon`; the same model, fitted on Iowa ff (one dynamic: softer notes darken by a hand-tuned 8 dB per octave above 1 kHz at `dyn` 0); breath noise; `vibrato` (flute 0.1, oboe 0.08 semitones by default). Third-octave distance to the recordings: flute 2.5 dB (`ney` 6.3), oboe 1.3, clarinet 1.6, bassoon 1.3 (`polysynth` 5.6-7.5). Default -10 dB.

**`timpani`** (polyphonic, mono): the head's air-loaded modes near 1 : 1.5 : 2 : 2.5 of the note (Rossing), their levels and decays fitted to seven VSCO-2-CE hits, a band layer for the dense upper modes and the stick (fitted closed-loop), a small pitch glide after the stroke. Write the notes F#2-G3. `/roll` makes a long note a single-stroke roll (`roll_rate` 18 per second), `/roll,cresc` a crescendo roll; `bright` (3: dB per octave of extra brightness at full velocity). Third-octave distance 4.1 dB (the `tom` stand-in 39). Default -6 dB.
**`bassdrum`** (drum): the concert bass drum (gran cassa), fitted to VSCO-2-CE: modes from 85 Hz (`tune`), `decay` (x the fitted decays), `beater` (0.3). Distance 2.9 dB (`toba` 17.3). **`taiko`** (drum): an ensemble of `players` (3) large drums at `tune` 72 Hz struck within `spread_ms` (12), `decay` 0.6 s; hand-tuned [H] (no recording to fit). **`suscymbal`** (pitch ignored): `mode` `swell` (default: a soft-mallet roll that crescendos over the note's length and rings on: end the note on the downbeat) or `hit` (a hard-mallet stroke); `decay` (5.9 s T60 at 1 kHz), `ring` (6 s); the spectra of both fitted closed-loop to VSCO-2-CE (distance: hit 3.8 dB, swell 1.6; a reversed `crash` 23.8). Known difference: the recorded drums fall 20 dB from their peak faster (timpani 440-720 ms, the model 880-1000; bass drum 102 ms, the model 340): their stroke transient stands further above the ring.

**Trailer effects**: **`braam`** (stereo): low brass (trombones an octave up, tuba) sfz into a swell, a distorted saw layer (`synth` 0.5) and a sine sub an octave down (`sub` 0.6), at the note's pitch (C2-G2) for its length (1-3 s). **`impact`** (drum): a sine boom falling from `boom_hz` (70) to half, a noise crack, a low rumble tail of `tail` s (2.5); send it to the hall. **`whoosh`** (stereo, pitch ignored): band noise sweeping up and back down (`peak` 0.6 of the note) and across (`pan_sweep` 0.8). **`shepard`** (stereo, pitch ignored): a Shepard-tone riser over the note: `voices` (6) octave-spaced tones climbing `octaves_per_s` (0.25) under a fixed spectral window (`direction` -1 falls). All hand-designed [H]: no CC0 recordings to fit.

**`hall`** (a return type: `"returns": {"hall": {"type": "hall", "predelay_ms": 20}}`): a large hall: early reflections (`taps` 14 per side between `itdg_ms` 20 and `er_ms` 80, darker above `er_lpf` 6 kHz, `er_level` 0.7) and a diffuse tail (the shimmer's 8-line delay network with no pitch path: `decay` 2.8 s, `size` 0.85, `damping` 0.35, `mod` 0.25), defaults `hpf` 80 Hz, `lpf` 10 kHz. Concert halls run 1.8-2.2 s with a first reflection near 20 ms (Beranek 2004); the cinematic brief asks for 2.5-3.5 s. **Measured** (Schroeder T20 per octave): 2.7-2.8 s from 125 Hz to 1 kHz, 2.6 at 2 kHz, 1.9 at 4 kHz, first reflection 20 ms; the algorithmic `reverb` at size 0.9 (the stand-in) runs 4.2-4.7 s with no early reflections (first energy at 25 ms). One hall return for the whole orchestra and choir puts the relay in one room.

**`vox2choir`** (monophonic line, **stereo**): a wordless choir section from `vox2`: `voices` (4) takes of the line, each its own seed, timing scatter (`spread_ms` 18), intonation (`detune_cents` 8) and place (`width` 0.6), vibrato narrowed (`vibrato` 0.2 by default: ensembles narrow their vibrato to blend); every `vox2` key works (`vowels`, `onset`, `rd`, `breath`...). One track instead of 6-12 `vox2` tracks by hand; under it a `choir` pad. No `singer` voice for a political subject (Lotte V's terms).

**`accordion`** (polyphonic, mono): free reeds in `ranks` (2: the second `musette` 12 cents sharp; 3 adds one as far flat), a reed spectrum, bellows `attack` (0.03 s) and `/swell`. Hand-tuned [H]: no CC0 accordion recordings in hand.

**Basses** (`strings_section` `"section": "basses"`): 4 players on a double-bass body fitted to 13 Iowa notes (E1-D3) the way the cello's was: 0.40 dB from the recordings on average (1/6-octave smoothed); third-octave distance 5.0 dB (celli an octave up plus `sub`: 14.2). Spiccato (`/sp`) notes are damped by the bow `sp_stop_ms` (750) after their onset, so an isolated spiccato note falls 20 dB in 455-645 ms (the recorded solo bass: 270-720). **Fast ostinati work**: 16ths at 100-140 bpm measure 5-8 dB of note-to-note contrast on the basses (the recorded notes overlap-added at the same rate: 1-6 dB), celli 11 dB with `/sp` and 7 without, violins 15-18 with `/sp` and 8 without.

### PATTERN
A pattern is a set of parts for several tracks, `bars` long. Sections loop patterns.
```json
"beatA": {"bars": 1,
  "kick":  "X.....x...x.....",
  "snare": "....X..o....X...",
  "hat":   "x.x.x.x.x.x.x.xr"}
```
- **Drum steps**: the string length / bars = grid (16 = 16ths, 12 = 8th-note triplets, 24 = 16th triplets, 32 = 32nds). `|` and spaces are ignored.
  `X` accent 1.0, `x` normal 0.82, `o` ghost 0.45, `g` faint 0.3, `1`-`9` exact velocity, `.` rest.
  Rolls inside one step: `r` 2 hits, `t` 3, `q` 4, `s` 6, `z` 8. Uppercase (`R`, `T`, `Q`, `S`, `Z`) = crescendo roll.
- **Notes** (808, bass, lead, any instrument as a melody): `"step:note:len"` tokens, step and len in 16ths from the pattern start.
  `"0:F1:6 6:F1:2 10:Ab1:6~ 16:Db2:8"`. Suffix `~` on len = glide into this note from the previous one, `!` = accent, `.` = soft; an optional 4th field sets velocity (`"8:C5:2:0.5"`). Articulations go after the length behind a `/` (`"8:G4:2/g-1,acc"`); only `violin` reads them (see "Lăutar band"), other instruments ignore them.
- **Chords** (polyphonic tracks): `{"chords": "Fm9:16 Dbmaj7:16 Bbm9:8 C7b9:8", "style": "sustain", "register": "Ab4", "voicing": "keys", "vel": 0.7}`
  Lengths in 16ths (16 = one bar). `-:8` = rest. Slash chords work (`Ab/C`). The hand voice-leads between chords.
  style: `sustain`, `strum` (strum_ms), `stabs` (with `"rhythm": "x..x..x...x....."` per bar, `stab_len`; the string length sets the grid like drum steps, so 12 characters = 8th-note triplets), `arp` (`rate` in 16ths, `pattern` up/down/updown/random, `octaves`).
  voicing: `keys` (left-hand root plus right-hand close voicing), `close`, `spread`. Instrument shapes (no voice leading; the lowest note lands within a tritone of `register`): `root` (just the root: bass lines), `octave` (root + octave), `power` (root, fifth, octave: guitar power chord), `guitar` (an open-chord shape: root, fifth, octave, third and any 7th/9th above, up to 6 notes).
  Strumming (guitars): stabs accept `"strum": "down" | "up" | "alt"`, `strum_ms` (time across the strings, default 12) and `strum_grid` (8 or 16: with `alt`, hits on the grid's on-beats strum down, the others up). Up-strokes start on the top string, are lighter, and skip the two lowest strings of big chords. `"style": "strum"` takes `"strum_dir"` the same way.
  Qualities: "", m, maj7, m7, 7, m9, maj9, 9, add9, madd9, 6, m6, sus2, sus4, 7sus4, dim, dim7, m7b5, aug, 7b9, 7#9, m7b9, 11, m11, 13, maj13, 6/9, maj7#11, 7b13, mmaj7, m(maj9), 5
- **Texture**: `"vinyl": "on"`

### SECTION
```json
{"name": "hook1", "bars": 8, "play": ["keysA", "beatB", "bassA"],
 "fill": {"pattern": "fillA"},                       // replaces those tracks in the last bar(s) of the section
 "bpm": [100, 130],                                  // optional: this section's tempo (a number, or [from, to] = a ramp)
 "auto": {"keys": {"lpf": [600, 8000], "hpf": [20, 800], "gain_db": [-6, 0], "mute": false}},   // ramps across the section
 "auto": {"pad": {"inst": {"detune": [12, 30], "lfo.0.depth": [0.1, 0.6]}, "fx": {"shimmer": {"mix": [0.2, 0.5]}}},
          "return:space": {"amount": [0.3, 0.9], "gain_db": [-6, 0]}},             // sounds that move (below)
 "enter": {"impact": 0.8, "crash": 0.6, "downlifter": 2},                       // on the first downbeat (downlifter in beats)
 "seam": "cut",                                      // optional: a deliberate hard cut INTO this section (listen --seams won't flag it; say why in the journal)
 "tension": "flat",                                  // optional: a deliberate plateau in a respiro or build (listen --tension won't flag it; say why)
 "loop": "long",                                     // optional: a deliberately long unchanged loop (listen --loops won't flag it; say why)
 "exit":  {"riser": 4, "reverse": 2, "rise": 16,                                // ending exactly at the section end (beats); rise = pitched build riser
           "cut": {"tracks": ["kick", "808"], "beats": 1},                       // drop-out before the next section ("all" for every track)
           "lpf": {"tracks": ["keys"], "beats": 4, "to_hz": 400},
           "tapestop": 2, "silence": 1}}
```
Note: an exit `cut` only silences its own window; 808 and reverb tails can ring on into the next section. For true silence in a section, also add `"auto": {"<track>": {"mute": true}}` there.

**The brake: stop-and-go** (since 2026-11-27, opt-in). `"tapestop": 2` stops the whole mix over the section's last two beats and holds it there until the line. The dict form `"tapestop": {"beats": 1, "hold": 1, "level": 0.5, "curve": 1.3, "fade_ms": 12}` brakes the whole premaster (every track and return) to a halt over `beats`, ending `hold` beats **before** the section end; the level falls with the speed (gain = speed^`level`, as a tape head's output does; 0 keeps the level) and fades out over `fade_ms` at the halt, so it never holds a DC value. The `hold` window is left alone: whatever the project leaves playing there plays at normal pitch. For a held breath of silence, add a `cut` of `hold` beats on every track except what should launch the "go", and mute the returns in that window (a reverb or delay tail would come back at normal pitch after the halt): `auto` `"return:<name>": {"gain_db": [...]}` with one value per 16th, -90 over the hold. What launches the go plays in the hold: an acid revving up (notes climbing with `~` glides, its `cutoff` per 16th flung open), toms or a snare speeding up (a 48-step string gives 16th triplets), an `exit` `rise` of one beat (the riser sits inside the hold). Keep noise instruments (claps, snares) at 16ths or slower in rolls: a 909 clap struck every 32nd fuses into an 80-100 Hz flutter of low-mid noise that reads as a wet "brrrt" (Redline v037, the owner's ear). Example: sessions/019-fast/build.py, `sg()`.

**Automating sounds** (since 2026-11-25): besides `lpf`, `hpf`, `gain_db` and `mute`, a track's `auto` can hold
- `"inst": {"<param>": value}`: the instrument's own parameters, any number in its `inst` (`cutoff`, `detune`, `drift`, `env`, `fdecay`, `reso`, `mix`, `knob`, `decay`, `accent`, `tune`...), with a dotted path into lists and sub-dicts (`"lfo.0.depth"`, `"lfo.0.rate"`, `"osc.1.level"`, `"osc2.detune"`, `"env2.depth"`). Each note (or drum hit) takes the value at its **onset**: a pad chord held for two bars keeps one value, so for a slow change inside long notes, retrigger them (stabs of a bar) or move a filter with `lpf`. It reaches every polyphonic instrument, every drum, `acid` (`cutoff`, `env_mod`, `reso`, `decay`, `accent`, `drive`) and, on a stack, every layer (`"auto": {"<track>": {"inst": ...}}`) or one layer (`"auto": {"<track>.<layer>": {"inst": ...}}`); other monophonic voices (808, bass, lead, sub, vox, singer...) and textures ignore it with a warning.
- `"fx": {"<fx id or type>": {"<param>": value}}`: the parameters of a `shimmer` (`amount`, `mix`), `granular` (`density`, `grain_ms`, `scan`, `position_ms`, `spray_ms`, `pitch`, `pitch_spread`, `mix`) or `suppress` (`depth`) in that track's, layer's (`"<track>.<layer>"`) or bus's `fx`, per sample; give the fx an `"id"` when the chain has two of a type.
- `"return:<name>": {...}`: a `shimmer` or `granular` return's parameters (as above), plus `gain_db`, `lpf`, `hpf`, `mute` on its output.

Values: a number (held through the section), `[from, to]` (a ramp across the section), `[a, b, c, ...]` (evenly spaced points: `[0.1, 0.8, 0.1]` rises and falls), with `"log"` last for a geometric ramp (frequencies: `[200, 3200, "log"]`). The track-level `lpf`, `hpf` and `gain_db` take the same forms (since 2026-11-26: a trailing `"log"` used to stop the render; their filter sweeps are always geometric, so it changes nothing there, and three or more points sweep through each in turn). Outside the sections that name a parameter it has the track's own value, so a move that should carry on is written in the next section too. Deterministic: the same project renders the same. Long-form movement the trance research asks for (docs/TRANCE.md G 9 and I 5): `drift`, LFO depth and rate, `detune` growing through a breakdown, a supersaw's `mix`, an acid's `cutoff` and `decay` ridden over 16 bars, a shimmer's `amount` and a cloud's `density` rising into the drop.
Section names must be unique. Bars per section should be multiples of 4 (2 for short breaks).

**Tempo changes** (`"bpm"` in a section): a number sets that section's tempo; `[from, to]` ramps the tempo across the section (linear in beats: an accelerando or a ritardando). Sections without it play at the project `bpm`, so a ramp that should continue needs the next section to start where it ended. Everything the hand plays (steps, swing, humanizing, rolls, note lengths) follows the local tempo, and so do the section's `exit` and `enter` lengths in beats. Two things stay on the project `bpm`: the shared delay (`mix.delay.beats`, and `delay` fx) and the `gate` fx grid, so don't rely on them in tempo-changed sections. The render prints each section's tempo, `listen --bars` and the zoom grid follow the real bar positions, and section names work as before. Only projects that set a section `bpm` are affected.

**Other meters:** a pattern bar is always four beats of the project `bpm`. For 2/4 music, one pattern bar holds two bars of 2/4 (steps 0-7 and 8-15); write sections in those double bars (the ears' bar numbers count them too). For 3/8 or 7/16 feels, see the step-grid notes above (12 or 24 characters per bar for triplets).

## Tips from the bench
- Trance: `"swing": 0.5` and `humanize_ms` 0-1 on drums and bass (machine-tight is the style). Sidechain bass, chords and pads to the kick (bass depth 0.8-0.9 / release 80-110 ms, chords 0.5-0.7 / 140-180 ms). A build: snare roll pattern, `"exit": {"rise": 16, "riser": 8, "cut": {"tracks": "all", "beats": 1}}` and `auto` `hpf` sweeps on the music. The breakdown has no kick: leave the kick's pattern out (a muted pattern still sends sidechain triggers).
- Pop rock: `humanize_ms` 4-8 on kit pieces, `push_ms` +3..+6 on the snare for a laid-back backbeat, `room` on kit pieces plus a shared reverb send. Guitars and bass are thin at 100-300 Hz: add body with `eq`/`lowshelf` there before mastering (the `rock_2015` target expects about +2 to +3.5 dB at 100-250 Hz relative to 1 kHz, and +4 dB at 80 Hz; the older `rock` curve asked for +5 to +7). Kick and bass guitar want different homes (kick body 60-80 Hz with `eq`, bass growl 150-250 Hz).
- Melodic and pad instruments sit far below drums by default; raise `gain_db` in steps of 2-3 dB.
- Hats live and die by `dyn`, `humanize_ms`, swing and rolls. The 808 should almost always sidechain to the kick or follow it.
- High-pass melodic tracks (`hpf` 120-200) so the 808 owns the low end.
- `lofi` plus `send_reverb` on keys gives the "sampled record" feel; `width` 0.3-0.7 on pads and keys opens the stereo field (the default is quite mono).
- For per-section track levels, read `stems_rms` in `renders/vNNN.meta.json` (one RMS value per 0.05 s per track); see `sessions/004-hustle/levels.py` or `sessions/005-fuck-war/stems.py`.
- The ears' "no contrast" warning only compares loudness, band levels and onset density; a purely rhythmic or textural change between sections can trip it. Check with a zoom before acting on it.
- The key detector reads modal material (Dorian, Romanian minor, Hijaz) as the nearest major/minor key; trust your own mode. In the zoom timing roll, row labels can look shifted by one track when several drum tracks share a hit; identify rows by their timing.
- `gain_db` acts before the track's fx: on tracks with heavy drive, saturation flattens the change; use a `gain` fx at the end of the chain to change the post-drive level.
- A render is about 20 s; an overview listen about 25 s. Zoom listens are faster. Projects with many acoustic cymbals or long distorted guitar parts render slower (the first cymbal hits are costly; later ones are cached).
