# Orchestration: how Spectre is run

This is the operator's playbook: how the orchestrating agent (the one Robert talks to) runs producer sessions, keeps them alive, delivers results and maintains the website. A new orchestrator session should read this together with [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) (the always-on rules and the routes into [docs/agents/](agents/)) before doing anything, so no working agreement is lost between sessions. For the current state (every session, open decisions, the engine and knowledge map), read [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md).

## The owner's standing rules
These were set by Robert (the repo owner) and apply until he changes them:
1. **The artist is called Spectre, everywhere.** The producer persona, the prompts, the session notes, the journals, the website. (An earlier persona name was retired; don't reintroduce it.)
2. **Never let a producer session stay stopped.** Check running sessions about every 15 minutes. If one stops without finishing, fix the cause if there is one, then resume it from its saved files. Always resume.
3. **Record lessons after every genre switch** in `docs/LESSONS.md` (session entry plus the "Across genres" section), and save new genre research as `docs/<GENRE>.md`.
4. **Keep the website's track list up to date** with every finished track, newest first.
5. **Sessions may run in parallel lanes**, one folder each.
6. **Delegate long UI work to a sub-agent** so the orchestrator's thread stays free.
7. **Genre settings come from research, not guesses.** Every per-genre value (length, form, tempo range, structure, mastering target) cites a source in the genre table below or in docs/<GENRE>.md. A value without a source is marked as hand-tuned and gets researched before the next session in that genre.
8. **Master like the industry does, per genre.** For every new genre, research its mastering practice thoroughly before its first master: loudness of current commercial releases (integrated LUFS, short-term peaks), true-peak ceilings, dynamics (PLR/LRA), tonal balance measured from modern references, low-end mono point, stereo width, and genre habits (e.g. club pumping, rock density). Aim the genre's configuration at what the industry does, with sources; where the engine can't match it yet, say so and why.
9. **Personal details stay out of the repo.** Anything personal Robert shares (family, who a track is for, life events) is context for shaping a brief, never content: don't write it into PROMPT.md, journals, READMEs, LESSONS, commit messages, the website or handoff notes. Translate it into the feeling and the brief, nothing more.
10. **Voices rest on research, split into general and per-language knowledge.** Speech and singing synthesis knowledge lives in docs/voice/: GENERAL.md for what applies to every language (speech production, prosody, coarticulation, singing technique, text-to-singing timing, synthesis methods), and one profile per language in docs/voice/languages/<code>.md (phonology, stress and rhythm, linking, vowel/consonant behaviour in singing, the singing voices available). Prefer peer-reviewed sources where they exist. Whenever a song is meant to be sung in a language without a profile, research and write that profile before the session starts. Voice decisions (how a voice is synthesized, timed, mixed and mastered) are made from research and measurement, not from Robert's suggestions: he is the ear tester and says whether it sounds right; his notes are symptoms to diagnose ("it sounds like reverb" is a hypothesis to test), and where research disagrees, follow the research and tell him why (Robert, 2026-11-24). Choirs and ensembles get their own research (docs/voice/MIXING.md, "Choir and ensemble voices").
11. **Genre research beats brief numbers that don't fit.** When a brief gives a concrete parameter (tempo, key, length, meter) that contradicts the researched genre configuration, don't follow it blindly: flag the conflict, check for a plausible reading (e.g. a tempo counted in half notes), and otherwise let the genre research and the producer's judgement decide. Note the choice in the journal.
12. **Always clean up after yourself (Robert, 2026-11-24).** Every agent deletes its own scratch (files in /tmp, test renders, downloads it no longer needs) when its task ends. At delivery, delete the session's render WAVs (keep `renders/*.json`, `*.mp3`, `*.png`: every version stays reproducible from its project JSON) and the producer's scratch outputs (listen/zoom dumps like `*.txt` and `z_*.png`, `__pycache__`); keep the journal, README assets and the helper scripts needed to rebuild. The workspace disk is small (a session writes about 2.5 GB of WAVs).
13. **Every production is unique; quotes are ingredients.** Quoting existing material (a public-domain melody, an anthem, a carol) is fine when its licence allows it, but the track as a whole must be Spectre's own: its own arrangement, harmony, form, sound and dramaturgy, never a cover or a re-arrangement of the quoted piece (Robert, 2026-11-24).
14. **Quoted melodies come segmented by phrase.** When a brief quotes an existing melody (anthem, folk tune, carol), the prep work verifies it from scores/recordings and writes it into the genre or culture doc as engine blocks plus a phrase table (each phrase from its own upbeat to its last note), so producers quote whole phrases and never end on the next line's pickup (Session 011's intro ended on a stray "bar-").
15. **Voices are instruments, not songs.** Spectre makes instrumental tracks. A voice, sung words included, is used like an instrument: short lines, hooks, calls, a refrain fragment or a choir texture woven into the arrangement, never a full sung song with verses carried by a lead vocal. The engine's voice work serves that role.
16. **Layered, carved and current (Robert, 2026-11-25, after a professional trance producer's feedback).** Every genre, existing and future, is produced with modern layering (a part is often several layers with distinct roles: sub, body, bite, top, air, transient, width), with each instrument carved by a researched EQ map so layers complement instead of compete, and mastered toward an era-appropriate target (what the genre sounds like now, not 1950-2010 averages). Genre research for any new genre must produce the layer roles, the EQ map and the era assessment (docs/PRODUCTION.md). Clarity is measured (masking between stems), not assumed.
17. **It's an experiment.** Report misses honestly, including in the README and lessons.
18. **Producer model: Claude Opus 5.5 by default (Robert, 2026-11-25).** Launch producer and remix agents on Opus unless Robert names another model for that session; record the model in the README and `tracks.json` as before.
19. **Never start a session without what the producer needs (Robert, 2026-11-25).** When the genre research or the brief shows an instrument, effect or engine control the track needs and the engine lacks it, build it first (engine rules, MANUAL, smoke test, an A/B for Robert's ear where it changes the sound), then write the brief. Don't ask the producer to work around a missing piece.
20. **No era named means the modern era (Robert, 2026-11-25).** A brief that names no era aims at the genre's modern era (its current practice; docs/PRODUCTION.md section 5, item 10). Within it the producer chooses the subdivision (sub-era, strand, region or scene) that best fits the feeling, and says in the journal which one and why.
21. **Nothing reaches the site without Robert's approval by ear (Robert, 2026-11-25).** A new track, and a new version of a published one (a remix, a remaster, a re-render), goes to Robert first; he listens and approves it in the chat before it is published. That covers its master upload (the site plays `<uuid>/rendition.mp3` straight from the bucket, so an upload is a release) and its line in `site/data/tracks.json`. `tools/upload_track.py` refuses a new track or a changed master without `--owner-approved`; give that flag only for the exact master he approved. Uploads that don't change the audio (journal, README, project files) need no approval.

## Step 0, before any session: engine setup (owner's rule, 2026-11-25)
Before working on any track (a new session, a resume, a remix, a master), the orchestrator makes sure everything the engine and its measurements need is installed:
1. Run `tools/setup.sh`. It installs the requirements, downloads every resource in `engine/third_party.json` that has a direct link from its official source (size and sha256 checked), pulls what is still missing from the optional private mirror if its environment variables are set, and ends with `engine_assets.py status`. It exits non-zero while a required resource is missing.
2. Resolve every resource `install` reports as `todo`, optional ones included (an optional entry only waits if the official search really finds nothing; say so in the manifest's `search_hint`). Read its reason: *download interrupted* (429, 5xx, timeout: `install` already retried with backoff) means the link works, so run `install <id>` again later before anything else; *no direct link recorded yet* means the official source is known (the entry's `page`) and only the file's URL is missing, so never report it as "no official link": find the file there and record its URL; *link dead* or a size / sha256 mismatch means the source moved. Where the GitHub API is blocked (403) but git works, list a repository with `git clone --filter=blob:none --no-checkout <repo>` and `git ls-tree -r --name-only HEAD`, and record each file as `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/<owner>/<repo>/<commit>/<url-quoted path>` (pinned to a commit). If a link fails: search the internet for the official source (the entry's `search_hint`; the author's own site or release first), check that the licence and the files match (licence text, size, sha256, folder layout), install it (`python3 tools/engine_assets.py import <zip>` for a zip, or place the files where the entry says), and **update the entry's URL** (and size and sha256 if it is a new official version with the same licence) in `engine/third_party.json`. Give producer and helper agents the links from the manifest; tell them to do the same search if a link fails.
3. Fall back to the mirror (`python3 tools/engine_assets.py pull`) only if nothing works. Its settings and keys come from the environment; ask the owner.
4. `python3 tools/engine_assets.py status --verify` must end without a missing required resource. Report every entry as installed (official source or mirror), retried, link found and recorded, or not found after searching (where you looked). Record every fix in `engine/third_party.json` (link, `search_hint`) so the next setup needs no extra information.

## Starting a new session
0. **If the genre is new, research and set up its configuration first, before anything else.** With sources for every value: typical length (and which cut: radio, album, extended), form and section lengths, tempo range, groove and meter, how often the arrangement changes, what the instrumental must allow (rap, voice, DJ mixing), the genre's mastering practice per standing rule 8 (loudness, true peak, dynamics, modern tonal balance, mono point, width), loudness norms and the tonal balance for mastering (add a `--target` curve in engine/master.py if no existing one fits, derived from published measurements, not guessed; see docs/MASTERING.md for how the existing ones were derived), and the craft schools for `{{HAND}}`. Add the genre's entry to `engine/genres.json`, its row and sources to "Genre conventions" below, and note anything still unsourced as hand-tuned. Since 2026-11-25 the genre brief must also contain the items of the checklist in docs/PRODUCTION.md, section 5 (layer roles per part, a per-instrument EQ map, how layers are glued, and an era-appropriate master target with its provenance): standing rule 16.
1. If the genre is new: research it (instruments, rhythms, modes, the modern reference artists, and the cultural context of the subject), write `docs/<GENRE>.md`, and build the genre's signature instruments in the engine first (generic synths can't fake them). Check them with spectrogram plots and a tiny project, document them in `docs/MANUAL.md`, smoke-test with `examples/engine-test.json`, commit. Engine edits while another session is rendering must be additive and written atomically (temp file plus rename).
2. Create `sessions/NNN-name/PROMPT.md` from `prompts/producer.md` (or from the latest session's PROMPT.md, which carries the newest lessons). Always include: the persona (Spectre), the brief, the **nearest wrong feelings** for this brief and what separates them, the genre doc to read, the lessons, the studio rules and the final report format. If Robert lets the agent choose tempo or feeling, say so and ask it to commit in its first journal entry.
3. Start a general-purpose sub-agent with a short prompt: "Read sessions/NNN-name/PROMPT.md in full and follow it exactly. If journal.md already exists, this is a resumed session: read the journal and helper scripts first, listen to the latest render before your next move, continue numbering. Your final reply must be the report described at the end of PROMPT.md." Because the full brief lives in PROMPT.md, the same short prompt resumes a stopped session.
4. Schedule a check-in (a one-shot scheduled message back into the session) for 15 minutes.

There is no default genre. Each value is backed by the sources below the table (or by docs/<GENRE>.md); add a row, a `genres.json` entry and sources when a new genre arrives.

## Keeping sessions alive
`sessions/<id>-<name>/` is the local working area (published sessions live in the S3 bucket, docs/agents/track-storage.md); the paths below are local and unchanged. To resume or remix a session whose folder is not in the workspace, restore it first with `python3 tools/upload_track.py --fetch <id>`.
- At each check: count `renders/v*.wav` (excluding master, premaster and solo files), note the newest render's time, look for `final.mp3`.
- Progressing: schedule the next check in 15 minutes. With several sessions running, one combined check is fine.
- No final report and no new render for 10+ minutes, or the agent is reported stopped: start a fresh agent with the resume prompt above.
- A known cause of stops: interrupting the orchestrator's own turn mid-work can take background agents down. New messages queued while the orchestrator works are safe.

## Delivering a finished session
1. **Verify:** final.mp3 exists and has the expected length; `project.json` equals `renders/vNNN.project.json`; the master report (`renders/vNNN.master.report.json`) shows the session's genre, about the target LUFS, true peak at or under -1 dBTP, no warnings (and ideally an empty `eq_at_limit`), `master_version` 2, `top_end_max_abs_dev_db` about 1.5 or less, and the phone check (`phone.out`) without notes; sections flagged by `listen --phone` go back to the producer as mix fixes.
2. **Send** the track (named after its title) and `journal.md` to Robert.
3. **Tidy the session folder:** rename final.mp3 to `<title-slug>-mastered.mp3`; copy `renders/vNNN.master.png` to `master-tonal-balance.png` and the report to `master-report.json`; save the overview ear as `ear-overview.png`; remove `__pycache__`; write `README.md` (table, arrangement, how it carries the feeling, files, Spectre's note; see earlier sessions). If the track uses `singer` with `"source": "svs"`, the README (and the site's track notes) must carry a **Credits** line with each voice's `credit` from `engine/voices.json` (e.g. "Vocals: Hoshino Hanami (DiffSinger voicebank by Lotte V, lottev.moe), Potluck Vocoder by tigermeat"); never use a Lotte V voice in a track with a political subject (docs/voice/VOICES.md).
4. **Repo docs:** add the row to the README results table, the session entry to `docs/LESSONS.md` and update "Across genres"; fix or document any engine quirks the producer reported (smoke-test fixes).
5. **Wait for Robert's approval by ear** (standing rule 21) of the exact master sent in step 2. Nothing goes on the site before it; a change he asks for means a new version, sent again.
6. **Website:** add the track to `site/data/tracks.json`, the canonical list (newest first; `id`, `artist: "Spectre"`, `title`, `file: "NNN-slug.mp3"`, `duration` from ffprobe, `bpm`, `model` (the producer agent's model, e.g. "Claude Opus 5.5", shown beside the tempo), `genre` (short, e.g. "Latvian folk trap"), `style`, `feeling`, 2-3 `colors` that fit the track; the page mutes them automatically). `file` is the track's slug plus `.mp3` (the slug is its URL); leave out `uuid`, publishing adds it.
7. **Publish** (the one step that sends the track to the S3 bucket; docs/agents/track-storage.md): `python3 tools/upload_track.py NNN --owner-approved`, only after step 5, with the workspace's read-write key in the environment. It gives the track its uuid in `site/data/tracks.json` (if new), uploads every new or changed publishable file of the session to `<uuid>/...` (`rendition.mp3`, `journal.md`, `readme.md`, `prompt.md`, `project.json`, `master-report.json`, `master-tonal-balance.png`, `ear-overview.png`, helper scripts under `tools/`) and verifies each one. **Only these go to the bucket:** the tool refuses any track not in `tracks.json` and never takes renders, WAVs, trial masters, A/B excerpts, listen dumps or other scratch, which stay local and are deleted in the clean-up. A remix or remaster of a published track is published the same way (same uuid; only the changed files are uploaded). Then check that `cd site && npm run build` passes; the website picks the track up on its next deploy. *Without a bucket* (no `SPECTRE_S3_*` variables, e.g. a fork or a fresh clone), the same command uploads nothing and exits 0 after the local checks, printing "No S3 bucket configured: nothing uploaded. Deliver these files to the user in the chat:" with the mastered MP3 and the other publishable files: the publish step is then sending those files to the user in the chat (SendUserFile or the platform's equivalent). The session folder stays local (it is the only copy; skip the deletion of published folders in the clean-up), and the site plays its master from it (site/README.md, "Audio").
8. **Clean up** (standing rule 12): delete the session's render WAVs and scratch outputs, and anything the agents left in /tmp.
9. **Commit and push** `site/data/tracks.json` and the cross-track files (README results table, LESSONS, docs, engine fixes) by path; the session folder is never committed (`sessions/` is in .gitignore since 2026-11-25): its files are in the bucket, or, without a bucket, with the user and in the local folder (end commit messages with the session's attribution lines).

### Website design decisions (agreed with Robert, keep unless he changes them)
- Dark, ghostly world: near-black blue, cold moonlight, a hooded apparition in mist, ice-cyan glow. Tones and haze, not crisp texture.
- Badge: a tonal hooded wraith carved out by cold backlight, eye glints, eclipse-like corona; also the favicon. No cartoon features, no line drawing.
- Title: SPECTRE as dark smoky letters against the same backlight, faded (~80% opacity), with an occasional horror-film blur-and-shake glitch every 8-20 s.
- No descriptions on the page: no tagline, no track style line, no footer prose. Allowed: the GitHub badge top right, the footer line "© 2026 Robert Ontiu", "BPM · genre" under each track, and a share button next to the playing title.
- Layout: top half brand banner; bottom half a blurred field of the playing track's colours; the player in the middle, raised over the top, dissolving into the bottom. Inside the player: bar visualizer, controls, playlist ("Spectre - Title", duration, BPM below), newest first.
- No EQ: tracks play exactly as mastered (removed at Robert's request).
- Track colours lead in the bottom field (moderately muted), bleed lightly into the top, and tint the mist. Fuck Every War uses the Ukrainian flag colours.
- Mist: several fog layers moving visibly (12-28 s passes) across both halves, denser at the top.
- One fixed full-viewport background stack (no section backgrounds, no clipping), so there are no seams; verify seams with a row-by-row luminance script, not by eye.
- Performance: the soft mist, fog and track-colour field are drawn on two small low-resolution canvases (one under, one over the apparition) at 30 fps with time-based motion, paused when the tab is hidden; track colours glide in OKLab there. Other moving parts move with transform and opacity only; never animate filters or blur. No DOM layers are created, hidden or shown on a track change. Card accents are `@property` colours with CSS transitions (no per-frame JS). The fixed background never resizes with the mobile address bar. Never read layout inside an animation loop. Everything calms down under prefers-reduced-motion.
- Touch: a vertical swipe over any slider scrolls the page; seek and volume drag only on a clearly horizontal gesture.
