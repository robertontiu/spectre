# Orchestration: how Spectre is run

This is the operator's playbook: how the orchestrating agent (the one Robert talks to) runs producer sessions, keeps them alive, delivers results and maintains the website. A new orchestrator session should read this together with AGENTS.md before doing anything, so no working agreement is lost between sessions.

## The owner's standing rules
These were set by Robert (the repo owner) and apply until he changes them:
1. **The artist is called Spectre, everywhere.** The producer persona, the prompts, the session notes, the journals, the website. (An earlier persona name was retired; don't reintroduce it.)
2. **Never let a producer session stay stopped.** Check running sessions about every 15 minutes. If one stops without finishing, fix the cause if there is one, then resume it from its saved files. Always resume.
3. **Record lessons after every genre switch** in `docs/LESSONS.md` (session entry plus the "Across genres" section), and save new genre research as `docs/<GENRE>.md`.
4. **Keep the website's track list up to date** with every finished track, newest first.
5. **Sessions may run in parallel lanes**, one folder each.
6. **Delegate long UI work to a sub-agent** so the orchestrator's thread stays free.
7. **Genre settings come from research, not guesses.** Every per-genre value (length, form, tempo range, structure, mastering target) cites a source in the genre table below or in docs/<GENRE>.md. A value without a source is marked as hand-tuned and gets researched before the next session in that genre.
8. **Master like the industry does, per genre.** For every new genre, research its mastering practice thoroughly before its first master: loudness of current commercial releases (integrated LUFS, short-term peaks), true-peak ceilings, dynamics (PLR/LRA), tonal balance measured from modern references, low-end mono point, stereo width, and genre habits (e.g. club pumping, rock density). Aim the genre's configuration at what the industry does, with sources; where the engine can't match it yet, say so and why.
9. **Personal details stay out of the repo.** Anything personal Robert shares (family, who a track is for, life events) is context for shaping a brief, never content: don't write it into PROMPT.md, journals, READMEs, LESSONS, commit messages, the website or handoff notes. Translate it into the feeling and the brief, nothing more.
10. **Voices rest on research, split into general and per-language knowledge.** Speech and singing synthesis knowledge lives in docs/voice/: GENERAL.md for what applies to every language (speech production, prosody, coarticulation, singing technique, text-to-singing timing, synthesis methods), and one profile per language in docs/voice/languages/<code>.md (phonology, stress and rhythm, linking, vowel/consonant behaviour in singing, the TTS voices available). Prefer peer-reviewed sources where they exist. Whenever a song is meant to be sung in a language without a profile, research and write that profile before the session starts.
11. **Genre research beats brief numbers that don't fit.** When a brief gives a concrete parameter (tempo, key, length, meter) that contradicts the researched genre configuration, don't follow it blindly: flag the conflict, check for a plausible reading (e.g. a tempo counted in half notes), and otherwise let the genre research and the producer's judgement decide. Note the choice in the journal.
12. **Voices are instruments, not songs.** Spectre makes instrumental tracks. A voice, sung words included, is used like an instrument: short lines, hooks, calls, a refrain fragment or a choir texture woven into the arrangement, never a full sung song with verses carried by a lead vocal. The engine's voice work serves that role.
13. **It's an experiment.** Report misses honestly, including in the README and lessons.

## Starting a new session
0. **If the genre is new, research and set up its configuration first, before anything else.** With sources for every value: typical length (and which cut: radio, album, extended), form and section lengths, tempo range, groove and meter, how often the arrangement changes, what the instrumental must allow (rap, voice, DJ mixing), the genre's mastering practice per standing rule 8 (loudness, true peak, dynamics, modern tonal balance, mono point, width), loudness norms and the tonal balance for mastering (add a `--target` curve in engine/master.py if no existing one fits, derived from published measurements, not guessed; see docs/MASTERING.md for how the existing ones were derived), and the craft schools for `{{HAND}}`. Add the genre's entry to `engine/genres.json`, its row and sources to "Genre conventions" below, and note anything still unsourced as hand-tuned.
1. If the genre is new: research it (instruments, rhythms, modes, the modern reference artists, and the cultural context of the subject), write `docs/<GENRE>.md`, and build the genre's signature instruments in the engine first (generic synths can't fake them). Check them with spectrogram plots and a tiny project, document them in `docs/MANUAL.md`, smoke-test with `examples/engine-test.json`, commit. Engine edits while another session is rendering must be additive and written atomically (temp file plus rename).
2. Create `sessions/NNN-name/PROMPT.md` from `prompts/producer.md` (or from the latest session's PROMPT.md, which carries the newest lessons). Always include: the persona (Spectre), the brief, the **nearest wrong feelings** for this brief and what separates them, the genre doc to read, the lessons, the studio rules and the final report format. If Robert lets the agent choose tempo or feeling, say so and ask it to commit in its first journal entry.
3. Start a general-purpose sub-agent with a short prompt: "Read sessions/NNN-name/PROMPT.md in full and follow it exactly. If journal.md already exists, this is a resumed session: read the journal and helper scripts first, listen to the latest render before your next move, continue numbering. Your final reply must be the report described at the end of PROMPT.md." Because the full brief lives in PROMPT.md, the same short prompt resumes a stopped session.
4. Schedule a check-in (a one-shot scheduled message back into the session) for 15 minutes.

## Genre conventions (fill the producer template from these)
Lengths and forms follow the genre, not one rule. Each genre has a configuration in `engine/genres.json` (mastering target and settings, plus length, form, tempo and role) and a brief with its sources; `studio.py master --genre <name>` takes every mastering value from it. There is no default genre. Each value is backed by the sources below the table (or by docs/<GENRE>.md); add a row, a `genres.json` entry and sources when a new genre arrives.

The form is craft (docs/CRAFT.md), unsourced.
- Trance: trance tracks run 6-8 minutes ([Mixgraph trance guide](https://www.mixgraph.io/mixing-guide/trance)) and club versions about 8 minutes against 3-minute radio edits ([Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/the-lost-art-of-long-tracks/)); section template ([BeatKey](https://beatkey.pages.dev/how-to-make-trance-music)); median 138 bpm over 25,494 tracks ([Mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/bpm-for/trance)); instruments added or removed every 4, 8, 16 or 32 bars ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trance_music)). Context: the Beatport Top 100 averaged 5:53 but excluded trance ([EDMProd](https://www.edmprod.com/beatport-analysis/)); extended mixes of all club genres ran a median 4:31 against 3:31 for radio edits ([Mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/glossary/intro)). No measured trance-length median was reachable (Beatport and Discogs block automated access).
- Songs: the average song in the 2020s runs 3:15, down from 4:14 in the 1990s (Washington Post via [HuffPost](https://huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/pop-songs-are-shorter-now_uk_66b0d65de4b022eb04aa13cb)); Billboard Hot 100 #1 hits averaged 3:30 in 2024, with the first chorus at 0:45 ([Hit Songs Deconstructed](https://hitsongsdeconstructed.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/The-Hit-Songs-Deconstructed-1-Hit-Focus-Report-Highlights.pdf)); 8-bar sections ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/song-sections-explained-intro-verse-chorus-middle8-outro-tag-bridge)); the tempo range is only partly sourced (no pop-rock dataset).
- Joc țigănesc (full table, sources and the mastering decision in docs/JOC_TIGANESC.md, researched 2026-11-24): length from seven Taraf de Haïdouks recordings on AcousticBrainz, 2:51-7:14, median 5:21, single dance tunes at the short end (e.g. [Sîrba, 3:19](https://acousticbrainz.org/4ae7b894-a617-44ec-a017-0b1a47325f59)); form and tempo from the reference score's practice ladder (one 24-bar tune at quarter = 50, 70, 90, 110), the Muntenian "Dans Țigănesc" slow 6/8 then fast 2/4 ([SoCal Folk Dance](https://socalfolkdance.org/dances/D/Dans_Tiganesc_Rar-Dans_Tignanesc_Repede.pdf)) and the slow-to-faster order and accelerations of Romani style ([Wikipedia, Gypsy style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsy_style)); the primaș leads ([Wikipedia, Romanian folk violin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_folk_violin)); a wedding band with violin, guitar, string bass, accordions and cimbalom ([Beissinger 2007](https://journals.ku.edu/folklorica/article/view/3757)); folk masters -14 to -10 LUFS with LRA at least 6 LU ([Sage Audio](https://www.sageaudio.com/articles/mastering-for-folk-music)); acoustic genres show a natural top-end decay (Pestana et al. 2013). **Hand-tuned:** the `rock` curve reused (not measured on folk), -11 LUFS as a choice inside the sourced range, `translate` 0.3, `eq` 0.7, the change rate.
- Mastering: the `hiphop`, `trance` and `rock` target curves are **measured**: genre average spectra of 772 #1 singles (Pestana, Ma, Reiss, Barbosa & Black, AES 135, 2013), converted to 1/3-octave bands (derivation, caveats and test results in docs/MASTERING.md). Loudness norms: charts -8.1 to -8.3 LUFS, streaming normalization -14 ([iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/mastering-trends), [Spotify](https://support.spotify.com/artists/article/loudness-normalization/)); Spectre's -10/-10.5 is an engine-limited choice. Still **hand-tuned**: `--translate` and `--eq` per genre, and the `beat` curve.

`{{HAND}}` is 3-5 bullets naming the genre's schools and craft (see the latest session's PROMPT.md in that genre); `{{GENRE_LINE}}` says whether the genre is fixed by the brief or the producer's choice.

## Keeping sessions alive
- At each check: count `renders/v*.wav` (excluding master, premaster and solo files), note the newest render's time, look for `final.mp3`.
- Progressing: schedule the next check in 15 minutes. With several sessions running, one combined check is fine.
- No final report and no new render for 10+ minutes, or the agent is reported stopped: start a fresh agent with the resume prompt above.
- A known cause of stops: interrupting the orchestrator's own turn mid-work can take background agents down. New messages queued while the orchestrator works are safe.

## Delivering a finished session
1. **Verify:** final.mp3 exists and has the expected length; `project.json` equals `renders/vNNN.project.json`; the master report (`renders/vNNN.master.report.json`) shows the session's genre, about the target LUFS, true peak at or under -1 dBTP, no warnings (and ideally an empty `eq_at_limit`), `master_version` 2, `top_end_max_abs_dev_db` about 1.5 or less, and the phone check (`phone.out`) without notes; sections flagged by `listen --phone` go back to the producer as mix fixes.
2. **Send** the track (named after its title) and `journal.md` to Robert.
3. **Tidy the session folder:** rename final.mp3 to `<title-slug>-mastered.mp3`; copy `renders/vNNN.master.png` to `master-tonal-balance.png` and the report to `master-report.json`; save the overview ear as `ear-overview.png`; remove `__pycache__`; write `README.md` (table, arrangement, how it carries the feeling, files, Spectre's note; see earlier sessions).
4. **Repo docs:** add the row to the README results table, the session entry to `docs/LESSONS.md` and update "Across genres"; fix or document any engine quirks the producer reported (smoke-test fixes).
5. **Website:** add the track to `site/data/tracks.json`, the canonical list (newest first; `id`, `artist: "Spectre"`, `title`, `src: "tracks/NNN-slug.mp3"`, `duration` from ffprobe, `bpm`, `model` (the producer agent's model, e.g. "Claude Opus 5.5", shown beside the tempo), `genre` (short, e.g. "Latvian folk trap"), `style`, `feeling`, 2-3 `colors` that fit the track; the page mutes them automatically). Run `python3 tools/sync_tracks.py` to copy the masters into `site/public/tracks/` and check that `cd site && npm run build` passes; the website picks the track up on its next deploy.
6. **Commit and push** (end commit messages with the session's attribution lines).

### Website design decisions (agreed with Robert, keep unless he changes them)
- Dark, ghostly world: near-black blue, cold moonlight, a hooded apparition in mist, ice-cyan glow. Tones and haze, not crisp texture.
- Badge: a tonal hooded wraith carved out by cold backlight, eye glints, eclipse-like corona; also the favicon. No cartoon features, no line drawing.
- Title: SPECTRE as dark smoky letters against the same backlight, faded (~80% opacity), with an occasional horror-film blur-and-shake glitch every 8-20 s.
- No descriptions on the page: no tagline, no track style line, no footer prose. Allowed: the GitHub badge top right, the footer line "© 2026 Robert Ontiu", "BPM · genre" under each track, and a share button next to the playing title.
- Layout: top half brand banner; bottom half a blurred field of the playing track's colours; the player in the middle, raised over the top, dissolving into the bottom. Inside the player: bar visualizer, controls, playlist ("Spectre - Title", duration, BPM below), newest first.
- No EQ: tracks play exactly as mastered (removed at Robert's request).
- Track colours lead in the bottom field (moderately muted), bleed lightly into the top, and tint the mist. Fuck Every War uses the Ukrainian flag colours.
- Mist: several fog layers moving visibly (12-28 s passes) across both halves, denser at the top.
- One fixed full-viewport background stack (no section backgrounds, no clipping), so there are no seams; verify seams with a row-by-row luminance script, not by eye.
- Performance: the soft mist, fog and track-colour field are drawn on two small low-resolution canvases (one under, one over the apparition) at 30 fps with time-based motion, paused when the tab is hidden; track colours glide in OKLab there. Other moving parts move with transform and opacity only; never animate filters or blur. No DOM layers are created, hidden or shown on a track change. Card accents are `@property` colours with CSS transitions (no per-frame JS). The fixed background never resizes with the mobile address bar. Never read layout inside an animation loop. Everything calms down under prefers-reduced-motion.
- Touch: a vertical swipe over any slider scrolls the page; seek and volume drag only on a clearly horizontal gesture.
