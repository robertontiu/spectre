# Psytrance: genre brief

Researched 2026-11-26 for the first psytrance session. Read with docs/TRANCE.md (the parent genre: its "Modern trance production" sections A-J hold the layering, glue and mastering practice psy shares) and docs/PRODUCTION.md section 5 (the checklist this brief follows). Labels: **[M]** measured (by Spectre or a cited measurement), **[S]** sourced (producer, engineer or reference text), **[H]** hand-tuned or derived here, to be checked by ear.

**The short version for a producer.** Psytrance is a kick and a bass. Every other sound is a voice in a conversation above them. The kick is short (no longer than an 8th) and tuned; the bass plays the three 16ths after it (K-B-B-B), each note a 16th with a fast filter envelope, the first one softer so the line rolls. The two never overlap, so the kick needs no sidechain to make room for the bass. The track is 7-9 minutes of 16-bar blocks with two or three drops and one big breakdown about two-thirds of the way in. Each drop comes after a build and a four-beat silence: that silence is the "cliff". There is no singer: a sampled line, or a wordless voice gated in rhythm, is enough.

## 1. Dimensions and where the boundaries are

Psytrance varies along **strand** (the axis the scene itself names: Beatport's Psy-Trance sub-genres Full-On, Progressive Psy, Psychedelic, Dark & Forest and Goa Trance) and along **time**. Region (Goa, Israel, the UK, Germany and Hamburg, Scandinavia, Finland, Brazil, Russia) mostly shows up as strands (Nitzhonot from Israel, Suomi from Finland, offbeat from Hamburg, forest from Scandinavia), so it is folded into them.

**Time, as history.** The boundaries below follow changes the sources name, not round dates.
- **Goa trance, about 1990-1997** [S]: psychedelic, Indian-flavoured melodies over a 4/4 kick; parties spread abroad from 1993, a commercial scene from 1994; "1994-1997 the golden age" (Wikipedia, flagged there as needing attribution). Tempo 135-150, oriental and mythological themes (Astral Projection, Hallucinogen, Man With No Name; psytranceguide.com). History only: a modern brief does not aim here.
- **The 2000s, full-on's golden age (about 2004-2009)** [S: psytranceguide.com]: positive, melodic, playful full-on at 140-148 with basslines that move across octaves (Astrix, Electro Sun, System Nipel); dark psy took shape after 2003 in Germany and Russia (Wikipedia); progressive psy took shape in Europe in the mid-2000s, slower and minimal (psytranceguide.com). Also history for this brief, though full-on is still made.
- **The modern era, 2015-2026** [M, S]: Mixgraph measured 13,035 psytrance tracks: median **142 bpm**, middle half 138-145, 10th-90th percentile 135-148. The yearly median is "remarkably stable", from 138 (2008) to 144 (2024). The festival psy of the main stages (Vini Vici, Astrix, Ace Ventura) sits at 138-145 and blends progressive and full-on, which psytranceguide.com calls "Psytrance (Classic/140)". The open catalogue's tonal data stops in 2021. No measurement shows a tonal change inside the modern era, so the era is one target here (`psytrance_2015`; 2022-2026 extrapolated).

**Shared core** (every modern strand; stop if a choice breaks one of these):
1. 4/4, a kick on every beat, straight 16ths, no swing (machine-tight is the style) [S: bonedo.de 1].
2. Kick and bass interlock: the bass fills the 16ths the kick leaves (rolling KBBB, or offbeat, or triplet in tribal psy) [S: bonedo.de 1; psytranceguide.com].
3. A short kick: "no longer than an eighth note", a sine with a fast pitch drop, with its 200-600 Hz dipped just after the transient [S: bonedo.de 1].
4. Leads, acid lines and FX are sound design that answer each other. Psy has no verse and chorus; the theme lives in the breakdown [S: bonedo.de 2-4].
5. 16- or 32-bar passages; something enters or leaves every 4-8 bars [S: bonedo.de; Wikipedia: "new layers every four to eight bars"].
6. Samples, not singers: "sampled quotes with a clear message", pitched or modulated, and long atmospheric voices behind a rhythmic gate [S: bonedo.de 4].
7. Long DJ tracks: 7-9 minutes [S: bonedo.de 4]; Wikipedia gives 6-10.

## 2. The modern strands

Each element is marked **shared** or **varies**. Tempi are from psytranceguide.com and Wikipedia [S] unless marked [M].

| | **Festival psy and full-on** (default) | **Progressive psy** | **Night full-on / twilight** | **Hi-tech** (on request) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tempo | 138-145 festival [M: Mixgraph middle half 138-145]; full-on 140-148 | 134-140 (guide 130-140); groove over speed | 145-150 | 170-200 |
| Kick | short, tuned to the root, hard click; ends before the bass's first 16th **(shared)** | the same, softer click, rounder | the same, harder | very short and clicky (the speed leaves no room) |

| Form | DJ intro, 2-3 drops, the big breakdown about two-thirds in (up to 64 bars) with a theme, builds of 8/16/32 bars, a 4-beat cliff before each drop [S: bonedo.de 4] | longer evolutions, smaller breaks, the drop as a return of the bass more than an explosion | short breaks (forest: "few breakdowns") | short sections |
| Length | 7-9 min [S] | 7-9 min, often longer intros | 7-9 min | 6-8 min [H] |
| Voice | film-style spoken lines; gated wordless voices; tribal chants in tribal psy | a few spoken words, processed | robotic, alien | silly samples |
| Master | `--strand fullon` or default | `--strand progressive` | `--strand night` | `--strand hitech` (not measured) |

**Neighbours that are not this brief** (the nearest wrong strands): dark psy (140-155; cold, spooky FX, horror-film samples: Wikipedia, psytranceguide.com) and forest (150-160, nature ambience and swarming FX, few breakdowns); psy-techno (125-137); psychill (90-120); Goa (the 1990s sound: Indian scales, a 909, long acid lines).

## 3. Layer roles and EQ map

From the producer sources where they exist [S]; the trance map (docs/TRANCE.md B) holds for the shared upper roles. `engine/eqmaps.json` "psytrance" inherits it and overrides the bass, the lead and fx. Leave `"eq"` on the stems unset or `"auto"` (as trance does).

| Role | Sources | Supplies | High-pass | Cut / control | Owns | Source / cross-check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| kick | `kicksub` + `kickpunch` + `kickclick` stack, `length` 0.18-0.22 s at 140-148 bpm | the beat's sub (45-70 Hz) for one 8th at most, the click | 20-30 | 200-600 Hz just after the transient | 45-80, 2-5 k | bonedo.de 1 (length, dip); the trance map (cut 200-500) |
| bass | `trancebass` played as notes (KBBB, a 16th each), **its own sub on** (`sub` 0.8-0.9) | the sub and body between the kicks, the "rolling" grit at 0.7-2 kHz | 18-30 (a phase trim, not a cut) | 250-400 | 40-120, 0.7-2 k | bonedo.de 1 (a saw through a fast low-pass; 18 Hz low cut to align with the kick's tail); trance's 90-150 Hz high-pass does **not** apply: in psy there is no separate sub [S] |
| lead, acid | `acid`, `fmgrowl` (FM), `polysynth`, `supersaw` model 2 in breakdowns | mid bite, the conversation | 150-250 | 250-400 | 1-4 k | bonedo.de 4: remove unnecessary low end from every sound beside kick and bass; trance map |
| fx | `zap`, section `riser` and `rise`, `whoosh`, filtered noise (`polysynth` noise osc with an `autofilter`), delay throws | movement and stereo | 150-300 | | 2-12 k | [H] from the workshop's rule |
| pad, atmosphere | `supersaw` model 2, `polysynth`, gated | width, the breakdown's air | as trance (about 210) | 316 | | trance map |
| hats, ride, perc | `metalhat` closed 16ths, open offbeat; `ride909`; snare and clap on 2 and 4 (909 snare + clap + a stereo snare) | the top, the swing of the 16ths | 300-500 | | 6-16 k | bonedo.de 1 (snare layering, open hat between the kicks, crashes at 16-bar boundaries) |

**Glue** [S, H]: drums on a bus with a clipper (as trance); kick and bass are the loudest elements and stay mono below about 120 Hz; check the mix in mono now and then and with a correlation meter, and lower the side of a sound where the trouble comes from (bonedo.de 4). Long sends (reverb, delay) are high-passed and ducked by the kick.

**Low end and top end facts** [M, S]: kick fundamental 45-60 Hz tuned to the root (F#1 46.2, G1 49.0, A1 55.0 Hz; psy keys commonly sit there [H]); the bass's fundamental is the same root an octave or two above, its sub between the kicks. The catalogue measures psytrance with **+3.5 to +5 dB more at 40-125 Hz than trance** (both 2015-2021) and the same top (section 6); the top is hats, acid and FX, not boosted noise.

**Characteristic imperfection vs wrong** [S, H]: grid-tight timing is right (no swing, `humanize_ms` 0 on kick and bass, 1 on hats); detuned and drifting synths are right. Wrong: a kick that rings into the bass (a boomy low end that pumps), a bass that sustains (it turns into trance's offbeat or a techno rumble), a swung groove (house), an EDM-style supersaw drop with no rolling bass.

**Nearest wrong sounds:** a long 909 kick with a rumble (techno); a 138 offbeat bass with sidechained supersaws (uplifting trance); a big-room build with a snare roll to a one-note drop (EDM festival); Indian scales and a 1990s 909 (Goa revival); horror samples, a 150+ bpm grind (dark/forest).

## 4. Arrangement: the build and the cliff

From bonedo.de workshop 4 [S] and the 28-track arrangement study reported on r/psytranceproduction ("at a macro level, 8-32 bar sections, not a lot of variation") [S]; the bar counts per section are [H] within those sources' ranges.

A festival or full-on extended mix at 143-145 bpm (16 bars ≈ 26.5 s):

| Section | Bars | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| DJ intro | 32 | kick alone 16, then kick + bass; hats join; one FX every 8 bars |
| Drive 1 | 32-48 | the acid or lead conversation enters in 8-bar answers; a filter opens across the section |
| Break 1 | 8-16 | kick and bass rest (bonedo: simple breaks of at most 16 bars); an atmosphere, a spoken line |
| Build 1 | 8-16 | snare or noise rise, filter up, then **4 beats of silence or a fill** |
| Drop 1 | 32-48 | in 16-bar blocks; a 4-beat gap between blocks re-arms the energy |
| The big breakdown | 32-64 (about two-thirds in) | the theme, pads, the most melody of the track; no kick |
| Build 2 | 16-32 | the longest build: riser, pitched `rise`, snare roll, the theme's last bars filtered up; **the cliff: 4 beats of silence** |
| Main drop | 32-64 | everything; the lead answers the theme; a second 4-beat gap halfway |
| DJ outro | 32 | elements leave until kick and bass remain (bonedo: "only kick and bass") |

Total about 7:30-8:30. Two or three drops [S]. The cliff is a cut of everything (`"exit": {"cut": {"tracks": "all", "beats": 4}}`, or `"silence"`), followed by an `impact` and a crash on the drop's first beat.

## 5. Voice

Psy uses samples, not singers [S: bonedo.de 4; docs/TRANCE.md "Vocals"]. Spectre does not sample films or other recordings, and sung words come only from the owner-approved svs voices (MANUAL `singer`). So a psy track here uses the voice as an instrument: a short spoken-style phrase (few syllables, low in the register, monotone) by an svs voice, pitched down or through `vocoder`, `comb` or a gate; or `vox2` and wordless svs vowels behind a rhythmic gate (`"gate"` fx) in the breakdown. Write original words; one line, repeated, works ("all hands", "go"). Do not imitate a film quote.

## 6. Mastering by strand

**What was measured** [M] (2026-11-26, `tools/refcurve.py catalogue`, MusicBrainz + AcousticBrainz, method as docs/TRANCE.md C and J; identifiers in data/catalogue_references.json):
- **`psytrance_2015`**: tags psytrance, psychedelic trance, psy-trance, full on, progressive psytrance; first release 2015-2021; **458 tracks**, 57% lossless; fit residual mean 0.17 dB. Whole-track loudness (from ReplayGain) **-11.6 LUFS** (middle half -12.8 to -10.2).
- **`psytrance_prog`**: by artist (Neelix, Ticon, Liquid Soul, Interactive Noise, Ace Ventura, Zen Mechanics, Captain Hook), 165 tracks 2000-2021, 60% lossless: **-12.1** (-12.9 to -11.1); against `psytrance_2015` +1 to +2 dB at 63-250 Hz and +1 to +1.5 dB at 6-12.5 kHz.
- *Full-on set, not kept as a target*: by artist (Bliss, Infected Mushroom, Ajja, Astrix, Tristan, Sonic Species, Electro Sun, Vini Vici...), 219 tracks 1996-2021, 39% lossless, about 3% unrelated matches from an ambiguous name: -11.9 (-13.4 to -10.8); its curve reads within **1.5 dB (mean 0.3)** of `psytrance_2015`, so full-on uses that target.
- Against `trance_2015`: psytrance is +3.5 to +5 dB at 40-125 Hz, +1 dB at 160-250 Hz, and within 1 dB from 400 Hz up.
- *The loud section*: Mixgraph's store previews (120 s, BS.1770, measured 2026-08-11): **Psy-Trance -7.9 LUFS** (-8.6 to -7.2; LRA 4.9) [M: docs/TRANCE.md J]. Whole tracks sit 4 dB lower than the previews because of long intros and outros and quiet breakdowns; both numbers are true of what they measure.
- No psy-specific true-peak, width or PLR data exist; the club practice of docs/TRANCE.md J applies (clipper before the limiter, mono under about 120 Hz, DJ playback with nothing normalised).

| Strand | `--strand` | Target | `lufs` (integrated) | Aim for the loudest 2 min | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Festival psy and full-on | (default) or `fullon` | `psytrance_2015` | **-8.5** [H] | about -8 (Psy-Trance previews -7.9) | measured target; level a choice near the previews |
| Progressive psy | `progressive` | `psytrance_prog` | **-9.5** [H] | -9 to -8.5 | quieter whole tracks (-12.1); no preview figure; Progressive House previews -10.1 is the nearest |
| Night full-on / twilight | `night` | `psytrance_2015` | -8.5 [H] | about -8 | no separate data |
| Hi-tech | `hitech` | `psytrance_2015` | -8.5 [H] | about -8 | not measured |

True peak -1 dBTP everywhere (streaming-safe; club masters often go over 0, docs/TRANCE.md J). `max_low_boost_db` 2: let the mix bring the low end, not the master's EQ.

**First engine check** (examples/psytrance-test.json, 33 s, 145 bpm, `master --genre psytrance`): -8.6 LUFS, TP -1.03 dBTP, PLR 7.6, LRA 5.1, short-term max -7.1. The master's EQ hit its limit cutting 40-50 Hz (-8 dB): the test's kick sub (F#1, drive 0.25) plus a bass with its own sub ran about 5 dB over the target there. **In a track, set the kick and bass sub levels against the target with `listen --clarity` before mastering**, not by the master's EQ.

## 7. Seasoning a club track with Christmas cues (no kitsch)

For a track whose feeling is drive and determination with a hint of the season (an end-of-year celebration). The season is a seasoning: if a listener's first word is "Christmas", it has gone too far; the right reaction is "is that... sleigh bells?" in the breakdown.

**Sounds** (engine):
- **Sleigh bells**: `sleighbells` (new, engine/psy.py; section 8): short damped shakes on the offbeat 8ths or on 16ths at -12 to -16 dB under the hats, high-passed at about 3 kHz, panned off centre. They sit in the hats' region (the CAROLING map: high-pass 1-2 kHz, own 5-8 kHz) and read there as percussion first. Use them in the breakdown and the build; in a drop, give them at most one 16-bar block.
- **Celesta and glockenspiel**: `celesta` and `glockenspiel` (measured, engine/chamber.py): bell colour for a theme, an octave or two above the lead, with long reverb and a dotted-8th delay. The celesta is softer; the glockenspiel cuts through a full mix.
- **Bells**: `bell` with `"clean": true`, a tubular or handbell tone for one struck note on a downbeat after the cliff.

**A carol quote** (public domain only; docs/CAROLING.md "Public-domain carols"). Quote 4-8 notes, never a whole verse; recast the rhythm to the 16th grid; harmonise it yourself (later arrangements are not public domain).
- **The First Nowell** (traditional, printed by Sandys 1833): **verified** in docs/CAROLING.md (two independent scores plus a machine-readable check; `examples/caroling/check/melodies.py`). Its falling-then-rising opening ("The first No-well") is a clean 6-8 note motif.
- **It Came Upon the Midnight Clear** (CAROL, R. S. Willis 1850): **verified** there too; a 6/8 tune, so needs re-barring to 4/4.
- Candidates **not yet verified** (the CAROLING rule: two independent scores and an engine check before quoting): *God Rest Ye Merry, Gentlemen* (traditional, Sandys 1833; its minor key suits psy best), *Jingle Bells* (Pierpont 1857; the familiar chorus is a later variant, recorded by 1898, still before 1929), *Carol of the Bells* (Leontovych's melody, before 1916; its four-note ostinato is close to a psy riff, but Wilhousky's English words of 1936 are protected, so melody only, and verify the edition). Do **not** use *We Wish You a Merry Christmas* (its familiar tune is first documented in a 1935 arrangement) or anything from the 1930s on.

**Where it sits in a psy arrangement:**
1. **The big breakdown**: the place for the quote. The glockenspiel or celesta states 4-8 notes of the carol slowly over a pad. Then the lead (acid or a supersaw) takes the same notes as the build's riff, an octave down and in the track's minor key, so the carol becomes the drive.
2. **The intro**: a sleigh-bell shake on the downbeat of bar 17 or 33 as the bass enters; one, not a loop.
3. **The main drop**: the hook doubled an octave up by the glockenspiel for one 16-bar block, at -10 to -14 dB. It should be felt as sparkle, not heard as a tune.
4. **The cliff**: a single `bell` or `glockenspiel` note in the four-beat silence, ringing into the drop's impact.

**Nearest wrong feelings:** a novelty Christmas remix (a famous carol over a beat, sleigh bells on every 16th, "ho ho ho"); office-party cheese (major-key bounce, jingles as the hook); generic festival EDM (a big-room snare build to a one-note drop with a carol topline); dark forest psy (the season turned eerie: detuned music boxes and horror bells). Keep the key minor (or modal: Dorian, Phrygian), keep the bass rolling, and let the season appear in two or three places.

## 8. Engine mapping

Everything a modern psy track needs exists. New in this research: `sleighbells` and `zap` (engine/psy.py; docs/MANUAL.md "Psytrance").

| Need | Engine | Status |
|---|---|---|
| psy kick: short, tight, tuned | `kicksub` (`note` = the root, `length` 0.18-0.22, `tail` 0.03-0.04, `punch` 14-16) + `kickpunch` + `kickclick` as a stack | exists (MANUAL "Production") |
| rolling bass, per-16th envelope, gated by the notes | `trancebass` played as **notes** (`"1:F#1:1:0.78 2:F#1:1 3:F#1:1:0.95 ..."`: a 16th each, the first softer), `fdecay` 0.04-0.05, `decay` 0.08-0.1, `sustain_level` 0, `release` 0.015, `sub` 0.8-0.9; no sidechain needed (the notes never touch the kick) | exists; recipe in examples/psytrance-test.json |
| full-on galloping bass across octaves | the same, notes on different octaves per 16th | exists |
| acid / squelch | `acid` (303: accent `!`, slide `~`, `cutoff` and `env_mod` automated per section) | exists |
| FM psychedelic sweeps and squelch | `fmgrowl` (FM; `mod` on `index` at note rates), `polysynth` with LFOs, `autofilter`, `comb`, `flanger` | exists (MANUAL "Bass music") |
| gated and filtered stabs | `polysynth` stabs; `gate` fx; section `lpf` and `inst.cutoff` ramps | exists |
| filtered noise sweeps, risers | section `exit` `riser`, `rise` (with `"fx_model": 2`), `whoosh`; a `polysynth` noise osc with `autofilter` | exists |
| delays with filtered feedback | `mix.returns` `delay` with `lpf` and `hpf`, `pingpong`, `feedback` 0.4-0.5 | exists |
| pitch-dive zaps, lasers | **`zap`** (new): `start`, `end`, `time`, `curve`, `fm`, `ratio`, `decay` | **built 2026-11-26** |
| sleigh bells | **`sleighbells`** (new): modal, fitted to VSCO-2 CE | **built 2026-11-26** |
| celesta, glockenspiel, bell | `celesta`, `glockenspiel`, `bell` | exist |
| voice as an instrument | `singer` (svs), `vox2`, `vocoder`, `gate` | exist |

**Measured** (`examples/production/psytrance/measure_sleighbells.py`, `sleighbells.json`): against the 11 single hits of the VSCO-2 CE sleigh bells (two straps; CC0; measured only), at the engine's defaults (8 seeds), 1/3-octave power over 300 ms within **2.7 dB on average from 2 to 12.5 kHz** (the three mode peaks at 2.5, 6.3 and 10 kHz within 2.1 dB), time to fall 20 dB 0.30 s against 0.27 s, spectral centroid 6.6 kHz against 7.4 kHz (slightly duller; `tone` 1 brightens it). Demo: examples/production/psytrance/sleighbells-zap-demo.mp3 (five shakes, a 16th jingle at 145 bpm, five zaps).

**Deferred** (not needed for a first track): a dedicated psy kick model (the stack does it); a 1/32 roll bass for hi-tech; a reference census of a published psy multitrack (no open breakdown was found); local reference masters for 2022-2026 (the open data ends in 2021); per-strand true peak, width and PLR.

## 9. Current practice (bleeding edge, 2023-2026)

- Tempo: the yearly median peaked at 144 in 2024 [M: Mixgraph]; festival psy at 140-145.
- Psy's crossovers: raw trance (130-160, 303 and 909, "'90s-inspired") and hypnotic psy (140-150) are the newer labels [S: psytranceguide.com; docs/TRANCE.md for raw trance]; psy-style rolling basses under techno and hard trance (docs/TRANCE.md I).
- Modern psy filters: "older psy relied on 24 dB filters for punchy basses; modern psytrance often uses 6 and 12 dB" [S: Gearspace]. The engine's `trancebass` filter is 2-pole (12 dB), which suits the modern sound.
- The engine can do all of it; no tonal measurement covers 2022-2026.
