# Lessons log

What each session taught us. Read this before changing the producer prompt or the engine, and add an entry after every session. **After every genre switch, update the "Across genres" section too.**

## Across genres (updated after each switch)
Genres so far: boom-bap/trap hybrid (001), rage-trap/industrial (002), oriental trap (003), Romanian folk trap (004), Ukrainian folk trap (005), Latvian folk trap (006), pop rock (008), trance (007).
- **What transfers everywhere:** the feel-listen-decide loop; naming the nearest wrong feelings in the brief; subtraction (drop-outs, sections where the beat leaves) as the strongest emotional tool; mixing low end before mastering; one session folder plus journal so a session can be resumed by a fresh agent.
- **Folk-fusion genres need three things before the session starts:** (1) research saved as a `docs/<GENRE>.md` brief (instruments, rhythms, modes, the modern reference artists), (2) the genre's signature instruments built and checked in the engine (generic synths can't fake them), (3) a warning against "postcard" use: the folk elements must carry the feeling, not decorate it. Session 003 showed it works: the listener's favourite moments were the oud and darbuka sitting inside the beat.
- **Each genre has its own trap:** briefed rage drifted toward hype and anxiety; rebellion toward triumph; the hustle toward celebration or nostalgia; anti-war toward a battle anthem. Name the trap in the brief.
- **A statement needs a dramaturgy, not just a mood (005):** an instrumental can say something explicit when the arc tells a story with two opposing forces (here a war machine against people's voices), when the hook IS the title (two choir hits), and when the opposing force is defeated by subtraction (the siren is cut off by every shout, then drowned out, then absent). Give each side its own sound world and its own time feel.
- **Two time feels as meaning (005):** machine elements locked dead to the grid, human elements humanized late; the contrast reads instantly as "mechanical vs alive". Reusable for any human-vs-system theme.
- **Watch for groove that means the wrong thing (005):** an even stomp on every quarter reads as an army. In folk-fusion work, check what each rhythm means in the culture, not just whether it bangs.
- **A folk rhythm can carry the theme (004):** the hustle's "carrying weight" came from an asymmetric folk meter (7/16 geamparale) folded into the trap grid as a 3-3-2 limp in hats, cobza and drum. Look for the culture's own rhythm before writing melody.
- **The same instrument can tell opposite stories (004):** the fiddle first danced like a wedding band (celebration, the wrong feeling); digging the bow in (grit, sawing, long notes) turned it into work. Articulation decides the feeling as much as the instrument choice.
- **A folk song form can be the hook (006):** the Suiti call, repeat and drone (one voice calls, the next repeats the words, a drone holds underneath) carried the subject on its own: a chain that grows each hook reads as a crowd joining. Look for the culture's singing form, not just its scales and instruments.
- **Triplets sway, straight 8ths and quarters march (006):** 8th-note triplets in hats and jingles made a crowd swaying arm in arm; a kick on 1 and 3 drifted toward a soldier's two-step and was moved. Together with 005: decide whether the rhythm is a crowd or an army.
- **Revolution doesn't have to be an attack (006):** framing the subject as endurance (holding the line through a cold night) gave the track a patient arc with no triumph; the cold (wind, raw voices, dark register) kept the communal singing from turning cosy.
- **The top end is where synthesized noise gives itself away (remaster of 001-006, 2026-11-24):** on a phone, which plays nothing below about 300 Hz and lifts 2-5 kHz, hats, rain, vinyl, breath and driven snare noise read as loud "static" even when the long-term spectrum looks fine. The first masters sat 3-5 dB over the measured hip-hop curve at 16 kHz (the hand-tuned `beat` curve asked for it, and the EQ under-corrected); master version 2 now puts the whole track on the curve, but it can't turn down one noise in one section. Noise-based sources must be shaped in the mix (lower, low-passed or high-shelved; rain, wind and breath under about 4-6 kHz) and kept under the measured curve, and every session checks the phone view (`listen --phone`) and solos the source of any section flagged bright or hissy. Measured culprits: 006's wind (the rain texture at +28 dB) is all of the 4-12 kHz content in its intro and "hour" sections; 004's nai breath (0.65) is all of it in the wall; in 002 about 95% of the 4-12 kHz energy lands on the snare hits and the 16th hats carry under 1%.
- **Time feel as dramaturgy in band music (008):** the "inside the weight" world (guitar figure and voice) played 20-30 ms behind the beat and the rest of the song on top turned "catching up with life" into something audible, not just a loudness change. It extends 005's machine-vs-human idea to a live band.
- **Fix low end by tuning, not EQ (008):** a kick head that is a pure sine plus a bass register on A1/B1 made a 63 Hz spike no notch could fix; tuning the kick to ~72 Hz, keeping the bass register off A1/B1 in verses and giving distorted guitars low-shelf body brought the master's moves inside its limits. Measure per-stem spectra before guessing.
- **Company, not victory (008):** for "getting through" subjects, add a second voice in steps and bring the dark figure back inside the final chorus and coda; it keeps the lift honest. Robert's verdict: "the emotions it portrays are real".
- **Trance euphoria is made before the release (007):** withhold the hook (an arpeggio carries the groove), keep the first drop in a smaller room (filtered, no pad, lower), start the breakdown from almost nothing, make the bar before the release true silence (`exit silence`, not `cut`, so tails stop); the release then needs only one changed note (a sus or minor note becoming the major third).
- **In four-on-the-floor music, measure unclipped per-track peaks (007):** solo renders clip at 0 dBFS and hid a clap peaking at +6.9 dBFS on 2 and 4 with the kick, the real crest problem. Saturate last; set the kick's low-end balance before the saturator.
- **Shared modes:** Ukrainian Dorian, Romanian minor and the klezmer "Misheberak" scale are the same Dorian #4; Hijaz / Phrygian dominant links oriental, Romanian and Ukrainian material. The key detector in the ears misreads these modes; ignore it there.

## Session 007: "First Light" (briefed: trance, ecstasy)
- **Result:** 7:25 extended mix, 138 bpm, F minor with major lifts, the first four-on-the-floor track. Released at -9.2 LUFS, -1.03 dBTP (master v2, `--genre trance`), no warnings. 28 renders, resumed twice after server restarts; Robert's final feedback is pending.
- **What worked (producer's view):** see "Across genres": the long dark and the held-back hook, one changed note as the landing, true silence before each release, a second wave that reaches a new top note.
- **Mix:** the clubkick at ~56 Hz with a narrow deep cut at 66 Hz and a boost at 105 Hz kept 50 and 63 Hz inside the master's limits; the 12-16 kHz sizzle was the open hat, not the shaker or clap.
- **Engine quirks:** master v1 needed about 1.2 GB per minute of audio and was OOM-killed on a 7:25 track (master v2 runs it, peaking near 5.7 GB, so only one heavy job at a time); solo renders are clipped at 0 dBFS (`np.clip`), hiding real track peaks; `listen --phone` defaulted to the hiphop target for a trance render; `sat` after an EQ cut re-inflates the kick's fundamental; the ears' tempo estimate on the master was wrong (~92 bpm).

## Session 008: "Anyway" (briefed: pop rock, thriving over depression)
- **Result:** 3:28, 122 bpm, E major / C#m verses, the first band track. Released at -8.6 LUFS, -1.0 dBTP (master v2, `--genre rock`), no warnings. 28 renders, resumed once after a server restart. Robert listened to it many times: "the structure, the drops ... the emotions it portrays are real and absolutely crazy good".
- **What worked:** see "Across genres": time feel as dramaturgy, company growing in steps, the dark figure kept to the end (ends on IV). A truly empty "bad day" bridge with the drums returning one at a time did more than a key change would have.
- **Weak spot:** the wordless `vox` doesn't read as a voice in a band mix (it has little 1.6-2 kHz and sat 1-3 dB under the guitars); Robert didn't notice a voice at all, and chose to keep the mix as is. The planned sung-vocal instrument (queued in ORCHESTRATION.md) is the real fix.
- **Engine quirks:** `kitkick`'s head is a pure sine at `tune` (EQ notches barely help; retune instead); bass register "E2" drops A and B to 55/62 Hz; `vox` needs +3-5 dB at 2.8 kHz and more `ring` to be heard; snare `sat` after a high shelf brings the wire fizz back, a 3 ms comp kills its crack; masters were OOM-killed while another session rendered (one heavy job at a time); the ears flag a doubled last chorus as "no contrast" even with melodic and drum changes.

## Session 006: "Līdz rītam" (briefed: Latvian folk trap, revolution)
- **Result:** 2:52, 136 bpm half-time with a triplet sway, G Dorian. Mastered at -10.5 LUFS, -1.02 dBTP, laptop loss -1.8 dB, no warnings. 26 renders.
- **What worked:** see "Across genres": Suiti call-and-repeat as the hook, the triplet sway, revolution as staying. The best moment is a single bar of the crowd singing the call over the drone alone before the last hook. Drifts caught and fixed: a jig in the first loop, a marching kick, and a kokle arpeggio that turned pastoral (replaced by struck strums).
- **Mix:** a drone, voices and kokle all piling up at 300-400 Hz gave a boxy master (+6.7 dB at 315 Hz in the first trial); cutting that band in each source got it to +2.9. An engine rumble on D1 (37 Hz) leaked into the sub band and was pumped by the master's low lift; it moved up to D2. The release master pushes quiet intros further down than the render's quick master (-14.4 to -19.4 LUFS here); fine for a cold opening, worth knowing.
- **Engine fixes after this session:** chord `stabs` rhythm strings now follow the drum-step grid (12 characters = 8th-note triplets) instead of always being 16ths; the manual now says so and notes that the key detector misreads modes and that zoom timing-roll labels can look shifted by one row. Triplet melodic notes work by writing fractional 16th steps (multiples of 4/3).

## Session 001: "Bleachers" (feeling: defiance)
- **Outcome:** the track came across closer to *longing* than defiance; Robert's feedback was that it "didn't feel aggressive enough". A near miss.
- **Why:** the core idea was "minor melodies that climb back up every time the chords pull them down". The producer meant it as refusal, but upward-reaching minor melodies are the textbook shape of yearning. The bridge's strings sinking by half-steps pushed further toward ache. The synthesized brass and organ lacked bite.
- **Lesson 1:** attitude lives in the sound (distortion, roughness, hard transients, density), not only in the notes. The ears can verify structure but not attitude.
- **Lesson 2:** at every stage, ask what the nearest wrong feeling is and what separates the two.
- **Lesson 3 (mix and master):** the 808 fundamental sat about 17 dB above the midrange; on MacBook speakers this sounded "clunky". This led to the mastering stage (`docs/MASTERING.md`) and the rule "get weight from saturation and mids, not sub; if the master has to cut near 8 dB, fix the mix."
- **Engine changes after this session:** premaster export, `master.py`, the `master` CLI command, manual updates.

## Session 002: "Lid Off" (briefed: rage, high tempo, aggressive)
- **Result:** 2:51, 160 bpm, E Phrygian with a flat 5, lifting a semitone for the last hook. Rage-trap crossed with industrial. Mastered at -10.1 LUFS, -1.04 dBTP, laptop loss -2.1 dB, no warnings.
- **What worked:** the "nearest wrong feeling" check from 001 drove most decisions. The producer removed elements that told the wrong story: a hissing snare tail read as anxiety, a riser read as festival hype, a half-time verse went cold like menace until a muted chug went under it. Hard cuts (an eighth of silence before the slam) replaced risers.
- **Low end:** the first trial master showed a +16 dB spike at 50 Hz because a kick tuned to 52 Hz was fighting the 808. Moving the kick to 62 Hz, notching the 808 and sidechaining it fixed this in the mix, so the final master's EQ moves stayed under 4.5 dB. Mastering as a feedback loop works.
- **Engine fix after this session:** hits pushed before 0:00 (negative `push_ms` on step 0) used to be dropped; they now play at 0:00. Added `engine/solos.sh` (the producer's own per-track level helper).

## Session 003: "Throw It Back" (briefed: oriental trap, rebellion)
- **Result:** 2:48, 140 bpm half-time, D Hijaz. Call and response (an oud or ney call, crowd sections answering) as the core idea of rebellion: many voices, growing. Mastered at -10.5 LUFS, -1.02 dBTP, no warnings.
- **What worked:** the brief listed the nearest wrong feelings (rage, defiance, triumph, menace, exotic tourism), and the producer explicitly kept the hype out: no risers, no major lift. The strongest sections are subtractions: the march with no kick or 808, and the standoff where the crowd shouts over nothing.
- **Mixing loop again:** the first trial master (v018) cut 40 Hz by 4.8 dB and boosted 50-100 Hz; notching the 808's sub after its drive, lifting its 52/85 Hz body and cutting the stomp at 170 Hz got the final EQ moves under 4.1 dB.
- **Process:** the session was interrupted at iteration 12 (an interrupted operator turn took the background agent down) and resumed by a fresh agent from `project.json` and `journal.md`. Forward-only versioning and the journal made the hand-over seamless. Operators: check long sessions periodically and resume them if they stop.
- **Engine changes:** new instruments `oud`, `ney`, `darbuka`. Fixes: `listen --wav vNNN.master.wav` now finds the render's meta; `listen` piped into `head` no longer crashes. Known: the key detector reads Hijaz as major (its raised third dominates); ignore it for maqam-based tracks.

## Session 005: "Fuck Every War" (briefed: Ukrainian folk trap, anti-war statement)
- **Result:** 3:01, 140 bpm half-time, E Ukrainian Dorian. Mastered at -10.5 LUFS, -1.01 dBTP, laptop loss -1.9 dB, no warnings. First session run under the artist name Spectre, and the first run in parallel with another session (004).
- **What worked:** see "Across genres": a story of two forces (war machine vs people), the hook as the title, silence as the cost, and two time feels (machine on the grid, people late). The producer removed a section because its stomp sounded like an army, exactly the wrong feeling named in the brief.
- **Mix:** a one-note 808 (E1) leaves gaps in the low spectrum; the master filled them with +4 dB lifts at 30-100 Hz without warnings. Varying the 808's notes would make the low end more even.
- **Engine fixes after this session:** `render --solo` no longer overwrites `renders/latest.json` (a following `listen` used to analyse the solo); the manual now explains that an exit `cut` doesn't stop tails (use section `mute` automation for true silence). Known: `solos.sh` measures only the first 6 s; for per-section levels use `stems_rms` in the render's meta (see the session's `stems.py`).

## Session 004: "Tura" (briefed: Romanian folk trap, the hustle)
- **Result:** 2:43, 144 bpm half-time, E Romanian minor. Mastered at -10.5 LUFS, -1.06 dBTP, laptop loss -2.1 dB, no warnings. Ran in parallel with 005.
- **What worked:** the 3-3-2 limp as the hustle's body; "the wall" section (trap out, a tired nai doina, the fiddle and the clock still working) as the emotional centre; ending where it started ("five in the morning again") instead of a victory lap. Two drifts caught and fixed: a fiddle that read as a wedding band, and a drill verse that turned into menace.

Known: the arrangement-lane plot can draw a silent track as continuous.
