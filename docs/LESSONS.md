## Across genres (updated after each switch)
*Read these as principles (subtraction, a dramaturgy of opposing forces, time feel as meaning), not as arrangements to reuse: copying a favourite track's structure made Spectre's trap tracks sound alike. Each session names its own habits and departs from at least one (see prompts/producer.md, stage 1).*
Genres so far: boom-bap/trap hybrid (001), rage-trap/industrial (002), oriental trap (003), Romanian folk trap (004), Ukrainian folk trap (005), Latvian folk trap (006), pop rock (008), trance (007), joc țigănesc / Romanian lăutari wedding dance (010), aggressive boom-bap hip hop on a national anthem (011), American Christmas caroling (012), trance radio edit (013), Latin pop / bolero-bachata (014), cinematic chamber score (015), modern trance extended mix (016), modern dubstep (017).
- **What transfers everywhere:** the feel-listen-decide loop; naming the nearest wrong feelings in the brief; subtraction (drop-outs, sections where the beat leaves) as the strongest emotional tool; mixing low end before mastering; one session folder plus journal so a session can be resumed by a fresh agent.
- **Folk-fusion genres need three things before the session starts:** (1) research saved as a `docs/<GENRE>.md` brief (instruments, rhythms, modes, the modern reference artists), (2) the genre's signature instruments built and checked in the engine (generic synths can't fake them), (3) a warning against "postcard" use: the folk elements must carry the feeling, not decorate it. Session 003 showed it works: the listener's favourite moments were the oud and darbuka sitting inside the beat.
- **Each genre has its own trap:** briefed rage drifted toward hype and anxiety; rebellion toward triumph; the hustle toward celebration or nostalgia; anti-war toward a battle anthem. Name the trap in the brief.
- **A statement needs a dramaturgy, not just a mood (005):** an instrumental can say something explicit when the arc tells a story with two opposing forces (here a war machine against people's voices), when the hook IS the title (two choir hits), and when the opposing force is defeated by subtraction (the siren is cut off by every shout, then drowned out, then absent). Give each side its own sound world and its own time feel.
- **Two time feels as meaning (005):** machine elements locked dead to the grid, human elements humanized late; the contrast reads instantly as "mechanical vs alive". Reusable for any human-vs-system theme.
- **Watch for groove that means the wrong thing (005):** an even stomp on every quarter reads as an army. In folk-fusion work, check what each rhythm means in the culture, not just whether it bangs.
- **A folk rhythm can carry the theme (004):** the hustle's "carrying weight" came from an asymmetric folk meter (7/16 geamparale) folded into the trap grid as a 3-3-2 limp in hats, cobza and drum. Look for the culture's own rhythm before writing melody.
- **The same instrument can tell opposite stories (004):** the fiddle first danced like a wedding band (celebration, the wrong feeling); digging the bow in (grit, sawing, long notes) turned it into work. Articulation decides the feeling as much as the instrument choice.
- **A folk song form can be the hook (006):** the Suiti call, repeat and drone (one voice calls, the next repeats the words, a drone holds underneath) carried the subject on its own: a chain that grows each hook reads as a crowd joining. Look for the culture's singing form, not just its scales and instruments.
- **Triplets sway, straight 8ths and quarters march (006):** 8th-note triplets in hats and jingles made a crowd swaying arm in arm; a kick on 1 and 3 drifted toward a soldier's two-step and was moved. Together with 005: decide whether the rhythm is a crowd or an army.
- **Revolution doesn't have to be an attack (006):** framing the subject as endurance (holding the line through a cold night) gave the track a patient arc with no triumph; the cold (wind, raw voices, dark register) kept the communal singing from turning cosy.
- **The top end is where synthesized noise gives itself away (remaster of 001-006, 2026-11-24):** on a phone, which plays nothing below about 300 Hz and lifts 2-5 kHz, hats, rain, vinyl, breath and driven snare noise read as loud "static" even when the long-term spectrum looks fine. The first masters sat 3-5 dB over the measured hip-hop curve at 16 kHz (the hand-tuned `beat` curve asked for it, and the EQ under-corrected); master version 2 now puts the whole track on the curve, but it can't turn down one noise in one section. Noise-based sources must be shaped in the mix (lower, low-passed or high-shelved; rain, wind and breath under about 4-6 kHz) and kept under the measured curve, and every session checks the phone view (`listen --phone`) and solos the source of any section flagged bright or hissy. Measured culprits: 006's wind (the rain texture at +28 dB) is all of the 4-12 kHz content in its intro and "hour" sections; 004's nai breath (0.65) is all of it in the wall; in 002 about 95% of the 4-12 kHz energy lands on the snare hits and the 16th hats carry under 1%.
- **Time feel as dramaturgy in band music (008):** the "inside the weight" world (guitar figure and voice) played 20-30 ms behind the beat and the rest of the song on top turned "catching up with life" into something audible, not just a loudness change. It extends 005's machine-vs-human idea to a live band.
- **Fix low end by tuning, not EQ (008):** a kick head that is a pure sine plus a bass register on A1/B1 made a 63 Hz spike no notch could fix; tuning the kick to ~72 Hz, keeping the bass register off A1/B1 in verses and giving distorted guitars low-shelf body brought the master's moves inside its limits. Measure per-stem spectra before guessing.
- **Company, not victory (008):** for "getting through" subjects, add a second voice in steps and bring the dark figure back inside the final chorus and coda; it keeps the lift honest. Robert's verdict: "the emotions it portrays are real".
- **Trance euphoria is made before the release (007):** withhold the hook (an arpeggio carries the groove), keep the first drop in a smaller room (filtered, no pad, lower), start the breakdown from almost nothing, make the bar before the release true silence (`exit silence`, not `cut`, so tails stop); the release then needs only one changed note (a sus or minor note becoming the major third).
- **In four-on-the-floor music, measure unclipped per-track peaks (007):** solo renders clip at 0 dBFS and hid a clap peaking at +6.9 dBFS on 2 and 4 with the kick, the real crest problem. Saturate last; set the kick's low-end balance before the saturator.
- **Many feelings need one constant mechanism (009):** a rhythmic cell (a 3-cycle against 4) living in the guitar figure, the hook's rhythm and the kick let every section change colour without the song falling apart; the peak can be the whole band adopting that cell instead of more loudness or a key change.
- **Put each feeling in time, not just in timbre (009):** the blur only read once the kit went half-time with a late snare; fear only once the heartbeat stopped for a bar; awe only once the pre stepped down in level instead of up. A tremolo or a minor chord alone was a label.
- **A subject can live in voice-leading (010):** "two souls become one" became audible inside one instrument: two motifs that reach the same note from opposite sides (his climb, her fall) are kept apart, then traded bar by bar, then fused as contrary-motion double stops into a unison. It read best when the band was held back for the first fused pass.
- **A climb-only arc still needs subtraction, placed inside the climb (010):** a dance that accelerates round by round has no room for a mid-track valley; removing the drummer and the push for the climax strain's first pass, then a level staircase into the peak, gave the peak somewhere to go. Ending at the peak (stop-time, one hit together) replaced the habitual wind-down outro.
- **Brief numbers are readings, not orders (010):** a tempo of 50 in a joc brief contradicted the genre's 100-140. The producer used 50 as a slow-dance door and let the dance climb to the researched range, and the climb became the dramaturgy. Standing rule 11 in docs/ORCHESTRATION.md.
- **A decay can be the form (011):** a hook that gets worse with each pass (clean, then a bad copy, then cut off) carries "something keeps being damaged" without despair, as long as the anger rises against it and the clean original gets the last word. It breaks the "last hook is the biggest" habit on purpose.
- **Give the damage one cause (011):** one foreign note (the ♭2 against the tonic) that moves from a hum to the bass to the harmony to the melody makes distortion mean something instead of shocking. A "bought" note that fits the corrupt harmony and clashes with the clean line reads as corruption, not mockery.
- **Aggression without shouting (011):** driving a snare added 4 dB and fizz (generic rage, phone static); shelving its top and pulling its post-drive level back to the clean snare's kept the harder tone without the volume.
- **Quote melodies by phrase, not by bar (011):** in sung melodies each line starts on an upbeat in the previous bar, so a quote of "bars 1-6" ended on the next line's first syllable ("bar-") followed by a gap, which Robert heard at once as a cut-off word. Reference docs now give phrase tables, and the producer prompt asks for phrase-boundary cuts.
- **A quote can be the hinge instead of the material (012):** find the one phrase whose shape already is the subject (an octave fall onto a note another voice can hold, for a reunion), write everything else yourself, and let the quote appear only where the story turns. The first plan arranged a whole carol and sounded like a rehearsal; standing rule 12 (every production unique) caught it.
- **A key change and a filter can be a place (012):** one lift placed inside a verse and tied to a change of space (dry to room, muffled to open, late to on the beat) reads as a door. The same lift before a last chorus is only a habit.
- **In beatless vocal music the top end is a casting decision (012):** give air to the group and keep a lone voice plain; measure exposed "oo" vowels on the phone view (a shelf that suits five voices is hiss on one); fill a presence gap with the accompaniment's real character (reeds in the organ) before boosting the voices.
- **Conservation keeps a subtraction from reading as a fade (013):** a hook that halves (8, 4, 2, 1 notes) held its tension only once every lost note was picked up by another sound and the top end rose across the passes. A voice replacing a saw darkens the mix by itself (its weight is at 500-1200 Hz against the saw's 3-6 kHz), so the replacement needs its own edge or the change sounds like dimming.
- **Tension without release comes from denying, not adding (013):** a roll that stops early into a dry held voice on the unresolved note, cut off by the drop; one missing beat of kick at each 8-bar boundary; no major chord but the ♭2; a kick that ends by doubling its gaps instead of a fade.
- **A mistake can be the motif (014):** one "ugly" detail (a guitar phrase crossed out with two dead strokes), taught in the first ten seconds and then counted down across the form (3, 1, 0), carries "finding the words" without a lyric. It only read once the accompaniment stopped with it and a bass note rang under the hole; pure digital silence read as a dropout.
- **The smallest section must be the clearest (014):** when the last chorus shrinks, the voice's consonants need gaps; moving the instruments off the beats that carry soft consonants recovered more words than level did.
- **Two feelings at once can be two tempos at once (015):** write the slow version at exactly 2:1 and keep the tune pentatonic, and the fast version can run inside the slow one and the slow one over the fast one without rubbing.
- **An unanswered gesture only lands if it has been answered before (015):** the final pickup had no weight until the close had asked and answered it twice.
- **Peaks come from the music, not the drums (016):** when kick and bass are constant they set the loudness line, and adding or removing hats, clap and ride moves a section by about 0.1 LU: the arc stays a ruler. Balance the rhythm section under the music first; then a bloom in the music (here an octave-up copy of the ostinato fading in over 12 bars) shapes the contour, and subtraction before a peak becomes audible.
- **A frozen moment needs level and length, or it reads as a dropout (016):** a granular freeze at defaults died in 3 s into silence; louder (+8 dB on its return) and with a 12 s `dissolve` it hung through the whole gap. Same family as 014's "pure digital silence read as a dropout".
- **Unison and feedback blur rhythm in FM (017):** a bass voice with operator feedback, unison, fold and OTT all on was a flat wash from 100 Hz to 8 kHz, and its synced index wobble never read as rhythm; with feedback 0 and a single voice each note drew clear arches and the per-note wub rates read as rhythm. Keep the core bare and articulate; put width and grit in separate layers.
- **Put roughness only in the voice that panics (017):** fast amplitude modulation (30-70 Hz) across the whole mix read as hiss; inside the one voice that screams (with the other parts thinned around it) it read as a scream.
- **How we finally got the voice right (009, 012, 013, 014; the owner, 2026-11-25: "We finally learned how to deal with voice"):** words sung by a model trained on singing (DiffSinger), so joins, timing, vibrato and breath come from one model of a singer, not from rules; research and measurement deciding every voice question, with the owner as the ear test and his notes treated as symptoms; a researched mixing guideline per genre plus docs/voice/MIXING.md; and Whisper word accuracy on the mastered mix as the check. The recurring faults were the same everywhere: a voice far wetter than its band, a dark voice carrying words, voices starting a word at different times, filters across consonants, brightening voices to chase a borrowed master curve.
- **One sound is several sources, and the target has an era and a genre (a trance producer's feedback, 2026-11-25; docs/PRODUCTION.md):** Spectre's tracks use one source per role (measured: 1-2.5 stems within 6 dB of the loudest in any band of a drop or chorus), so they are thin, not muddy, and most regions above 6 kHz hold one source or none. And the master targets were pop and rock number-ones from 1950-2010: measured against catalogue data they are 3-6 dB short in the kick-and-bass band, 3-5 dB heavy at 97-183 Hz and, for choirs and scores, 7-12 dB too bright, so producers were carving sounds to fit a wrong ruler (007's kick). Before blaming a mix, check where the target came from; before adding EQ, check whether a role is simply empty. Proposals, per-genre EQ maps and the remix plan for all 15 tracks are in PRODUCTION.md and each genre doc; nothing is adopted until it has been judged by ear.
- **A genre is several masters; know which axis splits it (granularity research, 2026-11-25):** in some genres the strand moves the target more than the era (pop rock: pop punk measures the same in 1997 and 2021; Latin pop: urbano and banda differ by 12-20 dB below 50 Hz; caroling: choir and a cappella by 20-25 dB at 50-100 Hz and 10 dB in loudness; trance: loudness by strand, not sub-era), in others the era does (hip hop: 1990s boom-bap about 4 dB quieter and darker than trap). The brief names its strand, era or region; the settings are in genres.json `master_by_strand`. Two limits hold everywhere: the open catalogue (AcousticBrainz) stops in 2022, so every 2023-2026 "newest sound" target is extrapolated, and no subdivision of any genre has true-peak, PLR or LRA data anywhere.
- **Shared modes:** Ukrainian Dorian, Romanian minor and the klezmer "Misheberak" scale are the same Dorian #4, but regionally, not nationally: the raised fourth is a Bihor, southern and lăutar trait in Romania, and the Hutsul mode in the Carpathians also raises the 7th (docs/ROMANIAN.md, docs/UKRAINIAN.md, granular sections); Hijaz / Phrygian dominant links oriental, Romanian and Ukrainian material. The key detector in the ears misreads these modes; ignore it there.

## Remix pass (2026-11-25): lessons across all 15 tracks
*Every track remastered against an era or genre target and remixed with no compositional change (events identical per track; method in docs/PRODUCTION.md "Remixing and remastering"). Numbers are from each session's journal; "gap" is the master's mean |gap| against its catalogue or reference medians (Bark bands, 32 Hz-15.5 kHz), published -> candidate.*
- **The target did most of the work.** The remaster alone (same mix, new target) closed most of the gap everywhere: 001 1.63 -> 0.71 dB, 010 2.67 -> 1.50, 012 3.94 -> 1.97, 015 2.41 -> 1.27. The mix moves mostly took the master's EQ off its limits; on the gap they added little or nothing (006 1.51 remaster, 1.53 remix; 012 1.97 -> 1.92). Make the remaster-only candidate first and keep it as the fallback.
- **Undo the old-curve carving first, in steps.** Notches at the kick's or 808's own fundamental and boosts made for the Pestana or rock curve caused most remaining misses: 007 and 013's kick cut 5-11 dB at 55-66 Hz (50-80 Hz -6.9 / -7.3 dB against the target), 011's kick -10 dB at 63 Hz and 808 -7 at 52, 008's kick -6 / -7 at 60 / 76 Hz, 002's 808 -8 at 45 Hz, 006's kick +4 at 100 Hz; in 010, 012, 014, 015 boosts at 140-200 Hz and above 4 kHz. Halve before removing: fully uncarved, 009's kick became the loudest stem under 150 Hz (master -4.4 dB at 63 Hz); 003's kick notch removed entirely sent the master to +4 at 50 Hz; 004's 41 Hz 808 notch at -3 sent it to -8 at 40 Hz (both restored).
- **Key the 808's (or bass's) ducking from every kick track, with a `dyneq` at the kick's tune** (-6 to -8 dB, cutting only when they collide) instead of a static notch: 001-006, 011, and the bass in 007, 008, 013. 011's sidechain listened to `kick` only, so where `kickX` played nothing ducked the 808 (hook3 59% masked, 88% by kickX -> 31%). A shorter kick tail under the 808 helped (001 0.45 -> 0.28 s, 011 0.35 -> 0.22 s).
- **Choose the target by measuring across all candidates.** `hiphop_2015` beat both `beat` and the Pestana curve on all six trap-family tracks (`beat` left 6.4-12 kHz 2-4.6 dB dark); for 014 `latinpop_dark` was 5-7 dB closer on top than `latinpop_ref` or `latinpop_bright`.
- **A tonal layer on a sustained cymbal can turn into static (008).** A `metalhat` layer on a ride with a 3 s ring (decay 0.9 s, partials 0.8) closed the top octave on paper (chorus 4, 8-12.5 kHz -4.9 -> 0.0 dB), and the owner heard a continuous metallic noise. The measure that showed it: 6-12 kHz energy between hits (150 ms after each hit to the next) rose 2.4-3.3 dB, the hit only 2.9 dB over its ring (4.0 before). Decay 0.2 s, partials 0.3, -16 dB made it a shimmer on the hit; the top paid 0.7 dB. Judge such layers by ear and between hits, not by the band average.
- **A tonal hat layer rarely takes the air from the snare** (001: hats +5 dB, the snare still owns the top; 005: the metalhat's energy sits mostly under 6 kHz). It worked as a second air source in 011 (stack +3 dB) and 007.
- **`--clarity` sums to mono and reads stems before buses.** Hard-panned guitars count as masked (009: 48-65% under the voice, unmoved by EQ slots), and bus processing (008's snare-keyed guitar dip) has to be measured on the mix.
- **Whisper: medium.en for A/B decisions.** small.en swung 20-30 points on one chorus between versions; medium.en repeated within a word or two (009). On a marginal line a 0.3 s window shift flips the result (013): read three window offsets (012).
- **Gated loudness misreads one-hit-plus-tail sections.** 009's coda read -3.0 LU against the published master; second by second the hit was 0.5-0.9 LU lower and the tail 0.3 higher. Same for 015's `again` (+1.0 LU, two notes) and 010's ring-out. Check those second by second before acting on the 1 LU guide.
- **The new targets move the contour, not only the mix:** sections without kick or 808 drop as the low end rises around them (003 march -1.0, 004 push -1.1, 005 cost -1.4, 006 night -0.9 LU); lone or sparse sections with little 100-250 Hz rise (008 intro +0.8, 011 end +0.8, 012 street / door +1.2 / +1.4). Hold them with gains on instruments that play in few other sections (005: count their events per section first), or accept and say so.
- **Leave a folk band's 150-400 Hz crowd to the arrangement (003-006).** 004's nai cut -3 dB at 350 Hz bought 1 dB on the spectrum and cost its own section 0.9 LU.
- **Stacks can flatten transients (007):** OTT on lead and pad plus a sustained kick sub took mid crest to 4.5 dB; OTT off, a shorter sub tail and a driven punch layer got it back except low-mid (2 dB softer: the stacks fill between kicks).
- **Frozen voices cap a remix (009, 013).** With the approved voice mix untouched, the masking under the voice could not move (009), and 013's "bigger drops" were a miss: cliffs 12.0 / 12.8 LU against 12.5 / 13.4 published, because raising the band cost the main call (31 -> 28 of 36 words). The real fix is section automation, which is arrangement.
- **A move that measures as nothing comes out again (012):** undoing the organ's top boosts left the master's 4-6 kHz cut unchanged (the top was the voices and bells), so they were restored.
- **One master EQ follows the loudest section (015):** bringing the swell onto the curve left the opening darker than published (2-5 kHz -0.7 -> -4.2 dB).
- **Per session, only what was specific:** 006: the remix separated the 808 but closed no gap beyond the remaster; the remaster alone is the simpler release if the A/B hears no difference. 007: in three steps the top went -4.5 (published) -> -4.2 (remaster) -> -1.4 (role EQ: the lead's own harmonics) -> -0.3 dB (stacks); the outro (kick and hats alone) went 8-9 dB over the curve on the phone check with brighter hats or click. 008: retuning the kick sub to the key's root E1 was a miss (40 Hz to its limit, 80 Hz stuck: kick head plus the bass's E2 on every downbeat); on A1 at -6 dB it fitted. 011: a project older than `"seeds": "per-track"` shifts every later track's random draw when layers are added (intro -0.1 LU, envelope correlation 0.95; accepted). 012: misses accepted: arc 1.2 LU flatter (LRA 6.4 -> 5.2), 97-183 Hz still 4-5 dB over the albums, the master still taking up to 7.9 dB off 4-6 kHz, -14 LUFS 5 LU over the albums' median (owner's call). 014: taking down the bass's 280 Hz and the guitars' 330 Hz shelves deepened the guitar's plate-mode hole at 250-315 Hz (master to its limit at 315; restored): an instrument matter; and a darker target makes the same breath measure worse (inhale +7.1 dB over the curve, unchanged audio).

## Session 016: "The Tide Doesn't Stop" (briefed: modern trance extended mix, infinity and beautiful impermanence)
- **Result:** 6:45 (228 bars, 6:42 plus the render tail), 136 bpm, E Dorian, progressive / melodic trance with deep-trance patience (2018-2026 sub-era), -9.1 LUFS, -1.03 dBTP (master v2, `--genre trance --target trance_2018`), no warnings; 25 renders on Claude Opus 5.5. Spectre's third trance track and the first extended mix of the modern era; no voice. The EQ sits at its limit only at 31.5 Hz: nothing below 40 Hz by choice (kick on G1). Not yet approved by the owner.
- **What worked (producer's view):** a five-note arp cycle in continuous 16ths that never restarts, against accents fixed to the bar in 3+3+2+3+3+2, so the accented notes make a melody that is always the same material and never the same line; the climb E - F♯ - G - A that always falls back, refused once by a Cmaj7 (Lydian light over the same five notes) in bd2; the one stop of the arp, its last bar frozen in the granular cloud, and its return alone at the point in the cycle it would have reached had it never stopped; peaks made by widening (no roll, no riser, no silence), a staggered return, and an ending where the arp walks on after the drums.
- **Peaks come from the music, not the drums:** with kick and bass constant from bar 17 to 200, taking out or adding hats, clap and ride moved section loudness by about 0.1 LU, and the first full render was a flat ruler (intro2 to peak1 -9.6 to -10.0 LUFS). The arc appeared only once the music bloomed (an octave-up copy of the ostinato fading in over 12 bars, raised to about 2-4 dB under the main arp) and the low end was rebalanced (rolling bass, kick and the breakdown's sub down). Balance the rhythm section before arranging by subtraction: a subtraction can only be heard against a level the music, not the kick, sets.
- **A freeze needs level and length to read as a moment:** a granular freeze at defaults died in about 3 s and left 4 s of black, which read as a dropout; +8 dB on the cloud return in that section and `dissolve` 12 s made the frozen bar hang through the whole gap and still be fading when the arp returned.
- **Tune the kick's knock into the hole (008, 017 again):** after the kick body came down, the master's EQ sat at its +4 limit at 80 Hz, with the knock (tuned 90 Hz) sitting on 100; retuning the knock to 78 Hz filled the hole (peak1 80 Hz -3.8 -> -1.0 dB) and the master left its limit, which no EQ move had done.
- **Process under a busy machine:** use light checks: per-section analysis from the meta (`stems.py`, `sections.py`), zooms, a 1/3-octave premaster check (`tonal.py`) and `--clarity` on one 8-bar window. A full overview listen ran 25+ minutes while other lanes rendered, before the 2026-11-26 speed fix (engine commit 3adad6e: renders about 3x faster, the overview listen from minutes to seconds).
- **What didn't:** the C surprise is light, not level (bd1 -12.4 -> bd2 -11.2 LUFS): it lives in the harmony and the brightness; peak2 ends 0.2 LU louder than peak1 (-7.9 vs -8.1) against the plan "wider, not louder" (it is wider, 0.30 vs 0.29, and has the theme); the ears still call lift = theme2 and peak2 = peak2a (the differences are a clap, a filter and the octave's bloom); intro1 is still about 3 dB bright on the phone check (the kick's click, left as the DJ's reference); the first two minutes rely on change inside sounds more than on arrivals.

## Session 017: "Captive" (briefed: modern dubstep, the soul held captive)
- **Result:** 3:53, 140 bpm half time, F♯ Phrygian, a deep / 140 frame carrying tearout in drop 1 and riddim in drop 2, -8.7 LUFS, -1.03 dBTP (master v2, `--genre dubstep --lufs -8.5`, target `dubstep_2015`), no warnings; 21 renders on Claude Opus 5.5. The first dubstep track; one short `svs` phrase (`hanami-soft`, "still here"). The EQ sits at its limit only at 31.5-40 Hz, below the root F♯1 (no note was written there to please the curve). Not yet approved by the owner.
- **What worked (producer's view):** see "Across genres": the bare FM wub as the captor, roughness inside the panicking voice. The energy falling on purpose (the peak in the first third, drop 2 quieter and simpler, an outro by removal) carried acceptance; the surprises measured as planned: drop 2's empty answer bars 14 dB under drop 1's answers at 400 Hz-2 kHz, the panic cut under -90 dB for two beats on the master, and "here" cut at the drop and finished in the breakdown.
- **Tune before you EQ, again (008):** the kick's sub layer and the sub both on F♯1 made one spike at 46-50 Hz and put the master's EQ at its limit at 50-100 Hz; retuning only the kick's sub layer to the fifth (C♯2, 69 Hz) gave the kick 63-80 Hz and the sub 46 Hz, and the master stayed inside its limits from 50 Hz up.
- **Gain on a track feeding a clipped bus barely moves it:** +3 dB on kick, bite, crack and hats moved the drop's bands by 0.3-0.5 dB; a final `gain` fx at the end of a stack's chain did what was asked.
- **What didn't:** drop 2 only 1.1 LU under drop 1 (planned 1.5-2); on small speakers the first step of the absence (mostly the motif leaving) reads smaller than on a system; the captor's clarity stack view came back empty in the last runs, so its top layer was judged only by the mix's band numbers.
- **Engine quirks:** the overview listen's 400 ms loudness curve is a direct convolution (ear.py, around line 164): 12+ minutes on 69 s of audio (an FFT convolution, the session's `fastlisten.py`, takes 9 s; an engine fix is in progress); section `auto` `lpf`/`hpf` ramps crash with a "log" third value; a `singer` part without lyrics sings an endless "ah", and a sung phrase rings past its section unless the voice is muted in the next; a granular freeze needs `position_ms` set back into the material when the section before it ends in a rest.

## Session 015: "Again, Again" (briefed: childhood, style of Spectre's choice)
- **Result:** 3:44, a waltz in D major, pentatonic tune, -15.0 LUFS (master v2, `--genre chamberscore`), 28 renders on Claude Fable 5.2. ## Session 001: "Bleachers" (feeling: defiance)
- **Outcome:** the track came across closer to *longing* than defiance; Robert's feedback was that it "didn't feel aggressive enough". A near miss.
- **Why:** the core idea was "minor melodies that climb back up every time the chords pull them down". The producer meant it as refusal, but upward-reaching minor melodies are the textbook shape of yearning. The bridge's strings sinking by half-steps pushed further toward ache. The synthesized brass and organ lacked bite.
- **Lesson 1:** attitude lives in the sound (distortion, roughness, hard transients, density), not only in the notes. The ears can verify structure but not attitude.
- **Lesson 2:** at every stage, ask what the nearest wrong feeling is and what separates the two.
- **Lesson 3 (mix and master):** the 808 fundamental sat about 17 dB above the midrange; on MacBook speakers this sounded "clunky". This led to the mastering stage (`docs/MASTERING.md`) and the rule "get weight from saturation and mids, not sub; if the master has to cut near 8 dB, fix the mix."
- **Engine changes after this session:** premaster export, `master.py`, the `master` CLI command, manual updates.
