# Latvian folk for trap: a working brief

For sessions that build on Latvian traditional music. A vocabulary, not a rulebook. The producer may research further on the web.

## The tradition
- **Dainas**: the core of Latvian folk song. Very short texts (usually four lines) in trochaic or dactylic metre, with pre-Christian themes and legends. Krišjānis Barons collected about 300,000 texts and variants (Latvju dainas, 1894-1915). Melodies are short, repetitive, modal, often within a narrow range, and repeated many times.
- **Drone singing**: a lead singer sings a line, others repeat it, and a vocal drone is held underneath. A 16th-century writer described Livonian singing as "howling like wolves": the earliest record of the drone tradition, still practised today.
- **Choirs**: Latvia's choir tradition is very strong. The Song and Dance Festival brings tens of thousands of singers together every five years.

## Instruments (and their engine names)
- **Kokle**: box zither, "the singing tree", diatonic, with low strings used as drones. The best-known Latvian instrument; reborn in the folklore movement of the 1970s-80s → `kokle`
- **Dūdas**: bagpipe, played since about the 16th century, mostly in Kurzeme → `dudas` (with `drone`)
- **Trīdeksnis**: a staff hung with metal plates and rings, struck or shaken → `tridksnis`
- **Taure / ganurags**: birch-bark trumpet and shepherd's horn → `trembita` (shorter, brighter)
- **Stabule**: pipe → `nai` or `ney`
- **Ģīga** (bowed zither), fiddle → `fiddle` or `lira`
- **Bungas** (drum), **sietiņš / bubins** (tambourine), **vargans** (Jew's harp)
- Voices: `choir` for drone and group singing, open fifths, unison lines

## Modes and rhythm
- Mostly diatonic and modal: Dorian, Aeolian, Mixolydian and Ionian, often in a narrow range (a fourth or fifth), with a drone on the tonic or fifth. Archaic songs use 3-4 note cells. No augmented seconds (unlike the Balkan and Ukrainian material). *(Mostly right; archaic songs with a diminished fourth and later ones with an augmented fourth are documented [MFS495], and Latgale's homophonic singing "majorizes" modal tunes [Beitāne 2016].)*
- Short repeating phrases (two-bar melodies repeated many times) suit trap loops directly.
- Even metres (2/4, 3/4, 6/8). The trochaic text metre gives strong-weak downbeat accents. *Qualified (2026-11-25): the group (choir) sections of the old recited songs run in a loose 2/4 under the trochee, but the soloist's sections were sung in strict, loose or free metre (senza metro) [Boiko 2025], and sung songs with shifting metres (5/8, 5/4, 7/8, 2/4+3/4) are documented [MFS495]. See "Granularity".*

## Revolution: the Singing Revolution
The Baltic independence movement of 1987-1991 is known as the **Singing Revolution**: songs were the unifying symbol and the nonviolent weapon. Choirs at Soviet-era song festivals would add a familiar folk song after the official programme that became a song of resistance. On 23 August 1989 about two million people formed the **Baltic Way**, a human chain from Tallinn to Vilnius, singing. Folk ensembles took part in the mass rallies and the January 1991 barricades in Riga.
A revolution in Latvian terms can mean: the many against the machine, unarmed voices that refuse to stop singing, a chain of people that grows. It doesn't have to be violent. Nearest wrong feelings: rebellion (session 003: the hot street fight), rage (002), war (005), triumphal anthem, pastoral idyll ("blonde maidens dreamily strumming the kokle" is the cliché).

## The modern reference
Skyforger (Latvian folk/pagan metal: kokle, dūdas, birch trumpet and folk singing ensembles with heavy metal), Tautumeitas (six singers: Latvian folk melodies and lyrics with powerful harmonies over modern beats and production).

## What session 006 learned
- Suiti drone singing (caller, a "bender" repeating the same words, drone singers holding one tone) works as a trap hook as it is: let the chain grow each hook.
- 8th-note triplets in the hats and the trīdeksnis give the crowd's sway; the trīdeksnis jingles ring about 0.4 s above 5 kHz, so keep it off the snare (beat 1 only) or it glitters into a crash.
- Strike the kokle (low damped strings, open-fifth strums); arpeggios turn it into a pastoral postcard.
- Drone, voices and kokle all crowd 300-400 Hz; cut that band per source before mastering.
- The taure's climbing harmonics make a good call between sections.

## Granularity: regions, styles, eras and the current practice (researched 2026-11-25; docs/PRODUCTION.md section 5, item 10)
The folk side of this brief, split along the dimensions in which Latvian traditional music really varies. Source keys in [brackets] are listed at the end of this section; [H] marks a hand-tuned value. The trap side is a separate brief.

### The dimensions, and why the boundaries are where they are
1. **Region.** Latvia's historical lands developed apart after 1561: Kurzeme (Duchy of Courland), Vidzeme (Swedish, then Russian Livland) and Latgale (Polish-Lithuanian), mostly Lutheran except Catholic Latgale and a western Kurzeme enclave (the Suiti) [Muk98]. The musical boundary follows confession and isolation more than the map: "major differences in musical style and repertoire exist between Protestant Vidzeme and Kurzeme on the one hand and Catholic Latgale on the other"; traditional singing survived far better in Latgale and western Kurzeme, while Vidzeme and most of Kurzeme moved to "modern lyrical and other popular styles" [Muk98]. Drone singing is documented across Latvia "but mostly in the south", "almost never around the Gulf of Riga and the northern part of Kurzeme and Vidzeme" [Kanons-drone]. Functional-harmonic homophony lives "chiefly ... in the Catholic south-east, with a small 'island' ... in the south-west" (Boiko in New Grove, quoted in [Beitāne]). So the useful zones are: **south and south-west Kurzeme with the Suiti** (drone), **Latgale, north and north-east** (drone plus the high "half part"), **Latgale and Augšzeme/Sēlija in the south-east** (thirds, homophony), **Vidzeme and northern Kurzeme** (the later, lyrical and choral layer), **Zemgale** (thin data: recited songs are documented; nothing distinctive found).
2. **Style (inside every region): recited versus sung.** Jurjāns (1894) divided Latvian folk song into speech-like and song-like melodies; the recited style (text over melody, narrow range, a soloist plus a group repeat and drone) is "the dominant style of the older layer", the sung style is mostly solo with a range that can exceed an octave [Boiko25], [Muk98]. A brief that names a region should also name the style.
3. **Timeline and scene.** Village tradition; the song-festival choir nation (from 1873); the Soviet stage folklore (1940s-1980s); the folklore movement (late 1970s) and the Singing Revolution; post-folklore (1990s-2000s); the 2015-2026 folk-pop and electronic scene (below).

### The shared core
- **The daina:** a self-contained quatrain of two unrhymed couplets; each couplet or line usually repeated when sung; quatrains chained freely by the soloist, so singing can last for hours [Muk98]. **shared**
- **Text metre:** the dipodic trochee, with the dactylic tetrameter as the second standard metre [Boiko25]; trochee in most dainas [Daina]. Strong-weak pairs land on the musical beats in the group sections. **shared**
- **A soloist leads, a group answers:** at least one recognised soloist starts; the group repeats and/or drones [Muk98]. **shared** (texture varies)
- **Refrain songs and the calendar:** līgotnes with the "līgo" refrain for midsummer (Jāņi), rotāšana songs ending in "rotā", wedding antiphony (apdziedāšanās: two groups teasing each other in turn, improvising the words) [Muk98], [Kanons-rot]. **shared**
- **Narrow, modal, repetitive melodies** in the old layer (3-4 tones in the most archaic songs) [MFS495], [Boiko25]. **shared** in the old layer; the sung and later layers vary.
- **The kokle as the emblem**, diatonic with drone strings; strummed with the right index finger while the left hand damps strings [Kokle], [Muk98]. **shared** (the type varies by region)

**South and south-west Kurzeme, the Suiti (Alsunga; Nīca and Bārta parishes)**

**Latgale (the Catholic east; north, north-east and south-east)**
| element | value | shared/varies | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| preservation | traditional singing "preserved much better" than elsewhere; the funeral vigil singing (vakēšana) survives only here | varies | [Muk98] |
| texture, north | drone singing and the **"half part" (pusbolss)**: a single woman with a "particularly high, penetrating voice" takes a solo upper part that enters after the half-cadence or just before the cadence, never at the start; in thirds to the main part or an octave above the lower part; 3-4 parts; "performed powerfully, at a slow tempo and often in the open air" | varies (north and north-east Latgale) | [Beitāne], quoting Boiko 2000 |
| texture, whole region | singing in **thirds with the melody on top**, influenced by liturgical singing; homophony that "majorizes" modal tunes; free harmonisation that can make three separate melodies | varies | [Beitāne], [MFS495] |
| village style | the Auleja women's **bolsi** style, unique to Auleja and different from the rest of Latgale (Aulejas sievas, founded 1940) | varies | [Auleja] |
| instruments | **Latgale kokle**: larger and heavier, with a "wing", up to 12 strings, held upright against the stomach; some players put fingers between the strings | varies | [Kokle] |

**Augšzeme and Sēlija (the south-east, along the Daugava)**
| element | value | shared/varies | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| texture | homophonic multipart singing as in Latgale ("Latgale and Augšzeme") | varies | [Beitāne] |
| recited songs | a recited melody with a *rudimentary* drone (Dignāja): first singer, second singer, and an older woman who "only holds [the drone] along" | varies | [Boiko25] |
| drone | earliest records of the bagpipe drone include southern Zemgale and Sēlija; the syllabic drone is frequent in Sēlija | varies | [Kanons-drone] |
| revival | the group Saucējas revived Sēlija's singing | | [Kanons-teik] |

**Vidzeme and northern Kurzeme (the Lutheran centre and north)**
| element | value | shared/varies | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| texture | drone "almost never"; the later lyrical and popular styles and choir harmonisation dominate | varies | [Kanons-drone], [Muk98] |
| recited songs | documented in eastern and south-eastern Vidzeme (Tirza, Sarkaņi, Lejasciems), so the old layer existed here too | varies | [Boiko25] |
| dance | ziga, a ring dance of Vidzeme and Latgale (late 19th century), usually with the violin | varies | [MFS495] |
| bagpipe | the dūdas tradition was destroyed mostly in Vidzeme (the Moravian Brethren); playing banned from 1753 | varies | [Dudas] |

**Zemgale:** recited lullabies and a flax-harvest recitative are documented (Skaistkalne) [Boiko25]; nothing region-specific beyond that was found: **thin**.

**Metre and mode across regions (old layer):** group sections in a loose 2/4 under the trochee; solo sections strict, loose or free, free metre becoming rarer in younger singers and newer recordings [Boiko25]. Sung songs with shifting metres (5/4, 5/8, 7/8, 2/4+3/4, 4/8+5/8) are reported, and straight 2/4 or 4/4 in the minority, with 3/4 a little more common (liner notes of the Latvian Folk Ensemble of New York, an arranging ensemble, so weaker evidence) [MFS495]. Archaic tunes of four tones; one with a diminished fourth, one with an augmented fourth; later songs triadic with parallel thirds [MFS495].

### Current practice (2023-2026): a checklist for briefs that ask for the newest sound
| element | where it comes from | engine today |
|---|---|---|
| a regional vocal style named and kept recognisable: Suiti teikšana with its drone, Latgalian thirds with the high half part, Auleja bolsi | Tautumeitas; [Beitāne], [Kanons-teik] | **partly**: `choir` and `vox2` for the drone and the parts; no Latvian-language singer |
| a female vocal ensemble as the lead instrument, several voices with distinct roles (caller, repeater, drone, high half part) | Tautumeitas | **by hand**: one track per role; the high penetrating solo voice is missing (no hard or bright folk voice) |
| drone as a sung vowel ("ē", "ē-o") rather than a synth pad | [Muk98], [Kanons-drone] | yes: `vox2` with vowel changes, `choir` |
| kokle strummed and damped, Kurzeme (few strings, on the lap) or Latgale (larger) colour | [Kokle] | yes: `kokle` (strums with `damped`; no type switch) |
| massed bagpipes and big drums ("etnotranss") | Auļi | **partly**: `dudas` (one drone); big drums via `tom`/`toba`; no tree-trunk drum |
| violin, mandolin, clarinet and flute colour in a pop band | Tautumeitas | violin (`violin`, `fiddle`) and flute (`ney`, `nai`): yes; mandolin and clarinet: **missing** |
| pop production with a club-remix afterlife | Tautumeitas 2024-2025 | yes: the engine's pop and trance tools |
| the trīdeksnis and shaken jingles in the ritual role | [Muk98] | yes: `tridksnis` |
| shifting or free metre in the solo sections | [Boiko25], [MFS495] | **partly**: free timing via fractional steps and tempo ramps; a pattern bar is always four beats, so 5/8 or 7/8 bars are not native (docs/MANUAL.md "Other meters") |

**Missing for this tradition:** vargans (jew's harp; also wanted for Ukraine), ģīga (bowed zither), stabule and other shepherd's pipes as their own models (`nai`/`ney` approximate), goat-horn and birch-bark taure as a short horn (`trembita` approximates), bungas and tree-trunk drums, a second kokle type, accordion. **Voices:** no DiffSinger bank sings Latvian; no hard, penetrating high folk voice ([docs/voice/VOICES.md](voice/VOICES.md) "What is missing"); the Lotte V voices forbid political use, which a "revolution" brief may touch (VOICES.md licence table).
