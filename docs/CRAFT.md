# Beat craft: a working brief

_Rooted in hip-hop and trap production, where Spectre started; the genre briefs in `docs/` extend it to other traditions._

Distilled from production guides, producer interviews and music-emotion research. This is a starting vocabulary, not a rulebook; the feeling decides when to break it.

## 1. Groove is the first emotion

- **Swing.** In hip-hop the even 16ths (the "e" and "a") get pushed late. 50% = straight. Lo-fi / boom-bap sits around 55–60%; heavy, lazy pocket 62–66%; trap is mostly straight (50–52%), with swing reserved for hat rolls.
- **Pocket and micro-timing.** Snare 5–15 ms late = laid-back, head-nodding. Kick a little early = urgency, forward lean. Dilla time: different elements on different feels at once (straight hats over swung kicks, a snare that drags, a kick that rushes 20–30 ms). It reads as human, unsteady and intimate.
- **Velocity.** No two hits are equal. Main hits around 0.85–1.0, offbeat hats lower, ghost snares around 0.3–0.45 (usually the 16th before or after the backbeat). Shape velocity as a phrase (a curve across the bar), don't just randomize it.
- **Space.** What you leave out is a decision. A missing kick on the 1 after a fill hits harder than a kick.

## 2. Drum vocabulary

- **Boom-bap** (85–95 bpm): kick on 1 and the "and" of 2 or similar syncopation, snare on 2 and 4, swung 8th or 16th hats, ghost notes, dusty and compressed. Nostalgic, grounded, cerebral.
- **Lo-fi** (70–90 bpm): soft and swung, rimshots instead of snares, muffled kick, vinyl and tape. Warm, private, wistful.
- **Trap** (130–160 bpm, felt in half-time at 65–80): snare or clap on beat 3 of each bar, hats on 8ths or 16ths with rolls (1/32, 1/16 triplets, 1/64 bursts) and pitched rolls. The 808 carries the bass and often the melody. Cold, menacing, flexing, numb or euphoric depending on the melody.
- **Drill** (140–145): sliding 808s, syncopated kicks and snares (snare on 3 plus an off-placed one), triplet hats. Tension, threat.
- **Rolls point at something.** A hat roll accents the beat it lands on. Use rolls to throw weight forward, not as decoration.
- **Layering.** Clap a few ms ahead of the snare = a bigger attack. Never stack two kicks that fight in the low end.

## 3. The 808 and the low end

- Tune the 808 to the key; it's a bass instrument, not a drum. Long decay = weight and dread; short = bounce.
- Glide (portamento) between notes = the signature slide. Sliding up = lift and swagger; down = sinking.
- Saturation gives the 808 harmonics so it's audible on small speakers; too much = aggression.
- Kick vs 808: either the kick is short and clicky and the 808 follows it, or sidechain the 808 under the kick. Don't let both own 50–60 Hz at the same instant.
- Sometimes the most powerful 808 moment is the silence right before it returns.

## 4. Harmony and melody (how the feeling gets its color)

- Mode and tempo are the two strongest emotion cues in research (Eerola et al. 2013; Micallef-Grimaud 2022), followed by register, dynamics, articulation and timbre.
- **Minor, slow, low register, soft, legato, dark timbre** → sadness. **Major, faster, higher, brighter, staccato** → joy. **Fast, loud, rough timbre, dissonance** → anger. **Unstable harmony, sudden dynamics, high register tension, sparse low drones** → fear.
- Hip-hop borrows jazz and soul harmony: m7, m9, maj7, add9, 7b9. Sevenths and ninths give "subtle sadness" or warmth without melodrama.
- Useful progressions:
  - i – VI (dark, cyclical): Am – F
  - i – iv – VI – V or i – VI – III – VII (sad, cinematic)
  - i – bII (Phrygian, menacing, drill/trap)
  - ii9 – V13 – Imaj9 (soulful, warm, resolved)
  - i9 – iv9 looping (lo-fi melancholy, unresolved)
  - Imaj7 – iii7 – vi7 – IVmaj7 (tender, bright-sad)
- Loops are 2, 4 or 8 bars. A hip-hop loop rarely resolves fully; the unresolved loop is the engine.
- Melody: a 2-bar motif that repeats with small changes. Simple beats complex. Leave room for a vocal in the 300 Hz–3 kHz zone during verses.

## 5. Sound and texture

- Timbre carries emotion: felt or electric pianos = intimacy and nostalgia; bells and music boxes = childhood and eerie fragility; choirs and strings = scale and grief; detuned synth leads = unease or euphoria; distorted 808 = aggression.
- "Sampled" sound: low-pass, sample-rate and bit reduction, wow and flutter, vinyl crackle, tape hiss. Pitched down = heavier and sadder; pitched up = chipmunk soul and nostalgia-joy.
- Reverb = distance and memory; dry = intimacy and confrontation. Delay throws fill space at phrase ends.
- Slight detune and imperfection make it human.

## 6. Arrangement (a "normal length" beat is about 2:30–3:30)

- Common form: Intro (4–8) – Hook (8) – Verse (16) – Hook (8) – Verse (16) – Hook (8) – [Bridge (8)] – Hook (8) – Outro (4–8). Everything in multiples of 4 bars.
- Intro: fewer elements, often the melody alone or filtered. Verse: a simpler beat that leaves room for a rapper. Hook: the fullest version of the beat.
- Change something every 4–8 bars: add or remove a layer, a hat variation, an 808 pattern flip, a counter-melody. A 16-bar verse with zero change is dead air.

## 7. Transitions: where the feeling moves

- **Drop-out:** cut drums or the 808 for the last beat or bar before a section. Silence = anticipation; the return = release.
- **Filter sweep:** low-pass the music for the last bars of a section and open it up on the downbeat (or close it for an exit).
- **Riser / reverse cymbal / reversed note** into the downbeat = lift. **Downlifter / impact** on the downbeat = weight.
- **Snare or hat roll** in the last half-bar = build.
- **808 stop**, then a slide back in on the hook.
- **Tape stop** = collapse, ending, exhaustion.
- **Half-time switch** (snare moves from 2 and 4 to 3) = weight drops and time slows: sadness, heaviness, swagger.
- **Bridge or switch-up**: a new chord, beat flip or key lift for the last section = a turn in the story.
- **Emotional mapping of transitions:** build and release = hope, triumph. Drop-out with no return = loss. Endlessly delayed resolution = longing. Abrupt cuts = shock and anger. Gradual erosion (elements leave one by one) = fading, memory, grief.

## 8. Mix and master

- Kick and snare are the loudest elements after any vocal; the 808 is felt more than heard; melodic elements sit under the drums in hip-hop.
- High-pass everything that isn't kick or bass (around 100–150 Hz).
- Glue compression on the drum bus; light saturation on the master; limiter to around -9 to -11 LUFS for modern hip-hop, -12 to -14 for lo-fi.
- Check in mono and low-passed: the groove should still hit.
