# Cinematic hybrid / epic for Spectre: a working brief

For sessions that make cinematic hybrid music: the "epic" orchestral-electronic idiom of film trailers, trailer-house albums, blockbuster and game scores, in its modern era. A vocabulary, not a rulebook. Spectre has made a whimsical chamber score (docs/CHAMBER_SCORE.md, where this idiom was measured once as a contrast) and trance and dubstep builds; this genre takes the orchestra of the first and the build-and-drop weight of the others, and its centre is different from both: **scale**. A small motif, stated by few, is answered by everyone; the music is written as a dramatic arc in acts, and its sound is an orchestra made larger than a concert hall by electronic low end, percussion layers and sound design. Researched 2026-11-26 for queue item 13 (docs/ORCHESTRATION.md: "united we stand, divided we fall", Ode to Joy passed between European folk colours). Composers, trailer houses and films are named as craft schools only, never as tracks to copy (standing rule 13).

Labels as in docs/PRODUCTION.md: **[M]** measured (a published measurement, or one made here from open data, method stated), **[S]** a named source, **[H]** hand-tuned or a judgement of this brief.

## The tradition and the timeline
Trailer music is production music for film previews: trailers are cut before the score exists, so editors use other scores, classical works, songs and **library cues written for trailers** ([Wikipedia, Trailer music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trailer_music); it describes the field as known for a "sound-design driven and hybrid orchestral style") [S]. Specialist trailer houses (Immediate Music, X-Ray Dog, Two Steps From Hell, Audiomachine, Brand X Music, later Really Slow Motion, Hidden Citizens and many more) wrote cues in three acts for editors; from about 2006-2010 they also released public albums, and an "epic music" listening audience grew around them (Globus's *Epicon* 2006, Two Steps From Hell's *Invincible* 2010 and *Archangel* 2011, Bergersen's *Illusions* 2011; [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/) release dates) [S]. In the same decade blockbuster scores turned hybrid (Zimmer, Jablonsky, Junkie XL, Balfe, Djawadi): orchestra plus synth bass, drum ensembles and sound design. Game scoring (Halo, Skyrim, God of War, Battlefield) took the same tools and added folk instruments as setting.

**The shared core** (holds in every subdivision; lose it and the music stops being "epic") [S, H]:
1. A dramatic arc in acts, with hard boundaries and a climax that is the loudest, densest and highest moment, prepared by a thinning just before it.
2. A short, singable theme or motif that grows from few players to everyone.
3. Momentum from repetition: ostinati (strings, pulses, percussion) under sustained or slow-moving melody.
4. Big low drums and a heavy low end; brass as the voice of power.
5. Scale beyond the concert hall: layers, width and a long space, and a master that holds the climax near full scale.

## Craft, element by element
**Act structure** [S unless marked]: setup (sparse, a motif or a solo, ambience), build (rhythm and density rising, a pulse or ostinato appears and climbs a register at a time), climax (everyone, the theme at full scale); between them hard cuts, a hit, or a bar of silence; the lead-in to the climax nearly empty of bass (Rare Form, UJAM, SOS above). Editors lay a "music bed" of about three cues, one per act (Filmmaker Magazine via search summary; [Filmmaker 2020](https://filmmakermagazine.com/?p=110546), not opened) [S, weak]. For a standalone track the three acts can carry a fourth movement (a coda, or the theme returning quietly) [H].

**Rhythmic ostinati** [S, H]: strings in 16th-note spiccato or marcato figures on one or two pitches, doubled in octaves; low strings and piano doubling the lowest note; a synth arpeggio or pulse locked to the same grid in the hybrid strand; the "short, marcato strings" pushed forward with saturation so they "stick above percussion" (Output, Greg Townley: [Output](https://shop.output.com/?p=1508599)); a transient designer on spiccato and staccato strings (epicomposer 2026). The 2017-2026 variant replaces the string ostinato with a tick or pulse (the *Dunkirk* watch).

**Percussion** [S]: drum ensembles recorded or sampled as rooms full of drums: Junkie XL's *Fury Road* percussion became a sample library ("the heart of his library", [Sound On Sound review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/orchestral-tools-tom-holkenborgs-percussion)); trailer drums "similarly bombastic" across blockbuster previews ([Vice 2014](https://www.vice.com/en/article/the-secret-to-successful-sound-in-movie-trailers/)); stacked hits with one layer moved 10-25 ms so they read as an ensemble, not a sample (epicomposer 2026). Roles [H, from the instrumentation]: taiko / large drums (the body, 50-150 Hz, room), toms (the patterns), timpani (pitched, rolls and the tonic-dominant hits of the climax), snare ensembles (military colour, a war-film risk: see "Nearest wrong feelings"), cymbal swells (suspended cymbal rolls into downbeats), electronic impacts and sub booms (the transient and the floor), risers, reverse swells and "whooshes" (the transitions).

**Brass** [S, H]: horns carry the theme ("the brass section, especially the French horns, often carries the main melody", epicomposer 2026), usually 4-8 in unison or octaves (heroic), stopped or *cuivré* for menace; trombones and tuba as low chords, swells and the braam's acoustic layer; trumpets for fanfares and the top of the climax (Beethoven adds trumpets and timpani only for the full statement of the theme at bar 164, below). Brass competes with the heaviest percussion and hybrid layers, so it is given slight saturation and separated by EQ and level (epicomposer 2026). Ranges (sounding; [Wikipedia: French horn, Trumpet, Trombone, Tuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_horn)): horn about B1-F5, trumpet in B♭ about E3-B♭5, tenor trombone E2-F5 (pedals lower), bass trombone to B♭1, tuba about D1-F4 [S].

**Choir** [S, H]: the 2000s sound is a large choir chanting (*Carmina Burana*, Verdi's *Dies irae* in the *Fury Road* teaser, Trailaurality 2017); in the hybrid strand the choir is a texture and a hit ("ah" stabs on downbeats); in 2017-2026 solo and processed voices (*Dune*: "the only thing that will survive across centuries ... the human voice"). For Spectre: see "Voices".

**Synth layers and bass** [S, H]: a sub and sub drops under the drums; braams (low brass with a distorted synth layer and a sub); pulses and arpeggios; pads under the strings; risers, Shepard-tone rises, reverse swells; distorted bass in the action strands (Fury Road's guitars and "orchestra on speed": [Motion Picture Association interview](https://www.motionpictures.org/?p=71787)).

**Harmony** [S]: Aeolian minor and modal loops for struggle; the canonical struggle-to-triumph is the **minor-to-major turn on the same tonic** (Beethoven's 9th itself: D minor to the D major Ode); Hollywood's climactic cadences include the "Picardy-aeolian" ♭VI-♭VII-I (Steiner's *Key Largo*, its "triumphal conclusion"), the mixed plagal cadence with its "transcendent" Romantic associations, and chromatically modulating cadences (the dominant resolving to a chromatic-mediant key, "treated as a new tonic") that Lehman ties to "wonderment" ([Lehman, Hollywood Cadences, MTO 19.4, 2013](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.13.19.4/mto.13.19.4.lehman.php)); Zimmer's *Man of Steel* I-♭VI-IV with polytonal tension for heroism ([Hooktheory](https://www.hooktheory.com/blog/cinematic-chord-progressions/)). In the catalogue the minor share falls from 61% to 48-53% after 2010 [M]: the modern idiom spends more time in major (or modal major) than the choir era.

**Mixing** [S]: trailer mixes are wider than an orchestra ("not about recreating the experience of a concert hall ... about maximum impact and scale": supporting strings, pulses, textures and hybrid percussion panned well beyond their orchestral seats), light compression on percussion and orchestral buses, a conservative limiter and glue on the mix bus, volume automation at the end (epicomposer 2026); low and low-mid energy of strings, woodwinds and brass is their warmth, so high-pass only where a problem is heard (same source and the search summary of [Thinkspace](https://thinkspace.ac.uk/?p=137203)). The film-score mixer's view (orchestra first, mild bus processing) is in docs/CHAMBER_SCORE.md.

## Layering and EQ carving (PRODUCTION.md checklist items 1-3, 5)
**Layer roles** [S where cited, else H]:
| Part | Layers and what each supplies | Must *not* |
|---|---|---|
| Sub / floor | a sine or synth sub under hits and braams (30-60 Hz), sub drops into act changes | follow every orchestral bass note; be wide |
| Low strings and bass | celli and contrabasses (body 60-250 Hz), doubled by a synth bass in the hybrid strand | fight the sub below 60 Hz: high-pass the acoustic layer near the sub's band when both play |
| Ostinato | violins/violas spiccato (attack 2-5 kHz), a pulse synth (transient), piano doubling (definition) | mask the theme's octave |
| Theme | horns (body 200-800 Hz), then strings in octaves (presence 1-3 kHz), then choir and trumpets (top) | sit in the same octave as the ostinato |
| Brass power | trombones, tuba (100-500 Hz), braams (sub + low brass + distorted synth 100-2,000 Hz) | stack with taiko at the same moment without carving |
| Percussion | taiko / big drums (50-150 Hz body + room), toms, timpani, snare ensemble, electronic impacts (transient, sub), cymbal swells (air) | read as one sample (offset layers 10-25 ms, epicomposer) |
| Choir | wordless chant or texture (300 Hz-3 kHz), the climax "ah" | carry words over the full orchestra |
| Air and width | violins' top, cymbals, choir air, reverb | come from added hiss |
| Space | a long hall with pre-delay for orchestra and choir; shorter rooms for drums | wash the ostinato's attacks |

Disagreement kept: chamber-score practice high-passes strings at 200 Hz in a blend; trailer practice keeps the low-mid warmth and high-passes only where needed (epicomposer). The map takes the lower point for strings because in this genre they double the theme an octave apart; tolerance 8 dB [H].

**Glue** [S, H]: percussion bus with light compression and some saturation (epicomposer); hybrid layers compressed together so stacked percussion and synths "hold in place" (epicomposer); brass bus saturation; one long hall for orchestra and choir (send the theme instruments from the same return so the relay sounds like one room), a shorter room for drums; the sub ducked by the big hits (sidechain) [H]; the master: glue plus heavy limiting for the trailer strands (DR4-6), mild limiting for the score strand (DR8-12).

**Low end and top end** (item 5) [M, S]: the measured curves put the trailer albums' weight at **+3 to +4 dB at 63-100 Hz** over 1 kHz (big drums, low brass, sub hits), far less than the bass-music genres (`dubstep_2015` +8) and close to `rock_2015` below 2 kHz (within 2.3 dB); the top is **4-6 dB darker than `rock_2015` at 5-12 kHz** and 5-7 dB brighter than `chamberscore_ref`: the air comes from violins, cymbals and choir, not from synth hiss.

**Characteristic imperfection** (item 6) [H]: ensemble scatter (players a few ms apart, a section's intonation spread: `strings_section` does this), the room's length, drum ensembles that are not quantised to one sample. Wrong: machine-gun repeated samples on the ostinato, a choir that is one voice copied, a quantised drum hit stack, a brassy synth pad pretending to be a horn section.

**Census of a reference arrangement** (item 8): no published multitrack breakdown of a trailer cue was found. **Remix table** (item 9): no earlier Spectre track is in this genre; session 015 (chamber score) is a neighbour whose idiom treated "trailer-hybrid" as a contrast; nothing in it changes.

**The derived loudest section.** DR is the peak minus the loudest 20% of the RMS (plus 3 dB, sine calibration). At a -1 dBTP ceiling, DR 5 puts the loud RMS at about -6 dBFS and the short-term loudness of the loud sections at roughly -8 to -6 LUFS (the RMS-to-LUFS offset depends on the spectrum, about 0-2 dB for full-range orchestral material) [derived, ±1.5 dB]. The released trailer-house album is therefore about as loud in its climax as a trance or dubstep master in its drop, and 4-5 dB less loud on average because its acts start quietly.

**True peak and playback** [S]: no published trailer-music true-peak practice was found; a trailer's level in cinemas is set by the re-recording mix to TASA's 85 dB Leq(m) (a 2-3 minute average, M-weighted; lowered from 87 in 1997), broadcast trailers by EBU R128 (-23 LUFS, which lists trailers among the items it covers), streaming by normalisation (Spotify -14 LUFS, -1 dBTP, -2 dBTP above -14: [Spotify](https://support.spotify.com/artists/article/loudness-normalization/)). Spectre releases a standalone stereo track on streaming: **-1 dBTP**. Stems for film or trailer delivery are -14 to -10 LUFS ([Sage Audio](https://www.sageaudio.com/articles/mastering-music-for-film-and-video)): not Spectre's case.

## The verified melody: Beethoven, "Ode to Joy" (Symphony No. 9, Op. 125, 4th movement)
**Only Beethoven's melody is used here.** The European anthem is Herbert von Karajan's 1972 arrangement (orchestra, wind band, piano; published by Schott), and "Herbert von Karajan kept all the copyrights to the recording and distribution of his arrangement" ([European Parliament Research Service, Story of the European Anthem, 2021](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/BRIE/2021/690568/EPRS_BRI(2021)690568_EN.pdf)) [S]. Nothing of that arrangement (its orchestration, its harmonisation, its tempo of quarter = 120, its introduction) is described or used; Beethoven's score (1824; Beethoven died in 1827) is in the public domain everywhere.

**Scores used** (both from IMSLP, public domain):
- **Breitkopf & Härtel, *Beethovens Werke*, Serie 1, Band 3, No. 9** (the 1863 complete edition; IMSLP #516486, "Beethoven Breitkopf Serie 1 Band 3 B 9", https://imslp.org/wiki/Symphony_No.9,_Op.125_(Beethoven,_Ludwig_van); PDF 47,612,465 bytes, pages 181-182 and 199). The authority for pitches, rhythm, key, meter and the tempo mark.
- **CCARH digital edition, 2009** (MuseData, encoded from the Breitkopf edition with bar numbers; IMSLP #28868, "beethoven-sym-9-ccarh", 50,573,721 bytes, page 195-198; http://www.musedata.org/beethoven/sym-9). Used for the bar numbers and as a second reading.
- Cross-check: the theme as LilyPond source in [Wikipedia, "Ode to Joy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ode_to_Joy) (G major), parsed and transposed: 62/62 notes match the blocks below in pitch, onset and length (script check, 2026-11-26).

**Facts verified in the score** [S: Breitkopf p. 181-182, 199; CCARH p. 195-198]:
- **Key D major, common time (C), "Allegro assai, half note = 80"** (printed in Breitkopf and CCARH) = quarter note 160.
- **The instrumental theme starts at bar 92** (CCARH numbering: the Allegro assai begins after bar 91), *piano*, in the **celli and basses alone**, and runs **24 bars (92-115)**: a (92-95), a' (96-99), b (100-103), a'' (104-107), then b and a'' **repeated** (108-115).
- **The theme passes between instrument groups** (Beethoven's own relay): celli and basses (92) → violas and celli with a bassoon counterpoint (116) → first violins *dolce* with the lower strings and bassoon (140) → the **full orchestra *forte* with trumpets and timpani** at bar 164 (CCARH p. 196-198). Each statement keeps the theme and adds counterpoint and weight. This is the model for the brief's relay (below).
- **Instrumental vs sung rhythm**: in the celli-and-basses statement Beethoven ties the repeated notes of the head into half notes (bar 92: F#3 half, G3, A3; the same in bars 94, 96, 98, 100, 106, 108, 114): the sung statement (the baritone's entry, bar 241 in the usual numbering, not checked against CCARH: "Freude, schöner Götterfunken", and the choir) has repeated quarter notes, one per syllable (Breitkopf p. 199: "wir be-tre-ten feu-er-trun-ken, Himm-li-sche, dein Hei-lig-thum! Dei-ne Zau-ber bin-den wie-der, was die Mo-de streng ge-theilt; al-le Men-schen"). The blocks use the sung (quarter-note) form; the instrumental form is given as a variant.
- **The famous syncopated anticipation**: at the end of b (bar 103, and its repeat 111) the melody drops a fifth to A3 on beat 3, and the **F# of the next phrase enters a beat early, on beat 4, tied over the bar line** (Breitkopf p. 182, CCARH p. 195, both with an explicit tie; in the sung text it is "al-" of "al-le Men-schen" held across the bar). It is the theme's only syncopation and its emotional turn: the return of a arrives early, as if it cannot wait.
- **Range one octave, A3-A4** (in D, sung octave written here; the celli sound an octave lower); almost entirely stepwise: scale degrees 3-3-4-5-5-4-3-2-1-1-2-3; the only leaps are in b (the drop of a fifth to the low 5 and the sixth back up into the anticipation).
- **Phrase endings**: a ends on the 2nd degree (E, "a half cadence": 3-2-2), a' and a'' end on the tonic (2-1-1: "E. D D").

**Which phrases are most identifiable** [H, reasoned; no listening study was found]: (1) **the head of a**, bars 1-2 (3-3-4-5 | 5-4-3-2: the rising and falling stepwise line on repeated notes) is the hook: two bars are enough to name the tune; (2) **the whole of a or a'** (four bars) states it unmistakably, a' adding the closing cadence "E. D D"; (3) **the anticipation into a''** (A3 then the early F#) is the theme's signature turn and the strongest *emotional* moment, but only after the theme has been heard; (4) **b alone** (the 8th-note neighbour figures) is the least recognisable and is the phrase that can be varied, fragmented or developed without losing the identity. ## The brief: "united we stand, divided we fall"
The brief (queue 13): triumph, encouragement and overcoming struggles together, to lift the spirit of Europeans; a cinematic hybrid where Beethoven's Ode to Joy theme is passed between the folk colours of several European traditions before the full ensemble takes it; identifiable elements of Ode to Joy; modern and powerful. Subject context: the anthem idea and the EU motto "United in diversity" (in use since 2000: [europa.eu](https://european-union.europa.eu/principles-countries-history/symbols/eu-motto_en)); the anthem "without words" because of the many languages (EPRS 2021) [S].

**Recommendation: the hybrid orchestral-electronic strand (modern, 2012-2026) as the frame, with the epic-choir strand's wordless choir for the climax and the game-scoring habit of folk soloists over the orchestra for the relay; master as the trailer-house albums (`cinematic_2012`).** [H, reasoned from the strands] The hybrid strand gives "modern and powerful" (pulse, hits, sub, braams for the struggle); the choir strand gives the communal voice (a crowd without words: no language is privileged, as with the anthem itself); game scoring is the one strand where European folk instruments as soloists over an orchestra are established practice (God of War, The Witcher 3). Not the sound-design strand (no room for a theme), not cinematic pop (a lead singer and words; standing rule 15), and the sports-broadcast strand only as a wrong feeling.

**Key, tempo, length** [H inside the measured ranges]: D major / D minor (Beethoven's key; the struggle in D minor, the triumph in D major: the 9th's own minor-to-major path), with one lift for the final statement (a chromatic-mediant or whole-step shift into the last tutti, Lehman's "wonderment" cadence; e.g. D → F or D → E). **96-104 bpm, aim 100**, the theme in quarters (broad, hymn-like, a third slower than Beethoven's quarter = 160, and deliberately not the anthem arrangement's quarter = 120), ostinati in 8ths and 16ths, drums half-time in the struggle and full-time in the triumph. **3:50-4:20 at 100 bpm = 96-108 bars.**

**The arc** (four acts; bars at 100 bpm; counts [H] inside the sourced three-act form):
| Act | Bars | Feeling | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. Struggle (divided) | 16-20 | apart, under pressure | D minor; a low pulse (a heartbeat or tick on the sub and a low drum), a braam or two, low strings in a broken ostinato that keeps stopping; the **head of a in minor** heard once, alone, on one folk voice far away, cut off before its end (a fragment, not a phrase: say so in the journal) |
| II. Encouragement (the relay) | 32-40 | one voice answers another | the theme in **major** passed between 4-5 folk soloists, **one phrase each** (a, a', b, a''): each enters on the previous one's last note, over one continuous ostinato that adds a layer at each hand-off (the strings first, then percussion, then low brass); the a'' soloist's **anticipation** is the moment two traditions overlap (the new voice takes the early F# while the previous one is still sounding) |
| III. Togetherness (the build) | 16 | everyone gathering | the soloists together in counterpoint (b fragments in canon, the head of a in imitation), the drums move from half-time to full time, a riser; then the sparse lead-in: one bar with almost no bass, one beat of silence |
| IV. Triumph | 24-32 | united | the **full theme (a, a', b, a'') in the full ensemble**: horns and strings in octaves, choir on "ah/oh", trumpets and timpani added only here (Beethoven's own order), the folk instruments on top as a descant layer, the anticipation hit by the whole ensemble; a key lift for the last a''; a final held tonic, no stinger |
| Coda | 4-8 | afterwards | the head of a once more on the first folk voice, alone, in major: the voice that was cut off in Act I now finishes the phrase |

## The folk relay: "united in diversity"
**How to make passing the theme sound like one story, not a medley** [S where cited, else H]:
1. **Beethoven's own model**: the theme passes celli/basses → violas → violins → full orchestra (bars 92-164), each statement adding counterpoint and weight, never stopping the pulse (score, above). Do the same: each hand-off adds a layer to a continuous accompaniment.
2. **One phrase per voice, overlapping**: a voice takes a phrase (a, a', b or a''), the next enters on the previous one's last note; the anticipation into a'' is the natural overlap point (two instruments sound the same F# at once).
3. **One pulse, one key, one room**: the ostinato (strings or a pulse) never stops during the relay; every soloist is in the same key (each tradition's modal colour goes in ornaments, not in changed pitches of the theme), on the same hall return, in a stage position of its own (pan), so the listener hears one place filled by different people.
4. **One continuous countermelody** (Beethoven's bassoon line from bar 116 is the model): a second line, on strings or a horn, runs through all the hand-offs and becomes the descant of the climax.
5. **Each colour returns at the climax**: the soloists do not disappear when the orchestra arrives; they double the theme or play the descant on top. Diversity is audible inside the unity.
6. **Idiom in ornaments, identity in the notes**: grace notes, slides, drones and tremolo by tradition; the theme's pitches and rhythm stay Beethoven's (standing rule 14: whole phrases), so the tune is recognised every time.

A relay of five that the engine can play today [H]: **nai** (a), **kokle** (a'), **hurdy-gurdy / `lira`** (b), **lăutar violin** (a'', taking the anticipation), with **cimbalom** tremolo under the build and **trembita** calls opening Act I and sounding the tonic in the triumph; dūdas drones optional. Missing instruments that would widen the map (west and south): accordion, Irish/Scottish pipes or whistle, mandolin or bouzouki, a frame drum. Equal representation is not claimed: the choice is a musical one, said as such in the journal.

**Voices** (standing rule 15; queue 13: the subject is political). **No Lotte V voicebank**: every `singer` voice in engine/voices.json (Hanami and LEONA banks, every id built on them) carries Lotte V's terms, "no political, hateful or illegal use"; the brief's subject (European unity) is political. So no `singer` at all. What the engine's own synthesised voices can do for a wordless choir:
- **`choir`**: a polyphonic pad with a vowel (`ah`/`oo`/`ee`/`oh`), attack and release: sustained chords under the climax; no articulation, so no chant rhythm.
- **`vox2`**: a monophonic wordless voice with an LF-model glottal source (`rd` from pressed to breathy), jitter and shimmer, a vowel per note (`vowels` cycling), soft consonant onsets (`onset`: `h`, `m`, `n`, `l`, `d`) for "da / na / la" syllables, glides: **chant-like syllables on the theme's rhythm** ("ah", "oh", "la") and a solo voice line; a section is several `vox2` tracks per part, 2-3 per part with different `seed`, `push_ms` spread ±10-25 ms on vowels, panned like a choir (docs/voice/MIXING.md "Choir and ensemble voices").
- **`vox`**: the older 5-formant wordless voice (soprano / alto / tenor): a supporting line.
- Wordless is also the subject's own logic (the anthem "without words" because of the many languages, EPRS 2021). Not tested: whether 8-12 `vox2` tracks read as a large epic choir at the climax or as a synth (A/B before the session; engine gap 6 below).
