# Known limits and open directions (in priority order)

Moved from AGENTS.md (section 6). Back to the router: [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md). The detailed list of engine gaps is in [docs/HANDOFF.md](../HANDOFF.md), "Engine and knowledge map".

1. **The ears can't hear attitude.** Structure reads well on spectrograms; timbre and emotional "bite" don't. Session 001 aimed for defiance and came across as longing. Ideas: an audio-capable model as a critic ("describe what you hear, without naming emotions"), CLAP-style embeddings as a weak signal, or a human checkpoint ear after the loop stage.
2. **Synth timbres lean soft.** No real samples. Real drum one-shots or sample packs (the user would provide them; the sandbox network is restricted) would raise quality a lot. Since 2026-11-25 the modern-trance tools exist (docs/MANUAL.md "Modern strands": `acid`, 909 snare/clap/ride fitted to a recorded TR-909, `shimmer`, `granular`, `suppress`, section automation of instrument and fx parameters); their limits: automation of instrument parameters is read per note onset and skips the monophonic voices other than `acid`; granular is an effect, not a sampler instrument; the 909 fits rest on one recording per sound. The bass-music tools (2026-11-25; docs/MANUAL.md "Bass music": `fmgrowl`, `wavetable`, `sampler`, synced per-note modulation, the vowel filter, multiband and distortion fx, vocoder, gated reverb) have no recorded references to fit to (growls are patches, not instruments): the vowel filter is fitted to Peterson & Barney's formants (within 2.5%), the modulation rates checked against their note values (within about 4%), the rest judged by ear; not built: formant shift on voices, wavetable warp modes, time-stretch in the sampler. Since 2026-11-26 the bass-music sounds have recorded references after all: 118 CC0 / CC BY Freesound sounds over 14 roles (engine/third_party.json `refs-dubstep-*`), and fitted presets (`growl`, `riddim`, `wobble`, `reese`, `screech`, `snare`, `clap`, `kick`, `hats`; docs/MANUAL.md "Bass music, fitted") and stereo transitions (`"fx_model": 2`); their limits: the references are store previews (MP3, 128 kbps: attacks and the side are approximate) and one-shots or loops of mixed provenance (the medians are the target, the spread is wide); the growl preset is noisier and brighter in its centroid than the references; the reese stays 7.5 dB less dense; a sine kick sub is a line where recorded kicks are a band (fitted within 7.7 dB RMS per third octave); the impact's low-mid is narrower and shorter than recorded ones; vocal chops were measured, not changed (the `singer` phrase is cleaner, more harmonic and mono: HNR 11.9 against 1.1 dB). The cinematic orchestra (2026-11-26; docs/MANUAL.md "Orchestra and trailer"): brass, woodwinds, timpani, concert bass drum and suspended cymbal are fitted to Iowa MIS and VSCO-2-CE recordings (third-octave distance 1.3-6.3 dB against 5.6-39 dB for the old stand-ins, measured on the same recordings they were fitted to); the model averages two collections that disagree on a few brass notes; recorded drums fall 20 dB from the stroke faster than the model; taiko, the trailer effects, the accordion and the hall's character are hand-tuned (no CC0 recordings to fit); woodwinds have one recorded dynamic (softer = a hand-tuned darkening); no stopped or cuivre horn, no flutter-tongue, no harp, no other folk instruments of the brief's gap 8. Techno (2026-11-26; docs/MANUAL.md "Techno, fitted", examples/production/techno/): the 909 kick, hats, toms and rim are fitted to one recorded TR-909 and on other 909 recordings the kick and hats are no closer than the old stand-ins (kick 7.2 against `clubkick` 6.8 dB, closed hat 13.1 against `metalhat` 12.4: two to four store previews, a mixed set); the rumble's fit rests on 8 loops whose kicks are not the engine's (its fitted tail, 0.67 s, is shorter than the sources' 3-5 s reverbs); `hardkick` is denser than recorded hard kicks (crest 3.8-4.1 against 7 dB) and `hoover` peakier (crest 17 against 12.4); the conga and bongo fits sit 10 dB RMS from mixed recordings (strokes and drums mixed; only the open stroke fitted, slap, mute and bass hand-set); a return still cannot feed a return (no delay-into-reverb rhythmic rumble); no tape echo.
3. **Vocals are new.** `singer` sings with DiffSinger voices (approved by the owner by ear, 2026-11-24), English and Spanish. Male voices: `hanami-male` and `hanami-male-es` (Hanami sung an octave up and moved down inside the synthesizer; approved by the owner by ear, 2026-11-25; docs/voice/VOICES.md "Male voices"). Romanian: `hanami-ro`, Hanami through a phoneme mapping, approved by the owner's ear as "good enough" (2026-11-26; foreign accent; never political: Lotte V's terms). A generative model as a "session musician" (stems on demand) is the planned v2 for metal and pop.
4. **Mastering targets** are per-genre configurations (`engine/genres.json`, `--genre <name>`, no default). Since 2026-11-25 every genre except `trap` masters toward an era target measured from open catalogue data (trance, hip hop, pop rock: 2015-2021) or from the reference records its genre doc chose (joc, caroling, Latin pop, chamber score), with provenance (`tools/refcurve.py`, docs/MASTERING.md "Era targets"); the older Pestana-derived curves stay available and `--published` reproduces the released masters' settings. Still open: the data end in 2021-2022, so the definitive targets are 20-30 current reference masters per genre measured locally (`refcurve.py measure`), with the owner.
5. **Speed.** A full-length render takes 20-45 s for a plain track; long modern-strand projects took much longer until the speed pass of 2026-11-26 (bit-identical output): the swept filter (`synth.tv_filter`, every polysynth/supersaw-2/air/sub note) re-scanned the rest of its cutoff curve at every 256-sample block and ran one scipy call per block; it now finds the settled point once and runs the blocks in one compiled loop (session 016's 6:30 trance with granular and shimmer returns: over 10 minutes per render, 721 s under the profiler, to 227 s, peak memory 5.0 GB; the dubstep test 41.4 s to 28.5 s; eight test and published projects render byte-identical premasters and quick masters). `listen`'s 400 ms loudness curve was a direct convolution with a 17,640-sample window (12-23 minutes on long tracks; session 017's `fastlisten.py` worked around it): now a running sum, the same values to float rounding (about 1e-12), 5 s on the engine test. The granular blur works per channel in place (peak memory 540 to 229 MB on 40 s). Still slow: role-EQ and mix-bus filters, `strings_section`'s per-player air envelope (a direct convolution kept for bit-identity), the bowed and sung voices themselves; per-track caching would make the loop faster.
6. **Fixed 2026-11-26 (reported by session 017):** section `auto` `lpf` / `hpf` ramps with a trailing `"log"` stopped the render (now accepted; more points sweep through each); a `singer` part without lyrics held one vowel through every rest and past the section end (now each phrase ends at its rest); a granular freeze after a section that ends in a rest froze silence (now `seek` moves the head back to the music).
