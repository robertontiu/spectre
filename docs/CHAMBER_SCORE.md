# Cinematic chamber score (whimsical and nostalgic): a working brief

For sessions that make an instrumental in the idiom of the childhood montage: the small, acoustic, slightly whimsical scores of animation and indie film (piano, a chamber string group with pizzicato, glockenspiel, celesta, vibraphone, a clarinet or flute, acoustic guitar or ukulele, a whistle, one orchestral swell), blended with the close, repeating piano of neo-classical music. A vocabulary, not a rulebook. Researched 2026-11-24 for session 015 (subject: childhood). Spectre makes instrumentals: a voice is an instrument here (a wordless line, a hum, a child-like colour), never a sung song.

### The mastering decision: `chamberscore`, the `rock` curve reused lightly, -15 LUFS
- **Loudness -15 LUFS.** The score albums released since 2005 sit at a median of -14.9 and neo-classical since 2005 at -16.7; Sage Audio's film range starts at -14, its classical range ends at -16. -15 sits between them, quieter than every Spectre genre but caroling's neighbour, so the solo-piano passages can stay quiet. A track that leans on the solo piano may go to `--lufs -16`, one that leans on the cinematic swell to `-14`: only with a reason, noted in the journal.
- **Curve: `rock`, reused, at `eq` 0.4** (hand-tuned): the measured curve with the steepest top (closest to an acoustic record's natural decay), applied lighter than any other genre because it was measured on pop and rock and asks for 100-400 Hz body and 31-63 Hz sub that a chamber ensemble does not have. `translate` 0.2: little bass-harmonic enhancement. **Status: reused measured curve, hand-tuned EQ strength, mono point and loudness choice; replace with a curve measured on scores or neo-classical records when one exists.**
- **Test** (`examples/chamberscore-test.json`: eight waltz bars at quarter = 168 on the 12-step grid: felt piano, `bell` as glockenspiel, `musicbox`, `violin` pizzicato, steel `acguitar`, `nai` as a whistle, `strings`): `master --genre chamberscore` reaches **-15.0 LUFS, -4.3 dBTP**, PLR 10.7, top end within 3.7 dB of the curve after the closed loop, phone check on the curve (-0.2 dB). The EQ sits at its +4 dB limit at **31.5-63 Hz** (no bass instrument: the borrowed curve, not a mix fault, as in caroling) and at 1.6 and 2.5-3.15 kHz (a sparse eight-bar test). Don't chase the sub with an 808-like bass; give the piano's left hand and any low strings body at 100-250 Hz and accept the rest.

## Which style, and why: the blend chosen for a childhood brief
The brief asks for joy and carelessness and, at once, the ache of looking back: "joyful and carefree, yet deeply emotional". That is the definition of nostalgia in the research: music-evoked nostalgia is "bittersweet, involving a mixture of sadness and wistful joy"; it "was associated with both joy and sadness", and mixed emotions were reported more than twice as often for nostalgic songs (14.21% against 6.01% for non-nostalgic autobiographical ones) ([Barrett, Grimm, Robins, Wildschut, Sedikides & Janata 2010, *Emotion*](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~crsi/Barrett,%20grimm,%20Robins,%20Wildschut,%20Sedikides,%20&%20Janata,%202010,%20Emotion.pdf)). The style has to hold both at once, in one theme.

| strand | what it is | carries | suits | risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Whimsical cinematic chamber score** (the childhood-montage idiom) | animation and indie-film scores: Hisaishi's Ghibli scores (*Totoro*, *Kiki*, *Kikujiro*, *Spirited Away*), Giacchino's *Up* and *Ratatouille*, Desplat's *Moonrise Kingdom* and *Fantastic Mr. Fox*, Tiersen's *Amélie*, Doyle's *A Little Princess*, Elfman's *Big Fish*, Thomas Newman's *Finding Nemo* and *WALL·E*. Its native subject is childhood, memory and a life passing in a few minutes | **joy and sorrow from the same theme** (tempo, density, the piano left alone); waltz time; small struck and plucked colours; one swell | the montage of a childhood: little moments, a life speeding past, the ache of the return | the Disney-sugar finale; the toy-box as decoration; stock "whimsical" library music |
| **Neo-classical piano with strings** | Einaudi, Yiruma, Ólafur Arnalds, Max Richter, Nils Frahm, Joep Beving, Dustin O'Halloran: a close (often felted) piano, repeating patterns with shifting layers, strings that swell slowly | intimacy, the present-tense listener remembering; the room and the felt | the adult looking back, the "memories you wish you could live again" | the sad piano elegy; the meditative bed that never moves; no carefree joy |
| **Indie-folk / chamber instrumental** | the Penguin Cafe Orchestra (ukulele, cuatro, harmonium, "exuberant folk music" with "a minimalist aesthetic"), Tiersen's *Rue des cascades*, The Album Leaf, Lullatone's toy-instrument miniatures | handmade sound, play, small instruments, a living room | the carefree, the handmade, a child's world | the twee advertising sound (ukulele, whistling, glockenspiel, "arpeggiated acoustic guitar"); the toy commercial |

**Chosen: the whimsical cinematic chamber score as the frame, with a neo-classical piano at its centre and indie-folk colours inside it.** Why: (1) the score idiom is the only one of the three whose craft is *built* to turn joy into ache inside one theme (the "Married Life" analysis: the same 16-bar theme carries the wedding and the loss, by tempo, dynamics and texture, in a major key); (2) its childhood subject is native, not imported (Totoro, Kikujiro, Moonrise Kingdom, A Little Princess); (3) its whimsical colours (glockenspiel, vibraphone, pizzicato, a whistle) are the theme's instruments, not decoration, which keeps it away from the toy commercial; (4) a close neo-classical piano gives the "looking back" a body in the present (the adult at the piano) that the score alone, with its orchestral distance, does not; (5) indie-folk alone drifts to the twee ad and has no cinematic swell, neo-classical alone drifts to the elegy and has no carefree joy. The producer may still lean one way (a waltz montage, a piano piece with whimsical guests), but the blend is the recommendation.

## The idiom: what makes a childhood montage
- **One theme a child could sing**, short and diatonic, heard first small (one instrument), then passed around: piano, glockenspiel, whistle, violin, the full group. The variation is the story; new material is rare ("samme grundlæggende melodiske og harmoniske temamateriale": the same thematic material throughout "Married Life").
- **Joy and ache from the same notes.** Fast, staccato, many colours = the moment; slow, legato, few colours = the memory of it. The turn happens in time: a sharp ritardando, a held chord, the theme stopping mid-phrase, the piano left alone and played *higher* in the sad passages ("the piano plays higher in the sad chapters than in the happy ones", AAU thesis).
- **A waltz is a dance, not a lullaby.** In "Married Life" the waltz at about 174 bpm is felt in one, with staccato piano accents on beats 2 and 3 in the happy passages; slowed to 75 it becomes a memory. A lullaby is slower, smoother and less rhythmic (infant-directed play songs are rated "more rhythmic" than lullabies: [Trainor 1996](https://trainorlab.mcmaster.ca/publications/pdfs/trainor_1996.pdf); lullabies are recognisable "on the basis of their musical features alone": [Bainbridge et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-020-00963-z)).
- **One swell, earned.** The cinematic element is one moment where the strings (and, in a full score, the horns) open up, prepared by subtraction. "Uplifting" is a single lift, not a constant pad.
- **The ending looks back.** The closing piano, slower, sometimes in a higher register, the theme incomplete or resolved late.

## Instruments and roles (the "Married Life" orchestra as a reference)
From the AAU analysis of "Married Life" ("acoustic, mostly symphonic instruments only; no electronic sounds"): **piano** (staccato accents on beats 2 and 3 in the happy passages; alone and soft in the sad ones); **strings** (legato, pizzicato and spiccato; "organic" legato textures); **muted trumpet** (harmon mute) carrying the first statement; **vibraphone and glockenspiel** ("klokkespil") for the dream of a child; **clarinet** (the stagnant sad tone, rising intervals of hope); **flute** (a rising motif opening a section); **harp** (light glissandi); **acoustic guitar** (strumming in the later chapters); **double bass and brushes** (the swing-waltz opening).
Other colours of the idiom: **accordion, piano, harpsichord, banjo, bass guitar, vibraphone** on *Amélie* (Wikipedia, Amélie (soundtrack)); **ukulele, cuatro, mandolin, harmonium**, viola and clarinet in the Penguin Cafe Orchestra (Simon Jeffes wanted folk music's "immediacy and spirit" and valued "randomness, spontaneity, surprise": [Wikipedia, Penguin Cafe Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penguin_Cafe_Orchestra)); a **felted, close-miked upright** in neo-classical piano (Frahm's *Felt*: felt "initially to dampen the sound for nighttime playing and later because he liked the sound it produced", "microphones placed deep within the piano", "a more intimate sound": [Wikipedia, Felt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felt_(Nils_Frahm_album)); "the ambient scrapes and creaks produced by the instrument and wooden floorboards" and his breathing are part of the record: [Textura](https://www.textura.org/reviews/frahm_felt.htm)).
Roles in short: the piano holds the harmony and is the narrator; a struck or blown lead (glockenspiel, celesta, vibraphone, whistle, clarinet) is the child's voice of the theme; pizzicato and guitar are the running feet; strings are time passing and the swell; a bass (pizzicato double bass) only where the waltz needs a floor.

## Harmony
- **Major with borrowed and modal colours, minor as a full option.** About 40% of the catalogue's tracks read as minor (above).
- **The Leittonwechsel, I-iii** (C major to E minor: the root steps down a semitone, the third and fifth stay): in Hollywood scores 1985-2024 it is "frequently associated with" "awe-inducing wonder, childlike belief, nostalgia, Christmas magic, and empathy"; in *Up* it is Ellie's theme, "the seemingly eternal loop between the I and iii" ([Dinsfriend 2025, University of Oregon MA thesis](https://scholarsbank.uoregon.edu/bitstreams/9f1fff42-205e-4ccd-95df-48dab2abfdc5/download)). "Married Life" itself is analysed as vi65-I43 (Hooktheory) and as F6/D preferred to Dm7, with **Fmaj7 the main emotional chord** (it contains a minor triad: the AAU thesis).
- **The borrowed minor iv** for the ache: the "emotive 'borrowed' iv chord" of the Sympathy theme in *Edward Scissorhands*, a change to the minor iv in the farewell (Dinsfriend 2025). The **minor tonic** at the end of a major phrase: "Spirited Away" vi-iii6-IV-I6-i6 (Hooktheory).
- **Lydian, the raised fourth, for wonder:** Horner uses Lydian "to establish an overall sense of wonder and delicateness" in *Jumanji*, a harp laying "a simple Lydian structure" under the oboe ([Lee 2025, *Frisson and Film*, University of Oregon MA thesis](https://scholarsbank.uoregon.edu/bitstreams/f551273b-5bf6-4964-b917-711f9b5fb009/download)); E.T.'s Flying theme gives "prominence to the note F# (the raised or Lydian fourth)" in its coda ([Pearson A-level notes, E.T. Flying Theme](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Music/2013/Teaching%20and%20learning%20materials/Unit-6-45-John-Williams-ET-1982-Flying-Theme.pdf)); the raised fourth leans up toward the fifth, "a harmony that lifts instead of settling", "awe rather than triumph" ([Mark Slater, Why Lydian sounds like wonder, 2024](https://markslater.net/?p=2051); a blog, an argument rather than a measurement). Use it as a colour (a major II under a sustained tonic), not as a mode for the whole track.
- **Suspensions and added notes:** sus2 and sus4 (River Flows in You vi-IVsus2-I-V; Merry-Go-Round's VIIsus2sus4 and iv9), sixths, sevenths and ninths over a diatonic frame (AAU: "diatonic sixths, sevenths, and ninths"); minor-key cues lean on i-III-v-VII and iv7 (Comptine, A Town with an Ocean View).
- **Modulation:** "Married Life" lifts a whole tone (F to G, via F#m♭5) for its closing period; a habit of the idiom, to use only if it means something (docs/LESSONS.md, 012: a key change can be a door).

## Form, in engine terms
- **The theme:** 8 or 16 bars (A); a variant A' (same harmony and rhythm, new colour or register); a contrasting B; periods chained like AABA, with an introduction, a break and a closing period. In a waltz the bars are short (at quarter = 168, a 3/4 bar is 1.07 s, 16 bars 17 s), so a 3:30 track holds about 190 waltz bars: plenty of room for a tempo arc.
- **The grid for 3/4:** a waltz bar is **12 steps**; 4 waltz bars = 3 engine bars (docs/CAROLING.md "The grid"; docs/MANUAL.md "Other meters"). Write patterns of 3 engine bars (= 4 waltz bars) and sections in multiples of 3 engine bars; count phrases in steps. Waltz bar *n* starts at step 12(*n*-1). Chord `stabs` rhythms repeat per engine bar, so write the waltz accompaniment as notes (bass on step 0, chords on steps 4 and 8 of each waltz bar) or as `sustain` chords with lengths of 12 steps.
- **Tempo arc:** section `bpm` ramps make the ritardandi (`"bpm": [168, 120]`), a slow close as its own section at 70-80 (see docs/MANUAL.md "Tempo changes"; the shared delay stays on the project bpm).
- **Neo-classical variant (4/4):** an ostinato (arpeggiated piano figure) that stays, with layers joining and leaving every 8 bars; the theme on top; the swell as the moment every layer plays together; the close with the ostinato alone.

## Mixing and recording practice
- **The piano close:** the neo-classical piano is close-miked, intimate and a little mechanical (felt, creaks, breath); keep it near and fairly dry, the narrator in the room with the listener.
- **Strings at a distance:** "relatively distant miking is the most common approach for a balanced sound"; a larger string group "needs space for the sound to integrate and become coherent", and a dry recording can be given "something a little longer and fuller to give some 'bloom'" ([Sound On Sound, Recording a string section](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/recording-string-section)). The piano near, the strings behind it in a hall: two depths, on purpose.
- **Struck colours** (glockenspiel, celesta, music box, vibraphone) carry most of the energy above 5 kHz in a sparse mix (the test: the `bell` lane was nearly all of the 8 kHz+ band). Treat their top as a casting decision (docs/LESSONS.md, 012): air for what is remembered and far, plainness for what is near; check them on the phone view.
- **One room, chosen.** The childhood world and the adult present can be two spaces (a bright room and the dry close piano) if the change of space means something.
- **No drum kit.** If a pulse is needed beyond the waltz: brushes, a light shaker, claps from a few hands (`handclap` with `clappers` 2-3), feet, never a backbeat.

## Voices (standing rule 15: voices are instruments)
- Optional. What a voice may do here: a wordless "ooh" or "la" doubling the theme once, a hum, a child-like colour in the swell. Never a song with words carried by a voice.
- The engine has only adult female svs voices: `hanami-soft` (soft, airy: a hum), `leona` and `leona-soft` (brighter, younger character voices: the closest to a child-like colour; `leona` is wrong for "mature, serious or sad lines", docs/voice/VOICES.md). No real children's voice exists; don't fake a children's choir with pitch-shifting (formant tricks past ±0.5 hurt diction and sound processed). Range A3-D5. If English words are used at all, a few, Spectre's own, no names (docs/voice/languages/en.md).
- Mix: the voice inside the room with the theme's instruments, behind a 60 ms reverb pre-delay (docs/MANUAL.md "Voices").

## Nearest wrong feelings (and what separates them)
- **A lullaby:** slow, smooth, rocking, low and soothing, made to send someone to sleep. A lullaby is less rhythmic than a play song (Trainor 1996). The target is awake: a waltz felt in one at dance tempo, staccato, playful, with the slowness saved for the memory.
- **A toy commercial / the twee ad:** ukulele, whistling, glockenspiel, handclaps, "arpeggiated acoustic guitar" and "whimsical vocals" as "prerequisites in advertising's obsession with the sound of twee" ([Creative Review, The rise of the twee](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/crblog/the-rise-of-the-twee/)); music that wants us "to be cheerful. But not for cheerfulness's sake", "the worst arranging trend of the 21st century" ([Lawrence Lanahan, Ukuleles and glockenspiels, 2025](https://rearranged.substack.com/p/ukeleles-and-glockenspiels)). What separates it: the whimsical instruments carry the THEME and its losses; nothing is cute for its own sake; the colours go quiet when the memory hurts.
- **A sad piano elegy:** solo piano, minor, slow from the first bar, nothing carefree; the neo-classical bed of grief. The target has the joy first and for real; the ache comes from losing pace, not from minor chords (AAU thesis: sad in a purely major passage).
- **Generic stock "inspirational" music:** four chords, a piano ostinato, strings and a build that could sit under any corporate video; stock libraries sell exactly this ("joyful ukulele, funny whistle", "fun quirky comedy cinematic": [MotionElements](https://www.motionelements.com/stock-music-11002047-my-happy-ukulele), [Shockwave-Sound, happy playful light-hearted](https://Shockwave-Sound.com/browse/genre/happy-playful-light-hearted)). What separates it: one specific theme, one specific memory (a sound, a game, a place), a swell that arrives once and is earned, and a form with a story.
- **The functional mood-playlist piano** (2026-11-25): an anonymous, slow, close felt piano made to fill "Peaceful Piano"-type playlists, much of it supplied by production-music libraries under pseudonyms ([MBW 2017](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/why-spotifys-fake-artists-problem-is-an-epidemic-literally/)). It has the felt strand's sound without a theme or a story; what separates the target is the same as for the stock bed: one theme, one memory, a form that goes somewhere.
- **Also near:** the Disney-sugar finale (a key lift and a tutti for applause); nostalgia for an era instead of for a person (a pastiche of a period style); a music-box horror (a detuned music box and minor chords read as creepy, not tender).

## Layering and EQ carving (researched 2026-11-25)
The cross-genre method is in [PRODUCTION.md](PRODUCTION.md). Labels: **[M]** measured, **[S]** a named source, **[P]** carried over from a professional trance producer's EQ map (2026) where it applies, **[H]** hand-tuned.

**Here orchestration is the mix.** The trance producer who prompted this research compared trance layering to classical music; the direction of the lesson runs the other way too. An orchestral sound is made of doublings (one line on two timbres in unison or at the octave) and of register spacing, and the mixing sources say plainly that pop habits harm it: carving each instrument its own frequency slot "breaks up the natural blend of sections"; EQ should be "gentle and broad, subtle tonal shaping, not frequency surgery"; fix balance with dynamics and voicing first; keep the dynamic contrast (Thinkspace) [S]. Strings work as three layers, highs (violins), mids (violas), lows (celli and basses), with parts spaced by fifths and octaves "rather than packing them closely" (Thinkspace) [S].

**Layer roles** (doubling as layering):
| Part | Layers and their jobs | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Theme** | the narrator instrument (piano, or a whimsical lead) plus one doubling colour: glockenspiel or celesta an octave or two above for the attack and the air; a violin or whistle in unison for sustain; pizzicato under a legato line for articulation | [S] "Instruments and roles" above; the pairings [H], standard orchestration practice (Rimsky-Korsakov's *Principles of Orchestration* treats such doublings; not re-read for this brief) |
| **Harmony** | piano left hand and guitar for the pulse; violas and second violins as the sustained middle; one pad-like source at a time | [S] this brief |
| **Bass** | celli (pizzicato or bowed) and the piano's bottom octave; no double bass in the engine | [M] "Engine mapping" above |
| **The swell** | all three string layers at once, the one place where everything doubles | [S] this brief |
| **Space** | the piano near, the strings behind it in a hall: two depths | [S] "Mixing and recording practice" above |

**EQ map** (the lightest of any genre: broad moves of 1-2 dB, mostly on groups):
| Instrument | High-pass | Cut / control | Emphasise | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Piano | 20-60 Hz (sub rumble only; its low octave is the bass here) | 200-500 Hz mud, 1-2 dB, when strings sit on it | 60-200 Hz body | iZotope EQ cheat sheet (piano) |
| Violins | about 200 Hz | harshness in 1-4 kHz, small | air above 10 kHz | Beat Kitchen; iZotope (orchestral strings) |
| Violas, celli | below 100 Hz (violas higher) | 200-500 Hz build-up: a broad 1-2 dB cut on the string *bus*, not per part | | Beat Kitchen; iZotope |
| Celesta, glockenspiel, vibraphone, music box | 250-500 Hz [H] | 3-5 kHz if they bite | their fundamentals; 8-12 kHz as a casting decision ("Mixing and recording practice") | [P] air layer; [M] this repo |
| Acoustic guitar, pizzicato | 70-100 Hz | 200-400 Hz | 1.5-2.5 kHz | iZotope (acoustic guitar) |
| Whistle, reed, wordless voice | 200-300 Hz [H] | 500 Hz-1.2 kHz nasal | 2-4 kHz | iZotope (vocals), applied |
| Hall reverb return | 200-300 Hz [H] (lower than in band genres: the hall is part of the low strings) | 300-600 Hz if it clouds | low-pass at 6-10 kHz | [P]; Beat Kitchen (one hall for the ensemble) |

**Glue** [S]: one hall reverb for the whole ensemble, sent pre-fader so the space stays constant; section buses compressed only if a section peaks badly; on the mix bus at most 2:1 with a slow attack and 1-2 dB of reduction; a limiter only to catch peaks (Beat Kitchen).

**Where Spectre stands** [M] (stems of session 015's tutti, rendered in memory, 2026-11-25; 10 sounding stems): seven sources within 10 dB of each other at 150-400 Hz (cello, piano, solo cello, violas, the reverb return, celesta, celli), the densest low-mid of the five tracks analysed, with the celli and the solo cello about half masked; six sources at 2-6 kHz; **only the glockenspiel above 6 kHz**. The fix is orchestration, not EQ: thin the 150-400 Hz doublings (one sustained source there at a time), move one of them up an octave, and let a string line or the room carry some of the air.

**How to build it in Spectre** (engine since 2026-11-25; docs/MANUAL.md "Production"). Set `"genre": "chamberscore"`: the lightest presets (-1.5 dB cuts, no boosts). Stacks are orchestral doublings:
- *Theme doubled*: `{"layers": [{"name": "piano", "inst": {"type": "piano"}, "role": "keys", "eq": "none"}, {"name": "glock", "inst": {"type": "glockenspiel", "hardness": 0.4}, "role": "bells", "octave": 2, "vel": 0.6, "gain_db": -4}]}`: the glockenspiel gives the attack and the top, the piano the body.
- *Strings on one bus*: violins, violas and celli (`strings_section`) with `"bus": "strings"` and a broad cut on the bus, not per part: `"buses": {"strings": {"fx": [{"type": "eq", "hz": 320, "gain_db": -1.5, "q": 0.7}]}}` (Beat Kitchen).
- *Two depths*: the piano near (a short `room` return or none), the strings behind it in a `hall` return (`predelay_ms` 20, `hpf` 220, `lpf` 8000).
- *No* `ott`, `clip`, stacks of synths or air layers: orchestration and balance do the job.

## Granularity: idioms, eras, schools and the current practice (researched 2026-11-25)
docs/PRODUCTION.md section 5, item 10. Labels: **[M]** measured here, **[S]** a named source, **[H]** hand-tuned or reasoned. Composers and records are named as craft schools only, never as pieces to copy (standing rule 13). Every [M] number below comes from MusicBrainz releases joined to AcousticBrainz (CC0; data end in 2022), tracks of 90 s and longer, medians with the middle half in brackets: est. LUFS = -23.7 - ReplayGain (±1-2 dB); the Bark bands in dB relative to each track's level at 510 Hz-2 kHz (the method of "Era assessment" above); **DC** = Essentia's *dynamic complexity*, "the average absolute deviation from the global loudness level estimate on the dB scale" (0.2 s frames, silence at the ends ignored) ([Essentia, DynamicComplexity](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_DynamicComplexity.html)): how far the loudness of a record moves around its own mean, the one dynamics measure AcousticBrainz holds (it has no true peak, so PLR cannot be measured from it). Album lists and the scripts' sets are in the proposal for `data/catalogue_references.json`.

### A. The dimensions, and why the boundaries are where they are
The genre varies along three dimensions. Each boundary below is where a measurement or a dated source shows a change, not a round date.

**1. Idiom (strand).** What the music is *for* decides most of its sound:
| Idiom | What it is for | Why it is its own strand (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| **Whimsical animation and indie-film score** (Pixar, Ghibli, Wes Anderson's and Jeunet's films) | cues for picture, released as a soundtrack album | cue length 2:17-2:32 median for 90 s+ cues, a full ensemble's tonal balance, est. -15 to -17 LUFS [M]; the reference idiom of this brief |
| **Neo-classical / modern-classical piano** (the post-2010 felt piano and the streaming-playlist strand) | a standalone track for listening, often in mood playlists | 3:47 median length, **10-15 dB darker above 2.7 kHz and 5.5-7 dB heavier at 140-500 Hz than the score albums** [M]; its own economy (Spotify's "Peaceful Piano" playlist, production-music libraries supplying it: [MBW 2017](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/why-spotifys-fake-artists-problem-is-an-epidemic-literally/)) |
| **Minimalist-influenced score** (Glass, Newman's *American Beauty*, Jóhannsson, Mansell; Hisaishi's own practice) | cues built from repeating figures | dark and low-mid heavy like the piano strand (4 albums, 44 cues: -23.8 at 6.4-7.7 kHz) and quiet (-18.7 LUFS) [M, thin]; Hisaishi names his minimal mode as distinct from his "classical symphonic" Miyazaki mode ([Wikipedia, Joe Hisaishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Hisaishi); [AV Club 2023](https://www.avclub.com/joe-hisaishi-interview-studio-ghiblis-legendary-composer-on-the-boy-and-the-heron)) |
| **European art-house chamber score** (Desplat, Tiersen, Portman, Doyle, Marianelli, Bertelmann) | small, "transparent" ensembles of unusual acoustic colours | darkest top of the score schools and quieter than Hollywood [M]; sources below |
| **Video-game chamber score** (Wintory's *Journey*, Larkin's *Hollow Knight*, C418, Berlinist's *GRIS*, Coker's *Ori*, ConcernedApe, Santaolalla's *The Last of Us*) | interactive music: loops and layers that follow play | heavier sub (+11 dB at 32-97 Hz), the quietest albums (-18.7 LUFS) [M]; written per level and rebuilt into album tracks afterwards ([Berklee paper on *Journey*](https://archives.berklee.edu/_flysystem/fedora/2022-10/49722-Original%20File.pdf)); in-game loudness standards of their own (F) |
| **Trailer-adjacent hybrid score** (Two Steps From Hell, Audiomachine, Bergersen, Zimmer's *Inception*), a **contrast**, not a strand to make | trailer cues: three acts, an intro, a build and a big ending ([Sound On Sound, All about library music, part 7](https://www.soundonsound.com/music-business/all-about-library-music-part-7)) | **-11.4 LUFS** (the only subdivision near pop), brightest top, sub as heavy as pop [M] |
Indie-folk / chamber instrumental (Penguin Cafe, Tiersen's albums, Lullatone, The Album Leaf), the third strand of the original blend ("Which style, and why"), is measured alongside: -19.8 LUFS, 3:44, a heavy 32-291 Hz and a top as bright as the scores [M].

**2. Timeline.** Four turns, each dated by sources or a measured change:
- **Before about 2005: live ensembles, quieter albums.** Score albums of 1988-2004 (11 albums) median -17.7 LUFS, the `soundtrack` tag 1995-2004 -19.2 [M].
- **2005-2010: the loudness step, and samples become finals.** Score albums of 2007-2012 sit at -14.9 and the `soundtrack` tag 2015-21 at -15.3: about **+3-4 dB** against the earlier period, while the dynamic complexity does not fall (albums DC 5.6 → 6.5-6.7, tag 6.5 → 6.6) [M]: louder by level, not by squashing. In the same years a sampled orchestra stopped being only a demo: "producers know they can shave off $20,000 from an orchestral budget" and TV budgets "usually don't support the luxury of using a live orchestra" ([Mix, Russ Landau](https://www.mixonline.com/technology/composer-landau-uses-vsl-orchestral-samples-score-post-380559)); mock-ups that imitate live orchestras became "a requirement in the film industry" ([Furduj 2019, JCU thesis](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/61619/1/JCU_61619_Furduj_2019_thesis.pdf)) [S].
- **2011-2019: the felt piano.** Frahm's *Felt* (2011: felt on the hammers, "microphones placed deep within the piano", creaks and breath on the record; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felt_(Nils_Frahm_album))); Native Instruments' *Una Corda* (2015), a Frahm-prepared piano sampled with felt and cotton layers and sold for "film music" ([NI](https://www.native-instruments.com/en/products/komplete/keys/una-corda/)), puts that sound in every composer's library; mood playlists ("Peaceful Piano") and production-music suppliers make it a streaming genre by 2017 ([MBW 2017](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/why-spotifys-fake-artists-problem-is-an-epidemic-literally/)) [S]. Measured on the `modern classical` tag, 2005-11 against 2015-21: **+6 dB at 32-97 Hz, +4.4 dB at 140-183 Hz, 3.5-4 dB darker at 4-12 kHz, +2.6 dB louder, DC 6.9 → 6.0** [M]: a closer, bottom-heavier, slightly more compressed piano. Score albums did not change that way (DC 6.5 → 6.6, tonal change within 2 dB on the tag) [M].
- **2020-2026: hybrid by default, minimal at the top.** Not measurable (the open data end in 2022); dated sources in G.
The open data cannot separate "sample era" and "live era" sound: AcousticBrainz holds no information on how a record was made.

**3. Region / school** (craft traditions, measured on the score albums):
- **Hollywood** (Giacchino, Newman, Elfman, Brion, Mothersbaugh; 9 albums, 121 cues): the brightest top of the schools (1.5-4 dB over the pooled curve at 4-12.5 kHz), a full low end (with the European school), the loudest (-15.0) [M]. Large rooms and the Decca tree with outriggers and many spot mics ([Sound On Sound, Downton Abbey Road](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/downton-abbey-road)); 2024's *The Wild Robot*: a full orchestra at AIR Studios ([Avid 2025](https://www.avid.com/resource-center/wildrobot-sibelius)) [S].
- **French and European** (Desplat, Tiersen, Portman, Doyle, Marianelli; 11 albums, 199 cues): **3-7 dB darker than Hollywood at 6.4-15.5 kHz**, -15.9 LUFS, the lowest DC of the schools (5.9) [M]. Transparent writing, "each instrument room to be heard" ([Film Independent](https://www.filmindependent.org/?p=156031)), with few instruments: "harp, flute, clarinet, some celeste ... some vibraphone ... not a huge amount of instruments" (Desplat on *Little Women*, [Deadline via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/little-women-composer-alexandre-desplat-170749846.html)); *Pinocchio* (2022) with "solely the wood instruments ... harp, guitars, mandolins, accordions, recorders" for "a rather more intimate and softer sound than if I had added the brass section, cymbals" ([whynow 2023](https://whynow.co.uk/read/alexandre-desplat-pinocchio-interview)); Tiersen's toy piano, melodica, accordion, harpsichord, mandolin, mostly played by himself ([Wikipedia, Yann Tiersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yann_Tiersen)); Bertelmann's processed harmonium motif in *All Quiet on the Western Front* (2022; [NPR 2023](https://www.npr.org/2023/03/03/1161051165/how-volker-bertelmann-created-the-score-for-all-quiet-on-the-western-front)) [S].
- **Japanese, Ghibli-adjacent** (Hisaishi; 7 albums, 103 cues; one composer, so a school only by proxy): **about 6 dB less at 32-97 Hz and 1-3 dB less at 97-183 Hz** than Hollywood and European (a lighter bass floor), a top like the European school, the quietest (-17.3) [M]. Hisaishi's minimalism comes from Glass, Riley and Adams ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Hisaishi)); Japanese TV anime is scored from a "music menu" written away from picture and fitted by the sound director ([Steinberg, Kevin Penkin](https://new.steinberg.net/stories/kevin-penkin/); [Anime Corner, Evan Call](https://animecorner.me/interview-evan-call-on-his-beginnings-composing-anime-music-and-influences/)), so cues are self-contained pieces, while Ghibli's films moved toward Hollywood-style synchronisation over four decades (dissertation abstract, [ProQuest 1476396384](https://www.proquest.com/docview/1476396384)) [S].
The school differences are medians over 7-11 albums with middle halves 6-15 dB wide: real (a median's standard error is about 1-1.5 dB here) but smaller than the gap between scores and the piano strand.

### B. The shared core (every subdivision of the chamber idiom)
1. **Acoustic instruments, or samples standing in for them, carry the music;** synths are colour or sketch, never the lead (all strands; Flow's synth sketches were orchestrated, Silksong's samples carry live soloists: G) [S].
2. **A theme that is passed around and recoloured** (the idiom, "Form" above) [S].
3. **Dynamics are kept.** Every chamber subdivision measures **DC 5.2-7.5** against **3.6 for pop rock 2015-21** (238 tracks) [M]; the trailer contrast sits at 4.8-5.1.
4. **The top falls away steeply above 2 kHz:** at 4.4-5.3 kHz every chamber set is 12-29 dB under its mids, pop rock 6.4 dB [M].
5. **Release loudness well under pop:** -15 to -20 LUFS medians [M]; masters made with "little limiting" ([Sage Audio](https://sageaudio.com/articles/how-to-master-dynamic-classical-music)) [S].
6. **No drum kit, no backbeat;** the pulse lives in bass, chords and plucks ("Meter and feel" above).
7. **Space is part of the sound:** a real room or a chosen one (C, F).

**What this means for Spectre's settings** (a Spectre track is always a standalone stereo release, so the album column applies, never the picture or game ones):
- **The loudness a listener hears is set by the peaks.** On Spotify a master of PLR *p* plays at most at -1 - *p* LUFS (positive gain stops 1 dB under full scale; Spotify's own example: a -20 LUFS master with -5 dBTP peaks is raised only to -16). A dynamic score master with PLR 14-16 therefore plays at about -15 to -17 whatever integrated level it was mastered to [S, derived]. Mastering it louder than about -15 only means limiting it; quieter than that it plays quieter than the playlist around it. **-15 LUFS stays right for score and blend briefs; -16 to -17 for a felt-piano brief** (the strand's median, and its classical-range advice) [M, S].
- **Dynamics are the gap.** 015 *Again, Again* (master v030, `chamberscore_ref`, -15 LUFS) has **DC 3.96** (Essentia 2.1-b6 on the released MP3, run 2026-11-25), PLR 13.9 and LRA 8.2 LU: its peaks are fine, but its loudness moves around its mean like a pop-rock record (3.6), not like a score (5.6-6.8) [M]. One track, and DC mixes the arrangement with the processing (and AcousticBrainz ran Essentia 2.1-beta2), but every chamber subdivision sits above 5: the arrangement should keep its quiet passages quiet (thin to the piano alone, as "The idiom" says) and the master should not ride them up. The engine's master always applies its 2:1 glue (1-2 dB) and the soft clipper; a gentler path is an engine proposal (G, gaps).
- **Width and low end:** `mono_below` 80 stays (hand-tuned; Sage: keep the low side image); a felt-piano brief can go lower (60) or off, since the piano's bass strings are deliberately on one side [S, H].
- **Targets:** see F.

- **Scores need no curve of their own.** The 27 score albums land within 1.5 dB of `chamberscore_ref` from 250 Hz to 12.5 kHz (1-1.5 dB less at 125-200 Hz, 2.5 dB more at 16 kHz, the least certain band) [M]: the pooled reference already is a score curve, because the piano records are a minority of it. The Hollywood albums alone are 1.5-4 dB brighter at 4-12.5 kHz and the European ones up to 3 dB darker; both differences sit inside the 8-13 dB spread, so they are orchestration choices, not targets.
- **The piano strand does.** Against `chamberscore_ref`: **12-13.5 dB darker at 4-12.5 kHz**, 5.5-11 dB darker at 2-3.15 kHz, **4-5.5 dB heavier at 125-400 Hz** [M]. The gap is as large as the piano set's own middle-half width up there (12-15 dB) and the medians are firm (150 tracks: a standard error of about 1 dB), so it is a real difference of the typical record, not of every record. Mastering a felt-piano track to `chamberscore_ref` would ask for 12 dB more air than its genre has; the master caps boosts at +2 dB above 5 kHz, so in practice it would leave the top alone and lift 125-400 Hz less than the strand has. Use `eq` 0.3: a guard, not a mould, as for caroling. The darkness has to come from the piano (`felt`, the register) and the room, not from the master.

**Engine gaps that matter for this genre, in order of effect** [H]: (1) the piano's top end and close-up detail (the narrator of every strand; HANDOFF section 6); (2) no orchestral woodwinds and no brass (the European palette and the Hollywood swell); (3) a dynamic master path: a genre setting that skips the soft clipper and lightens the glue; (4) no harp, toy piano, marimba, accordion or harmonium (`engine/chamber.py`'s docstring lists a `toy_piano` that is not implemented); (5) no piano mechanism noise; (6) no double bass; (7) stereo only.

**What could not be sourced:** a tempo census per strand (no published one; the AcousticBrainz tempo estimates are unreliable on rubato and triple meter); PLR, LRA and true peak of commercial score and piano albums (AcousticBrainz has no peak data; DC stands in); anything measured after 2022; the instrument census of a Hisaishi or Desplat cue from a published multitrack (none found); per-school recording-room practice outside Hollywood; trailer loudness from a primary source (only this catalogue measurement and forum talk).
