# Techno for Spectre: a working brief

For sessions that make techno in its modern era and its strands. A vocabulary, not a rulebook. Spectre has made trance (four on the floor, a long arc of breakdowns and drops) and dubstep (the bass as lead, build-and-drop); techno shares trance's kick and DJ purpose but almost nothing of its dramaturgy: **techno is a machine for a dancefloor, made of a kick and its low end, percussion, and one or two repeating sounds that change slowly**. Its arc is a long rise in density and pressure, not a story of drops. Researched 2026-11-26 for queue item 14 (docs/ORCHESTRATION.md). Artists, labels and clubs are named only as craft schools, never as tracks to copy (standing rule 13).

Labels as in docs/PRODUCTION.md: **[M]** measured (a published measurement, or one made here from open data, method stated), **[S]** a named source, **[H]** hand-tuned or a judgement of this brief.

## The tradition and the timeline
Techno began in Detroit in the mid-1980s: the Belleville Three (Juan Atkins, Derrick May, Kevin Saunderson); Model 500's "No UFO's" (1985) "is generally considered the first techno production"; the UK compilation *Techno! The New Dance Sound of Detroit* (1988) named it (Wikipedia "Techno") [S]. Germany's line ran through Frankfurt and, after reunification, Berlin: Tresor opened in 1991 and *Tresor II: Berlin & Detroit – A Techno Alliance* (1993) joined the two cities; Belgian new beat and EBM and Ghent's R&S Records fed the harder European side; London's Trade (1990), the acid-techno free-party underground and Birmingham's loop techno (Surgeon, Regis, Downwards) the UK side (Wikipedia "Techno", "Acid techno"; [DJ Mag on Jeff Mills' *Waveform Transmission*](https://direct.djmag.com/features/how-jeff-mills-waveform-vol-1-started-new-era-techno)) [S]. Robert Hood and Daniel Bell started minimal techno in the early 1990s as a reaction to techno becoming "too ravey" (Wikipedia "Minimal techno") [S]; Basic Channel invented dub techno in Berlin from 1993 ([Futureproof Genre Explorer, dub techno](https://genreexplorer.futureproofmusicschool.com/genre/dub-techno)) [S]. These roots are history for this brief, not its era.

**The shared core** (holds in every subdivision; lose it and the track stops being techno) [S, as above]:
1. Four on the floor at 120-160 bpm, made for a continuous DJ set: long, mixable ends, a steady tempo.
2. The kick is the centre of the record: tuned, mono, with the low end built around it (a rumble or an offbeat bass, never both fighting the kick).
3. Repetition with slow change: one loop of 1-8 bars, changed by filters, decay, density and layers, an element every 8-16 bars; few full stops.
4. Rhythm and timbre over melody: the hook is a sound (an acid line, a stab, a distorted riff, a loop), not a tune.
5. Machine sounds (808 / 909 / 303 lineage, synthesis, processed samples), voices as fragments.

The boundaries in measurement: the catalogue's median tempo rises from 123 (1990) to 136-138 (1995-2004), falls to 125-128 (2007-2018) and turns up from 2019 (130); the sub at 31.5-63 Hz grows by 4.5-7 dB between the 2000s and 2008-14 and then holds; tracks are longest in 2009-18 (about 6:10) and shorter from 2019 (5:29) ("Catalogue measurements") [M].

Disagreements kept: the rumble's low-pass runs from 96.6 Hz (MusicRadar) through 150-250 Hz (Futureproof, Bonedo, MusicRadar 2023) to 800 Hz (Computer Music): the lower, the more it is sub only; the higher, the more "rattle" and grit is heard. **Glue** [S]: the kick and rumble on one group with a glue compressor (about 5 dB of gain reduction in Attack's reverb-kick walk-through) and overdrive or a limiter "to control the transient peak"; a drum bus with drive and low boost (Ableton Drum Buss drive 17%, "boom" 45% at 55 Hz, out -3 dB: Attack); rumble, bass and sometimes the lead sidechained to the kick (Futureproof); the ride and pads ducked by the kick (MusicRadar 2025, Attack); a small hall for mid-sized clubs, a large hall or warehouse space for big rooms (MusicRadar 2023); on the master, mid-side: cut 200-400 Hz in the sides, a high shelf above 8 kHz on the sides (Futureproof).

**Low end and top end facts** (checklist item 5) [S]: kick fundamental 45-60 Hz tuned to the key; rumble under 100-250 Hz, mono; mono below 120-150 Hz; the top from 909 hats and ride (6-bit sampled metal), distorted percussion and the acid's resonance: tonal and gritty, not hiss.

**Characteristic imperfection** (item 6) [S, H]: hardware grit (6-bit cymbals, 303 slides that overshoot, overdriven mixers: [Attack, distorted 90s techno drums](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/tutorials/in-the-red-mixer-distorted-90s-techno-drums/)), swing on percussion, loops that "groove at their own pace" against the grid (hard groove), a ride or open hat that varies hit to hit. Wrong in every strand: a kick that drifts in time, a wide or phasey sub, a rumble that masks the next kick (no ducking), distortion that eats the kick's transient, a tempo that changes inside the track (DJs cannot mix it).

**Census of a reference arrangement** (item 8): no published multitrack of a modern techno record was found. The walk-throughs count about 6-9 sources at the peak: kick, rumble, closed hat, open hat or ride, clap, one or two percussion loops, the acid or lead, a texture (Futureproof, MusicRadar 2023, Attack) [S, counted]. **Remix table** (item 9): no earlier Spectre track is techno; the trance tracks share the four-on-the-floor kick, and nothing in them changes with this brief.

## Strands of the modern era (2009-2026), with scenes as craft schools
Scenes are schools of craft, not borders: Berlin (Berghain / Ostgut Ton, Tresor: heavy, dry, functional, mid-130s; the hypnotic side), Detroit (soul, swing, chords, the 909 and the long tradition), Belgium and the Netherlands (the 2010s-2020s peak-time and hard schools: KNTXT, Lenske, the hard-techno festival circuit; the older new-beat and EBM line), the UK (Birmingham loops and industrial techno, Surgeon, Regis, Perc; the hard-groove line from Ben Sims; Glasgow and Manchester in the fast revival), Tbilisi (Bassiani: fast, hard, a club as a political space), Italy and Spain (hard groove and schranz revivals). Reading it:
1. **Techno is the heaviest genre measured in this project**: `techno_2015` holds **+15 dB at 63 Hz** over 1 kHz (dubstep +8, trance +8, hip hop +8.5) and +11 at 100 Hz. Read through the Bark analysis, `trance_2015` is **7.4 dB short at 32-97 Hz**, 4.5-4.9 dB short at 97-183 Hz and 4 dB short at 183-291 Hz (`refcurve.py check`); `dubstep_2015` 6.8 / 5.5 / 3.7 dB short; `hiphop_2015` 6.6 dB short at 32-97 Hz and 2.5-4 dB too dark above 4 kHz. From 400 Hz to 12 kHz trance and dubstep read within 1.5 dB. The reason is in the music: a kick and rumble against sparse mids (the reference band is 510 Hz-2 kHz). So techno gets its own curve, and the mix must supply the low end (kick and rumble), not the master's EQ.
2. **The era moved the sub, not the top**: the 1990s and the minimal 2000s carry 4.5-7 dB less at 31.5-63 Hz than 2015-21 and are 1-2 dB brighter at 2-5 kHz; **2008-14, 2015-21 and 2018-21 agree within 0.5-1.5 dB in every band**: the modern low end arrived around 2008 (the Berghain and rumble decade) and has held since.
3. **The strands differ more than the recent years**: the hard school is 1-2 dB brighter at 2-8 kHz and 1-1.5 dB leaner at 250-500 Hz (distortion and a mid-forward kick); dub techno is 3.5-5.5 dB darker from 5 kHz and the quietest (-15.1); melodic techno has 4-4.5 dB less at 50-160 Hz (a clean kick and offbeat sub, not a rumble) and is darker; minimal and acid sit within 1.5 dB of `techno_2015` below 12 kHz.
4. **Whole tracks against loud sections**: whole-track medians (-11.4 to -12.1 since 2015; the hard school -12.0) sit 4-5 dB under the loud sections of current releases (Hard Techno -7.3, Peak Time -8.6, Mixgraph): long, thin DJ intros and outros and breakdowns, as in psytrance (docs/TRANCE.md: 4.4 dB). A Spectre master's integrated level is therefore set from the loud section, not from the whole-track median ("Mastering per subdivision").

**Album dynamics** [M] (Dynamic Range Database, https://dr.loudness-war.info/, read 2026-11-26; DR = crest-based album dynamic range, higher is more dynamic): 1990s albums **DR 9-14** (Robert Hood *Internal Empire* 1994 and *Nighttime World* 1995: 13; Plastikman 1993-98: 10-12; Jeff Mills 1996-97: 9-12; Speedy J 1993-97: 9-12; Basic Channel *BCD*: 14); the 2000s loudness war **DR 6-8** (Speedy J *A Shocking Hobby* 2000 and *Loudboxer* 2002: 6; Robert Hood *Minimal Nation* CD 2009: 8); 2010s **DR 9-10** (Blawan 2011, 2015, vinyl: 10; Marcel Dettmann 2014, vinyl: 9; Plastikman *EX* 2014: 9; Tale of Us *Endless* 2017: 9). Most 2020s hard-techno artists have no entries. Techno albums stay moderately dynamic (DR 9-10, against DR 4-6 for trailer music), partly because many are vinyl rips; single-track club masters are louder (the -7.3 to -8.6 loud sections).

## The strand for this brief: "Fast & Furious"
The brief: *speed, drive and fury*; not the film: no film themes, titles, logos, soundtrack references, car samples or catchphrases.

**Recommendation: hard techno at 150 bpm as the frame (speed and fury), with hard groove's looped, swung percussion inside it (the drive, and what keeps fury from turning into mindless aggression) and a distorted acid line as the fury's voice; industrial grit on single layers only. A DJ-tool arc of about 6:00 that never stops: speed is held, fury is built by density, distortion and subdivision.** [H, reasoned from the strands above]
- *Speed* is the tempo first: 150 is the centre of the strand the sources call peak-time hard techno (150-155, Futureproof), inside the measured middle half of hard techno (136-155, Mixgraph) and 10 bpm over techno's 2025 yearly median (140): fast against the genre's own norm, not a novelty tempo. Above 155-160 the sources place hardstyle, hardcore and hardtekk; there the kick turns into a "donk" and the crowd into "fist-pumping rather than actual dancing" (Attack 2023). Speed is also *rate*: 16th hats from the first minutes, 16th-note acid, 16th-triplet rolls at section ends, a rumble that pumps on every beat. Tempo stays constant (a DJ tool; and a ramp would read as a film's chase): the feeling of acceleration comes from subdivision and density, not bpm.
- *Drive* is the kick and rumble that never leave for long (one short breakdown) plus groove. The groove research says the pleasure of wanting to move peaks at medium syncopation (an inverted U: Witek et al., *PLOS ONE* 2014) and that the "optimal tempo for groove" for drum breaks is about 100-120 bpm (Etani et al., *Frontiers in Psychology* 2018): at 150 the body cannot take every beat as a step, so the drive must come from a steady pulse with **syncopated, swung percussion on top** (hard groove's congas, toms and chopped loops, swing about 52-54% on them only) [S]. Hard groove "hit in the hips" where industrial techno "drove forward" (Bandcamp Daily 2024): this brief wants both, the motor of hard techno and the hips of hard groove.
- *Fury* is distortion, resonance and pressure that rise and are held: a distorted kick and rumble, an acid line whose cutoff, resonance and drive climb over 16-32 bars, a distorted lead-bass riff answering it, noise hits, louder and denser layers. Faster tempo raises arousal (Husain, Thompson & Schellenberg, *Music Perception* 2002) [S]; roughness (fast amplitude modulation, 30-150 Hz) is the acoustic niche of alarm (Arnal et al., *Current Biology* 2015; docs/DUBSTEP.md) [S]: a resonant acid pushed into self-oscillation and a distorted rumble supply it. Fury needs an opponent to push against: the groove that stays, so the distortion has something to drive.
- *Why not the other strands*: peak-time at 132-138 carries drive but not speed; industrial techno at 130-140 is dread and pressure, darker than fury; schranz at 150+ is fury without a hook (a percussion assault, little to remember); hypnotic, dub, minimal and melodic are the wrong energy (trance, depth, restraint, cinematic sadness); acid techno alone is a colour, used here inside the hard frame.

**Arc** (224 bars = 5:58 at 150 bpm; counts [H] inside the sourced form):
| Section | Bars | Feeling | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ignition (intro) | 16 | engine on | kick and rumble only (DJ-mixable), the rumble's low-pass closed, opening a little; closed hats from bar 9 |
| Engine | 32 | the machine runs | 16th hats, offbeat open hat, clap on 2 and 4, a ride from bar 17; a hard-groove percussion loop (congas / toms, swung) gives the hips; the acid enters filtered nearly shut |
| Drive | 32 | acceleration felt | the acid opens over 16 bars, a distorted lead-bass riff of one or two notes answers it; the rumble's drive rises; a kick repeat or a tom fill at every 8th bar |
| Pressure (breakdown) | 16 | held breath, not rest | the kick out, the rumble and acid continue, filtered and more resonant; a noise sweep, a short shouted or spoken fragment (optional), a 909 snare roll in the last 2 bars climbing to 16th triplets; **no silence, no pad lift** |
| Fury (peak) | 48 | full speed | kick back at once with everything, the acid fully open and distorted, a rave stab or hoover hit as a weapon (8-bar calls), a polymetric 3-step tom loop over the 4/4 in the second half; something changes every 8 bars |
| Overdrive | 32 | past the limit | the densest section: rides to 16ths, percussion doubled, the acid at self-oscillation edge, the rumble at its most distorted; the hook sound reduced to its most aggressive fragment |
| Exhaust (outro) | 48 | cooling metal | elements leave every 8 bars (stab, acid, lead-bass, percussion), the last 16 bars kick, rumble and hats (DJ-mixable) |

**Nearest wrong feelings** (editorial [H]; what separates each from the brief):
| Wrong feeling | What it sounds like | What separates the brief |
|---|---|---|
| **A car-chase film score** | orchestral string ostinati and brass stabs, taiko hits on picture cuts, tempo and dynamics that follow an imagined scene, a heroic theme, a final hit | no picture: four on the floor at one tempo for six minutes, repetition as the drive, no orchestra, no cue-like hits or stingers, no theme; the energy is a dancefloor's, not a scene's |

| **Mindless aggression without groove** | every element distorted all the time, a straight kick at 160+, no swing, no percussion interplay, no change for minutes, or changes for their own sake; "fist-pumping rather than actual dancing" (Attack 2023) | syncopated, swung percussion and ghost notes against the straight kick; a rumble that breathes with the kick; distortion that rises and is placed (layer by layer, section by section); something changes every 8-16 bars |
| **A video-game racing-soundtrack cliché** | major-key synth-rock or eurobeat leads, engine revs, tyre screeches, nitro whooshes, countdown beeps, 8-bit arps, a "turbo" riser on every section | no sound effects that illustrate cars or speed; no major-key hero lead; speed is abstract (tempo, subdivision, pressure), minor or modal one- and two-note riffs |

| also: **gabber / hardcore** | 170-200 bpm, a "donk" or hardstyle kick with a long pitched tail, screamed samples | 150, a short tuned kick plus a separate rumble; fury with a groove |

**Nearest wrong sounds** (checklist item 7) [H, from the sections above]: a big-room "tok" kick and white-noise riser builds (EDM); a supersaw anthem breakdown (trance); orchestral hits and taiko (score); car and engine samples (illustration); a wide, chorused sub or a rumble without ducking (mud); a hardstyle reverse-bass or donk kick (hardstyle / hardtekk); trap hi-hat rolls (another genre); a clean, undistorted 2010s Drumcode kick at 150 (reads sped-up peak-time, not hard techno).

## Engine mapping
What modern techno needs, against what the engine has (docs/MANUAL.md "Production", "Modern strands", "Bass music", "Bass music, fitted"; engine/synth.py, engine/tr909.py, engine/acid.py, engine/mixbus.py, engine/bassfx.py, engine/studio.py, read 2026-11-26). | Need | Engine |
|---|---|
