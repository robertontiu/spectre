# Journal: 017 captive (Spectre)

## 1. Sit with it (no render)

**In the body.** It starts in the chest as a weight that is not mine: something lower than my own voice puts a hand flat on the sternum and leans. The fight is the diaphragm pushing back in short bursts, breath caught between two pushes. The panic is not speed; it is the inside going rough, the way a held scream feels in the throat before it is let out: the same heartbeat, but everything on top of it shaking faster and faster. The acceptance is cold in the hands and feet: the same pressure, but I have stopped pushing, so it no longer hurts; it just repeats. The absence is the moment you notice the weight is gone and so is the thing it was pressing on: a room after the sub stops on a system, where your ribs still expect it.
**In time.** 140 written, 70 felt, and I never touch the tempo: captivity is a clock that does not care. Inside it the rate climbs (8ths, triplets, 16ths, then modulation above 30 Hz) and then falls to one looping bar. **In space.** Drop 1 is close and narrow (a struggle at arm's length, mono low end, the soul's voice only a little wider than the captor's); the breakdown opens into a long dark hall; drop 2 is dry and narrow again, with no room at all; the absence is the hall without the source, then nothing.

**Nearest wrong feelings, and what separates them** (docs/DUBSTEP.md table, made mine):
- *A horror trailer* (braams, stingers, a drone with no groove, a final hit): every threat here has a groove under it, there is no stinger and no last hit; the track ends by removal, not by a bang.
- *Generic festival brostep aggression* (every drop at max, triumphant riffs, drop 2 = drop 1 louder): drop 2 is quieter by design (about 1.5-2 LU under drop 1), simpler, with no response voice; build 2 *loses* energy.
- *A video-game boss fight* (orchestral hits, heroic minor lead, escalation to victory): no lead melody at all above the basses, no choir stabs, no phase two that wins; phase two is surrender.
- *Sad ambient* (warm pads, piano, tears): no warm pad, no piano anywhere; the breakdown's atmosphere is a filtered cold texture plus the soul's voice, and acceptance carries on mechanically.
- *Shock value without feeling* (screams, harshness everywhere): the roughness (the 30-150 Hz modulation) peaks exactly once, in the panic, and drains; the voice is two sung words, not a scream sample.
- *Melodic-dubstep catharsis*: no major chord, no lift; the only "harmony" is the ♭2 rubbing against the root.

**Decisions, committed.**
- **Tempo 140**, half time, straight grid in the drops (the captor is a machine), the soul's parts slightly late (+12 ms push: the human side; lesson 005's two time feels).
- **Key: F♯ Phrygian** (F♯ minor with G natural as the ♭2). Sub root F♯1 = 46 Hz, inside the sourced 40-60 Hz. The **♭2 (G) is the captor's note**: whenever the captor leans on G over the F♯ sub, the grip tightens. The soul's notes reach upward (A, C♯, E: the minor third, fifth, seventh), never resolving.
- **Corner of the genre:** a **deep / 140 frame** (tempo, space, the sub as the subject, reverb as composition in the breakdown) carrying **tearout** in drop 1 (two bass voices in call and response, mechanical stops, a metallic snare with a gated tail) and **riddim** in drop 2 (one motif looping, no answer, kick on the snare, triplet hat), returning to the deep strand's emptiness. Bleeding-edge checklist items I use: 1 (140 for the UK side), 1b (length about 3:55), 2 (a drop whose character keeps changing: per-note modulation rates, a resampled chop in 1C), 3 (precision and silence: answers cut off), 4 (control and minimalism in the absence). Master: `--genre dubstep --lufs -8.5` (target `dubstep_2015`, -1 dBTP), reading drop 1's short-term loudness (aim about -6.5 to -7) and not letting the outro be lifted.
- **The three sounds the track stands on:**
  1. **The captor**: an `fmgrowl` stack, low and heavy, long calls with the FM index wobbling at slow note values (1/4, 1/8), a dark vowel (oh-ah), multiband (clean low, folded mids, clipped top). It holds; it does not plead.
  2. **The soul**: a second mid-bass voice an octave and more above, a `wavetable` on the `vocal` table (formant-ready), talking "ee-ah", short phrases that rise in pitch and get cut off. It is the voice that fights.
  3. **The sub**: one clean `sub`, mono, under both, the body that is held. It is the last thing to leave.
- **Parts and layers** (DUBSTEP.md layer table):
  - *Kick* (stack): `kicksub` tuned F♯1 (sub layer, 45-60 Hz), `kickpunch` ~120 Hz (punch), `kickclick` (click 2-5 kHz). Drum bus with `clip`.
  - *Snare* (stack): `snare909` tuned 200 Hz (body), `clap909` high-passed (crack and top, the only transient besides the 909's), a gated-reverb return as tail.
  - *Hats*: `metalhat` closed, sparse 8ths in drop 1, triplets in drop 2 (riddim), doubled in the panic.
  - *Sub*: `sub` sine (role sub), sidechained to kick and snare.
  - *Captor* (stack): body (`fmgrowl` low operators, role upper-bass, 100 Hz-1 kHz), bite (a folded / clipped copy, 1-4 kHz, role support), optional comb screech top (4-10 kHz, role air) only if the stack view says the top is empty.
  - *Soul* (stack): talking body (`wavetable` vocal, role upper-bass, high-passed ~180 Hz), air (role air) only if needed.
  - *Atmosphere*: a cold filtered texture (`polysynth` noise and a fifth-less drone) in intro and breakdown; the granular freeze of the captor's motif in the absence.
  - *Voice*: a `singer` phrase of two words, "still here", the soul's own, sung once clean in the intro; in the breakdown pitched down and darkened with the `vowel` filter; never in the drops. In the absence it is not there: the grain cloud is of the captor's motif, not of the voice (the voice is what was taken).
  - *FX*: a sub drop into drop 1, one reverse swell, silence. No noise riser into drop 2.

**Breaking my own habits.** Reading the READMEs of the half-time tracks (001-006, 011) and the build-and-drop ones (007, 013): I keep (a) **cutting to one beat (or half a beat) of silence right before every hook or drop** (002 every 4 bars, 003, 004, 005, 007, 013); (b) **ending by returning to the intro's lone element** (004 "the intro again", 006 the drone, 011 the clean piano, 013 the lone G, 015 the far "again"); (c) **putting the biggest section last** (002 hook 3, 003 hook 3, 005 hook 3, 006 hook 4, 007 second wave, 013 main drop); (d) an intro of one lone instrument filtering open. **Departures, for this feeling:**
1. **The peak is the first third.** Drop 1 (around 0:41-1:22) is the loudest, busiest moment of the track; everything after it is less. Energy falls; that *is* captivity accepted. This breaks (c) and the festival habit at once.
2. **No silence before drop 1.** The taking is not anticipated, it is an interruption: the soul's sung phrase is still sounding when the captor lands on top of it. Silence is saved for the panic's end (the only hard cut to nothing in the track) and for the ending.
3. **The ending does not return to the intro.** The intro's voice never comes back. The outro is removal (mid-bass, drums, sub, then the frozen cloud of the captor's motif dissolves) and then real silence, several bars of it, inside the track's length.
4. **The intro starts with the sub alone** (the body, before anything happens to it), not a lone melodic instrument behind a filter.

## 2. Sound stage: three captors, two souls (v001)
Heard (overview, zooms on bars 1-2, 17-18, 25-28): the soul as a `wavetable` on the vocal table (soul A) talks: every note draws formant arches that rise with the pitch envelope, a voice reaching up; the FM soul (B) is thin and 1.7 LU quieter with almost nothing at 250 Hz-2 kHz. **Soul A chosen.** All three captors are a wash: capB is hiss (centroid 5.2 kHz, the comb plus inharmonic ratios: shock value, out), capA and capC fill 250 Hz-2 kHz evenly but the 1/8 index wobble does not show as rhythm at all; with feedback, fold and OTT on legato notes the captor is a wall, not a voice that grips. A captor that does not articulate cannot have a dialogue. capC (harsh wavetable, darker, smoother) is kept in reserve for drop 2's cold riddim motif. Move: rebuild the captor's articulation: rests between its notes, a synced cutoff and level movement on top of the index, less feedback and OTT, and compare two ways of making the grip rhythmic.

## 3. Sound stage: why the captor was a wall (v002 + a scratch test)
v002's two re-articulated captors finally have gaps between notes, but inside each note the spectrum is still a flat hiss-wash from 100 Hz to 8 kHz: no wub, no honk; the 1/16 level square of A3 shows only as faint stripes. A scratch test of the bare `fmgrowl` (feedback 0, one voice, index on a falling saw at the note's own rate) shows each note as a row of clear falling arches, and the per-note rates read as rhythm (the 1/8t bar is seven hits). Operator feedback and unison are what turned it to noise, and the OTT and three compressors flattened what movement remained. **The captor is the bare FM wub:** feedback 0, one voice in the body, the vowel sweeping with the wub; width and grit only in a separate bite layer an octave up; light multiband. It is a clearer, colder machine: what a grip should be.

## 4. The loop: the captor has no weight (v003)
The core loop (captor call 2 bars, soul answer 2 bars, kick on 1 with a ghost on the and-of-2, snare on 3) reads right in the picture: the captor's falling FM arches against the soul's clean, rising formant ladders, two worlds. But `--clarity` says the grip has no hand: 150-400 Hz holds 5% of the energy and 100-250 Hz sits 7.3 dB under `dubstep_2015`; the vowel and the role preset's 245 Hz cut scoop the captor exactly where a weight on the chest lives (the demo's warning). The kick's punch layer is 13 dB under its sub. Move: a third captor layer, `weight`, a clean low FM body (100-400 Hz, mono, following the same wub) and no preset cut on the body; the kick's punch up 4 dB.

## 5. The loop: a conversation, not a fight (v004)
The weight layer works: 100-250 Hz from -7.3 to -4.0 dB against the target and 315-400 Hz on it, the captor now owning 150-400 Hz; the punch sits 9 dB under the kick's sub instead of 13. But the loop takes turns politely: two bars each, a gap, the other speaks. That is a debate. A fight is overlap: the soul starts before the captor has finished and the captor comes back before the soul is done. Move: the soul's answer starts on the and-of-4 of bar 2, under the captor's last note, and the captor re-enters on beat 4 of bar 4 with a short pickup that cuts the soul's held A3 (the captor always wins the bar line); the soul's attacks get harder (more drive, a fold) so it pushes rather than sings.

## 6. The loop carries it (v005)
Bars 1-4 now show the fight: the soul's first notes land under the captor's last F#, its ladder climbs (A2 to A3) and its held A3 is chopped by the captor's G1 pickup on beat 4 of bar 4, before the bar line. On repeat it is a struggle the captor keeps winning by a 16th. This is drop 1A. Stage 3 and the arrangement follow in the next entry.

## 7. Design the surprise (no render)
**(a) Certainties.** (1) The call and answer: the captor calls for two bars, the soul answers and is cut by the captor's pickup on beat 4 of every 4th bar: stated twice in 1A, twice in 1B (the answer shorter), twice in 1C (shorter still): six times before drop 2. (2) Kick on 1, snare on 3, unchanged from bar 9 to bar 56. (3) The soul's ladder always climbs (A, C#, E, F#, A). (4) After the intro's single sung phrase, the voice is "the soul's".
**(b) The surprises.** *S1, a promise broken (the main one):* in drop 2 the captor plays one bar and then leaves the next bar empty above the sub, exactly where the answer has always come; nothing answers, for 24 bars. A sudden change of texture in the downward direction, inside a frame that is otherwise certain (same kick and snare places, same root). It serves the cold acceptance: the soul has stopped answering. *S2, the only silence in the middle of the track:* the panic (subdivision and roughness climbing) is cut to two beats of digital silence at bar 56; nothing before it has stopped. It serves the panic's end, the moment the struggle breaks.
**(c) One flaw, taught early:** the sung "still here" is cut on "here" by the first drop (bar 25): the word never finishes. In the breakdown it returns pitched down and darkened, and this time it finishes (exhausted, changed); it never returns after that.
**(d) Proof:** S1: the 400 Hz-2 kHz level (mid band) in the second bar of each drop-2 phrase against the same bars of drop 1 (expect 15 dB or more down; the soul stem in drop 1 against nothing). S2: the loudness curve at bar 56-57 (the cut to under -60 dB) and the spectral flux step. The flaw: the voice stem stops within the first 16th of bar 25.

**Arrangement plan (136 bars, 3:53):** intro A 8 (the sub alone, a cold texture) · intro B 8 (a sparse deep-140 beat with a long dub reverb on the snare, the soul's ladder clean, "still here" at bar 13) · build 1 8 (the captor appears under a closing then opening filter, the soul's ladder cut shorter, "still here" again, the drop lands on "here") · drop 1A 8 · 1B 8 (the answer shorter, 16th hats, a resampled chop of the captor) · 1C 8 (a machine-gun chop, the answer cut to two notes) · panic 8 (the captor's wub rate pushed from 8 Hz into 30-90 Hz, the soul repeating in 16ths then 16th triplets, hats to 32nds; two beats of silence) · breakdown 16 (no drums, the hall, the voice pitched down and finished, the sub barely there, the captor a filtered shadow) · build 2 8 (energy falls: heartbeat kick, filters closing, no riser) · drop 2 A/B/C 24 (riddim: the `husk` voice, one bar of motif and one bar of nothing, kick on the snare, triplet hats; its drive, wavetable movement and filter drained across the 24 bars) · outro: mid-bass gone 8 (drums, sub and a frozen grain cloud of the motif) · drums gone 8 (sub and cloud) · sub gone 12 (the cloud fading) · silence 4.

**Expectation ledger** (to verify after render): intro teaches "the soul sings alone"; build 1 teaches "the captor is coming"; 1A teaches the call-and-answer and the pickup cut; 1B/1C keep the frame, shrinking the answer (expected direction, still certain); the panic breaks the rate certainty upward, then S2; breakdown re-states the flaw and finishes it; drop 2 breaks the answer (S1); the outro removes in the order the listener can follow.
