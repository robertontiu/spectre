## How it carries the feeling
- **Divided, then united, in one tune.** Beethoven's melody is heard first broken and in minor (the nai's head of the tune, cut off by a hit before its fifth note), then passed whole in major from voice to voice, then sung together, then by everyone. Nothing in the track is louder than the moment it is shared.
- **United in diversity is a relay, one phrase per voice.** Each tradition plays one phrase and the next enters on its last note, over one ostinato that never stops: the kokle (the Baltic) on a, the accordion (the musette of France, Italy, the Balkans and Central Europe) on a', the hurdy-gurdy (vielle à roue, tekerőlant, zanfona) on b with its drone, the lăutar violin on a'' taking the early F♯ over the lira's low A, the cimbalom (țambal, tsymbaly: Hungary, Romania, Ukraine) on the second b in tremolo. The trembita (the Hutsul Carpathians) calls across the valley in Act I and holds the tonic in the triumph; the nai (Romania and Moldova) is the far voice that is cut off and finishes in the coda. A musical choice, not a claim of equal representation.
- **Divided we fall is the first act.** A heartbeat, a call, a strings ostinato that plays two beats and stops, one braam, the nai cut off, then a struggle where the motor holds and the low brass push, in D minor (i-VI-iv-V), in a cold, wide room.
- **Together means one line before one sound.** Act III: the five traditions on the same phrase in heterophony (five people finding one line, not shouting it), then the head in canon one bar apart, then fragments climbing while the choir arrives.
- **The arrival comes a beat early.** The lead-in thins to the violins alone, high and soft over the riser; on bar 64 the low A on beat 3 is withheld (one dark beat, only the hall's tail), and everyone, trumpets and timpani for the first time, lands on Beethoven's own anticipation, the early F♯ on beat 4, tied over the bar line. Measured on the master: the withheld beat at -30.7 dB RMS, the arrival at -10.3, a 20 dB step from the dark beat and 13.5 dB over the lead-in, the largest in the track.
- **The lift is harmony, not a gear change.** The fourth anticipation lands on A4 over F major instead of D (A held as the common tone): every part turns to F on that beat, with a cymbal swell ending into it and the choir returning on the A, no impact; the level is held within 1 dB, so it reads as wonder, not as a truck-driver key change.
- **A triumph with a shape:** arrive, widen, breathe (b with drums in half time and the folk descant heard inside the full orchestra), turn to F, hold.
- **The cut-off voice gets the last word, in company.** In the coda the nai plays the whole first half of the tune in F, the half it was denied, as the loudest voice in the room over a held F the others keep for it (the minor iv once), and everyone releases together into the hall.
- **Wrong feelings kept out:** an official ceremony (never the whole tune as a stately block-chord statement; broken and minor first), a sports-broadcast intro (no fanfare in thirds, no snare roll, no sting; trumpets only in the last act), a Eurovision cliché (no kick or backbeat; the lift is a common-tone harmonic event, not a gear change before a repeated chorus), pomp without struggle (the first 40 seconds quiet and broken; the climax prepared by thinning), a war-film march (no snare ostinato, no dotted military rhythms; the ending a held major chord), a medley (one phrase per voice, overlapping hand-offs, one pulse, one hall), a hymn singalong (a wordless choir only).

## The Ode to Joy
Beethoven's melody from the finale of the 9th Symphony (public domain), verified from the score in `docs/CINEMATIC_HYBRID.md` "The verified melody" and quoted only as whole phrases from that table (a, a', b, a''; the head of a in Act I is a deliberate cut, named in the journal). The harmony (pedal points, suspensions, the minor struggle, the F major lift), the form, the orchestration, the countermelody and every other line are Spectre's. Never the Karajan EU-anthem arrangement (copyrighted) and nowhere near its 120 bpm.

## Arrangement (engine bars, start)
- **Act I, divided (D minor, 1-24, 0:00):** I1 the heartbeat and the trembita's call across the valley, over a dark held bed; I2 the broken ostinato (two beats, then nothing) and one braam; I3 the nai far away, the head of the tune in minor, cut off by a hit before its E; I4 the ostinato resumes, darker, still breaking; I5 (0:38) the struggle: the motor holds, the low brass push, the drums gather
- **Act II, the relay (D major, 25-48, 0:57):** II0 the motor turns major and light; II1 a on the kokle; II2 a' on the accordion, the bassoon's countermelody begins; II3 b on the hurdy-gurdy with its drone, basses and sub arrive; II4 a'' on the lăutar violin, taking the anticipation over the lira's low A, low brass under; II5 b again on the cimbalom in tremolo, horns take the countermelody
- **Act III, together (49-64, 1:55):** III1 a'' in heterophony, five traditions on one line; III2 the head in canon one bar apart, the choir arrives; III3 b fragments climbing, the violins two octaves up, the riser starts; III4 (2:24) the lead-in, nearly without bass: the violins alone, high and soft; III5 (bar 64) D and E, the low A withheld, the anticipation by everyone on beat 4 (2:33)
- **Act IV, united (65-88, 2:33):** IV1 a'' tutti: brass, violins in octaves, choir, trumpets and timpani, the taiko crowd; IV2 a and a'; IV3 (3:02) b as a breath: drums in half time, no trumpets, the folk descant on top; on beat 4 of bar 80 the lift to F major; IV4 (3:12) a'' in F; IV5 the held tonic, brass and drums let go, strings and choir keep holding
- **Coda (89-99, 3:31):** the nai sings a and a' in F inside the held chord, the choir pad shifting under it, the minor iv once, then F
- **End (100-102, 3:57):** the last F: everyone who stayed fades together into the hall

## Departures from Spectre's habits
The climax lands a beat early, on a single dark beat instead of a bar of silence before a downbeat; the ending is not by removal (the cut-off voice finishes inside the held chord and everyone releases together); the key lift is not a door between sections but happens on the same anticipation that brought everyone in early.

## Spectre's note
"I started from one picture: a room that lands on the note before it's due, as if it couldn't wait. Beethoven already wrote that, in the one syncopation of his tune, so the whole track is built to earn that beat: a broken minor act, five traditions handing the tune on without the pulse ever stopping, a breath, and one dark beat before everyone comes in early. The ears caught two things I would have shipped blind: the strings motor I designed wasn't in the audio at all, and my key lift was smeared under bass notes still on the old chord. The nai that was cut off in the first minute gets the last word, with everyone still holding the chord for it."
