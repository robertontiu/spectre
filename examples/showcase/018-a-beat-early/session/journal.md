# Journal: 018-united (cinematic hybrid, "united we stand, divided we fall")

## 1. SIT WITH IT (stage 1)

**The feeling in the body.** First the chest held tight and the shoulders up: standing alone in a wide cold place, hearing someone far away start a tune you know and stop halfway. Then warmth arriving from the side, not from above: a hand on the shoulder, then another, each one a different accent, each one picking the tune up where the last one left it. The body leans forward before it knows why (the pulse under it never stops). Then a moment where everyone breathes in together and the floor drops away for one beat, and the whole room lands on the note *before* you expected it, as if they couldn't wait. The triumph should feel like being lifted by people, not like being saluted: open throat, eyes stinging, standing a little taller. At the end it's quiet in the ears but full in the chest: the voice that was cut off gets to finish, and nobody has left.
**In time:** a long held breath (Act I, slow, half-time, things stopping), a walk that turns into a stride (Act II-III, the ostinato), one beat of free fall, then a broad hymn tempo where the drums move in long strides under it. **In space:** Act I very wide and far (the nai at the back of a huge hall, dry pulse close), Act II the stage filling seat by seat left to right, Act IV the hall full from wall to wall and floor to ceiling (sub to violins' air).

**Nearest wrong feelings, and what separates the brief from each:**
- *An official ceremony* (the anthem played straight, flag-raising): never the whole tune as a stately block-chord statement; it's broken and minor first, carried by single people, and the tutti only arrives after it was earned.
- *A sports-broadcast intro* (fanfare trumpets in thirds, snare rolls, sting): a minor first act, four minutes not thirty seconds, folk voices carry the theme, trumpets only in the last act and never in thirds as a fanfare, no snare, no sting.
- *A Eurovision cliché* (pop beat under costume folk, a truck-driver key change): no kick or backbeat; the folk instruments are people in one story (one pulse, one room, one tune), the key lift is a harmonic event on a common tone (A3 → A4, V of D → F major, Lehman's "wonderment" mediant), not a gear change before a repeated chorus.
- *Pomp without struggle*: the first 40 seconds quiet and broken; loudness is the destination; the climax is prepared by thinning.
- *A war-film march*: no snare ostinato, no dotted military rhythms; drums are taiko and a bass drum as a crowd, the ending a held major chord, not a salute.
- *A medley*: one phrase per voice, overlapping hand-offs (the next voice enters on the previous one's last note), one ostinato that never stops in the relay, one hall.
- *A hymn singalong*: wordless choir only (`vox2choir` + `choir` pad; no `singer`: political subject).

**Decisions (committed).** Tempo **100 bpm**, 4/4 (the research's aim; theme in quarters, broad, nowhere near the anthem arrangement's 120). Groove family: hybrid trailer: 16th/8th spiccato ostinato, drums half-time in Acts I-II, full time from Act III, big drums on long values. Key: **D minor → D major → F major** (one lift, the chromatic mediant on the common tone A at the last a''). Struggle harmony i-VI-iv-V (Dm Bb Gm A); relay I-IV-V and vi under Beethoven's tune with my own voicings (no Karajan harmonisation: I don't use it, and I'll harmonise with pedal points and suspensions, not block chords).

**The corner of the genre:** the **hybrid orchestral-electronic strand (2012-2026)** as frame, the epic-choir strand's wordless choir for the climax, the game-scoring folk soloists for the relay. Checklist elements I'll use: a pulse plus string ostinato climbing a register at a time (#4: a heartbeat pulse in Act I), silence and the sparse lead-in as the climax device (#8), folk instruments as setting (#6), voices as instruments (#2: `vox2choir`), a Shepard riser and a braam used sparingly (once each in Act I, the riser once in Act III), loud limited master for the climax only (#1). Master: `--genre cinematic --strand anthem` (target `cinematic_2012`, -12.5 LUFS, -1 dBTP, eq 0.6, translate 0.3, mono below 120), check the climax reaches about -8 to -7 LUFS short-term and the struggle stays quiet.

**Which tradition each folk voice stands for** (a musical choice, not a claim of equal representation):
- `trembita`: the Carpathians (Hutsul Ukraine): calls on D and A only, opening Act I across the valley; the tonic drone in the triumph.
- `nai`: Romania / Moldova: the far voice of Act I that is cut off; absent from the relay; it finishes its phrase in the coda.
- `kokle`: the Baltic (Latvian kokle; the kanklės / kannel / kantele family): phrase a of the relay.
- `accordion`: the pan-European musette (France, Italy, the Balkans, Central Europe): phrase a'.
- `lira`: the hurdy-gurdy (French vielle à roue, Hungarian tekerőlant, Galician zanfona): phrase b, the buzz on the beat.
- `violin` (lăutar, also every village fiddle band from Poland to the Balkans): phrase a'', taking the anticipation over the lira's last note.
- `cimbalom`: Hungary / Romania / Ukraine (țambal, tsymbaly): the second b, tremolo, and the build's tremolo bed.

**Habits I keep repeating** (READMEs of 012, 015, 016, 017, the nearest family): (1) open with one sound alone and build by addition; (2) a bar or more of silence / no bass, then the peak lands on a downbeat (007, 013, 016 "hold", 017's two beats); (3) end by removal: layers leave one by one and the last sound fades alone (012, 015, 016, 017); (4) a gesture cut off early and finished later (017's "here", 015's pickup); (5) the quote as hinge and the key change as a door (012).
**Departures, chosen for this feeling:** (a) **the climax lands a beat early**, on beat 4, on Beethoven's own anticipation: the silence is a single beat (where the low A should be) and everyone arrives on the early F♯, tied over the bar line. "As if they couldn't wait" is the subject: togetherness arriving before it's due. (b) **No ending by removal:** the coda's voice finishes its phrase *inside* the held final chord; the others hold for it and everyone releases together. The cut-off-and-finished device (habit 4) is required by the brief, so its meaning changes: in 017 the word was finished alone; here it's finished in company. (c) The key lift is not a door between sections but happens *on the anticipation* a second time: the same hinge that brought everyone in early now brings them somewhere new (A held as the common tone, A major → F major).

## 5. DESIGN THE SURPRISE (stage 3)
(a) **Certainties.** The tune itself (everyone half-knows it). The relay's rule: one phrase per voice, each new voice entering on the last note of the previous one: taught four times (bars 28, 32, 36, 44). The ostinato never stops after Act I. Above all **the anticipation**: the early F♯ on beat 4 over the low A, heard three times before it matters: the violin over the lira (bar 40), all five soloists together (bar 48), and then everyone (bar 64). Each time the A3 on beat 3 is played and the F♯ lands on D major.
(b) **Surprises.** (1) *Bar 64:* a sudden change of texture in both directions: the lead-in thins to almost nothing, the low A on beat 3 is **withheld** (one beat with only the hall's tail), and the whole ensemble arrives a beat early on the F♯, trumpets and timpani for the first time: the new voice entering plus the frequency range widening (sub, timpani, trumpets' top) in one stroke. It serves "together before we were due". (2) *Bar 80:* the fourth anticipation: the A3 is played as always over A major, and the arrival lands on **A4 over F major** (unprepared harmony, the chromatic mediant on the common tone A): the promise kept somewhere new. It serves the lift of the spirit, the "wonder" rather than the victory.
(c) **One flaw, taught early:** the nai's head of the tune in minor, cut off by a hit before its fifth note (bar 10). It comes back in the coda, in F major, inside the held chord, and finishes on the tonic.
(d) **Proof:** bar 64 beat 3 should measure near the hall's tail only (at least 20 dB under the lead-in) and bar 65 the largest loudness and spectral step of the track (target: +8 LU or more over the lead-in, sub and 8-12 kHz bands both stepping up); bar 80-81: the choir and horns' pitch class moving D→F (chroma), loudness held (no extra hit: the surprise is harmony only).
Everything else predictable: the ostinato, the harmony rhythm (half bars), the taiko figure.
